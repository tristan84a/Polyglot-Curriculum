LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC GUIDANCE
Haitian Creole has predominantly SVO order, largely invariant verbs, preverbal tense/aspect/mood markers, postposed definite articles, and no grammatical gender agreement. It is not simply French with simplified spelling. Use contemporary official-style Haitian Creole spelling with accents and nasal vowel distinctions. Avoid French spelling, silent conjugation, or gender agreement. Standard answers must be independent of neighboring cards, with English prompts distinguishing habitual, progressive, past and future where these would yield different Haitian Creole answers.

Article forms depend on the phonological ending of the preceding word: kay la, liv la, timoun nan, chen an and machin nan. Teach these as a productive system, not interchangeable spellings. The definite plural marker yo follows the noun phrase. Indefinite yon precedes the noun. Adjectives commonly follow nouns, but important common adjectives such as gwo and ti precede them.

Copular constructions differ: Sa se yon liv, but Kay la gwo, and Mwen la. Do not insert se indiscriminately. Teach negation with pa and its interaction with tense/aspect markers. Unmarked verbs can have present or completed interpretations depending on context; use explicit time adverbs or markers when an exact answer is needed. The progressive marker ap contracts with preceding pronouns: m ap, l ap, n ap, y ap. Distinguish te (past), ap (progressive and some future uses), pral (prospective/near future), konn (habitual) and ta (conditional).
