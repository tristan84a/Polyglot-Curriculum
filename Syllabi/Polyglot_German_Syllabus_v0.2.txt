PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for German in
Polyglot. It specifies what is taught, its dependency order, and how
major concepts should be represented in cards. It does not record
learner or deck progress. Routine card generation therefore does not
amend this document.

Vocabulary acquisition is continuous throughout the curriculum. Grammar
units define structural progression but do not freeze vocabulary. Units
have no fixed card quota. Introduction and establishment are distinct:
a unit may begin, become intelligible, and continue toward establishment
through later double-duty cards while another compatible unit begins.

The syllabus is a roadmap, not a requirement that card generation follow
every numbered unit as a sealed block. A prerequisite-safe local detour,
early appearance of a useful later form, or delayed completion of an
earlier paradigm does not require a syllabus amendment when the sequence
remains pedagogically clear and later converges on the roadmap.

German is not treated as French with different vocabulary. Case, clause
architecture, verbal brackets, separable verbs, adjective declension,
word formation, modal particles, and register are introduced according
to their importance within German itself.

TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED — A form, word, construction, or distinction has begun to be
demonstrated.

ESTABLISHED — The canonical curriculum has demonstrated the item
sufficiently for its basic operation to be assumed later. This describes
the curriculum, not learner performance.

REUSABLE — An established item or structure may appear naturally in
later cards without being the principal teaching objective.

LEXICAL ANCHOR — A word deliberately selected to make a grammatical or
semantic pattern visible. Anchors do not limit additional vocabulary.

CANONICAL SEQUENCE — The static pedagogical ordering of cards and
concepts. Scheduling frequency may adapt to performance; curriculum
order does not.

COMPLETION STANDARD — The coverage required before a unit’s principal
concept may be treated as established.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED — Material postponed because it would obscure the
current pattern, depends on later knowledge, or deserves separate
treatment.

COURSE-WIDE GERMAN PRINCIPLES

1.  Vocabulary grows throughout all stages; grammar is not demonstrated
    indefinitely with a tiny closed vocabulary.
2.  When grammar is new, lexical novelty is initially restrained enough
    that the pattern remains visible. Lexical freedom increases as the
    pattern becomes established.
3.  Productive rules precede exceptions and competing paradigms.
4.  Grammar is normally learned through controlled examples rather than
    terminology questions.
5.  German nouns are capitalized from the beginning and grammatical
    gender is treated as a persistent lexical property.
6.  German word order is taught as a clause system. Verb-second order is
    foundational rather than an SVO rule with later “inversions.”
7.  Case is developed through meaningful grammatical functions and
    governing structures rather than presented initially as one large
    declension table.
8.  Separable verbs, modal constructions and the verbal bracket are
    introduced early enough that ordinary German vocabulary need not be
    artificially avoided.
9.  Established grammar may be combined freely in later units.
10. Common contemporary German precedes literary, archaic and unusually
    formal structures.
11. Register differences, including spoken/written tense preferences and
    modal particles, are taught explicitly.
12. Germany-oriented neutral Standard German provides the initial
    reference point; major Austrian, Swiss and regional standard usage
    is introduced later without being treated as defective German.

OPENING PACING DIRECTIVE

Use a compact seed before productive German begins. Haus, Mann, Frau, Kind
and groß, klein, gut, schlecht are the preferred default anchors unless a
specific card-design reason calls for substitution. Move promptly into
nominative articles, predicative description, pronouns, and sein rather
than completing broad noun/adjective lists first.

The early French deck may be copied closely for pacing and lexical
interleaving wherever German structure permits. German article gender,
V2, case, and verb behavior remain authoritative where the languages
diverge. DE-01 and DE-02 are continuing lexical strands, not gates that
require dozens of standalone vocabulary cards. DE-03 through DE-10 are
likewise early structural strands rather than a demand for serial mastery.
Pronouns, sein, haben, ordinary lexical verbs, V2, negation, first case
contrasts, and basic questions may overlap once each new pattern is clear
enough not to obscure the next.

STAGE 1 — BOOTSTRAP

DE-01 — Core concrete nouns

PREREQUISITES: None. OBJECTIVE: Establish a useful stock of highly
concrete nouns while making capitalization and grammatical gender
visible from the beginning. CORE PATTERN: German nouns are capitalized
and belong lexically to masculine, feminine or neuter gender. LEXICAL
ANCHORS: Haus, Mann, Frau, Kind, Buch, Auto, Katze, Hund. CARD STRATEGY:
Begin with high-frequency imageable nouns. Reuse them quickly in later
descriptive and verbal sentences. Gender should accompany the developing
noun system rather than be repaired later. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Add
concrete everyday nouns useful across many later constructions.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Large thematic lists, rare synonyms, specialist
vocabulary and exhaustive plural morphology. COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough
varied nouns exist to support early sentences without constant lexical
repetition, and noun capitalization/gender are established expectations.
LEADS TO: DE-03, DE-05 and later case/noun-phrase work.

DE-02 — Core adjectives

PREREQUISITES: DE-01 beginning noun stock. OBJECTIVE: Introduce common
descriptive meanings before the full adjective-declension system.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: groß, klein, gut, schlecht, neu, alt. CARD STRATEGY:
Use predicative adjectives first where possible so lexical meaning can
be established without prematurely requiring adjective endings.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Add high-frequency physical and evaluative
adjectives. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full attributive adjective declension
and comparison. COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough adjective vocabulary exists
to support varied description. LEADS TO: DE-05, DE-52 onward.

DE-03 — Definite and indefinite articles: nominative

PREREQUISITES: DE-01. OBJECTIVE: Establish nominative der/die/das/die
and ein/eine/ein as ordinary noun-phrase components. CORE PATTERN:
Gender and number determine nominative article form. CARD STRATEGY:
Contrast genders across known nouns in simple subject noun phrases. Do
not yet present the entire four-case article table. VOCABULARY
DEVELOPMENT: New nouns continue reinforcing gender. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Accusative, dative and genitive paradigms. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Masculine, feminine, neuter and plural definite forms and
singular indefinite forms are established in nominative contexts. LEADS
TO: DE-21 onward.

DE-04 — Personal pronouns

PREREQUISITES: Minimal vocabulary. OBJECTIVE: Establish ich, du, er,
sie, es, wir, ihr, Sie, sie as subject forms. CARD STRATEGY: Make
person, number and informal/formal distinctions explicit through use.
Preserve capitalization of formal Sie. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED:
Oblique-case pronouns until the relevant cases are introduced.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Subject pronouns can support systematic verb
paradigms. LEADS TO: DE-05 onward.

DE-05 — Sein: identity and description

PREREQUISITES: DE-02–04. OBJECTIVE: Establish present sein through
identity, classification and predicative description. CORE PATTERN: bin,
bist, ist, sind, seid, sind. CARD STRATEGY: Build forms through
meaningful sentences rather than an isolated table; mix persons after
systematic introduction. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Identities,
occupations, nationalities and descriptive adjectives may grow
cautiously. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Compound tense auxiliary use and
passive-related structures. COMPLETION STANDARD: All present forms occur
in ordinary sentences and later mixed contexts. LEADS TO: DE-11 and
numerous later structures.

DE-06 — Haben: possession

PREREQUISITES: DE-04 and basic noun phrases. OBJECTIVE: Establish
present haben and ordinary possession. CORE PATTERN: habe, hast, hat,
haben, habt, haben. CARD STRATEGY: Build the paradigm with familiar
concrete possessions before broader uses. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT:
Possessions, family and everyday objects. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Perfekt
auxiliary use until DE-60. COMPLETION STANDARD: All present forms are
demonstrated and reused. LEADS TO: DE-60, DE-68.

DE-07 — Productive present-tense verbs

PREREQUISITES: Enough personal reference for finite clauses. OBJECTIVE:
Begin the productive present-tense system with transparent weak verbs
while allowing high-value non-weak verbs to appear when they support
useful early sentences. CORE PATTERN: -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en, with
appropriate treatment of predictable phonological/orthographic variants
after the basic model. LEXICAL ANCHORS: machen plus at least one other
useful regular verb. CARD STRATEGY: Introduce infinitives, then
systematic person/number forms; use additional verbs to demonstrate
productivity. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Add useful high-frequency actions
without obscuring morphology. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Dense undifferentiated irregular paradigms.
COMPLETION STANDARD: The weak-verb pattern is demonstrated across
person/number with more than one verb and later reuse; useful
stem-changing or irregular verbs may already have been introduced
lexically or in controlled sentences.
LEADS TO: DE-08, DE-11.

DE-08 — Basic declarative word order and V2

PREREQUISITES: Usable finite verbs. OBJECTIVE: Establish the finite verb
as the second constituent of an ordinary independent declarative clause.
CORE PATTERN: One constituent occupies the first position; the finite
verb follows it. CARD STRATEGY: Begin with subject-first clauses, but
explicitly connect them to the broader V2 system rather than teaching
SVO as the fundamental rule. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Normal.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Complex subordinate-clause verb-final
architecture. COMPLETION STANDARD: Subject-first declaratives are
understood as instances of V2 and the curriculum is prepared to front
other constituents later. LEADS TO: DE-29 and advanced clause
architecture.

DE-09 — Basic negation with nicht

PREREQUISITES: Basic declarative clauses. OBJECTIVE: Establish common
sentence/predicate negation with nicht. CARD STRATEGY: Begin with
transparent predicates and avoid pretending that nicht has one universal
mechanical position. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full scope-sensitive
placement until DE-36. COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic verbal/adjectival
negation is reusable. LEADS TO: DE-36.

DE-10 — Kein and noun negation

PREREQUISITES: DE-03, DE-09. OBJECTIVE: Establish kein as the ordinary
negative determiner in appropriate noun phrases and contrast it with
nicht. CARD STRATEGY: Use paired contexts that make the noun-negation
distinction visible. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full case declension of kein
until cases are introduced. COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic nicht/kein
selection is established. LEADS TO: Later case forms and DE-36.

STAGE 2 — PRESENT-TENSE VERBAL SYSTEM

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE: The rebuilt opening deliberately reaches visible
masculine accusative, basic yes/no and wh-questions, nicht/kein, a
stem-changing verb, and first locative dative examples before every
earlier paradigm is exhaustively established. These are valid
prerequisite-aware detours, not a competing curriculum. Later units
systematize and broaden what the opening has already made intelligible.

DE-11 — Present-tense person/number agreement

PREREQUISITES: DE-04–07. OBJECTIVE: Generalize finite-verb agreement
beyond memorized paradigms. CARD STRATEGY: Vary pronoun and lexical-noun
subjects; mix sein, haben and regular verbs. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Agreement is demonstrated with pronouns and noun subjects across
person/number. LEADS TO: All later finite verb systems.

DE-12 — Common stem-changing verbs

PREREQUISITES: DE-11. OBJECTIVE: Establish frequent present-tense vowel
changes without obscuring the otherwise regular ending system. CARD
STRATEGY: Introduce one change pattern at a time and emphasize where the
change occurs. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: High-frequency everyday verbs.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: An undifferentiated list of all strong verbs.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Major high-frequency stem-change patterns have
multiple usable representatives. LEADS TO: DE-13 and later strong-verb
morphology.

DE-13 — High-frequency irregular verbs

PREREQUISITES: DE-11–12. OBJECTIVE: Add indispensable verbs whose
present forms require individual or family-specific treatment. CARD
STRATEGY: Establish one high-value verb/family at a time before mixing.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Continuous. COMPLETION STANDARD: A practical
irregular core supports ordinary present-tense communication. LEADS TO:
DE-20.

DE-14 — Separable-prefix verbs

PREREQUISITES: DE-08. OBJECTIVE: Establish separation of common verbal
prefixes in finite independent clauses and the resulting verbal bracket.
CORE PATTERN: finite verb stem in V2 position; separable prefix at
clause end. CARD STRATEGY: Begin with semantically transparent, frequent
verbs and simple clauses; vary the first constituent after the basic
bracket is visible. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Daily routine, movement and
practical actions. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Participles until DE-63 and
complex subordinate placement until Stage 9. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Several prefixes/verbs demonstrate the productive clause pattern. LEADS
TO: DE-63, DE-83 and advanced word order.

DE-15 — Inseparable-prefix verbs

PREREQUISITES: DE-14. OBJECTIVE: Contrast common inseparable prefixes
with the separable system. CARD STRATEGY: Establish representative verbs
and prevent overgeneralization of prefix separation. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Full derivational semantics until DE-147–148. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Common inseparable prefixes are recognized through multiple
lexical verbs. LEADS TO: DE-64 and word formation.

DE-16 — Modal verbs I: können, müssen

PREREQUISITES: DE-11. OBJECTIVE: Establish two central modal paradigms
and the finite-modal + clause-final infinitive bracket. CARD STRATEGY:
Introduce each modal separately, then combine with familiar infinitives.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Both paradigms and their infinitive construction
are usable across persons. LEADS TO: DE-17–18.

DE-17 — Modal verbs II

PREREQUISITES: DE-16. OBJECTIVE: Add wollen, sollen, dürfen and mögen in
their major contemporary uses. CARD STRATEGY: Keep semantic distinctions
visible; avoid presenting all modals as interchangeable translations of
English auxiliaries. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Intentions, rules,
preferences and permissions. COMPLETION STANDARD: Major modal meanings
are established in ordinary contexts. LEADS TO: DE-18, DE-69, DE-97.

DE-18 — Infinitive placement with modal verbs

PREREQUISITES: DE-16–17. OBJECTIVE: Generalize the German verbal bracket
created by a finite modal and clause-final infinitive. CARD STRATEGY:
Vary subjects, first constituents, objects and adverbials while
preserving the bracket. COMPLETION STANDARD: Modal infinitive placement
remains stable as sentence complexity increases. LEADS TO: DE-83 and
advanced clause-field work.

DE-19 — Imperative

PREREQUISITES: Established present verbs. OBJECTIVE: Establish common
du, ihr and Sie commands, including important irregular forms. CARD
STRATEGY: Use practical high-frequency directives. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Major imperative patterns are established across regular and common
irregular verbs. LEADS TO: Natural conversation and reported commands.

DE-20 — Present-tense synthesis

PREREQUISITES: DE-11–19. OBJECTIVE: Integrate regular, irregular,
separable, inseparable and modal verbs under the established V2 system.
CARD STRATEGY: Mix verb classes and increase lexical/structural variety
without labeling the required paradigm. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT:
Accelerate ordinary vocabulary. COMPLETION STANDARD: Present-tense
clause construction functions as reusable background grammar. LEADS TO:
Stage 3 onward.

STAGE 3 — ACCUSATIVE AND THE EXPANDING SIMPLE SENTENCE

DE-21 — Direct objects and the accusative

PREREQUISITES: DE-20 and nominative noun phrases. OBJECTIVE: Establish
accusative as a grammatical case prominently used for direct objects
without defining the case solely by that function. CARD STRATEGY: Begin
with semantically clear transitive verbs and contrast subject/object
roles. COMPLETION STANDARD: Direct-object case is meaningful before full
article paradigms expand.

DE-22 — Definite articles: nominative versus accusative

OBJECTIVE: Establish der→den in masculine accusative while reinforcing
unchanged forms elsewhere. CARD STRATEGY: Use controlled contrasts
across genders. COMPLETION STANDARD: Nominative/accusative definite
forms are routinely selected.

DE-23 — Indefinite articles: nominative versus accusative

OBJECTIVE: Establish ein→einen and corresponding feminine/neuter forms.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Indefinite noun phrases work across
subject/direct-object roles.

DE-24 — Personal pronouns in the accusative

OBJECTIVE: Establish mich, dich, ihn, sie, es, uns, euch, Sie, sie. CARD
STRATEGY: Replace familiar object noun phrases after meanings are clear.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Accusative pronouns are usable across persons.

DE-25 — Possessive determiners: basic forms

OBJECTIVE: Establish mein/dein/sein/ihr/unser/euer/Ihr and basic
nominative/accusative inflection. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Family,
possessions and relationships. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full four-case
paradigm until the case system expands. COMPLETION STANDARD: Major
possessors and nominative/accusative noun combinations are established.

DE-26 — Demonstratives and dieser-type forms: introduction

OBJECTIVE: Establish common demonstrative reference and basic dieser
inflection within known cases. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full paradigm
until later case synthesis.

DE-27 — Basic adverbs

OBJECTIVE: Add common time, frequency, manner and degree adverbs.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Time, routine, manner and frequency.

DE-28 — Time, manner and place

OBJECTIVE: Develop natural ordering tendencies among common adverbials
without teaching them as an exceptionless formula. CARD STRATEGY:
Controlled comparisons followed by increasingly natural sentences. LEADS
TO: DE-139.

DE-29 — V2 with a non-subject first constituent

PREREQUISITES: DE-08. OBJECTIVE: Demonstrate fully that the first
constituent need not be the subject. CORE PATTERN: Heute fahre ich … /
In Berlin wohnt er … and analogous structures. CARD STRATEGY: Contrast
subject-first and adverbial/object-first clauses while preserving finite
verb in second position. COMPLETION STANDARD: Non-subject-first clauses
are understood as ordinary V2, not exceptional inversion. LEADS TO: All
later main-clause word order.

DE-30 — Accusative/simple-sentence synthesis

OBJECTIVE: Integrate objects, possessives, demonstratives, adverbs and
flexible V2 openings. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Broad everyday vocabulary.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Expanded main clauses support substantial lexical
variety.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1.  Unit completion means its canonical sequence adequately demonstrates
    its objective; it does not claim learner mastery.
2.  Once established, grammar becomes reusable unless temporary
    simplification is pedagogically useful.
3.  Vocabulary introduced at any point remains reusable.
4.  Later cards increasingly combine multiple established structures
    naturally.
5.  New grammar should normally be the principal structural novelty in
    its earliest cards.
6.  The scheduler, not curriculum order, supplies extra repetition for
    difficult material.
7.  Existing valid German cards remain valid even where they predate
    this syllabus. Future generation should fill meaningful prerequisite
    gaps and converge on the canonical sequence rather than restart the
    language.
8.  Future amendments may split, merge, reorder or refine units without
    renumbering existing card IDs.

AMENDMENT POLICY

This syllabus changes only when the canonical German curriculum changes.
Generating cards or advancing through a unit does not amend it.

An amendment should identify affected units, state the pedagogical
change, say whether it affects future cards only or requires
existing-card correction, increment the version, and preserve a concise
amendment record.
