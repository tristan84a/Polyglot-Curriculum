POLYGLOT SPANISH SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1
Status: Current
Charter version: 0.8
Encoding: UTF-8

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for Spanish in
Polyglot. It was written after the first 100-card prototype and uses that
prototype as empirical evidence rather than treating another Romance
language as a template.

French is an especially useful comparison and successful French sequencing
may be reused closely where Spanish genuinely behaves similarly. Spanish
structure remains authoritative whenever the languages diverge.

The syllabus is a roadmap, not a transcript. A useful construction may
appear before its numbered unit is fully developed, and an introduced
construction may be reinforced much later. Do not delay useful natural
Spanish merely because every earlier pattern has not been exhaustively
drilled. Introduction is not establishment; later double-duty cards may
supply establishment and reinforcement.

REFERENCE VARIETY

Contemporary Spanish from Spain is the unmarked reference variety for this
curriculum. Peninsular forms such as vosotros and ordinary Spain vocabulary
may therefore appear without regional labels.

The curriculum is not intended to imply that Peninsular Spanish is more
correct or more general than American varieties. Major American usage,
including ustedes as the normal plural second person, seseo, important
lexical differences, and voseo where broadly relevant, is later taught
explicitly as part of educated-adult Spanish competence.

Orthography follows contemporary standard Spanish. InputHint is normally
blank. Written accents, ñ, inverted question/exclamation marks and normal
punctuation are part of the target.

OPENING EVIDENCE AND PACING

The 100-card prototype establishes the approved opening evidence for
Spanish. It begins with casa, hombre, mujer, niño, grande, pequeño, bueno
and malo, then moves quickly into articles, gender/number agreement, ser,
natural subject omission, plural noun phrases, estar for location, tener,
regular -ar verbs, no-negation, questions, dónde + estar and existential
hay.

This opening demonstrates several durable principles:

1. Spanish can borrow much of the efficient Romance pacing seen in French
   without copying French syntax card for card.
2. Subject pronouns should be taught, but ordinary finite clauses should
   normally omit them when Spanish naturally does so.
3. Ser, estar and hay should be made distinct through use and then refined
   longitudinally; they need not be exhaustively mastered before other
   structures proceed.
4. Person/number morphology is worth systematic early exposure because
   verb endings themselves carry subject information.
5. New structures may appear relatively early when examples are
   comprehensible. The curriculum can drill and refine them later rather
   than withholding useful language until every earlier topic is complete.

STAGE 1 — BOOTSTRAP AND THE BASIC CLAUSE

ES-01 — Core nouns and adjectives
OBJECTIVE: Establish a small reusable concrete seed and common descriptive
adjectives. INITIAL ANCHORS: casa, hombre, mujer, niño; grande, pequeño,
bueno, malo. CARD STRATEGY: Move rapidly from citation forms into phrases
and sentences; continue vocabulary growth longitudinally.

ES-02 — Gender and articles
OBJECTIVE: Establish grammatical gender as a persistent noun property and
introduce el/la/los/las and un/una/unos/unas. CARD STRATEGY: Use ordinary
noun phrases across gender and number. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Fine article
omission and specialized uses.

ES-03 — Adjective agreement and basic placement
OBJECTIVE: Establish gender/number agreement and ordinary adjective
placement. CORE PATTERN: regular -o/-a and plural agreement first, while
showing invariant forms such as grande naturally. CARD STRATEGY:
Predominantly use natural postnominal examples initially; later teach
meaning, discourse and lexical effects of position rather than presenting
postnominal placement as an absolute rule.

ES-04 — Subject pronouns and pro-drop
OBJECTIVE: Establish yo, tú, él, ella, nosotros/nosotras,
vosotros/vosotras, ellos/ellas and the fact that finite subjects are
normally omitted when recoverable. CARD STRATEGY: Pronoun vocabulary cards
may teach the forms; ordinary sentences should model natural omission.
LEADS TO: all finite verb work and later contrastive/emphatic pronouns.

ES-05 — Ser: identity, classification and basic description
OBJECTIVE: Establish present ser: soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son.
CARD STRATEGY: Use identity, classification and relatively characteristic
description while reinforcing agreement and pro-drop. Do not pretend that
a single permanent/temporary rule adequately explains ser/estar.

ES-06 — Number and agreement synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Make noun/adjective/article number agreement reusable in
ordinary clauses. CARD STRATEGY: Mix singular/plural noun phrases and
lexical subjects rather than mechanically repeating every adjective.

ES-07 — Estar: location and initial state
OBJECTIVE: Establish present estar first through transparent location,
then begin common state uses. CORE PATTERN: estoy, estás, está, estamos,
estáis, están. LEADS TO: ES-08, progressive constructions and later
ser/estar refinement.

ES-08 — Ser, estar and hay: initial functional contrast
OBJECTIVE: Distinguish identity/classification/description with ser,
location/state with estar, and existence/presence with hay in clear
contexts. CARD STRATEGY: Controlled contrasts followed by longitudinal
reuse. This is an early functional map, not the final theory of the three
constructions.

ES-09 — Tener
OBJECTIVE: Establish present tener for possession and begin high-frequency
tener expressions where natural. CORE PATTERN: tengo, tienes, tiene,
tenemos, tenéis, tienen. LEADS TO: age, physical states and later
periphrastic expressions.

ES-10 — Basic negation
OBJECTIVE: Establish no before the finite verb and reuse it across ser,
estar, tener and lexical verbs. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: nadie, nada, nunca
and the full negative-concord system until later.

STAGE 2 — PRESENT-TENSE FOUNDATION

ES-11 — Regular -ar verbs
OBJECTIVE: Establish the productive present -ar paradigm.
CORE PATTERN: -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: hablar, trabajar and other useful regular verbs.
CARD STRATEGY: Systematic person/number exposure is useful, but later
verbs need not repeat a full paradigm once the pattern is established.

ES-12 — Regular -er verbs
OBJECTIVE: Establish the productive present -er paradigm using useful
high-frequency verbs. Demonstrate its relationship to the already-known
person/number system.

ES-13 — Regular -ir verbs
OBJECTIVE: Establish the productive present -ir paradigm and make the
-er/-ir overlap and differences visible.

ES-14 — Present agreement and pro-drop synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Generalize finite morphology across regular classes while
keeping natural omission of recoverable subjects routine. Include lexical
noun subjects and explicit pronouns where contrast, emphasis or
disambiguation warrants them.

ES-15 — High-frequency irregular present verbs I
OBJECTIVE: Introduce indispensable verbs such as ir and hacer as distinct
high-value paradigms. CARD STRATEGY: Establish useful forms in natural
sentences rather than building an undifferentiated irregular list.

ES-16 — Stem-changing verbs
OBJECTIVE: Establish common e→ie, o→ue and e→i patterns and their
person/number distribution. Use several frequent lexical representatives
once each pattern is intelligible.

ES-17 — First-person and other common irregularities
OBJECTIVE: Develop common present irregular families such as -go forms and
other high-frequency departures from productive paradigms. Organize genuine
families where useful without forcing unrelated verbs together.

ES-18 — Present-tense synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Make regular, stem-changing and common irregular present forms
reusable background grammar while vocabulary expands.

STAGE 3 — NOUN PHRASES, OBJECTS AND EVERYDAY RELATIONS

ES-19 — Direct objects
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary transitive clauses with lexical noun
objects. Use clear semantic roles before object pronouns.

ES-20 — Personal a
OBJECTIVE: Establish a with appropriate specific animate direct objects,
especially people. Contrast with non-personal direct objects through
natural examples rather than an oversimplified universal rule.

ES-21 — Indirect objects with a
OBJECTIVE: Establish basic recipient/beneficiary phrases before clitic
doubling is developed systematically.

ES-22 — Possessive determiners
OBJECTIVE: Establish mi, tu, su, nuestro and vuestro families, agreement
where applicable, and the ambiguity of su. Later discourse may refine
possessive alternatives.

ES-23 — Demonstratives
OBJECTIVE: Establish este/ese/aquel families with gender and number and
their principal spatial/discourse contrasts in Peninsular reference usage.

ES-24 — Basic prepositions
OBJECTIVE: Develop a, de, en, con, sin, por and para first in transparent
high-frequency relations. Do not attempt the full por/para distinction in
one early block.

ES-25 — Contractions al and del
OBJECTIVE: Make a + el → al and de + el → del automatic while contrasting
forms that do not contract.

ES-26 — Location, destination and origin
OBJECTIVE: Integrate estar, ir, en, a and de with places and movement.

ES-27 — Numbers, age, dates and time
OBJECTIVE: Develop practical numbers and calendar/time language, including
tener + age.

ES-28 — Everyday noun-phrase synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Mix articles, agreement, possession, demonstration,
prepositions, objects and expanding concrete vocabulary.

STAGE 4 — QUESTIONS, NEGATION AND COMMANDS

ES-29 — Yes/no questions
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary interrogative intonation and written ¿...?,
without inventing an English-style auxiliary. Vary subject expression
naturally.

ES-30 — Basic interrogatives
OBJECTIVE: Establish quién, qué, cuál, dónde, cuándo, cómo, por qué and
cuánto in their principal early uses. Preserve written accents.

ES-31 — Expanded negation and negative concord
OBJECTIVE: Introduce nada, nadie, nunca, tampoco, ningún and related
patterns, including Spanish negative concord through controlled examples.

ES-32 — Indefinites and polarity
OBJECTIVE: Develop algo/alguien, alguno/ninguno and related positive and
negative contrasts.

ES-33 — Tú and vosotros imperatives
OBJECTIVE: Establish common informal Peninsular commands, including
important irregular forms.

ES-34 — Usted and ustedes
OBJECTIVE: Establish formal second-person reference and third-person verbal
agreement. In the Spain-reference curriculum, ustedes is formal plural;
later regional units explicitly teach its ordinary plural-second-person
role across most of the Spanish-speaking world.

ES-35 — Negative commands and initial present subjunctive forms
OBJECTIVE: Introduce the forms required for ordinary negative commands.
This is legitimate early exposure to subjunctive morphology; full
subjunctive function is developed later.

ES-36 — Question/negation/command synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Integrate interrogatives, polarity and directives with the
established present system.

STAGE 5 — OBJECT PRONOUNS, REFLEXIVES AND GUSTAR-TYPE STRUCTURES

ES-37 — Direct-object clitics
OBJECTIVE: Establish me, te, lo/la, nos, os, los/las as direct objects,
including gender/number distinctions in third person.

ES-38 — Indirect-object clitics
OBJECTIVE: Establish me, te, le, nos, os, les in recipient and other
high-frequency functions.

ES-39 — Clitic position with finite verbs
OBJECTIVE: Make preverbal placement routine in ordinary finite clauses,
including negation.

ES-40 — Double-object clitics and se
OBJECTIVE: Establish combinations and le/les → se before third-person
direct-object clitics.

ES-41 — Clitic doubling
OBJECTIVE: Develop common Spanish doubling patterns and distinguish them
from English object expression.

ES-42 — Reflexive pronouns
OBJECTIVE: Establish me/te/se/nos/os/se with transparent reflexive actions
before broader pronominal uses.

ES-43 — Pronominal verbs
OBJECTIVE: Develop high-frequency verbs whose se-constructions are lexical,
aspectual or otherwise not reducible to literal reflexivity.

ES-44 — Gustar-type constructions
OBJECTIVE: Establish experiencer clitics with gustar and selected related
verbs. CARD STRATEGY: Use natural Spanish prompts/targets without forcing
the construction into an English subject-object analysis.

ES-45 — Object/pronominal synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Reuse clitics, lexical objects, personal a and pronominal verbs
in varied present-tense contexts.

STAGE 6 — PAST TIME

ES-46 — Preterite: regular -ar
OBJECTIVE: Establish completed-event preterite morphology for regular -ar
verbs.

ES-47 — Preterite: regular -er/-ir
OBJECTIVE: Establish the regular non--ar preterite pattern.

ES-48 — High-frequency irregular preterites
OBJECTIVE: Develop ser/ir, tener, estar, hacer, poder, poner, venir, decir
and other high-value irregular families in controlled groups.

ES-49 — Imperfect: regular formation
OBJECTIVE: Establish -aba and -ía paradigms and their person/number forms.

ES-50 — Imperfect irregulars
OBJECTIVE: Establish ser, ir and ver.

ES-51 — Preterite versus imperfect
OBJECTIVE: Develop aspectual choice through controlled contrasts:
bounded/completed events, background, habituality, ongoing states and
narrative framing. Avoid reducing the contrast to English tense labels.

ES-52 — Present perfect
OBJECTIVE: Establish haber + past participle and its major contemporary
Peninsular uses. Explicitly recognize that distribution differs across
Spanish-speaking regions.

ES-53 — Past perfect
OBJECTIVE: Establish había + participle for anterior past events.

ES-54 — Past-time expressions and narration
OBJECTIVE: Expand temporal adverbs, sequencing and ordinary narrative
vocabulary.

ES-55 — Past synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Integrate preterite, imperfect, present perfect and pluperfect
in progressively natural narration, with Peninsular usage as the unmarked
production reference and major regional differences later made explicit.

STAGE 7 — NONFINITE FORMS AND VERBAL PERIPHRASES

ES-56 — Infinitives and verb + infinitive
OBJECTIVE: Develop high-frequency complement patterns such as querer,
poder, deber and necesitar + infinitive.

ES-57 — Gerund formation
OBJECTIVE: Establish regular and common irregular gerunds.

ES-58 — Estar + gerund
OBJECTIVE: Establish the progressive as an aspectual construction without
mapping it mechanically onto every English progressive.

ES-59 — Past participles
OBJECTIVE: Consolidate regular and high-frequency irregular participles
beyond their earlier perfect-tense use.

ES-60 — Acabar de + infinitive
OBJECTIVE: Establish recent-past expression.

ES-61 — Ir a + infinitive
OBJECTIVE: Establish planned/imminent future alongside later simple future.

ES-62 — Tener que / hay que / deber
OBJECTIVE: Develop common obligation and necessity constructions and their
semantic differences.

ES-63 — Other common verbal periphrases
OBJECTIVE: Expand into useful aspectual and modal constructions such as
seguir + gerund, volver a + infinitive and dejar de + infinitive.

COMMON OBJECTIVE: Move toward the breadth, precision, phraseology,
register awareness and regional comprehension expected of an educated
adult speaker/reader without turning the curriculum into an inventory of
every attested Spanish word.

GLOBAL CARD RULES

1. Spanish from Spain is the unmarked reference variety.
2. Natural pro-drop is the default. Explicit subject pronouns are used
   where contrast, emphasis, disambiguation or teaching purpose warrants.
3. Do not teach ser/estar as merely permanent versus temporary. Build the
   distinction through constructions and meanings, then refine it.
4. Do not map English progressive forms mechanically to estar + gerund.
5. Written accents and inverted punctuation are required target
   orthography.
6. Tú/vosotros belong to the unmarked Peninsular backbone; usted/ustedes
   are also taught early enough for ordinary interaction. American
   second-person systems are explicitly developed later.
7. High-frequency material may appear before its formal syllabus unit.
   Later cards may drill and refine it. Do not postpone useful language
   merely to preserve a pristine unit boundary.
8. Exact matching requires prompts to distinguish meanings where English
   underdetermines a Spanish contrast.
9. Grammar is normally embodied in useful target-language examples rather
   than terminology questions.
10. Vocabulary grows continuously and increasingly carries established
    grammar as the curriculum matures.
11. Later cards should increasingly combine several established structures
    naturally.
12. The scheduler supplies repetition; the curriculum supplies varied
    examples rather than duplicate cards.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

Unit numbering indicates a useful path, not a prohibition on early
exposure. A concept is established when the canonical deck has made its
basic operation sufficiently clear for later reuse; establishment may
occur longitudinally across later units.

Once established, grammar and vocabulary remain reusable. Future
generation may take prerequisite-safe local detours without amending this
syllabus. Repeated evidence that the preferred route itself has changed is
a reason to revise the syllabus.

AMENDMENT POLICY

Amend this syllabus when the preferred Spanish curricular path or durable
language-specific policy changes, not when routine card generation merely
advances the frontier.
