PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary Standard Swahili in Polyglot. It governs systematic development after the standardized 15-card micro opening. Vocabulary, noun-class morphology and verbal structure develop together in a widening spiral rather than in isolated tables.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND WRITING

Contemporary Standard Swahili (Kiswahili Sanifu) is the unmarked reference variety. Targets use ordinary Latin-script Swahili orthography. InputHint is normally blank. Widely encountered East African regional and colloquial variation belongs to later comparison work rather than the initial production baseline.

COURSE-WIDE CARD PRINCIPLES

1. Write natural ordinary English prompts and natural Swahili Targets. Do not make either language imitate the other for flashcard convenience.
2. Apply established course defaults silently. Parenthetical qualifiers are sparse early scaffolding and should not merely reveal the target construction.
3. Exact matching identifies the canonical natural Target selected for a card; it does not assert that no alternative Swahili expression exists.
4. Noun class is a central longitudinal system. Learn nouns together with enough agreement behavior to make their class productive; do not turn the curriculum into a detached noun-class chart.
5. Agreement extends across adjectives, demonstratives, possessives, subject markers, object markers and other modifiers. Introduce these systems progressively through reusable lexical material.
6. Swahili verbs package subject, tense/aspect, object and derivational information inside the verbal word. Build this template one slot at a time rather than presenting a giant formula before its parts are meaningful.
7. Independent subject pronouns are often unnecessary because subject markers identify participants. Establish pronouns early but increasingly prefer natural omission when no contrast is intended.
8. Infinitives with ku- are useful lexical anchors and also belong to noun class 15. Treat both facts naturally rather than forcing an English infinitive model onto every verbal construction.
9. Standard Swahili has no grammatical gender distinction corresponding to English he/she in ordinary third-person singular agreement. Use natural English prompts and context rather than manufacturing gender in the Target.
10. Adjectival agreement is lexical and class-sensitive; not every English adjective maps to a simple agreeing adjective stem. Prefer natural Swahili constructions.
11. Establish representative paradigms clearly at first; thereafter reduce paradigm density and favor selective contextual forms while vocabulary continues to expand.
12. Avoid commas in card sentences whenever a natural alternative is available because the simple CSV workflow is comma-sensitive.
13. Check the entire live deck plus each new batch for duplicate IDs and duplicate EnglishPrompt values before committing.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 2900000-2900014 are the standardized 15-card micro opening. They preview noun-class agreement in nyumba kubwa, gari jekundu and magari manne; present subject/tense marking in ninaona; and possession with ana. Preserve them as footholds rather than treating those systems as complete.

Cards 2900015 onward form the syllabus-governed seed and continuing canonical deck.

STAGE 1 — CORE CLAUSES AND FIRST NOUN CLASSES

OBJECTIVE: Build reusable people, objects, places and everyday nouns while exposing several high-frequency noun classes naturally.

OBJECTIVE: Establish mimi, wewe, yeye, sisi, ninyi and wao while preparing for natural omission.

OBJECTIVE: Establish identity and class-membership predicates with ni without importing English person agreement.

SW-05 — Adjectival predication
OBJECTIVE: Establish natural descriptive predicates and their noun-class agreement.

OBJECTIVE: Establish representative class 1/2 nouns, singular/plural relations and agreement through people vocabulary.

SW-07 — KI-VI class
OBJECTIVE: Establish representative class 7/8 nouns and agreement.

SW-08 — N class
OBJECTIVE: Establish representative class 9/10 nouns including common singular/plural identity of the noun form and class-sensitive agreement.

OBJECTIVE: Establish representative class 5/6 nouns including gari/magari and agreement.

OBJECTIVE: Contrast productive singular/plural pairings without implying every noun is predictable from form alone.

SW-11 — Basic adjective agreement

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Unit completion establishes canonical coverage rather than learner mastery.
2. Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3. New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled lexical load while vocabulary continues growing.
4. Stable live card IDs are not renumbered merely because syllabus organization changes.
5. Previewing a later construction does not move the Manifest frontier.
6. Parallel units may interleave once prerequisites are satisfied.

CURRENT SEED ALIGNMENT

The expanded 100-card seed establishes SW-01 through SW-17: core vocabulary, pronouns, ni identification, descriptive predication, representative M-WA, KI-VI, N and JI-MA noun-class behavior, plurals, adjective agreement, basic negation/questions, infinitive ku- forms, present -na- and subject markers. Several cards also preview later possession, direct objects and interrogatives without moving the main frontier.

The forward frontier after the 100-card seed is:
