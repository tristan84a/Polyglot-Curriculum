PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary Georgian in Polyglot. It governs systematic development after the standardized 15-card micro opening. Vocabulary grows continuously while grammar develops in a widening spiral.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND WRITING

Contemporary standard Georgian is the unmarked reference variety. Targets use modern Mkhedruli script from the first card. Georgian spelling is learned directly; transliteration is not part of the ordinary target and InputHint normally remains blank.

COURSE-WIDE CARD PRINCIPLES

1. Write natural ordinary English prompts and natural Georgian Targets. Do not make either language imitate the other for flashcard convenience.
2. Apply established course defaults silently. Parenthetical qualifiers are sparse early scaffolding and should not merely reveal the target construction.
3. Exact matching identifies the canonical natural Target selected for a card; it does not assert that no alternative Georgian expression exists.
4. Georgian allows extensive subject omission because verbal morphology often identifies participants. Establish independent pronouns early but increasingly prefer natural omission when no contrast is intended.
5. Georgian verb morphology is structurally central but should not be introduced as giant isolated tables. Establish representative verbs and person patterns clearly; thereafter reduce paradigm density and develop preverbs, version, screeves and argument marking longitudinally.
6. Case marking and verbal argument structure interact. Do not teach the seven cases as if they were a simple one-to-one equivalent of Indo-European case systems.
7. Georgian alignment changes across screeve series. Nominative, dative and ergative-pattern argument marking must be built progressively from actual verb classes and tense/aspect forms rather than reduced to a single permanent subject/object rule.
8. The language has no grammatical gender and no articles. Do not manufacture English-side cues for categories Georgian does not encode.
9. Georgian postpositions and case forms should be taught through useful spatial, temporal and relational expressions.
10. Establish natural word order as discourse-sensitive rather than mechanically fixed while giving learners neutral patterns first.
11. Avoid commas in card sentences whenever a natural alternative is available because the simple CSV workflow is comma-sensitive.
12. Check the entire live deck plus each new batch for duplicate IDs and duplicate EnglishPrompt values before committing.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 2800000-2800014 are the standardized 15-card micro opening. They preview predicative copular forms, dative-marked direct objects with ხედვა, dative experiencer-like possession with აქვს and numeral noun phrases. Preserve them as footholds rather than treating those later systems as complete.

Cards 2800015 onward form the syllabus-governed seed and continuing canonical deck.

STAGE 1 — CORE CLAUSES AND NOMINAL FOUNDATIONS

KA-01 — Core concrete vocabulary
OBJECTIVE: Build reusable people, objects, places and everyday nouns in authentic Mkhedruli.

KA-02 — Core adjectives
OBJECTIVE: Establish common qualities and colours and their ordinary attributive/predicative use.

KA-03 — Personal pronouns
OBJECTIVE: Establish მე, შენ, ის, ჩვენ, თქვენ and ისინი while preparing for natural subject omission.

KA-04 — Present copular predication
OBJECTIVE: Establish ვარ, ხარ, არის/ა, ვართ, ხართ and არიან in identity and description while exposing common enclitic third-person forms naturally.

KA-05 — No articles and basic noun phrases
OBJECTIVE: Establish that bare Georgian nouns may correspond to English definite or indefinite noun phrases according to context.

KA-06 — Basic adjective + noun phrases
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary adjective placement and reusable descriptive noun phrases without inventing gender agreement.

KA-07 — Basic negation
OBJECTIVE: Establish არ with familiar copular and verbal predicates.

KA-08 — Yes/no questions
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary polar questions through intonation/punctuation and context rather than importing English auxiliary structure.

KA-09 — Plural nouns
OBJECTIVE: Establish productive -ები plurals and several common lexical examples.

KA-10 — Demonstratives
OBJECTIVE: Establish ეს, ეგ and ის progressively with natural proximity/discourse distinctions.

KA-11 — Numbers and basic numeral noun phrases
OBJECTIVE: Establish a useful initial number set and the characteristic singular-form noun after numerals.

KA-12 — Early noun-phrase and clause synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine vocabulary, pronouns, copular predication, negation, questions, plurals, demonstratives and numbers.

STAGE 2 — FIRST VERBAL SYSTEM

KA-13 — Verbal citation forms and lexical anchors
OBJECTIVE: Establish common verbs using practical Georgian lexical forms while recognizing that Georgian does not map neatly onto an English infinitive-based dictionary system.

KA-14 — Present series: first representative verb
OBJECTIVE: Establish a high-frequency Class I verb across enough persons to make subject/object person marking visible.

KA-15 — Additional present-series verbs
OBJECTIVE: Broaden present morphology with declining paradigm density across useful lexical verbs.

KA-16 — Subject omission
OBJECTIVE: Prefer natural clauses without overt pronouns when verbal morphology makes the subject recoverable.

KA-17 — Direct-object marking with familiar verbs
OBJECTIVE: Develop object case and agreement through actual verb frames including ხედვა rather than a universal English-style transitive template.

KA-18 — Verbal negation and questions
OBJECTIVE: Extend არ and question formation across familiar verbs.

KA-19 — Question words
OBJECTIVE: Establish ვინ, რა, სად, როდის, როგორ, რატომ, რამდენი and related forms progressively.

KA-20 — Present-series synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine person marking, omission, objects, negation and questions with expanding vocabulary.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Unit completion establishes canonical coverage rather than learner mastery.
2. Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3. New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled lexical load while vocabulary continues growing.
4. Stable live card IDs are not renumbered merely because syllabus organization changes.
5. Previewing a later construction does not move the Manifest frontier.
6. Parallel units may interleave once prerequisites are satisfied.
