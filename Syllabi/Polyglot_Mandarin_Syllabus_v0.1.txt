POLYGLOT MANDARIN SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1
Status: Current
Charter version: 0.8
Encoding: UTF-8

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for Standard Mandarin
in Polyglot. It was written after an initial 100-card seed and treats that
seed, together with successful Polyglot curricula, as design evidence.

"Mandarin" is the language/deck name. "Chinese" is an umbrella label and
does not imply that Cantonese, Hokkien, Hakka, Wu or other Sinitic varieties
are regional subcourses of Mandarin. Those may receive independent Polyglot
curricula.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a transcript. Useful structures may
appear before their formal units when comprehensible, and introduced systems
may be reinforced longitudinally while new material opens.

REFERENCE VARIETY, SCRIPT, AND INPUT

The initial production target is Standard Mandarin (Putonghua) with
Simplified Chinese characters as the default orthography. Pinyin is not an
alternative answer, but it is useful input/pronunciation metadata and may be
used broadly in InputHint, especially while the Mandarin input workflow is
being established.

Traditional characters are a later longitudinal literacy strand within
Mandarin rather than a separate Mandarin language deck. Regional Mandarin
variation, Taiwan usage and other standards/registers may likewise be added
with explicit labeling when exact matching requires it.

COURSE-WIDE MANDARIN PRINCIPLES

1. Use ordinary Chinese characters from the first card. Do not require a
   preliminary pinyin-only vocabulary stage.
2. InputHint may give Hanyu Pinyin with tone marks. It is metadata for
   pronunciation/input support, not an accepted Target.
3. Tones are lexical. Pinyin hints should preserve lexical tone and normal
   tone-sandhi notation conventions consistently enough to support learning.
4. Characters are learned through real words and sentences. Do not make a
   complete character inventory or radical course a prerequisite to language.
5. Simplified characters are the initial production standard. Traditional
   equivalents enter later as literacy mappings and should not compete as
   multiple exact-match answers on the same card.
6. Mandarin nouns lack grammatical gender and verbs do not conjugate for
   person. Do not manufacture European-style agreement paradigms.
7. Spoken tā does not distinguish he/she/it; the written standard does
   distinguish 他/她/它. Teach the written distinction while noting the
   shared pronunciation through InputHint and later listening work.
8. Classifiers are central noun-phrase grammar. 个 is an early default but
   should not displace frequent lexical classifiers such as 本 and 辆.
9. 一 changes pronunciation by context while remaining written 一. InputHint
   may reflect ordinary spoken tone sandhi in phrases.
10. Mandarin has no ordinary article system equivalent to English a/the.
    English prompts with articles should map to natural Mandarin noun phrases,
    using numeral-classifier phrases only when indefiniteness/quantity is
    genuinely expressed.
11. Adjectival predicates commonly occur without 是. 很 frequently appears
    in neutral adjective predicates and must not be taught as a mechanical
    translation of English "very."
12. 是 is primarily copular/equative, not a universal translation of English
    be. Location/existence use 在/有 and adjectival predication follows its
    own patterns.
13. Negation distinguishes 不 and 没(有), among other constructions. Develop
    them by function rather than as interchangeable words for "not."
14. Polar questions with 吗 are an early productive pattern; A-not-A
    questions and other question strategies should follow.
15. Question words normally remain in the syntactic position of the answer.
    Do not import English wh-fronting into Mandarin.
16. Aspect particles such as 了, 过 and 着 are not simple tense endings.
    Teach them through event structure, completion/change, experience and
    state rather than English past/present labels.
17. Mandarin word order carries substantial grammatical work. Time, location,
    manner, objects, complements and information structure require controlled
    sequencing and longitudinal reinforcement.
18. Resultative, directional and potential complements are central to the
    verbal system and should become major curriculum spines rather than
    late idiom lists.
19. Serial-verb, coverb and disposal/passive constructions should be learned
    as Mandarin structures, not forced into European preposition/voice labels.
20. Vocabulary grows just in time. Character novelty alone is not a reason
    to postpone a useful word.
21. Written Standard Chinese and ordinary spoken Mandarin overlap strongly
    but are not identical. Mature stages should develop spoken discourse and
    formal written syntax/register separately where useful.
22. Cantonese, Hokkien and other Sinitic languages are outside the Mandarin
    syllabus except for comparative/cultural awareness.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1–100

The seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- Simplified characters as the Target and tone-marked pinyin as InputHint;
- early numeral-classifier phrases with 个;
- personal pronouns and plural -们;
- 是 for identity/classification;
- 在 for location;
- neutral adjective predicates with 很;
- 这/那 plus classifier;
- invariant verbal predicates through 说 and 工作;
- 不 negation and 吗 polar questions;
- 哪里, 什么 and 谁;
- 有/没有 possession;
- first lexical classifiers 本 and 辆;
- 看 versus resultative 看见;
- 想 + verb;
- 吃/喝 and a few high-frequency everyday nouns.

The opening deliberately exposes several systems without exhausting them:
classifiers, adjective predicates, location versus copula, negation,
resultative morphology and modal/serial verb structure.

INITIAL STRUCTURAL ROADMAP

MN-01 — Core nouns and character literacy
Continue useful concrete nouns in normal Simplified orthography. Character
recognition develops through vocabulary, not isolated character quotas.

MN-02 — Core adjectives and neutral predication
Develop common gradable adjectives in noun phrases and predicates. Contrast
neutral 很 predicates with genuinely intensified 很 and contexts where the
adjective can stand without it.

MN-03 — Pronouns and reference
Develop 我/你/您/他/她/它/我们/你们/他们 etc. Distinguish written gender/
animacy where standard orthography does while remembering spoken homophony.

MN-04 — 是 and nominal predication
Identity, classification, names, occupations and emphatic/contrastive
patterns with 是. Explicitly contrast with adjectival and locative
predicates.

MN-05 — 在 and location
Develop 在 as a location verb and later as a coverb/preposition-like element.
Introduce common place words and localizers longitudinally.

MN-06 — Numeral + classifier + noun
Establish 一/两 and 个, then high-frequency lexical classifiers. Do not
replace a classifier with 个 where normal educated usage strongly prefers
another.

MN-07 — Demonstratives
这/那 + classifier + noun, 这里/那里 and common colloquial variants
这儿/那儿 where appropriate.

MN-08 — Basic verbal predicates
Develop high-frequency transitive/intransitive verbs with invariant person
forms and ordinary SVO clauses.

MN-09 — 不 negation
Establish 不 with habitual, volitional, stative and future-oriented contexts.
Tone-sandhi pronunciation may be reflected in InputHint while orthography
remains 不.

MN-10 — 吗 questions
Develop polar questions and natural short answers. Mandarin often answers
by repeating/negating the relevant predicate rather than relying on one
universal yes/no pair.

MN-11 — Content questions in situ
Develop 谁, 什么, 哪, 哪里/哪儿, 什么时候, 怎么, 为什么, 几 and 多少 in the
position occupied by their answers.

MN-12 — 有 and 没有
Possession, existence and absence. Establish 没有 before broadening 没 as
verbal negator.

NOUN PHRASES AND MODIFICATION

MN-13 — 的 possession
Develop pronoun/noun + 的 possession, then natural omission of 的 in close
relationships where appropriate.

MN-14 — Attributive modification with 的
Adjectives, nouns and phrases before nouns; contrast simple lexical
adjectives that may occur without 的 with structures that require/prefer it.

MN-15 — Plurality and 们
Clarify the restricted distribution of 们 with human pronouns/nouns and
contrast grammatical plurality with unmarked noun number.

MN-16 — Numbers
Develop cardinal numbers, 两 versus 二, larger numbers and the Chinese
grouping system. Numbers should grow longitudinally rather than in one
detached block.

MN-17 — Measure words and quantities
Broaden classifiers and measure words through useful lexical pairings,
quantities, containers, currency and units.

MN-18 — Localizers
里, 上, 下, 前面, 后面, 旁边, 中间 and related spatial expressions with
在 and noun phrases.

TIME, WORD ORDER, AND BASIC COMPLEMENTS

MN-19 — Time expressions
Today, tomorrow, yesterday, clock time, weekdays, dates and durations.
Develop Mandarin ordering of larger-to-smaller time units through examples.

MN-20 — Time and place in clause order
Establish common subject–time–place–verb sequencing and variants licensed by
topic/information structure.

MN-21 — Modal verbs
想, 要, 会, 能, 可以, 应该 and related forms, with controlled contrasts in
volition, futurity, learned ability, circumstantial ability and permission.

MN-22 — Serial verb sequences
Purpose, motion and linked actions in ordinary multi-verb clauses. Do not
force every sequence into an English infinitive analysis.

MN-23 — Manner with 地
Develop adjective/adverbial modification where 地 is natural, alongside
common lexical adverbs that do not require it.

MN-24 — Degree complements
很, 太, 真, 非常, 比较 and 得-based degree descriptions in contexts that
make scope clear.

ASPECT AND EVENT STRUCTURE

MN-25 — Perfective 了
Introduce bounded/completed events with verbal 了 in contexts where the
aspectual contribution is clear. Avoid equating 了 with English past tense.

MN-26 — Sentence-final 了 and change of state
Develop clause-final 了 for new situations/change and contrast it with
perfective verbal 了, including sentences where both analyses interact.

MN-27 — 没(有) verbal negation
Develop 没/没有 with completed/experiential contexts and contrast with 不.

MN-28 — Experiential 过
Life experience and prior occurrence, including 没...过 and time-span
constraints.

MN-29 — Durative/state 着
Ongoing resultant states and simultaneous manner where 着 is natural.

MN-30 — Aspect synthesis
Interleave unmarked, 了, 过, 着 and progressive constructions in contexts
that select a clear event interpretation.

RESULTS, DIRECTIONS, AND MOTION

MN-31 — Resultative complements
Build transparent compounds such as 看见, 听懂, 做完, 写好 and 找到.
Treat each lexicalized combination naturally while exposing the productive
result pattern.

MN-32 — Directional complements
来/去 and simple compounds such as 进来, 出去, 回来, 上去, followed by
compound directional complements.

MN-33 — Potential complements
得/不 between verb and result/direction to express possibility, contrasted
with modal 能/可以.

MN-34 — Motion and destination
来, 去, 到 and location phrases; develop goal/source ordering and common
motion sequences.

MN-35 — 得 complements
Develop degree/result descriptions after verbs and distinguish complement 得
from possessive/attributive 的 and structural 地 through usage rather than
mnemonic terminology alone.

OBJECTS AND INFORMATION STRUCTURE

MN-36 — Object specificity and quantity
Bare objects, quantified objects, definite/topic objects and the discourse
conditions that influence natural ordering.

MN-37 — 把 construction
Introduce 把 only after ordinary transitive clauses, definiteness and
resultative complements are familiar. Use affected/specific objects and
clear disposal results.

MN-38 — 被 passive
Develop 被 passives and agentless/adversity-neutral modern uses, contrasting
them with active/topic structures.

MN-39 — Topic-comment
Develop sentence-initial topics, zero arguments and discourse-linked
interpretation as ordinary Mandarin organization rather than deviations from
SVO.

MN-40 — Double-object and 给
Recipients, 给 as verb/coverb and common double-object patterns.

COMPARISON, QUESTIONS, AND NEGATION

MN-41 — 比 comparison
Develop A 比 B + adjective and degree differences, then negative comparison
patterns.

MN-42 — 最 and superlative-like meanings
最, 更, 比较 and contextual standards of comparison.

MN-43 — A-not-A questions
是不是, 有没有, 去不去 and productive A-not-A patterns, with constraints on
aspect/modal combinations introduced through examples.

MN-44 — Alternative and tag questions
还是 versus 或者, 对不对, 是吗 and other common conversational patterns.

MN-45 — Negative words and scope
不, 没, 别, 不要, 从来不/没, 什么也不 and related scope/negative-polarity
patterns.

CLAUSE LINKING

MN-46 — Coordination
和, 跟, 也, 又, 但是, 可是, 或者 and common conjunction/adverb patterns.

MN-47 — Because/therefore
因为...所以... and natural omission of one correlate where discourse allows.

MN-48 — If/then
如果...就..., 要是...就... and ordinary conditional relationships.

MN-49 — Although/but
虽然...但是... and related concessive patterns.

MN-50 — Before/after/when
以前, 以后, 的时候, 一...就... and temporal sequencing.

MN-51 — Relative clauses with 的
Verb phrases and clauses before nouns with 的. Start with semantically
transparent examples and expand toward longer modifiers.

MN-52 — Complement clauses and reported content
说, 觉得, 知道, 希望 and other verbs taking clause-like complements without
assuming an English-style complementizer is required.

MN-53 — Purpose and causation
为了, 让, 叫, 使 and common causative/permissive constructions with register
distinctions.

VERB-OBJECT LEXICON AND DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS

MN-54 — Separable verb-object compounds
Introduce high-frequency 离合词 such as 睡觉, 见面 and 帮忙 with the word-order
behavior they require.

MN-55 — Reduplication
Verb reduplication for delimitative/light actions, adjective reduplication
and classifier/noun distributive patterns.

MN-56 — Verb complements and lexical families
Develop common verbs through their natural result, direction, degree and
object collocations rather than one-word translation equivalence.

MN-57 — 成, 到, 住, 开 and other productive results
Broaden resultative semantics through common productive complement families.

MN-58 — Aspect with complements
Combine 了/过/着/没 with resultative and directional structures after each
component is independently intelligible.

PRONUNCIATION, CHARACTERS, AND LITERACY

MN-59 — Pinyin and tone awareness
InputHint continues to expose standard pronunciation, tone changes and
syllable segmentation. Dedicated pronunciation cards may be added when a
contrast cannot be learned adequately from lexical examples.

MN-60 — Tone sandhi
Develop third-tone behavior, 一 and 不 sandhi and neutral tone through real
words/phrases. Standard character spelling remains unchanged.

MN-61 — Neutral tone and erhua
Teach frequent neutral-tone words and, later, erhua where relevant to
Standard Mandarin and regional comprehension. Do not force Beijing-specific
erhua into every production target.

MN-62 — Character components
Use recurring semantic/phonetic components to support literacy once enough
characters make the patterns meaningful. Components are aids, not a parallel
character taxonomy course.

MN-63 — Traditional characters
Map high-frequency Simplified forms to Traditional forms progressively.
Reading competence may expand faster than active Traditional production.

MN-64 — Simplified/Traditional lexical conventions
Develop cases where character choice, vocabulary or written convention
differs across Mainland, Taiwan, Hong Kong or other communities, with clear
labels rather than treating script conversion as always one-to-one.

REGISTER AND VARIATION

MN-65 — Spoken discourse
啊, 呢, 吧, 嘛 and other sentence-final particles; fillers, repair,
turn-taking and common conversational ellipsis.

MN-66 — Politeness and social language
您, 请, 麻烦, 客气 expressions, titles, kin terms and context-sensitive
requests without importing a European T/V model wholesale.

MN-67 — Mainland Standard Mandarin
Develop ordinary Putonghua vocabulary and institutional conventions as the
unmarked initial production standard.

MN-68 — Taiwan Mandarin
Introduce important vocabulary, pronunciation and usage differences with
explicit labels, together with Traditional-character literacy.

MN-69 — Regional Mandarin variation
High-value lexical/phonological/syntactic variants may enter for
comprehension after the standard backbone is secure.

MN-70 — Written Standard Chinese
Develop formal written vocabulary, compact noun phrases, conjunctions and
structures that are common in educated prose but less characteristic of
ordinary conversation.

MN-71 — Classical inheritances
Introduce common chengyu, literary morphemes and Classical-derived syntax
that an educated modern reader encounters. Classical Chinese itself is a
distinct historical curriculum, not the default Mandarin target.

MATURE EDUCATED-ADULT DEVELOPMENT

MN-72 — Idiom and chengyu
Develop frequent idioms and four-character expressions with real register,
collocation and pragmatic constraints rather than treating every dictionary
chengyu as equally useful.

MN-73 — Institutions and public life
Education, health, law, economics, government, work, media and civic
vocabulary in neutral and formal registers.

MN-74 — Science and technology
Broad educated scientific/technical-general vocabulary, productive compounds
and explanatory language.

MN-75 — Arts, literature and culture
Film, music, visual arts, literature, criticism and culturally important
references.

MN-76 — Semantic precision
Near-synonyms, complement selection, collocation, register, polysemy and
character-based lexical families.

MN-77 — Extended discourse
Narrative sequencing, reference tracking, argumentation, topic continuity,
formal exposition and multi-clause cohesion.

MN-78 — Educated-adult long tail
Continue vocabulary, phraseology, literacy, register and semantic breadth
without manufacturing obscure grammatical difficulty merely because the
deck is mature.

CARD-SPECIFIC RULES

1. EnglishPrompt must be unique and select one intended Mandarin Target.
2. Language field is "Mandarin", not "Chinese".
3. Targets use Simplified Chinese unless a card is explicitly labeled for
   Traditional or another written convention.
4. InputHint may contain tone-marked Hanyu Pinyin broadly. It is not an
   alternative answer.
5. Pinyin InputHint should correspond to the whole Target closely enough to
   help the learner type/read it; punctuation need not be transliterated.
6. Do not use 是 in ordinary adjective predicates merely because English has
   "is/are".
7. Do not translate English articles mechanically. Use classifiers/numerals
   only when Mandarin meaning calls for them.
8. Classifier prompts must provide enough lexical context to select one
   intended classifier.
9. Question words remain in normal Mandarin position.
10. Aspect-sensitive prompts must contain enough context to select the
    intended particle/construction; avoid English past tense alone as a
    supposed definition of 了.
11. Distinguish 看 from 看见 and similar verb/result pairs in English prompts.
12. Mandarin has no universal yes/no lexical pair matching English. Short
    answers should use the relevant predicate, 对/不对, 有/没有 or another
    natural response according to the question.
13. Written 他/她/它 distinctions may be taught despite identical spoken tā.
14. Do not create cards whose only purpose is memorizing radicals, stroke
    names or grammatical terminology unless those items acquire independent
    learner value.
15. Cantonese, Hokkien and other Sinitic-language forms do not enter Mandarin
    cards as regional variants.

DEVELOPMENT POLICY

The first 100 cards are the empirical opening. Correct genuine errors, but
do not rebuild it merely to make later syllabus numbering perfectly serial.

The next expansion should broaden classifiers, adjective predicates and
ordinary verbs while developing 的 possession/modification, plural/reference
patterns, 在 location, time expressions and word order. Aspect may begin
early through clear 了/没 contrasts; it need not wait for an exhaustive
classifier inventory. Resultative compounds such as 看见 should continue
to appear naturally and later become an explicit productive system.

Pinyin InputHint is deliberately broad in the seed because Mandarin is the
first Polyglot deck where the typing hint also supplies essential
pronunciation information not recoverable reliably from unfamiliar
characters. This policy may be refined after practical app use.

AMENDMENT NOTE

Version 0.1 is the first Mandarin syllabus. It was written after a 100-card
prototype. The prototype showed that the shared Polyglot seed works if
Mandarin is allowed to assert classifiers, copular versus adjectival versus
locative predication, invariant verbs, in-situ questions, 有/没有 and
resultative structure immediately. The course is named Mandarin so that
Cantonese, Hokkien and other Sinitic languages remain available as
independent future curricula.
