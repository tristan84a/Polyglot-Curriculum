LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC AUTHORING GUIDANCE
Preserve standard Yoruba letters ẹ, ọ, ṣ and all normally written tone marks. High tone is acute, low grave and mid normally unmarked. Tone can distinguish lexical words, pronouns and grammatical forms; do not automatically strip or normalize accents. Use consistent Unicode NFC, spacing, and punctuation; InputHint remains blank. A learner is expected to type the target directly.

Yoruba is chiefly SVO and analytic. Verbs do not conjugate for person like French or Russian. Distinguish subject forms (mo, o, ó, a, ẹ, wọ́n), which can vary by syntactic context and tone, from independent forms (èmi, ìwọ, òun, àwa, ẹ̀yin, àwọn). English he/she often maps to the same third-person form. Do not force separate gender readings in the target.

Nouns generally lack obligatory plural inflection. Àwọn marks plurality when appropriate, and numerals normally follow nouns. Noun modifiers, demonstratives, and postnominal definite náà should be taught with meaningful contrasting examples. Noun identity can use ni; property predicates commonly lack a separate copula (Ilé náà tóbi). The source micro cards already preview both patterns, possessive ní and transitive rí.

Tense, aspect and modality depend heavily on particles and context, not person-based suffixes. Teach ongoing ń first, then habitual máa ń, prospective máa and completed ti in distinct contexts. An unmarked Yoruba clause may have several English time readings; use overt time context to create self-contained exact-answer cards where needed. Distinguish negative clauses, polar question ṣé, content question constructions and focus ni. Yoruba serial verbs and information structure deserve sustained later development.

The English prompt must be unique; answers are single canonical translations, not claims that alternatives are ungrammatical. New cards preserve the original first 15 rows and introduce lexical breadth together with grammar. Ordinary dictionary verb prompts beginning 'to' are allowed, but for Yoruba many bare verb forms change tone in constructions; prioritize unambiguous natural clauses when helpful. Revisit orthographic and pronunciation distinctions through authentic examples, including short publicly available phrases where reliability is a concern.
