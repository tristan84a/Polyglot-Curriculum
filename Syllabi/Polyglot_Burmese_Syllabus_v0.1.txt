PURPOSE
This is a language-specific map for graduating the 15-card Burmese micro deck (6300000–6300014) to a 100-card seed (6300000–6300099) and for subsequent expansion toward educated-adult competence. The 15 original cards are preserved exactly. Syllabus units mark the introduction of a productive branch, not its completion. Revisit prior constructions, handwriting/script issues and vocabulary through a widening spiral.

REFERENCE VARIETY, REGISTER AND SCRIPT
Use contemporary spoken Burmese grammar rendered in standard Myanmar Unicode orthography, not a mechanical translation into formal literary Burmese. Standard Burmese has a substantial colloquial-versus-literary diglossia: conversational တယ်, ပါ, လား, လဲ, ဘူး and related particles are not freely interchangeable with literary သည်, ပါသည်, မည်, etc. Teach literary grammar later as a separate productive register. Regional pronunciation and lexical differences should be introduced explicitly rather than silently mixed.
Use Myanmar script in Target, with correct stacked consonants, vowel signs, medials, tone/creaky markers, and ordinary spacing. The script is an abugida; symbols around a consonant are not all 'tone marks.' Teach Unicode typing and non-phonemic spelling conventions gradually. InputHint is empty in the seed; if a transliteration standard or keyboard-specific help is subsequently adopted, apply it consistently and do not turn it into a second answer. Preserve Burmese punctuation ။ for sentences where used.

LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC OPENING PRINCIPLES
1. Preserve existing IDs 6300000–6300014 verbatim; they already preview က topic/subject marking, ကို object marking, မှာ possession/location, ရှိ existence, and human/vehicle classifiers.
2. Burmese is predominantly SOV and head-final. Verbs usually appear clause-final with particles; subjects and objects may be omitted when recoverable. Natural neutral Burmese often omits explicit pronouns.
3. Establish the colloquial declarative particle တယ်, polite ပါ, and everyday negative မ…ဘူး before complex tense/aspect. Distinguish nominal identity with ဖြစ်/ဟုတ် patterns from simple adjective predicates; do not introduce an English-style universal 'be.'
4. First-person forms vary with gender and register (ကျွန်တော်, ကျွန်မ), and second-person address is socially sensitive. The seed uses a male-speaker ကျွန်တော် and male-addressee ခင်ဗျား convention for controlled examples, not as a universal default. Later expand respectful address by names, kinship terms and titles; avoid robotic overuse of explicit 'you.'
5. Introduce noun modifiers and postnominal numeral–classifier constructions early; ordinary unmarked nouns need not display grammatical plural, and classifier choice is lexical/semantic.
6. Distinguish topic က, object/destination ကို, location မှာ, possessive ရဲ့/colloquial noun juxtaposition, and existential/possessive ရှိ/မရှိ. Avoid claiming a single English preposition maps to one particle.
7. Everyday yes/no questions can use လား; information questions commonly use လဲ with the wh expression. Other question particles and the role of polite ပါ are later developments.
8. Build lexical breadth with ordinary nouns, food, people, motion, communication and practical phrases while grammar is opened. Do not require dictionary verb forms with artificial infinitival endings: use the bare stem as a citation-form flashcard when appropriate.
9. Burmese reference and pronouns do not require English grammatical gender agreement; prompts must distinguish male/female forms of occupations or personal address when the intended Target does.
10. Exact matching selects one reasonable canonical answer, not the only natural translation. Avoid artificially stilted English prompts; qualify only material contrasts such as a gendered first-person form or register.
11. Written syllables and word spacing do not map one-for-one onto English words. Preserve normal orthography; do not insert spaces inside complex written syllables.
12. Supplement common spoken constructions with literary language, idioms, collocations, professional vocabulary and authentic-text backward validation as proficiency grows.

CARD GENERATION RULES
- Allocation: 6300000–6399999. Existing 6300000–6300014 stable and unchanged; new seed cards 6300015–6300099; continue at 6300100.
- CSV schema: ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech. Use UTF-8 BOM where practical and correct CSV quoting. EnglishPrompt values must be unique.
- Canonical Target: natural modern Unicode Burmese, standard script, colloquial core style. Do not romanize Target or mix literary verb endings into neutral spoken clauses.
- Seed uses male-speaker first-person and a polite masculine second-person form where explicitly qualified in the English prompt. Broaden reference forms soon rather than hard-coding these as universal.
- InputHint blank unless a consistent, pedagogically justified keyboard/romanization scheme is chosen later.
- Every card must be independently interpretable after shuffling, with a single intended canonical answer; maintain natural English and avoid exact duplicates.
- Teach classifiers in realistic phrases, verbal particles in coherent clauses, and interleave vocabulary with structural developments.
- Revisit register, pronunciation and script distinctions in small increments. Real-world language examples may be adapted when generation confidence is limited.
