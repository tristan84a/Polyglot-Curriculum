PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical progression for contemporary Norwegian in Polyglot. The reference production variety is standard written Bokmål; Nynorsk and spoken dialects are treated explicitly as legitimate Norwegian varieties and later receptive/comparative strands. Norwegian has no single nationwide standard spoken pronunciation. The default seed uses ordinary broadly neutral Bokmål and familiar spoken-compatible constructions.

REFERENCE AND WRITING

Use standard Bokmål spelling with æ, ø and å. The seed uses feminine -a forms in a small set of very common nouns (jenta, boka, avisa, elva), while retaining en in indefinite contexts; this is grammatical Bokmål, not a claim that all Bokmål speakers choose these forms. The feminine indefinite article ei is introduced as a legitimate option in NO-04 and systematically revisited in the variation stage. Do not require one-to-one mapping between Danish and Norwegian forms. InputHint is normally blank.

COURSE-WIDE CARD PRINCIPLES

1. Natural English prompts and natural Bokmål Targets, with one canonical answer per card.
2. The syllabus governs the order in which new patterns open, not the order of exhaustive completion. Previously opened branches remain available in a widening spiral.
3. Introduce a representative contrast clearly, then broaden vocabulary through meaningful sentences. Avoid mechanical paradigms for every new lexical verb.
4. Norwegian finite verbs do not inflect for person or number; do not invent person paradigms.
5. Common/neuter gender, optional feminine forms, suffixed definiteness, double definiteness and adjective agreement are taught through noun phrases and sentences.
6. V2 in main clauses, verb-first yes/no questions, and subordinate clause placement of ikke require distinct controlled examples.
7. Pronunciation including pitch accent and dialect diversity needs dedicated treatment; do not encode invented phonetic spelling in Target.
8. Norwegian Bokmål and Nynorsk are two written standards, not two separate languages in the curriculum's conceptual model; preserve variety labels in later comparisons.
9. Each card stands independently after shuffling. Check unique EnglishPrompt, unique ID, monotonic IDs and UTF-8 CSV structure before writing.
10. Expand lexical range across everyday life, culture, institutions and educated-adult domains from early stages; do not wait for the lexical stages.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 3600000–3600014 are the original 15-card micro deck and remain unchanged. Cards 3600015–3600111 are the 97-card syllabus-governed seed expansion. Early noun phrases, gender, plural/definite forms, simple clauses, questions, negation, lexical present and V2 have been introduced. These are openings, not exhaustive mastery of the units. The next active frontier is NO-15 (infinitives with å); reinforce earlier noun-phrase systems longitudinally.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

Early lexical verbs should be introduced with their infinitives and selected finite uses, not as long disconnected verb lists. Bokmål has regular weak-verb past patterns as well as strong verbs; avoid treating one weak class as universal. Introduce preposed definiteness in modified definite noun phrases separately from suffixed definiteness. Show gender variation as variation rather than correcting one accepted Bokmål form into another. The written/spoken relationship and dialects are revisited progressively; do not teach one Oslo accent as the sole correct spoken Norwegian.
