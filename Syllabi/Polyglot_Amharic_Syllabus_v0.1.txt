PURPOSE
Graduate the original fifteen-card micro deck, unchanged, into a 100-card systematic seed and establish a long-range introduction map. Units describe new branches of learning, not completed textbook lessons. Continue to recycle established features while adding new vocabulary and structures.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND SCRIPT
Write standard Amharic in Ethiopic/Geʽez (fidel) Unicode script in Target. The script is an abugida organized into consonant orders reflecting vowel qualities, with some historical spellings that do not represent contemporary phonemic contrasts. Use conventional spellings, no romanized Target, and leave InputHint blank. Ethiopian punctuation such as ። is acceptable for statements, and ordinary ? for questions. Keep spelling stable across cards and distinguish Amharic from related Tigrinya and Geʽez.

AUTHORING PRINCIPLES
1. Preserve cards 9800000–9800014 exactly, including their existing English prompts and targets.
2. Prefer neutral standard everyday Amharic. Label formal, literary, regional, honorific or colloquial alternatives when systematically introduced.
3. Typical unmarked clause order is subject-object-verb. Adjectives normally precede nouns; definiteness is usually suffixed, not a free-standing English-style article.
4. Gender matters in some second- and third-person forms, the copula and verb conjugation; English prompts must disambiguate masculine/feminine second-person reference when needed.
5. Pronouns are often omitted because verbs encode subject information. Early overt-pronoun paradigms are intentional scaffolding, not a claim that they are always necessary.
6. Teach the present nominal copula ነኝ, ነህ, ነሽ, ነው, ናት, ነን, ናችሁ, ናቸው as contrasts; later revisit respectful and formal variation.
7. Distinguish nominal-copular negation (አይደለም and its inflected forms) from negative lexical verbs (e.g. አልበላም) and existential negation (የለም).
8. Plural -ዎች and other patterns are not mechanically uniform across all nouns; note irregular and lexicalized plurals such as መጽሐፍት.
9. Possession and existence use አለ and person-marked forms such as አለኝ/አለው/አላት. Teach possessive noun phrases separately from this existential pattern.
10. Amharic uses templatic Semitic verb morphology with person/gender prefixes and tense/aspect/person suffixes; do not describe the language as an invariant-verb SVO system. Teach high-frequency verbs through actual forms rather than treating infinitives as predictable stems.
11. Early finite examples mainly use the imperfective/nonpast; later teach the perfective, compound progressive, future/prospective, auxiliaries, negation and imperative as distinct systems.
12. Require unique EnglishPrompt values, continuous new IDs inside 9800000–9899999, and exactly six CSV columns (ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech). Each target must be independently understandable after shuffling; one canonical target is not necessarily the only grammatical translation.
13. Script practice, phonology, register, and natural usage remain longitudinal strands. Avoid making a syllabus milestone imply a complete survey of an entire morphological system.
