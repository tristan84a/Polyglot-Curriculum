LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC AUTHORING GUIDANCE
Use the standard Latin alphabet of Azerbaijan, retaining ə, ı (dotless), i (dotted), ö, ü, ğ, ş and ç. Distinguish dotted capital İ and dotless I in context; do not substitute Turkish forms or Iranian Azerbaijani Arabic-script conventions. Some capitalizations vary across names and conventions; choose ordinary contemporary Azerbaijan orthography. Maintain Unicode consistency and the six-column CSV with UTF-8 BOM, blank InputHint unless needed.

Azerbaijani is Turkic and typically SOV, with no grammatical gender, agglutinative case/possessive morphology and extensive vowel harmony. It shares broad structural principles with Turkish but must be authored as Azerbaijani: e.g., görmək, oxumaq, yaxşı, deyil, var/yox, -ır/-ir/-ur/-ür present/habitual in common verbs, and the Azerbaijani nominal -dır/-dir/-dur/-dür marker. The third-person predicative marker is common in careful declaratives, while informal speech can omit it. Preserve the micro deck's explicit -dır/-dir forms; teach their use against natural copular zero forms later without treating the marker as a full equivalent of English 'is'.

Pronouns: mən, sən, o, biz, siz, onlar. Sən is familiar singular; siz is polite singular or plural. O makes no he/she distinction. Azerbaijani is pro-drop when context and agreement suffice, though retaining pronouns is often sensible in controlled beginner contrasts. Possessive morphology must be learned in actual words, including vowel and consonant interactions, with genitive pronouns and noun possessive suffixes; possession/existence frequently uses var and absence uses yoxdur/yox.

The four-way vowel harmony system influences accusative, possessive, personal, case and tense suffixes. Plural -lar/-lər follows two-way harmony. Nouns following numerals normally remain unmarked for plural: iki kitab, üç alma. Direct objects show important specificity contrasts: Mən kitab oxuyuram versus Mən kitabı oxuyuram, rather than a direct mechanical equivalence to English definite articles. Introduce dative -a/-ə (with buffers as needed), locative -da/-də, ablative -dan/-dən, genitive -ın/-in/-un/-ün and accusative -ı/-i/-u/-ü across stems and animate nouns in later units.

Finite verbs: first teach common progressive/nonpast -ır/-ir/-ur/-ür patterns through görürəm, oxuyuram, içirəm, yeyir and their personal agreement. Develop verb stem phonology as observed (oxu-yur-am, ye-yir). Negative finite forms such as görmürəm, oxumur require their own contrast against nominal deyil. Polar questions in contemporary Azerbaijani are often expressed by intonation and question punctuation; the question particle -mı/-mi/-mu/-mü also occurs, especially in careful or emphatic usage and with its own distribution. Do not copy Turkish's obligatorily separate written mı/mi into Azerbaijani examples.

The opening micro deck already previews accusative evi, present görürəm, genitive onun, possessed maşını, existence var, predicative -dır and singular count nouns after dörd. Preserve all fifteen as stable live identities. New prompts stand alone after shuffling and are unique within this deck; choose neutral idiomatic translations, not artificially literal ones. Some variants in verbal and predicate expression are possible; each CSV row chooses one canonical answer.
