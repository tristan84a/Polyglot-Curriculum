POLYGLOT HUNGARIAN SYLLABUS


CARD-FORM AND MORPHOLOGY CONVENTIONS

Lexical verb cards use the Hungarian -ni infinitive as the citation form:
látni, olvasni, írni, enni, inni, venni, dolgozni. Finite dictionary stems
or third-person forms are not used as targets for an English "to ..." lexical
prompt.

Hungarian's rich suffixal morphology should normally be revealed in natural
phrases and especially sentences rather than through long inventories of
isolated inflected nouns. Accusative, local-case, possessive and other
case-marked forms should usually enter while doing communicative work in a
sentence. A standalone inflected form is appropriate when isolating the form
itself teaches a contrast that would otherwise be difficult to perceive.

Possessive suffixes follow the same principle. One controlled series may be
used when several cells establish genuinely new morphology, but subsequent
possessive forms should increasingly be distributed through sentences that
also expand vocabulary and recycle case, location, tense and other active
systems.

New nouns and verbs should continue entering while morphology develops.
Do not freeze the lexicon while walking exhaustively through case,
possessive or conjugational tables. Once a productive pattern is
intelligible, sample it across new lexical material and allow later grammar
to join the same widening spiral.

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern standard
Hungarian in Polyglot. It follows the Polyglot Curriculum Charter v0.5:
a small reusable lexical seed moves immediately into productive
structure; vocabulary then expands just in time alongside grammar.
Hungarian structure is authoritative. Cross-language sequences may be
reused when they fit naturally, but Hungarian is not to be distorted to
resemble French, German, Polish, or another language.

The syllabus is designed toward ordinary educated-adult competence
rather than a finite beginner endpoint. Vocabulary, morphology, syntax,
phraseology, register, orthography, and natural usage continue into an
expandable long tail.

OPENING PROTOTYPE AND PACING DIRECTIVE

The initial Hungarian prototype was designed before this syllabus as a
deliberate stress test of the language. It establishes approximately 36
cards and should be audited against this syllabus before publication
rather than treated as automatically canonical.

Preferred initial lexical seed:

ház — house férfi — man nő — woman gyerek — child nagy — big kicsi —
small jó — good rossz — bad

The seed is followed immediately by productive Hungarian structure
rather than by a large preliminary vocabulary inventory.

The prototype establishes these principles early:

1.  Present-tense third-person nominal and adjectival predicates
    normally have no overt copula: A ház nagy. A férfi orvos.

2.  The definite article has the ordinary a / az alternation and this
    should be exposed early rather than postponed as a separate advanced
    rule.

3.  Hungarian has no grammatical gender in personal pronouns; ő covers
    he/she.

4.  Personal pronouns are introduced as lexical forms but should not be
    mechanically retained in ordinary sentences where Hungarian normally
    omits them. Explicit pronouns should later appear where contrast,
    focus, disambiguation, or another genuine discourse reason makes
    them natural.

5.  First- and second-person present copular forms are established
    through natural predicative sentences: Jó vagyok. Jó vagy. Jók
    vagyunk. Jók vagytok.

6.  Third-person zero-copula forms should not lead to the false rule
    that van/vannak are absent from Hungarian. Existential, locative,
    possessive, and other ordinary uses of van/vannak receive explicit
    later treatment.

7.  Plural predicative adjective agreement is allowed to emerge through
    useful sentences rather than through a detached paradigm.

The opening prototype is a pacing model, not a permanent 36-card quota.
Future Hungarian rebuilding may alter individual cards if this syllabus
reveals a better prerequisite order.

STAGE 1 — SMALL SEED AND PRESENT PREDICATION

HU-01 — Core concrete nouns and authentic Hungarian orthography

OBJECTIVE: Establish a very small set of reusable nouns while exposing
ordinary Hungarian spelling, including long vowels and accented letters
from the beginning. ANCHORS: ház, férfi, nő, gyerek. CARD STRATEGY:
Isolated lexical cards are appropriate for the initial seed; move into
phrases and sentences almost immediately. COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough
nouns exist to support early predication and article use. Broader noun
vocabulary develops continuously.

HU-02 — Core descriptive adjectives

PREREQUISITES: HU-01 begun. OBJECTIVE: Establish a small useful
adjective seed. ANCHORS: nagy, kicsi, jó, rossz. CARD STRATEGY: Teach
citation forms, then reuse them immediately in predicative sentences.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Broad adjective lists and full
attributive/comparative behavior. COMPLETION STANDARD: The seed supports
productive description.

HU-03 — Definite article a / az

PREREQUISITES: HU-01. OBJECTIVE: Establish the ordinary phonological
alternation between a and az. CARD STRATEGY: Add a vowel-initial lexical
anchor such as alma and contrast a ház with az alma in useful phrases
and sentences. COMPLETION STANDARD: a/az is reusable without treating
the two forms as separate articles.

HU-04 — Present adjectival predication and zero copula

PREREQUISITES: HU-01–HU-02. OBJECTIVE: Establish natural third-person
present adjectival predicates without overt van. CORE PATTERN: A ház
nagy. Az alma jó. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Existential/locative van,
past/future copular forms, and contexts requiring overt van. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Singular third-person adjectival predication is established as
ordinary Hungarian structure.

HU-05 — Present nominal predication and zero copula

PREREQUISITES: HU-01, HU-03–HU-04. OBJECTIVE: Show that ordinary
third-person present nominal predicates likewise use zero copula.
ANCHOR: orvos. CORE PATTERN: A férfi orvos. A nő orvos. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Nominal and adjectival zero-copula predicates are
distinguished from English overt-copula structure and are reusable.

HU-06 — Personal pronouns and absence of grammatical gender

PREREQUISITES: HU-01. OBJECTIVE: Establish én, te, ő, mi, ti, ők,
including the gender-neutral third-person singular ő. CARD STRATEGY:
Pronouns may be learned explicitly as lexical forms. Do not mechanically
carry them into ordinary sentences. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED:
Oblique/case-marked forms and emphatic discourse uses. COMPLETION
STANDARD: The six basic personal pronouns are recognized as reference
forms and their ordinary omission is anticipated.

HU-07 — Present forms of lenni with personal predicates

PREREQUISITES: HU-02, HU-04–HU-06. OBJECTIVE: Establish first- and
second-person present forms through natural predicates while preserving
third-person zero copula. CORE PATTERNS: Jó vagyok. Jó vagy. Jó. Jók
vagyunk. Jók vagytok. Jók. CARD STRATEGY: Prefer omitted subjects where
natural. Explicit pronouns are reserved for later contexts in which
Hungarian actually motivates them. COMPLETION STANDARD:
First/second-person forms and third-person zero-copula contrast are
established across singular/plural; plural predicative adjective
agreement has been introduced.

STAGE 2 — BASIC NOMINAL AND VERBAL CLAUSES

HU-08 — Indefinite article and numeral egy

OBJECTIVE: Establish the core uses of egy as numeral “one” and as an
indefinite determiner, including contexts where Hungarian naturally
omits an indefinite article. CARD STRATEGY: Avoid mapping English a/an
mechanically to egy. COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic one/indefinite contrasts
are usable without assuming obligatory article correspondence.

HU-09 — Noun plural -k

OBJECTIVE: Establish common plural formation and relevant stem/vowel
behavior through high-frequency nouns. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Exhaustive
stem alternations. COMPLETION STANDARD: Common noun plurals can be
introduced and reused naturally.

HU-10 — Attributive adjectives

PREREQUISITES: HU-02, HU-09. OBJECTIVE: Contrast attributive adjectives
with predicative adjectives, especially the absence of plural agreement
on ordinary attributive adjectives before plural nouns. CORE CONTRAST:
nagy ház / nagy házak versus A házak nagyok. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Attributive versus predicative behavior is established.

HU-11 — Basic demonstratives ez / az

OBJECTIVE: Establish proximal/distal reference as pronouns and begin the
special agreement behavior of demonstratives before case-marked nouns
only when prerequisites permit. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full
demonstrative + case agreement until case suffixes are established.

HU-12 — Basic present-tense verbal clauses

OBJECTIVE: Introduce a small set of high-value verbs in finite present
forms through natural sentences. CARD STRATEGY: Add verbs just in time;
do not create a preliminary infinitive inventory. Subject pronouns
remain omitted unless discourse requires them. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Basic intransitive and simple transitive clauses are established
sufficiently to support object marking.

HU-13 — Accusative -t

PREREQUISITES: HU-12. OBJECTIVE: Establish direct-object marking and
common morphophonological/stem behavior progressively. CARD STRATEGY:
Begin with transparent anchors, then broaden. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Accusative objects are reusable in ordinary clauses.

HU-14 — Indefinite conjugation: present

PREREQUISITES: HU-12–HU-13. OBJECTIVE: Establish the present indefinite
conjugation as a productive verbal system. CARD STRATEGY: Use subjects
that are naturally omitted and vary person/number through context and
English prompts. COMPLETION STANDARD: Core present indefinite endings
are established across persons with representative verb types.

HU-15 — Definite conjugation: present

PREREQUISITES: HU-13–HU-14. OBJECTIVE: Introduce the definite
conjugation through clear definite-object contexts. CORE PRINCIPLE:
Conjugation choice reflects Hungarian object definiteness/reference
structure and must not be reduced to an English tense/person mapping.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Every special object class at first exposure.
COMPLETION STANDARD: The central definite/indefinite contrast is
inferable and productive.

HU-16 — Definite versus indefinite conjugation synthesis

OBJECTIVE: Contrast matched clauses differing principally in object
definiteness and consolidate the conjugation choice. COMPLETION
STANDARD: The learner can treat the two present conjugations as a
coherent Hungarian system rather than two unrelated paradigms.

STAGE 3 — NEGATION, QUESTIONS, EXISTENCE, AND LOCATION

HU-17 — Basic verbal and predicative negation

OBJECTIVE: Establish nem in ordinary nominal, adjectival, and verbal
clauses. CARD STRATEGY: Recycle established structures before adding
lexical complexity. COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic negation is reusable
across established clause types.

HU-18 — Yes/no questions and intonation

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary Hungarian yes/no questions without
inventing an obligatory question particle. CARD STRATEGY: English
punctuation identifies question force; target sentences use natural
Hungarian orthography. COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic polar questions are
established across familiar clause types.

HU-19 — Core interrogatives

OBJECTIVE: Introduce ki, mi, hol, hová, honnan, mikor, hogyan, miért and
other high-value question words progressively. CARD STRATEGY: Add only
as their grammatical prerequisites become available.

HU-20 — Existential and locative van / vannak

PREREQUISITES: HU-04–HU-07. OBJECTIVE: Correctly delimit the earlier
zero-copula rule by establishing contexts in which van/vannak are overt
and necessary. CORE CONTRAST: A ház nagy. / A könyv az asztalon van.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Zero copula versus overt van/vannak is structurally
motivated rather than memorized as an exception list.

HU-21 — Basic location: superessive -n/-on/-en/-ön

OBJECTIVE: Introduce one high-value local case family through concrete
location. CARD STRATEGY: Teach suffix selection through examples; do not
begin with a complete case table.

HU-22 — Inessive -ban/-ben

OBJECTIVE: Establish “in/inside” location and make front/back vowel
alternation visible in a meaningful suffix family.

HU-23 — Illative -ba/-be and elative -ból/-ből

OBJECTIVE: Develop the location → destination → source relationship for
the interior local-case family.

HU-24 — Sublative -ra/-re and delative -ról/-ről

OBJECTIVE: Complete the corresponding surface-oriented directional
family after the superessive is established.

HU-25 — Adessive -nál/-nél, allative -hoz/-hez/-höz, ablative -tól/-től

OBJECTIVE: Establish the proximity/at-to-from local family and reinforce
vowel harmony through productive morphology.

HU-26 — Local-case synthesis

OBJECTIVE: Integrate hol / hová / honnan with the major three-way local
case families. COMPLETION STANDARD: Location, destination, and source
are understood as an organized Hungarian morphological system.

STAGE 4 — VOWEL HARMONY AND SUFFIX ARCHITECTURE

HU-27 — Front/back vowel harmony as a productive system

OBJECTIVE: Make explicit through accumulated examples that suffix
allomorphy tracks vowel harmony. CARD STRATEGY: Grammar remains learned
through real words and sentences; do not replace usage with abstract
vowel-class quizzes. COMPLETION STANDARD: Common two-way suffix choices
are predictable for ordinary stems.

HU-28 — Rounded front-vowel three-way harmony

OBJECTIVE: Establish suffix sets such as -hoz/-hez/-höz where rounding
creates a third form.

HU-29 — Linking vowels and stem alternations

OBJECTIVE: Introduce common morphophonological changes required by
suffixation without implying that every stem is mechanically
transparent.

HU-30 — Suffix stacking

OBJECTIVE: Establish Hungarian words as ordered sequences of stem +
derivational/possessive/plural/case and related morphology as
prerequisites permit. CARD STRATEGY: Build from already understood
suffixes rather than presenting long artificial forms.

STAGE 5 — POSSESSION

HU-31 — Possessive suffixes: singular possessed noun

OBJECTIVE: Establish core possessed-noun morphology for common
possessors. CARD STRATEGY: Prefer natural possessed forms over
English-style possessive pronoun substitution.

HU-32 — Possessor constructions

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary possessor + possessed relationships,
including article behavior and nominative/dative possessor patterns
progressively.

HU-33 — Plural possession and multiple possessors

OBJECTIVE: Extend possessive morphology across number while keeping
morpheme order visible.

HU-34 — Possessive pronouns and emphatic ownership

OBJECTIVE: Introduce independent possessive forms when natural discourse
requires them rather than using them as the default translation of
English “mine/yours.”

HU-35 — Possession with van

OBJECTIVE: Establish Hungarian existential possession constructions and
contrast them with English “have.” COMPLETION STANDARD: Ordinary
possession is understood through Hungarian structure rather than forced
into an English have-verb template.

HU-37 — Instrumental-comitative -val/-vel

HU-38 — Causal-final -ért

OBJECTIVE: Establish common “for/because of” functions through natural

HU-39 — Translative-factive -vá/-vé

HU-44 — Case/postposition synthesis

STAGE 7 — VERBAL SYSTEM EXPANSION

HU-51 — Future reference

LATE CURRICULUM — PARALLEL DEVELOPMENT

From approximately this point onward, unit numbering records an editorial introduction order, not a requirement that one late domain be exhausted before the next begins. Once prerequisites are available, register, phraseology, broad lexical domains, educated-adult vocabulary, and long-tail material should develop in parallel and reinforce one another.

Later units should therefore be introduced in roughly the numbered order, relatively quickly, and then revisited longitudinally. The learner need not master register switching before beginning phraseology, nor exhaust scientific vocabulary before cultural or other lexical domains begin. Numbering remains useful for first introduction and controlled-document organization; it is not a mandate for serial mastery of mature-language domains.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1.  Unit completion means its canonical sequence adequately demonstrates
    its objective; it does not claim learner mastery.
2.  Once established, grammar becomes reusable unless temporary
    simplification is pedagogically useful.
3.  Vocabulary introduced at any point remains reusable.
4.  Later cards increasingly combine multiple established structures
    naturally.
5.  New grammar should normally be the principal structural novelty in
    its earliest cards.
6.  The scheduler, not curriculum order, supplies extra repetition for
    difficult material.
7.  Personal pronouns should not be inserted into neutral Hungarian
    sentences merely because the English prompt contains an explicit
    subject.
8.  Vowel harmony should be learned through productive morphology and
    accumulated examples rather than treated as a detached preliminary
    memorization exercise.
9.  Hungarian “case” should develop by communicative and morphological
    function rather than through an early exhaustive case table.
10. Definite/indefinite conjugation, verbal prefixes, and information
    structure are central organizing systems and must receive
    longitudinal treatment rather than isolated one-unit exposure.
11. Future amendments may split, merge, reorder, or refine units without
    renumbering existing stable card IDs.

AMENDMENT POLICY

This syllabus changes only when the canonical Hungarian curriculum
changes. Generating cards or advancing through a unit does not amend it.

An amendment should identify affected units, state the pedagogical
change, say whether it affects future cards only or requires
existing-card correction, increment the version, and preserve a concise
amendment record.

Version 0.1 - Initial Hungarian syllabus under Polyglot Curriculum
Charter v0.5. - Uses the collaboratively designed 36-card Hungarian
prototype as an empirical stress test rather than deriving the opening
solely from another language. - Adopts the shared
house/man/woman/child + big/small/good/bad seed. - Establishes a/az,
nominal and adjectival zero-copula predication, gender-neutral ő,
natural subject omission, and plural predicative adjective agreement in
the opening. - Deliberately postpones overt van/vannak until
existential/locative functions can distinguish them from zero-copula
predicates. - Develops Hungarian case morphology by functional families
rather than by an early exhaustive case inventory. - Treats vowel
harmony, definite/indefinite conjugation, verbal prefixes, possession,
and information structure as major longitudinal systems. - Reserves
explicit personal-pronoun sentence use for contexts where discourse
actually motivates it. - Uses completion standards and an expandable
educated-adult long tail rather than fixed card quotas.
