PURPOSE
This is the canonical introduction map for Vietnamese. The original 15-card micro deck (3800000–3800014) is preserved verbatim; 85 additional cards (3800015–3800099) establish a 100-card systematic seed. Progress toward educated-adult language breadth through a widening spiral. Each unit opens a branch; it does not close previous branches or demand immediate mastery.

REFERENCE VARIETY, WRITING SYSTEM AND EXACT MATCH
Use standard modern Vietnamese quốc ngữ with full tone and vowel diacritics in Target. InputHint is normally blank: the user types Vietnamese directly. Preserve the distinctions between ă, â, ê, ô, ơ, ư, đ and all tone marks. Vietnamese tones vary in realization by region, and the Northern reference is not a claim that Southern or Central speech is incorrect. Mark dialect-specific vocabulary, pronunciations and particles when introduced. The first 15 micro cards contain some neutral and Southern-compatible vocabulary such as xe hơi; preserve them and later teach alternatives such as ô tô with appropriate context.

Vietnamese is predominantly analytic and uses no person-based verb conjugation or grammatical gender agreement. Teach productive clause order, classifiers, aspect/time expressions, and context-sensitive reference rather than inventing European-style inflectional tables. The English prompt must distinguish kinship/social-role pronouns, inclusive/exclusive we, and demonstrative distance whenever those distinctions affect a single exact-match Target. The introductory pronoun tôi is useful but is not the universal default for all social situations; bạn as 'you' assumes an appropriate peer context.

OPENING PRINCIPLES
1. Preserve IDs 3800000–3800014 unchanged. The initial house, water, man, doctor, car, colour, number and sentence examples preview là, adjectival predicates, thấy, có and classifiers.
2. Begin with concrete nouns, adjectives, personal reference and simple clauses. Expand the lexicon alongside grammar, not in a large initial vocabulary dump.
3. Use là for nominal identity/classification, not as a general copula with ordinary adjectival predicates. Contrast Anh ấy là bác sĩ with Ngôi nhà lớn.
4. Teach không for ordinary negation; không phải (là) for negated identity. Distinguish these patterns before adding aspect-sensitive negatives.
5. Introduce yes/no questions with có ... không and alternative natural patterns. Avoid treating có as a required universal question marker.
6. Personal pronouns and forms of address depend on relative age, relationship, gender, status and context. Begin with tôi, bạn, anh ấy, cô ấy and họ; develop anh, chị, em, cô, chú, bác and kinship-based address longitudinally.
7. Teach inclusive chúng ta versus exclusive chúng tôi; do not leave 'we' unqualified when the distinction is being tested.
8. Introduce classifiers early: con for animals, quyển/cuốn for books, chiếc for vehicles and ngôi for houses. Classifier use varies with definiteness, counting and style; do not claim classifiers are always obligatory.
9. Teach numbers and quantity with natural numeral–classifier–noun order, and later distinguish một, những, các and plural interpretation.
10. Use ở for location and đâu for 'where'; teach đây, đó, kia and demonstrative noun phrases with context.
11. Teach simple subject–verb–object clauses with ăn, uống, thấy, nói, hiểu, thích and muốn. Verbs do not change for person.
12. Distinguish có possession/existence from ở location and là identity. Contrast có with không có.
13. Introduce gì, ai, ở đâu, khi nào, tại sao and thế nào in natural question positions; avoid imposing English wh-fronting.
14. Teach tense/aspect through context and particles such as đã, đang, sẽ, rồi, chưa and từng, not a rigid one-to-one past/present/future paradigm.
15. Develop noun phrase ordering, adjectives after nouns, possessive của, demonstratives, and relative modifiers with mà.
16. Later address comparison hơn, nhất, bằng, negation scope, modals, serial verbs, resultative/directional constructions and pragmatic particles.
17. Vietnamese word spacing is syllable-based, not always lexical-word-based; preserve normal orthography rather than joining compounds mechanically.
18. Expand vocabulary through home, family, education, work, food, travel, health, science, government, arts, society and educated-adult abstract concepts.
19. Introduce regional variants with labels and avoid silent mixing when it changes the canonical answer. Existing cards remain intact unless explicitly corrected in place.
20. Every card must be standalone in the shuffled deck; use prompts that resolve important ambiguity without relying on neighboring cards.

SEED AUDIT
3800000–3800014: original exploratory micro deck, unchanged.
3800015–3800099: systematic seed additions, 85 cards.
The seed introduces woman/child, school, teacher/student, animals, food/drink, adjectives, numerals, neutral personal pronouns and inclusive/exclusive we. Its controlled sentences open nominal identity, adjectival predicates, negation, yes/no questions, có possession, common classifiers, location with ở, content questions and common verbs. Early exposure is not a claim of full mastery.

CARD GENERATION RULES
- Six CSV columns: ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech.
- Reserved Vietnamese ID range: 3800000–3899999.
- Preserve original 15 cards and their IDs; append new cards sequentially.
- Keep EnglishPrompt unique within Vietnamese; one intended canonical Target.
- Standard Vietnamese quốc ngữ with full diacritics in Target; InputHint normally blank.
- Avoid introducing là as an English-style adjective copula.
- Label kinship pronoun roles and inclusive/exclusive we where needed.
- Use classifiers naturally, not mechanically, and keep syllable-based word spacing.
- Use ordinary punctuation and sentence capitalization consistently.
- Avoid redundant conjugation paradigms in this analytic language.
- Recycle previously introduced patterns with new vocabulary and controlled examples.
- Preserve the existing neutral/Southern-compatible micro-deck vocabulary; introduce alternative regional forms with explicit context.
- Treat CSV card order as canonical introduction order, not conversational adjacency.

CURRENT FRONTIER
At 100 cards the forward frontier is VI-20: classifier expansion and optionality. Next consolidate con, quyển, chiếc and ngôi, add cái, tờ, chiếc/cuốn contrasts, teach demonstrative noun phrases and possessive của, then deepen kinship pronouns. Move into đã/đang/sẽ/ rồi/ chưa with clear temporal context while continuing to introduce everyday verbs and nouns. Avoid presenting grammatical markers as mandatory English tense equivalents.
