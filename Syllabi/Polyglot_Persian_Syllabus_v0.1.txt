PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern Persian in Polyglot. It governs systematic development after the standardized 15-card micro opening. Vocabulary grows continuously and grammar develops in a widening spiral rather than in sealed paradigm blocks.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND WRITING

Contemporary standard Iranian Persian is the unmarked reference variety. Targets use ordinary contemporary Persian script and normal written orthography. InputHint is normally blank. Transliteration is not a parallel target system.

The curriculum begins with broadly neutral standard Persian while progressively teaching the relationship between formal written forms and very common spoken Iranian forms. Spoken reductions and colloquial forms are introduced explicitly rather than silently substituted for written-standard forms. Dari and Tajik are later comparison strands and do not displace Iranian Persian as the initial reference variety.

COURSE-WIDE CARD PRINCIPLES

1. Write natural ordinary English prompts and natural Persian Targets. Do not force Persian to mirror English structure.
2. Apply established course defaults silently. Add qualifiers only when the intended Target deliberately departs from a default and the distinction cannot reasonably be inferred from ordinary English or context.
3. Parenthetical qualifiers are sparse early scaffolding. Prefer natural situational cues.
4. Exact matching identifies the canonical natural Target selected for the card; it does not imply that no alternative Persian wording exists.
5. Persian normally omits subject pronouns when person is clear from the verb. Establish explicit pronouns early but increasingly use natural pro-drop.
6. The ezafe construction is a central longitudinal system. Introduce it early through natural noun phrases and expand it across adjectives, possession and longer noun phrases.
7. Persian has no grammatical gender. Do not invent gender distinctions where Persian does not make them.
8. Indefiniteness, specificity and definite direct-object marking with را develop through contextual contrasts rather than a mechanical English article mapping.
9. Compound verbs are central to Persian verbal vocabulary. Treat light-verb constructions as lexical units while also developing their productive patterns.
10. Present stems and past stems are both fundamental. Infinitive cards remain useful lexical anchors, but finite forms should increasingly be learned in natural sentences.
11. Establish one representative productive pattern clearly; thereafter reduce paradigm density and continue lexical expansion.
12. Avoid commas in card sentences whenever a natural alternative is available because the simple CSV workflow is comma-sensitive.
13. Check the entire live deck plus every new batch for duplicate IDs and duplicate EnglishPrompt values before committing.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 2400000-2400014 are the standardized 15-card micro opening. They preview را, present verbal morphology, possession, adjective placement and numeral noun phrases before those systems are formally established. Preserve them as useful footholds rather than treating the corresponding syllabus units as complete.

Cards 2400015 onward form the syllabus-governed seed and continuing canonical deck.

STAGE 1 — SCRIPT, CORE LEXICON AND SIMPLE PREDICATION

FA-01 — Core concrete vocabulary in Persian script
OBJECTIVE: Build useful nouns in ordinary Persian spelling without an alphabet-name or transliteration bootstrap.

FA-02 — Personal pronouns and reference
OBJECTIVE: Establish من, تو, او, ما, شما, آنها and useful demonstratives while preparing for natural subject omission.

FA-03 — Copular predication with بودن
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary present nominal and adjectival predicates and the common enclitic copula forms in neutral written Persian.

FA-04 — Basic negation and yes/no questions
OBJECTIVE: Establish negative copular predicates and natural polar questions without importing English auxiliary structure.

FA-05 — Ezafe with adjectives
OBJECTIVE: Establish noun + adjective phrases such as خانهٔ بزرگ and ماشین قرمز as a productive Persian noun-phrase pattern.

FA-06 — Ezafe possession
OBJECTIVE: Extend ezafe to possessors such as کتاب من and خانهٔ او before longer chains.

FA-07 — Demonstratives and basic noun phrases
OBJECTIVE: Establish این and آن with familiar nouns and descriptions in natural phrases and clauses.

FA-08 — Plural with ها
OBJECTIVE: Establish common productive plural formation while leaving lexical plurals and Arabic plural patterns for later treatment.

FA-09 — Numbers and noun phrases
OBJECTIVE: Establish basic numeral + singular-noun behavior with familiar vocabulary. The micro card چهار ماشین is an early preview.

FA-10 — Early nominal synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine pronouns, copular predicates, ezafe, demonstratives, adjectives, possession, plurals and numbers with expanding vocabulary.

STAGE 2 — FIRST VERBAL SYSTEM

FA-11 — Infinitives, past stems and present stems
OBJECTIVE: Establish that Persian verbal morphology draws on both past and present stems. Introduce this relationship through high-frequency verbs rather than detached stem tables.

FA-12 — Present indicative with می‌
OBJECTIVE: Establish the ordinary present indicative across a representative verb with person endings and correct نیم‌فاصله orthography where appropriate.

FA-13 — Additional present verbs and declining paradigm density
OBJECTIVE: Expand present morphology through high-frequency simple and compound verbs while moving rapidly from paradigm establishment to contextual use.

FA-14 — Verbal negation
OBJECTIVE: Establish نـ / نمی‌ patterns in natural present clauses.

FA-15 — Question words
OBJECTIVE: Establish کی, چی/چه, کجا, کی/چه وقت, چرا, چطور and other high-value interrogatives in natural questions. Neutral written forms precede explicitly colloquial alternatives where they differ materially.

FA-16 — داشتن and possession
OBJECTIVE: Establish داشتن as the ordinary have construction across useful persons and contexts.

FA-17 — Definite direct objects with را
OBJECTIVE: Establish را with contextually definite or specific direct objects. Avoid teaching it as a mechanical equivalent of English the.

FA-18 — Bare and indefinite direct objects
OBJECTIVE: Contrast ordinary objects without را and begin the interaction of indefiniteness and specificity.

FA-19 — Indefinite marker ی and یک
OBJECTIVE: Establish common indefinite noun phrases and the relationship between یک and enclitic -ی without claiming a one-to-one English article system.

FA-20 — Early verbal synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine present verbs, negation, questions, possession and direct-object marking with a widening lexicon.

STAGE 3 — COMPLEMENTS, PREPOSITIONS AND COMPOUND VERBS

FA-21 — Common prepositions
OBJECTIVE: Establish در, به, از, با, برای and other high-value prepositions in concrete contexts.

FA-22 — Location and direction
OBJECTIVE: Contrast location, destination and source through familiar places and motion verbs.

FA-23 — Object pronouns and enclitic pronouns
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common independent and enclitic object/possessive forms progressively and contextually.

FA-24 — خواستن and infinitival complements
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary want constructions and first subordinate infinitival relationships.

FA-25 — توانستن and ability
OBJECTIVE: Establish common ability constructions and their verbal complement behavior.

FA-26 — باید and necessity
OBJECTIVE: Establish necessity/obligation with باید in natural clauses.

FA-27 — First compound verbs
OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency light-verb combinations with کردن, شدن, دادن, زدن and گرفتن as lexical units.

FA-28 — Compound-verb productivity
OBJECTIVE: Broaden familiar light verbs across new nominal elements while respecting lexicalized meanings.

FA-29 — Separable elements and word order in compound verbs
OBJECTIVE: Develop the placement of objects, adverbs, negation and other material around compound predicates.

FA-30 — Stage synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine prepositions, complements, object marking and compound verbs in natural everyday sentences.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Unit completion establishes canonical coverage rather than learner mastery.
2. Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3. New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled lexical load while vocabulary continues growing.
4. Stable live card IDs are not renumbered merely because syllabus organization changes.
5. Previewing a later construction does not move the Manifest frontier.
6. Parallel units may interleave once prerequisites are satisfied.

CURRENT SEED ALIGNMENT

The 15-card micro deck previews several later systems. The expanded 100-card seed establishes FA-01 through FA-10 and begins the first verbal system through FA-16: Persian-script core vocabulary, pronouns, copular predication, negation/questions, ezafe adjective and possession patterns, demonstratives, productive ها plurals, basic number phrases, present indicative morphology, verbal negation, interrogatives and داشتن possession.

The forward frontier after the 100-card seed is:

FA-17 — Definite direct objects with را

The original micro card من خانه را می‌بینم is a preview and does not by itself establish FA-17.
