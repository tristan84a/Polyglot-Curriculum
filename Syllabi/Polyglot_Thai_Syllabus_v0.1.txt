PURPOSE AND BASELINE

This syllabus governs a modern Standard Thai course using ordinary Thai
script as the canonical production target. Contemporary educated Bangkok/
Central Thai is the unqualified initial reference. Other Thai regional
varieties and related Tai languages are not silently substituted for it.

The initial 100-card seed grows from an existing 15-card micro deck.
Those first 15 cards were already in the live app and are preserved without
renumbering. The seed tests short SVO clauses, nominal identity with เป็น,
bare adjectival predicates, มี possession, เห็น perception, อยู่ location,
ไม่ negation, ไหม polar questions and early numeral classifiers.

Thai is a tonal, largely analytic language. It does not use person or
number conjugation of verbs, grammatical gender agreement, or a European-
style article system. The curriculum should make productive Thai clauses
possible early without inventing inflectional paradigms.

WRITING SYSTEM AND INPUT

Use standard Thai spelling from the beginning. Do not require romanized
answers and do not replace written Thai with a phonetic transcription.
InputHint normally remains blank in the early deck; targeted reading and
pronunciation cards may later use carefully identified romanization or
pronunciation guidance when useful. Distinguish Thai letters, vowels,
consonant classes, syllable-final consonants, live/dead syllables, tone
marks and their combined role in tone realization. Tone marks alone do not
determine the spoken tone.

Thai words are generally written without spaces between words. Spaces
often separate clauses or larger units. Preserve natural Thai spacing
rather than inserting artificial English-style word boundaries.
Thai writing does not normally use a question mark in ordinary text;
unmarked Thai questions are acceptable canonical Targets.

The keyboard and exact-match app require special care with vowel signs,
tone marks and combining marks. Never normalize away linguistically
significant orthographic distinctions. Teach spelling and keyboard entry
longitudinally using actual familiar words rather than postponing useful
Thai until every consonant is memorized.

PRINCIPLES

1. Start with a small useful lexicon and rapidly reuse it in short clauses.
2. Use naturally selected personal pronouns; Thai pronoun choice is
   sensitive to relationship, status, gender, politeness and context.
   ฉัน and คุณ are introductory anchors, not universal translations of
   English I and you. Broaden ผม, ดิฉัน, เรา, เธอ and other common forms
   with explicit social context.
3. เขา is not grammatically gendered; English he or she may serve as an
   example prompt when the target form is shared.
4. Distinguish เป็น for identity/classification, อยู่ for location,
   and bare adjective predicates such as บ้านใหญ่. Do not mechanically
   translate every English 'is' with เป็น.
5. Teach คือ as an identificational/explanatory construction in context.
6. Verbs do not inflect for person or number. Once demonstrated, do not
   fill redundant subject paradigms.
7. Teach ไม่ in ordinary negation and expand its scope and interactions
   with auxiliaries and aspect markers.
8. Teach ไหม questions early, followed by natural alternative polar
   question patterns and short-answer constructions.
9. Question words generally remain in the position corresponding to
   their role; do not impose obligatory English-style wh-fronting.
10. Teach มี for possession and existence; distinguish it from อยู่ and
    existential constructions by meaning.
11. Thai numeral classifiers are central, not an optional advanced
    feature. Introduce familiar classifier families early, including
    คัน, หลัง and คน, and expand to ตัว, เล่ม, แก้ว and others.
12. Teach quantity and noun phrase order through whole expressions,
    including classifier and demonstrative placement.
13. Plural reference, specificity and definiteness are often contextual;
    introduce overt markers such as พวก, หลาย and บาง as useful forms,
    not obligatory translations of English plurals/articles.
14. Introduce tense/aspect/modality with time expressions and markers
    such as แล้ว, กำลัง, จะ, เคย and ยัง in meaningful contrasts.
15. Verbs and serial-verb constructions require Thai-specific semantic
    analysis. Do not translate each English preposition or auxiliary as
    an independent Thai word.
16. Politeness particles ครับ and ค่ะ/คะ should be taught with their
    actual distribution, speaker use and question/statement context.
    Do not attach them indiscriminately to every seed sentence.
17. Negation, questions, imperatives, requests and particles should be
    introduced cleanly and then reinforced in varied later cards.
18. Thai classifiers, measure expressions, reduplication and compounds
    should recur as ordinary productive grammar.
19. Introduce pragmatic omission of subjects and objects in context;
    the absence of agreement does not make omission ungrammatical.
20. Standard contemporary Thai remains the default. Regional,
    colloquial, literary and historical language receives explicit
    later treatment with clear labels when it changes the answer.

EMPIRICAL SEED: 3300000-3300099

The original micro deck (3300000-3300014) is retained verbatim, including
house, water, man, doctor, car, red, big, four and seven short sentences.
The subsequent 85 cards introduce woman/child, size/quality, pronouns,
identity and possession, common verbs, Thai as a language, ไม่ and ไหม,
อะไร/ใคร/ที่ไหน, อยู่ location and early classifiers. A number of short
subject variations make invariant verbal morphology visible, but future
expansion should not mechanically reproduce the same pattern for every
new verb.

The numbered units are an introduction map, not serial mastery blocks.
Script and pronunciation must be reinforced from the beginning even when
their systematic roadmap units appear later. Lexical domains develop
longitudinally.

CARD RULES

- Preserve six CSV columns: ID, Language, EnglishPrompt, Target,
  InputHint, PartOfSpeech.
- Keep EnglishPrompt unique within Thai and avoid exact duplicate cards.
- Use standard Thai spelling, normal spacing and appropriate tone marks.
- Do not require romanized Target answers.
- Use InputHint only where it genuinely helps input or pronunciation.
- Avoid commas in prompts and Targets when natural alternatives exist.
- Distinguish person/social register where it changes the correct answer.
- Do not mechanically translate English articles, copulas or tense forms.
- Choose a single natural intended answer and disambiguate prompts where
  multiple Thai constructions would otherwise be equally plausible.
- Use classifiers in natural Thai order and contextualized sentences.
- Do not repeat identical target sentences merely to vary English subjects.
- Maintain all live IDs; correct errors in place rather than renumbering.
- No artificial spaces between Thai words.

NEXT FRONTIER

The next cards should consolidate pronoun/social-register choices, question
answers, basic time expressions, demonstrative classifier phrases and
aspect markers. Introduce ครับ/ค่ะ/คะ in realistic contexts, alongside
further classifier families. Expand lexical variety while reusing the
established verbs. Develop the writing and tone system through familiar
words rather than an isolated alphabet-first phase.
