PURPOSE AND REFERENCE VARIETY

Canonical introduction map for contemporary Standard Lithuanian (bendrinė lietuvių kalba), in ordinary Lithuanian orthography. The initial production standard is broadly neutral modern Lithuanian. Regional dialects and historical variation may enter later as comparisons. Lithuanian letters and diacritics (ą, č, ę, ė, į, š, ų, ū, ž) are required in Targets. InputHint normally remains blank.

COURSE-WIDE RULES

1. Follow the Card Generation Protocol v1.0 and Charter. Syllabus units open branches; they are not closed chapters or fixed-size blocks.
2. Introduce noun and adjective declension as productive form-and-function contrasts, not as hundreds of isolated case tables.
3. Lithuanian has seven traditional nominal cases: nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental, locative and vocative. Some historical or dialectal locative variants belong to later comparison, not the first productive paradigm.
4. Nouns have masculine or feminine grammatical gender. Adjectives agree in gender, number and case; definite/pronominal adjectives receive separate treatment.
5. Establish the most common present tense classes with meaningful person/number contrasts, then widen lexical verbs through natural sentences.
6. Distinguish simple past, habitual past and future, and do not mechanically transfer English progressive/perfect categories into Lithuanian.
7. Pay special attention to genitive under negation and with quantity, absence, prepositions and verbal government.
8. Allow omitted subject pronouns where natural. Keep English prompts idiomatic and distinguish register only when needed for exact-match Targets.
9. Build a widening spiral: reuse known morphology in varied contexts while adding new vocabulary at a fairly aggressive but intelligible rate.
10. Avoid repeated mechanical sentence frames and duplicate English prompts. Check grammar, diacritics, UTF-8 and ID uniqueness before commits.

TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED: First shown in the canonical sequence.
ESTABLISHED: Demonstrated sufficiently for later reuse, not a claim about learner mastery.
REUSABLE: May appear in later cards even when not the main novelty.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough meaningful contrasts for a productive pattern to be recognizable; never a fixed card quota.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Material postponed because its dependencies or complexity would obscure the current contrast.
