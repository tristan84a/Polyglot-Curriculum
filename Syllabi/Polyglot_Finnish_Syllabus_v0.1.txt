PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical introduction path for modern standard Finnish in Polyglot. It follows the Card Generation Protocol v1.0: the existing 15-card micro deck is retained as the opening exposure, then the seed deck develops Finnish through a widening spiral of vocabulary and productive structure. Units are introductions rather than sealed chapters.

Finnish structure is authoritative. Natural Finnish targets should not imitate English merely to make exact matching easier. Standard written Finnish is the unmarked production reference. Ordinary colloquial Finnish is a later explicit strand and should not be silently mixed into the early standard-language course.

CARD-FORM AND MORPHOLOGY CONVENTIONS

Lexical verbs use the first infinitive as the citation form: olla, nähdä, asua, lukea, kirjoittaa, syödä, juoda.

Finnish case morphology should normally be introduced through useful phrases and sentences rather than isolated declension tables. One controlled contrast may be dense when it establishes genuinely new morphology; later nouns should sample established patterns while vocabulary continues to expand.

Consonant gradation, vowel harmony, stem alternations and partitive formation should emerge through real lexical items before being generalized. Do not imply that every Finnish form can be produced by attaching a suffix mechanically to the dictionary form.

Personal pronouns may be taught explicitly. In standard written Finnish, first- and second-person subject pronouns may be omitted when the verb ending identifies the subject; third-person hän/he normally remains overt. Do not force pronoun omission or retention merely to parallel English.

The partitive is a central Finnish system and must not be taught as a single English-equivalent case. Its major functions should be opened progressively and later synthesized.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 2200000-2200014 form the standardized micro deck. They establish talo, vesi, mies, lääkäri, auto, punainen, iso, neljä and several natural preview sentences. Those previews deliberately expose morphology before explanation: talon, hänellä on and neljä autoa. Preserve them as early footholds; the syllabus below explains and generalizes their structures rather than requiring the micro deck to have derived them.

STAGE 1 — BASIC PREDICATION AND NOMINAL STRUCTURE

FI-01 — Personal pronouns and olla in the present
OBJECTIVE: Establish minä, sinä, hän, me, te, he and the core present forms of olla through natural nominal and adjectival predicates. Make Finnish's gender-neutral hän explicit. Use pronoun omission naturally for first and second person once forms are clear.

FI-02 — Nominative singular and basic noun phrases
OBJECTIVE: Establish the unmarked nominative as the citation/basic subject form and reinforce the absence of articles. Do not manufacture Finnish equivalents of English a/the.

FI-03 — Predicative adjectives
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary singular adjective predicates with olla and broaden the adjective lexicon beyond iso and punainen.

FI-04 — Attributive adjectives and agreement preview
OBJECTIVE: Establish adjective-before-noun order in simple nominative phrases. Full case/number agreement develops longitudinally as cases open.

FI-05 — Basic nominative plural -t
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common plural subjects and noun phrases. Begin visible stem behavior without attempting an exhaustive noun-class inventory.

FI-06 — Plural predication
OBJECTIVE: Reuse olla and adjectives with plural subjects and establish plural adjective agreement in natural contexts.

STAGE 2 — PRESENT VERBS AND SIMPLE CLAUSES

FI-07 — First infinitive as lexical citation form
OBJECTIVE: Introduce a small set of high-frequency verbs in their ordinary infinitive forms while moving quickly into finite use. ANCHORS: olla, nähdä, asua, lukea, kirjoittaa, syödä, juoda.

FI-08 — Present tense: first common verb patterns
OBJECTIVE: Establish present personal endings through representative verbs. A first productive pattern may receive relatively dense coverage; later verbs should use declining paradigm density.

FI-09 — Subject pronoun omission
OBJECTIVE: Make natural first/second-person omission visible in standard Finnish while retaining hän/he where ordinary standard usage calls for them.

FI-10 — Basic negation with the negative verb
OBJECTIVE: Establish en, et, ei, emme, ette, eivät with familiar present clauses. Treat the negative verb as a Finnish conjugational system rather than mapping English not mechanically.

FI-11 — Polar questions with -ko/-kö
OBJECTIVE: Introduce ordinary yes/no questions and vowel-harmonic question clitic selection through familiar predicates and verbs.

FI-12 — Core interrogatives
OBJECTIVE: Introduce kuka, mikä, missä, mihin, mistä, milloin, miten, miksi and other high-value interrogatives as prerequisites become available.

STAGE 3 — OBJECTS AND THE PARTITIVE OPENING

FI-13 — Genitive -n
OBJECTIVE: Establish basic possession and noun modification with -n. Revisit micro-deck talon in a systematic context.

FI-14 — Total-object opening
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common total-object forms through simple affirmative clauses without prematurely reducing Finnish object case to a single rule. Reuse nähdä and concrete nouns.

FI-15 — Partitive singular: basic forms
OBJECTIVE: Establish common partitive morphology through high-frequency nouns, including transparent vowel-final forms and selected stem changes. Revisit autoa from the micro deck.

FI-16 — Partitive with numerals greater than one
OBJECTIVE: Generalize the micro-deck pattern neljä autoa and establish singular partitive after cardinal numerals greater than one in ordinary count phrases.

FI-17 — Partitive with mass and indefinite quantity
OBJECTIVE: Establish high-value quantity uses with substances and non-bounded amounts, including vesi and food/drink vocabulary.

FI-18 — Partitive objects: ongoing/unbounded activity
OBJECTIVE: Introduce the central aspectual object contrast through natural pairs where the event is incomplete, ongoing or otherwise unbounded.

FI-19 — Partitive objects under negation
OBJECTIVE: Establish the strong association between negative object clauses and partitive objects using already familiar verbs.

FI-20 — Total versus partitive object synthesis I
OBJECTIVE: Contrast bounded/resultative and unbounded/negative object clauses without pretending the entire object system has been exhausted.

STAGE 4 — LOCAL CASES AND EXISTENCE

FI-21 — Inessive -ssa/-ssä
OBJECTIVE: Establish location inside/in with concrete places while making vowel harmony visible.

FI-22 — Elative -sta/-stä
OBJECTIVE: Establish movement/source out of/from and pair it with inessive locations.

FI-23 — Illative
OBJECTIVE: Establish movement into with common illative patterns. Introduce stem-specific forms progressively rather than presenting one misleading universal suffix.

FI-24 — Interior local-case synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Integrate missä / mistä / mihin with inessive, elative and illative.

FI-25 — Adessive -lla/-llä
OBJECTIVE: Establish surface/at location and begin its broader relational uses.

FI-26 — Ablative -lta/-ltä and allative -lle
OBJECTIVE: Complete the external at-from-to local family.

FI-27 — External local-case synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Integrate external location/direction and contrast it with the interior family through natural place vocabulary.

FI-28 — Possession with adessive + olla
OBJECTIVE: Generalize the micro-deck Hänellä on auto. Establish Finnish possession as possessor-adessive + olla rather than an English-style have verb.

FI-29 — Existential clauses
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common Finnish existential word order and the interaction of existence with location and quantity.

STAGE 5 — MORPHOPHONOLOGY AND NOMINAL EXPANSION

FI-30 — Vowel harmony
OBJECTIVE: Make explicit the front/back harmony already visible in case suffixes and -ko/-kö. Keep practice attached to meaningful words and sentences.

FI-31 — Consonant gradation: first productive contrasts
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common strong/weak grade alternations through forms already motivated by case or conjugation. Do not teach gradation as an isolated consonant table.

FI-32 — Common noun stem alternations
OBJECTIVE: Broaden beyond transparent stems: e-type, i-type and other frequent patterns as encountered. Use lexical families rather than exhaustive declension classes.

FI-33 — Adjective case agreement
OBJECTIVE: Generalize adjective-noun agreement across cases already established.

FI-34 — Partitive plural opening
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common plural partitives after singular partitive functions are secure.

FI-35 — Genitive plural opening
OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-frequency plural possession/modification patterns progressively.

FI-36 — Remaining core locative/relational cases
OBJECTIVE: Add essive -na/-nä, translative -ksi and other high-value cases in communicative contexts.

FI-37 — Abessive and instructive/comitative awareness
OBJECTIVE: Introduce lower-frequency case functions only when useful; avoid a ceremonial complete-case inventory.

STAGE 6 — POSSESSION, PRONOUNS, AND REFERENCE

FI-38 — Personal pronoun object and case forms
OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-frequency oblique forms of personal pronouns in contexts supported by established cases.

FI-39 — Possessive suffixes
OBJECTIVE: Establish common possessive suffixes and their relationship to genitive possessors and ordinary modern usage. Avoid mechanically translating every English possessive with a suffix.

FI-40 — Demonstratives tämä / tuo / se
OBJECTIVE: Establish core proximal/distal/anaphoric reference and ordinary inflection through already known cases.

FI-41 — tämä / nämä and other demonstrative number contrasts
OBJECTIVE: Extend demonstratives across number and agreement without a detached full-case table.

FI-42 — Indefinite and negative pronouns
OBJECTIVE: Introduce joku, jokin, kukaan, mikään and related forms progressively with negation and case morphology.

STAGE 7 — PAST, PERFECT, AND EVENT STRUCTURE

FI-43 — Simple past (imperfect): regular opening
OBJECTIVE: Establish common past formation and personal endings through familiar verbs.

FI-44 — Past stem alternations
OBJECTIVE: Add frequent irregular or morphophonologically changing verbs while maintaining lexical growth.

FI-45 — Past negation
OBJECTIVE: Establish negative past clauses with the negative verb and past participial form.

FI-46 — Perfect with olla + past participle
OBJECTIVE: Introduce the Finnish perfect through natural experiential/result-relevant contexts rather than English-form matching.

FI-47 — Pluperfect
OBJECTIVE: Extend the established perfect architecture to anterior past contexts.

FI-48 — Object case across tense/aspect
OBJECTIVE: Recycle total/partitive object contrasts through present, past and perfect contexts.

STAGE 8 — MODALITY, INFINITIVES, AND IMPERATIVES

FI-49 — High-frequency modal and control verbs
OBJECTIVE: Establish voida, haluta, osata, saada, pitää and related constructions through natural complements.

FI-50 — Infinitive complements
OBJECTIVE: Develop first-infinitive complements and introduce other infinitival constructions only when structurally motivated.

FI-51 — Necessity with pitää / täytyy
OBJECTIVE: Establish Finnish necessity constructions and their subject marking naturally.

FI-52 — Imperative singular and plural
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common commands and requests with declining paradigm density after the core pattern is clear.

FI-53 — Negative imperative
OBJECTIVE: Establish älä / älkää and related forms in natural prohibitions.

FI-54 — Conditional -isi-
OBJECTIVE: Open conditional morphology through wants, politeness, hypotheticals and consequences.

FI-55 — Potential mood awareness and productive core
OBJECTIVE: Introduce the potential mood after more frequent modality is secure; calibrate coverage to contemporary usefulness.

STAGE 9 — CLAUSE LINKING AND COMPLEX SENTENCES

FI-56 — Coordination
OBJECTIVE: Establish ja, mutta, tai, vaan and other common coordinators through independent natural sentences.

FI-57 — että complement clauses
OBJECTIVE: Introduce reporting, thinking and knowing complements with established verbs.

FI-58 — koska and causal clauses
OBJECTIVE: Establish common reason clauses and ordinary clause order.

FI-59 — jos conditionals
OBJECTIVE: Combine conditional and indicative resources in natural real and hypothetical conditions.

FI-60 — kun temporal/subordinate clauses
OBJECTIVE: Develop common temporal and contextual subordination.

FI-61 — Relative pronoun joka
OBJECTIVE: Introduce relative clauses and progressive case inflection of joka as required by its role inside the relative clause.

FI-62 — mikä and broader relative/reference patterns
OBJECTIVE: Distinguish common joka/mikä functions through authentic contexts rather than English labels alone.

STAGE 10 — NON-FINITE AND PARTICIPIAL SYSTEMS

FI-63 — Present active participle
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common adjectival and clause-related uses of -va/-vä forms.

FI-64 — Past active participle
OBJECTIVE: Extend -nut/-nyt and plural forms beyond their earlier role in negative past/perfect constructions.

FI-65 — Passive participles
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common -ttu/-tty and related forms in natural lexical and verbal contexts.

FI-66 — Infinitive constructions beyond the first infinitive
OBJECTIVE: Open high-frequency MA-infinitive and other non-finite constructions gradually through real functions such as movement, location and manner.

FI-67 — Agent participle and advanced nominalization
OBJECTIVE: Introduce productive advanced participial structures once case and possession systems support them.

STAGE 11 — PASSIVE AND IMPERSONAL EXPRESSION

FI-68 — Finnish passive: present
OBJECTIVE: Establish the impersonal/passive construction as Finnish uses it rather than forcing an English passive-agent mapping.

FI-69 — Passive past and perfect
OBJECTIVE: Extend passive morphology across established tense systems.

FI-70 — Passive in ordinary spoken reference preview
OBJECTIVE: Prepare for the colloquial use of passive forms with first-person plural meaning while keeping standard written Finnish distinct.

STAGE 12 — COMPARISON, QUANTITY, AND NUMERALS

FI-71 — Comparative adjectives
OBJECTIVE: Establish -mpi comparatives and common stem changes in useful comparisons.

FI-72 — Superlatives
OBJECTIVE: Establish -in superlatives and frequent irregular forms.

FI-73 — Comparative constructions with kuin and partitive
OBJECTIVE: Develop natural comparison syntax and case choices.

FI-74 — Numerals and case interaction
OBJECTIVE: Extend beyond the micro-deck neljä through cardinal numbers, case inflection and noun-number behavior.

FI-75 — Ordinals, dates and time
OBJECTIVE: Introduce ordinal morphology and everyday calendar/time expressions.

STAGE 13 — DERIVATION AND LEXICAL EXPANSION

FI-76 — Common noun derivation
OBJECTIVE: Develop productive nominal suffixes through lexical families rather than abstract affix lists.

FI-77 — Common adjective derivation
OBJECTIVE: Introduce frequent adjective-forming patterns and semantic relationships.

FI-78 — Verb derivation
OBJECTIVE: Open frequent causative, reflexive/automative and other productive verb-forming patterns as lexical evidence accumulates.

FI-79 — Compound words
OBJECTIVE: Establish Finnish compounding and ordinary orthographic conventions through high-frequency compounds.

STAGE 14 — INFORMATION STRUCTURE AND CLITICS

FI-80 — Flexible constituent order and neutral order
OBJECTIVE: Establish Finnish word order as discourse-sensitive while identifying common neutral clause patterns.

FI-81 — Topic, focus and contrast
OBJECTIVE: Use natural paired contexts to show how constituent order packages given/new and contrastive information.

FI-82 — Clitics -kin and -kaan/-kään
OBJECTIVE: Establish additive and negative-polarity meanings through natural contexts.

FI-83 — Clitics -han/-hän and -pa/-pä
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common pragmatic meanings without forcing one-word English equivalents.

FI-84 — Question and discourse clitic synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Integrate -ko/-kö and other clitics with word order and discourse function.

STAGE 15 — STANDARD AND COLLOQUIAL FINNISH

FI-85 — Colloquial personal pronouns
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common mä/sä and related spoken forms explicitly as colloquial Finnish after the standard system is secure.

FI-86 — Colloquial verb forms
OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-frequency spoken reductions and person/number patterns without replacing standard written targets retroactively.

FI-87 — Spoken passive for first-person plural
OBJECTIVE: Establish the very common colloquial mennään-type first-person plural pattern and contrast it with standard menemme.

FI-88 — Common spoken phonological reductions
OBJECTIVE: Build recognition and selective production of widespread colloquial forms with regional variation labelled when material.

FI-89 — Register switching
OBJECTIVE: Develop the ability to choose standard written, neutral spoken and deliberately colloquial forms according to situation.

STAGE 17 — BACKWARD VALIDATION

FI-104 — Authentic contemporary written Finnish
OBJECTIVE: Sample journalism, nonfiction, fiction, government, professional and other educated-adult writing to identify productive gaps.

FI-105 — Authentic contemporary spoken Finnish
OBJECTIVE: Sample appropriate conversation, interviews, broadcasts and other speech across registers and identify gaps in colloquial morphology, vocabulary, discourse and listening-oriented structures.

FI-106 — Long-tail productive gap filling
OBJECTIVE: Continue indefinitely by adding the smallest natural cards or prerequisite-aware families needed to reproduce language encountered in authentic sources.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE

Teach Finnish as Finnish. Let cases, object marking, consonant gradation, vowel harmony, possession, non-finite constructions and discourse-sensitive word order emerge in prerequisite-aware layers while ordinary vocabulary keeps expanding. Establish representative patterns clearly, then reduce paradigm density and reuse them in increasingly natural language. The goal is not to complete a grammar table but to build an indefinitely extensible productive model of modern Finnish.
