PURPOSE
Canonical introduction roadmap for the Eastern Armenian seed and subsequent open-ended Polyglot development. Units are openings for productive structures and lexical domains, not sealed textbook chapters. Build a widening spiral in which earlier forms recur while useful vocabulary continues to expand.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND SCRIPT
Use contemporary standard Eastern Armenian, the Republic of Armenia's reformed orthography, and neutral colloquial-to-standard constructions. Write Armenian script directly in Target from the first card. Do not silently mix Western Armenian verb forms, pronunciations, classical spellings or diaspora orthography into the Eastern baseline. Those varieties are worthy of later receptive comparison but not interchangeable answers. Keep InputHint blank unless a consistent input convention is expressly introduced.

LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC GENERATION RULES
1. Preserve micro cards 3400000–3400014 exactly. Their definite nouns, present verbs, word order, numbers and possession preview structures taught more fully downstream.
2. Armenian has no grammatical gender, and նա can refer to he or she. Do not invent gender agreement. Let the original micro English pronouns remain as written.
3. The definite article is normally suffixed (-ը or -ն) and its surface form depends partly on phonological environment; teach this in natural contexts, not as a mechanical English 'the' substitution.
4. Eastern Armenian independent pronouns are useful but are often omitted when person marking and context suffice. Begin with overt subjects for controlled paradigms and increasingly prefer neutral omission.
5. The present imperfective uses a -ում participial form with inflected auxiliary եմ/ես/է/ենք/եք/են. Demonstrate the full first paradigm, then broaden verbs with declining density.
6. Preverbal negation with չեմ/չես/չի/չենք/չեք/չեն combines with the participial form; question marks (՞) attach to a stressed element and need not be located where English would put '?'.
7. Animate and inanimate objects differ in case behaviour. Teach the dative/accusative overlap with examples; avoid claiming that every direct object uses one universal suffix.
8. Noun plurals commonly take -եր/-ներ. Stem shapes, stress and declension alternations require later longitudinal development.
9. Most neutral clauses prefer subject-object-verb order; the auxiliary, information structure, topicalisation and focus create normal alternatives. Do not mechanically mimic English word order.
10. Teach infinitive, finite tense/aspect, participles and moods by natural usage, with high-frequency irregulars introduced as needed.
11. Use canonical English prompts unique within Armenian.csv, no exact duplicate cards, sequential IDs inside 3400000–3499999, correct UTF-8 Armenian and six-column CSV schema. Each card must stand independently when shuffled.
12. Grammar is normally taught through complete useful examples rather than metalinguistic prompts; a reference to a unit's introduction does not certify its exhaustive coverage.

SEED CHECKPOINT AND NEXT ACTION
At 3400099 the canonical forward frontier is AM-16 — Present verbal negation and polar questions. Develop negative verb forms and natural question prosody/orthography with several -ել/-ալ verbs, then introduce object marking (AM-17/AM-18), omission (AM-19) and questions (AM-20). Continue frequent vocabulary in parallel. The original micro previews do not leapfrog the frontier.

LONGITUDINAL POLICY
The later language-wide topics, including word formation, authentic reading, register, law, technology, literature and science, are domains to open and revisit rather than exhaustive lists. Once the major map is substantially traversed, identify productive gaps in real contemporary Armenian writing and speech and build targeted new cards. Existing live IDs remain stable.
