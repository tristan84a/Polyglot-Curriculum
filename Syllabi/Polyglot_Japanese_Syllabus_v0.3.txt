PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for Japanese in
Polyglot. It specifies what is taught, its dependency order, and how
major concepts should be represented in cards. It does not record
learner or deck progress. Routine card generation therefore does not
amend this document.

Vocabulary acquisition is continuous throughout the curriculum. Grammar
units define structural progression but do not freeze vocabulary. Units
have no fixed card quota. Introduction and establishment are distinct:
a pattern may begin in a few controlled cards and continue developing
through later cards whose principal novelty lies elsewhere.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a requirement that generation
follow every numbered unit as a sealed block. Japanese card generation
may take prerequisite-safe local detours, introduce a useful later
particle or form early, or postpone part of an earlier unit when doing so
produces clearer natural Japanese. No syllabus amendment is needed merely
because the route differs locally, provided the curriculum subsequently
converges on the roadmap.

The French and German syllabi provide a default structural model, but
consistency is not an end in itself. Equivalent organization should be
retained where it fits Japanese naturally. Any change that materially
improves the Japanese curriculum should be made, even if the required
divergence is small. No structure should be changed merely to make
Japanese look different.

Japanese literacy develops continuously through the ordinary curriculum.
Hiragana, katakana, and kanji are encountered from the beginning through
naturally written Japanese vocabulary and sentences. Later
literacy-focused units develop orthographic knowledge explicitly, but
there is no preliminary script or transliteration bootstrap.

TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED — A form, word, construction, script element, or distinction
has begun to be demonstrated.

ESTABLISHED — The canonical curriculum has demonstrated the item
sufficiently for its basic operation to be assumed later. This describes
the curriculum, not learner performance.

REUSABLE — An established item or structure may appear naturally in
later cards without being the principal teaching objective.

LEXICAL ANCHOR — A word deliberately selected to make a grammatical,
semantic, or orthographic pattern visible. Anchors do not limit
additional vocabulary.

CANONICAL SEQUENCE — The static pedagogical ordering of cards and
concepts. Scheduling frequency may adapt to performance; curriculum
order does not.

COMPLETION STANDARD — The coverage required before a unit’s principal
concept may be treated as established.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED — Material postponed because it would obscure the
current pattern, depends on later knowledge, or deserves separate
treatment.

COURSE-WIDE JAPANESE PRINCIPLES

1.  Vocabulary grows throughout all stages; grammar is not demonstrated
    indefinitely with a tiny closed vocabulary.
2.  Structural consistency with other Polyglot syllabi is the default
    where the language permits it. Divergence should reflect a genuine
    pedagogical or linguistic need, not novelty for its own sake.
3.  When even a small divergence materially improves the curriculum, it
    should be made without reluctance.
4.  Productive patterns precede exceptions and competing patterns where
    Japanese structure permits.
5.  Grammar is normally learned through controlled examples rather than
    terminology questions.
6.  Japanese is not forced into an English-style subject–verb–object
    analysis. Topic structure, particles and ordinary omission of
    recoverable arguments are introduced as normal properties of the
    language.
7.  Dictionary forms are treated as genuine lexical/grammatical forms,
    not as derivatives of the polite -ます form.
8.  Polite and plain styles are both foundational; their social and
    discourse distribution is developed progressively.
9.  Japanese uses natural contemporary Japanese orthography from the
    first vocabulary card. There is no preliminary transliteration,
    hiragana-decoding, or katakana-decoding deck.
10. Hiragana, katakana and kanji are acquired through real Japanese words
    and sentences. The curriculum controls difficulty through lexical and
    grammatical selection rather than by withholding normal orthography.
11. Kanji begins immediately wherever it is the ordinary spelling of an
    early lexical item. Readings are learned primarily as properties of
    words and contexts rather than as exhaustive detached character lists.
12. InputHint may provide practical romaji input/readings after an answer
    where useful, but romaji is not an accepted Japanese Target and does
    not replace Japanese orthography.
13. Every Japanese Target should be natural for its intended meaning and
    register and worth retaining permanently. Recoverable arguments,
    ordinary orthographic choices, and other normal Japanese features are
    not made artificially explicit merely for beginners.
14. Particles are taught through their functions and contrasts rather
    than through one-word English equivalents.
15. Counters, giving/receiving perspective, honorific systems and
    discourse particles receive explicit curricular treatment because
    they are structurally central to natural Japanese.
16. Established grammar and script knowledge become reusable in later
    units.
17. Contemporary broadly neutral Japanese provides the initial reference
    point. Register, regional, generational and stylistic variation is
    added explicitly later.

OPENING PACING DIRECTIVE

Japanese follows the Charter 0.7 small-seed principle without pretending
that French grammar maps directly onto Japanese. Begin with only a small
set of highly reusable nouns and descriptions in authentic orthography,
then move promptly into productive Japanese structure.

JA-01–JA-10 are parallel early lexical/literacy strands, not ten gates
that must be completed as standalone vocabulary blocks before structural
Japanese begins. The rebuilt opening demonstrates the intended scale:
a very small noun/adjective/verb seed can move directly into です,
は, polite verbal clauses, を, に, か, の, も, で, question words,
and verbal negation while new vocabulary continues to arrive just in
time. Continue script exposure through the new words and sentences
themselves.

The early French deck is a pacing model here only in the abstract sense:
small lexical injection followed immediately by structural reuse.
Japanese lexical choice, omission, particles, copular behavior, adjective
classes, register, and writing conventions determine the actual cards.

STAGE 1 — AUTHENTIC ORTHOGRAPHY, SMALL SEED, AND EARLY STRUCTURE

Japanese begins as Japanese. The learner is not required to complete a
script course before learning words. Early vocabulary is selected to be
useful, concrete, memorable, and cumulatively helpful for encountering
hiragana, katakana, and common kanji in their ordinary contexts.

JA-01 — Core concrete nouns and immediate kanji

PREREQUISITES: None. OBJECTIVE: Establish a first useful lexical base
using ordinary Japanese spellings such as 水, 本, 家, 人, 犬, 猫, 山 and
other suitable high-frequency concrete words. CARD STRATEGY: English
meaning → Japanese Target. Use kanji immediately where that is the normal
contemporary spelling. InputHint may give the practical reading/input
sequence after answering. COMPLETION STANDARD: A useful first group of
concrete nouns has established Japanese script as the normal medium of
the course.

JA-02 — Everyday nouns across all ordinary scripts

OBJECTIVE: Expand concrete vocabulary while naturally encountering words
written principally in hiragana, katakana, kanji, and mixed
kanji-kana forms. CARD STRATEGY: Script variety follows actual lexical
orthography rather than a script-teaching quota.

JA-03 — Core descriptive vocabulary

OBJECTIVE: Introduce useful い-adjectives and other immediately useful
descriptive lexical items in their normal written forms. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Full adjectival inflection, which receives systematic treatment
later.

JA-04 — Core action vocabulary in dictionary form

OBJECTIVE: Establish a useful set of high-frequency verbs such as 食べる,
飲む, 見る, 読む, 書く, 行く and other suitable anchors. CARD STRATEGY:
Lexical verb cards use dictionary forms from the beginning. Verb-class
morphology is not yet the principal teaching objective.

JA-05 — Reading and input emerge through vocabulary

OBJECTIVE: Ensure that early lexical selection cumulatively exposes the
learner to common kana patterns, okurigana, and useful kanji readings.
CARD STRATEGY: Do not generate kana→romaji or romaji→kana cards merely
to complete a syllabary. Repetition comes from real words. External TTS,
IME suggestions, reference tables, or handwriting practice may be used
by the learner without becoming permanent curriculum cards.

JA-06 — Conventional orthographic choice

OBJECTIVE: Establish from the outset that the Target uses the ordinary
contemporary spelling appropriate to the lexical item. Do not replace
normal kanji with kana merely because a character is new. Do not force
kanji where kana is conventional. Katakana loans are written in
katakana.

JA-07 — Lexical reading as part of the word

OBJECTIVE: Treat pronunciation/readings as lexical knowledge attached to
actual words. A character's unrelated readings are not bundled into its
first encounter. InputHint may record the practical reading needed to
enter a Target but is not itself an accepted answer.

JA-08 — Core people, places, time, and daily-life vocabulary

OBJECTIVE: Broaden the lexical base into people, common places, time
expressions, food, transport, and daily objects while maintaining
authentic orthography and manageable semantic load.

JA-09 — Early lexical contrasts

OBJECTIVE: Introduce useful contrasts such as 大きい/小さい,
新しい/古い, and other high-value pairs without turning the unit into a
dense list of easily confused vocabulary.

JA-10 — First lexical synthesis

OBJECTIVE: Establish enough nouns, adjectives, verbs, readings, and
orthographic familiarity that short Japanese sentences can begin without
a separate script prerequisite.

STAGE 2 — FIRST STRUCTURAL JAPANESE

The units below describe the main early strands, not a mandatory serial
block order. The canonical seed deck may interleave them whenever the
newest pattern remains clear.

JA-11 — Copular sentences: です
OBJECTIVE: Establish natural polite nominal predicates and basic
identification/classification using established vocabulary.

JA-12 — Topic particle は and descriptive predicates
OBJECTIVE: Establish は through simple topic-comment sentences, including
natural い-adjective predicates. Do not reduce は to an English-style
subject marker.

JA-13 — Dictionary forms and first polite verbal clauses
OBJECTIVE: Establish a small set of high-value verbs in dictionary form
while using natural polite non-past sentence forms early. LEXICAL
ANCHORS: 食べる, 見る, 読む, 行く, with additions such as 飲む as useful.
CARD STRATEGY: Dictionary forms remain the lexical form; sentence cards
may use -ます immediately. Do not require a long verb inventory or full
conjugation table before productive verbal sentences begin.

JA-14 — を: first direct objects
OBJECTIVE: Establish を with prototypical transitive objects using known
verbs and nouns. Ordinary omitted subjects are normal from the beginning.

JA-15 — に: first destination and time functions
OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-value に functions one controlled pattern at a
time, beginning with transparent destinations where useful.

JA-16 — Questions with か
OBJECTIVE: Establish basic polite yes/no questions with both copular and
verbal predicates.

JA-17 — これ / それ / あれ and basic question words
OBJECTIVE: Establish proximal/medial/distal demonstrative pronouns and
high-value interrogatives such as 何, 誰 and どこ as vocabulary permits.

JA-18 — の: possession and noun modification
OBJECTIVE: Establish transparent productive noun-to-noun relationships.

JA-19 — も: inclusion
OBJECTIVE: Establish basic additive functions and broaden particle
contrasts gradually.

JA-20 — で: first action-location function
OBJECTIVE: Establish core action-location で with familiar verbs and
places. Instrumental/means uses may follow later.

JA-21 — Ordinary argument omission
OBJECTIVE: Establish from the beginning that Japanese does not require
English subjects or pronouns to be copied into every translation.

JA-22 — Early polite verbal negation
OBJECTIVE: Introduce -ません with familiar verbs once affirmative polite
clauses are intelligible. A small controlled set is enough for
introduction; negation continues longitudinally.

JA-23 — Early structural synthesis and lexical growth
OBJECTIVE: Combine authentic orthography, です, は, polite verbal clauses,
を, に, か, の, も, で, demonstratives/question words, argument omission,
and continuing just-in-time vocabulary.

STAGE 3 — EXPANDING THE BASIC CLAUSE

JA-24 — Pronouns, names and personal reference
OBJECTIVE: Add useful personal reference without compulsory English-style
pronoun insertion.

JA-25 — この / その / あの
OBJECTIVE: Establish demonstrative determiners and contrast them with
これ/それ/あれ.

JA-26 — と: accompaniment
OBJECTIVE: Establish transparent accompaniment with と. Quotation uses
may follow when later clause material supports them naturally.

JA-27 — から / まで
OBJECTIVE: Establish source/start and endpoint ranges in time and space.

JA-28 — へ: direction
OBJECTIVE: Establish directional へ and contrast its role gradually with
destination に.

JA-29 — が: subject/focus introduction
OBJECTIVE: Introduce が without reducing は/が to a one-line subject
versus topic rule. Full discourse-sensitive treatment remains later.

JA-30 — Basic numbers and counters: introduction
OBJECTIVE: Establish basic numbers and the fact that counting commonly
requires classifiers/counters.

JA-31 — Particle and clause synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine known particles in natural short clauses while
previously introduced questions, negation, demonstratives and vocabulary
continue to recur.

JA-32 — Verb-class awareness
OBJECTIVE: Make ichidan, godan, する and 来る behavior progressively
visible as later inflection requires it. Do not front-load classification
terminology or multiple full paradigms merely to label classes.

JA-33 — Polite non-past reinforcement
OBJECTIVE: Broaden -ます and -ません across known verb types and vocabulary
until polite non-past clauses are established rather than merely
introduced.

STAGE 5 — EARLY CLAUSE EXPANSION

JA-39 — First existence preparation
OBJECTIVE: Build the vocabulary and particle patterns needed for ある and

JA-40 — Early sentence synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Continue combining established and partly established

JA-41–JA-43 — Flexible bridge space
OBJECTIVE: Use these numbers only if future card generation reveals a
useful prerequisite-safe bridge before adjective/existence work. They
need not be filled merely because they exist.

JA-44 — Advanced は / が foundation
OBJECTIVE: Revisit topic/focus contrasts when richer clause types make

JA-45 — Basic particle synthesis checkpoint
OBJECTIVE: Confirm that the major early argument and adjunct particles

JA-46–JA-51 — Polite verbal system checkpoints
OBJECTIVE: Close any remaining gaps in -ます, -ません, -ました,

coverage, not instructions to reteach forms already established earlier.

false synonymy.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED Narrow specialist vocabulary unless commonly
encountered by educated nonspecialists.

STAGE 21 — MATURE LANGUAGE COMPETENCE / LONG TAIL

JA-200 Major regional variation. JA-201 Common dialect features
encountered in national media/culture. JA-202 Generational and
contemporary usage. JA-203 Formal and literary vocabulary. JA-204
Historical/older language still encountered in modern texts, quotations,
institutions and culture. JA-205 Specialized vocabulary of broad
cultural relevance. JA-206 Rare but reasonably educated-adult
vocabulary. JA-207 Idiomatic and figurative long tail. JA-208 Continuing
lexical and literacy expansion.

COMMON OBJECTIVE Approach the practical asymptote of the Polyglot
definition of a complete modern language curriculum: vocabulary,
grammar, constructions, script/literacy, idioms, registers and
cultural-linguistic distinctions a typical educated adult might
reasonably know and encounter.

COMPLETION STANDARD There is intentionally no absolute terminal card or
kanji count. JA-208 is expandable. “Complete” describes direction and
scope, not exhaustion of every possible Japanese word, character reading
or expression.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1.  Unit completion means its canonical sequence adequately demonstrates
    its objective; it does not claim learner mastery.
2.  Once established, grammar becomes reusable. Pedagogical control may
    simplify which content is selected, but Japanese Targets themselves
    should remain natural and permanently valid.
3.  Vocabulary introduced at any point remains reusable.
4.  There is no kana or transliteration bootstrap. Hiragana, katakana,
    kanji, readings, and orthographic conventions develop continuously
    through authentic vocabulary and sentences from the beginning.
5.  Later cards increasingly combine multiple established structures
    naturally.
6.  New grammar should normally be the principal structural novelty in
    its earliest cards.
7.  The scheduler, not curriculum order, supplies extra repetition for
    difficult material.
8.  Existing valid Japanese cards remain valid even where they predate
    this syllabus. Future generation should fill meaningful prerequisite
    gaps and converge on the canonical sequence rather than restart the
    language.
9.  Future amendments may split, merge, reorder or refine units without
    renumbering existing card IDs.
10. Cross-language structural consistency is preferred where
    pedagogically neutral. It must yield whenever Japanese structure
    makes even a modest change pedagogically preferable.

AMENDMENT POLICY

This syllabus changes only when the canonical Japanese curriculum
changes. Generating cards or advancing through a unit does not amend it.

An amendment should identify affected units, state the pedagogical
change, say whether it affects future cards only or requires
existing-card correction, increment the version, and preserve a concise
amendment record.

AMENDMENT HISTORY

Version 0.1 - Initial draft. - Establishes JA-01 through JA-208. - Uses
the French/German syllabus architecture as the default while allowing
language-driven divergence wherever pedagogically useful. - Creates a
finite hiragana/katakana transliteration bootstrap. - Makes
kanji/literacy a parallel curriculum beginning with ordinary vocabulary
rather than a late isolated prerequisite. - Treats kanji readings
primarily as lexical/contextual properties rather than exhaustive
character-level lists. - Gives Japanese-specific systems dedicated
space: particles/topic-focus, て-form constructions, counters,
giving/receiving perspective, transitivity/voice, honorifics and
discourse/evidential structures. - Separates curriculum specification
from progress. - Uses completion standards rather than fixed card
quotas. - Preserves continuous vocabulary acquisition throughout
grammatical progression.


Version 0.2
- Adopts Charter 0.3 writing-system policy.
- Removes the finite hiragana/katakana transliteration bootstrap and
  Language=Transliteration phase.
- Begins ordinary Japanese vocabulary, hiragana, katakana, and kanji
  together from JA-01 using conventional contemporary orthography.
- Replaces JA-01–JA-22 with authentic-orthography lexical and structural
  units while preserving later unit numbers.
- Makes kanji a normal part of lexical acquisition from the first cards,
  not a literacy hurdle introduced after kana.
- Retains InputHint as optional practical romaji reading/input guidance
  after answering; it is not an accepted Japanese Target.
- Establishes permanent natural Japanese as the standard for every
  Target: beginner difficulty is controlled by content selection and
  sequencing, not by beginnerized Japanese.
- Strengthens ordinary argument omission and other language-specific
  naturalness from the beginning.


Version 0.3
- Adopts Charter 0.5 small-seed and just-in-time pacing.
- Clarifies that JA-01–JA-10 are parallel lexical/literacy strands rather
  than prerequisites requiring a long vocabulary block before JA-11.
- Applies the early French model as a pacing principle only; Japanese
  structure remains authoritative.


Version 0.4
- Adopts Charter 0.7 and explicitly distinguishes introduction from longitudinal establishment.
- Clarifies that the syllabus is a roadmap; prerequisite-safe local detours during card generation do not require amendment when the curriculum later converges on the intended path.
- Rebuilds JA-11–JA-51 around the successful 60-card seed experiment. Desu, wa, dictionary-form verbs, early polite verbal clauses, o, ni, ka, no, mo, de, question words, omission, and polite negation now develop together instead of being repeatedly introduced in separate early stages.
- Preserves later unit numbering from JA-52 onward so the mature Japanese roadmap remains stable.
- Treats the current 60-card deck as an empirical pacing model rather than a card-order mandate.
