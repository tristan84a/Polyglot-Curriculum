PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary
Portuguese in Polyglot. Spanish, French and Italian provide useful Romance
comparison points, but Portuguese structure, pronunciation, vocabulary and
usage remain authoritative.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a transcript. Vocabulary and
previously introduced grammar continue to develop longitudinally. Useful
structures may appear before their numbered unit is fully established, and
later double-duty cards may reinforce earlier material while advancing the
frontier.

REFERENCE VARIETY

Contemporary European Portuguese is the unmarked initial production
reference. Unqualified prompts therefore target ordinary educated usage in
Portugal.

Brazilian Portuguese is a major standard variety and is not treated as an
error or a peripheral dialect. It receives an explicit later strand covering
pronouns, address, clitic placement, progressive constructions, vocabulary,
pronunciation-sensitive contrasts and other systematic differences. Other
Lusophone varieties may be added explicitly where they contribute to
educated-adult competence.

Targets use contemporary Portuguese orthography, including diacritics and
ç, from the first card. InputHint is normally blank.

COURSE-WIDE PORTUGUESE PRINCIPLES

1. Begin with a small reusable Romance seed and move rapidly into productive
   phrases and sentences.
2. Noun gender is lexical. Definite and indefinite articles should reinforce
   gender whenever useful.
3. Adjectives agree in gender and number where the adjective permits it.
   Regular -o/-a forms are introduced before less transparent patterns.
4. Portuguese normally allows subject omission when finite morphology and
   context identify the subject. Teach pronouns explicitly, but model
   natural pro-drop in ordinary clauses.
5. European Portuguese uses tu naturally in informal singular address and
   vocês commonly for plural address. Formal and regionally variable address
   with você, o senhor/a senhora and related forms must be taught explicitly
   rather than collapsed into one English “you”.
6. Ser and estar form a central functional contrast. Introduce them early
   through clear contexts and refine the distinction longitudinally rather
   than relying on a simplistic permanent/temporary rule.
7. Há is introduced early for existence. Ter is taught for possession and
   common expressions. Brazilian existential ter belongs to the later
   Brazilian strand rather than the unmarked European baseline.
8. Present-tense verb morphology carries person and number. Establish
   productive -ar, -er and -ir patterns before broad irregular families.
9. The infinitive is a major structural resource. Ordinary infinitival
   complements are introduced early; the personal infinitive is developed
   later as a distinct Portuguese system.
10. The future subjunctive is unusually productive in Portuguese and deserves
    explicit treatment rather than being postponed as an exotic literary
    form.
11. Object clitics are structurally central. European Portuguese proclisis,
    enclisis and mesoclisis should be taught by environment and register,
    beginning with the high-frequency patterns that ordinary learners will
    encounter.
12. Clitic placement differs substantially in Brazilian Portuguese. Exact-
    match cards must not silently mix the two systems.
13. European Portuguese commonly forms the progressive with estar a +
    infinitive. Brazilian estar + gerund is introduced in the Brazilian
    strand.
14. Contractions of prepositions with articles and demonstratives are common
    and should be learned as productive written forms: do/da, no/na, ao/à,
    pelo/pela and related plurals.
15. Por and para require longitudinal semantic development. Do not attempt
    to reduce the distinction to a single English gloss.
16. Personal a is not a general Portuguese counterpart of Spanish personal
    a. Direct-object marking and clitic behavior must follow Portuguese usage.
17. Contractions and crasis involving à are part of standard written targets.
18. Negation with não precedes the finite verb in basic clauses. Expanded
    negative-concord patterns enter later.
19. Polar questions usually rely on intonation and context rather than an
    English-style auxiliary.
20. Portuguese pronunciation cannot be inferred perfectly from spelling.
    European vowel reduction, nasal vowels/diphthongs, open/closed vowels,
    lh, nh, r/rr, sibilant variation and stress deserve longitudinal
    attention without replacing normal orthography with phonetic targets.
21. The spelling system includes useful written contrasts whose pronunciation
    may merge in some varieties. Written exact-match targets remain standard.
22. Plural formation is mostly transparent but includes useful consonant,
    -ão and other patterns that should be introduced through real nouns.
23. Possessives normally agree with the possessed noun, not the possessor.
    Article use with possessives differs by variety and context; European
    baseline usage should be established before variation.
24. Demonstratives encode spatial/discourse distinctions and interact with
    contractions.
25. Relative clauses, complement clauses and conjunctions are developed
    after a stable simple-clause foundation, but common que clauses may appear
    earlier when transparent.
26. Perfective/imperfective past contrasts are central. Pretérito perfeito
    and imperfeito should be developed through narrative meaning, not only
    paradigm memorization.
27. The compound perfect tenho falado does not simply equal English “have
    spoken”; its iterative/continuative value in contemporary Portuguese
    requires separate treatment.
28. Future and conditional synthetic forms are productive in writing and
    formal speech; common periphrastic futures also deserve early practical
    coverage.
29. The present subjunctive, imperfect subjunctive and future subjunctive are
    treated as functional systems tied to triggers and clause meanings.
30. Inflected infinitives, clitic combinations, impersonal se and passive se
    are developed only after the prerequisite clause and pronoun systems are
    sufficiently clear.
31. Vocabulary grows just in time. Similarity to Spanish, French or Italian
    may speed lexical selection, but false friends and Portuguese-specific
    usage must be respected.
32. Contemporary neutral educated Portuguese precedes strongly regional,
    archaic or literary material.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1-100

The initial seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- masculine/feminine definite and indefinite articles;
- basic adjective agreement;
- personal subject pronouns;
- natural subject omission;
- present ser in identity and description;
- estar for location;
- aqui and ali;
- ter for possession;
- falar and trabalhar as productive -ar models;
- não negation;
- polar questions;
- onde, o que and quem;
- direct objects with ver;
- querer + infinitive;
- existir with há;
- first plural noun phrases;
- European Portuguese vocês as the ordinary plural “you” baseline.

The seed intentionally keeps object clitics, contractions and past tense
outside the first hundred so the opening first exposes the basic clause and
present morphology cleanly.

DEVELOPMENT DIRECTIVE

Future batches should continue from the live CSV rather than mechanically
rebuilding these units. Reinforce the opening through double-duty cards while
opening regular -er/-ir verbs, contractions and everyday noun-phrase growth.
Past-time language may begin once the present system is stable enough to make
the contrast intelligible; there is no requirement to exhaust every article,
pronoun or preposition combination first.

Brazilian Portuguese should enter as an explicit strand after the learner has
a coherent European baseline. When a Brazilian target differs materially,
the EnglishPrompt should label it clearly enough for exact-match production.

The syllabus may be revised if actual card generation reveals a better
prerequisite order, but ordinary local detours should converge on this path
without amendment.
