LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC AUTHORING GUIDANCE
Use contemporary Standard Uzbek in Latin script. Represent o‘ and g‘ with the same U+2018 left single quotation mark convention as the original micro deck (o‘, g‘, to‘rt, ko‘rmoq); retain sh and ch digraphs. Do not mix U+2018 with straight ASCII apostrophes or typographic right apostrophes within new cards. The Latin convention is the standard for this course, although Uzbek also appears widely in Cyrillic. InputHint is normally blank. Use conventional capitalization and sentence punctuation.

Uzbek is Turkic, typically SOV and agglutinative, with no grammatical gender and no articles. Nominal case is primarily suffixal: unmarked nominative, accusative -ni, genitive -ning, dative -ga/-ka/-qa, locative -da and ablative -dan. Introduce familiar case uses through meaningful contrasting phrases and sentences; retain the distinction between often-unmarked indefinite objects and marked definite/specific objects. Nouns after numerals ordinarily remain singular, and counting suffix -ta is common.

Noun plurality is often expressed by -lar, but plural marking is not automatic after numbers or whenever English uses a plural. Possessive suffixes combine with genitive possessors (mening kitobim, sening kitobing, uning kitobi); existence and possession are often conveyed by bor, absence by yo‘q. These are not direct equivalents of English 'have.' Common personal pronouns include men, sen, u, biz, siz and ular. Sen is familiar singular, siz is respectful singular or plural, and sizlar is explicitly plural; the seed has an independently prompted 'you guys' card with sizlar. Maintain politeness decisions without adding repetitive glosses when context supplies them.

Present/general nonpast is commonly conveyed by -a/-y plus personal agreement (ko‘raman, ko‘radi, o‘qiyman), but do not reduce the Uzbek tense/aspect system to simple English present tense. In particular, -yap- progressive, -moqda formal continuous, -di definite past, -gan resultative/experience, -ar/-r aorist/habitual, and future/intention constructions need separate later introduction. Negation of ordinary finite verbs regularly uses -ma-; polar interrogatives use -mi, attached orthographically. Subject pronouns may be dropped when clear, while the seed sometimes keeps them for ease of learning and contrast. Choose naturally plausible defaults as the deck matures.

The original 15 cards preview definite accusative -ni, genitive-plus-possessive construction and numeral -ta. Preserve these rows exactly as live identities. The seed then establishes lexical nouns/adjectives, pronouns, predicative person endings, attributive adjectives, plural nouns, counting expressions, possessive suffixes, bor/yo‘q, emas, polar and content questions, locative -da, and the opening of nonpast finite verb patterns. A few locative and negated constructions are previews of deeper branches.

All EnglishPrompt values must be unique within Uzbek, with natural canonical targets. The app shuffles introduced cards, so every prompt must stand alone. Verbs take dictionary infinitive -moq ('to see' = ko‘rmoq). When an English prompt cannot reliably distinguish formal vs informal address or specific vs indefinite objects, select a consistent default or rewrite minimally rather than creating unnecessary English metalinguistic labels. Be mindful that Uzbek has no he/she distinction in u; the seed includes an existing 'He is a doctor' and new 'She reads a book' that both use u naturally.
