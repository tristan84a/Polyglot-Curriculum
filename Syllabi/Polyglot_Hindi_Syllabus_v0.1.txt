POLYGLOT HINDI SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1
Status: Current
Charter version: 0.8
Encoding: UTF-8

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary
Standard Hindi in Polyglot. It was written after an initial 100-card seed
and therefore treats that seed as design evidence rather than forcing Hindi
into the sequence of an older syllabus.

The syllabus is a roadmap, not a transcript. Early comprehensible forms may
preview later systems, and introduced material may be established
longitudinally while new structures begin.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND REGISTER

The initial production backbone is contemporary educated Standard Hindi as
used in India, written in Devanagari. Ordinary colloquial vocabulary and
natural Hindi are preferred to unnecessary Sanskritization. Common
Hindi-Urdu shared vocabulary belongs naturally in the curriculum. Later
stages explicitly develop formal/Sanskrit-derived vocabulary, Persian-
Arabic-derived vocabulary, register contrasts, and comprehension of the
Hindi-Urdu continuum where useful.

Urdu script is not part of the initial Hindi production target. It may be
developed later as a related literacy/comprehension strand if the broader
Polyglot curriculum calls for it.

COURSE-WIDE HINDI PRINCIPLES

1. Use normal Devanagari from the first card. Transliteration is not an
   alternative Target.
2. Preserve ordinary Hindi orthography, including vowel signs, anusvara/
   chandrabindu and nukta where conventional.
3. Hindi is head-final. Postpositions, SOV tendencies, subordinate
   structures and verb complexes should be learned as Hindi patterns, not
   rearranged to resemble English.
4. Hindi has masculine/feminine grammatical gender. Agreement is central
   across adjectives, participles and many verbal constructions.
5. The three-way second-person system तू / तुम / आप is structurally and
   socially important. तुम provides a useful early familiar default; आप
   enters early as the respectful form; तू is taught but should not be
   overproduced without an appropriate intimate context.
6. वह and यह do not encode English he/she. English prompts may use he or
   she as ordinary examples while the same Hindi pronoun remains available
   across genders; gender is often revealed elsewhere by agreement.
7. Present habitual clauses combine a participial form with forms of होना.
   Speaker/subject gender is therefore visible very early. Do not hide this
   by choosing only invariant constructions.
8. Hindi has no ordinary indefinite article equivalent to English a/an.
   Do not mechanically insert एक unless the numeral/indefinite meaning is
   actually intended.
9. Case relations are developed through direct versus oblique noun forms
   plus postpositions. Teach visible consequences in phrases and sentences
   rather than a detached case-label course.
10. को is not merely an English to marker. Develop its recipient, animate/
    specific object and experiencer functions through controlled contrasts.
11. Possession is not a single have verb. के पास, genitive constructions,
    होना and experiencer patterns should be taught according to meaning.
12. Compound/light-verb constructions are central to ordinary Hindi and
    should enter early enough that vocabulary is not artificially restricted
    to simplex verbs.
13. Aspect and agreement interact strongly with tense, transitivity and
    ergativity. Past/perfective development must explicitly include ने and
    its effects rather than treating it as an exception to an English-style
    subject agreement rule.
14. Progressive रहा and habitual ता are distinct aspectual systems. Perfective
    morphology and common irregular perfectives receive their own controlled
    development.
15. The infinitive in -ना is both a useful citation form and participates
    in nominal/oblique constructions. Lexical verb cards normally use the
    -ना form.
16. Natural null arguments may occur where discourse licenses them, but Hindi
    subject pronouns need not be systematically suppressed in the manner of
    Spanish. Model ordinary discourse rather than an imported pro-drop rule.
17. Vocabulary grows just in time. Do not finish declension or conjugation
    inventories before permitting useful everyday language.
18. Exact-match prompts must identify speaker/subject gender whenever English
    alone would leave a Hindi agreement form underdetermined.
19. Politeness, gender, number and animacy/specificity distinctions should be
    made explicit in English prompts when they select materially different
    Targets.
20. Schwa deletion is part of pronunciation, not something represented by
    deleting letters from standard Devanagari spelling. Literacy uses normal
    orthography.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1–100

The seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- Devanagari as the sole ordinary Target script;
- early masculine/feminine adjective agreement;
- बच्चा/बच्ची as an immediately useful gender contrast;
- मैं, तू, तुम, आप, वह, यह, हम, वे;
- present copular forms हूँ/हो/है/हैं;
- habitual present agreement through बोलना and काम करना;
- नहीं negation and क्या polar questions;
- कहाँ, क्या and कौन;
- possession with के पास;
- first direct-object contrasts with and without को;
- an early visible oblique form बच्चे;
- चाहिए and चाहना as distinct high-frequency ways of expressing wants;
- respectful आप and gender-sensitive English prompting.

The opening intentionally exposes postpositions, agreement and an oblique
noun form before any exhaustive declension treatment.

INITIAL STRUCTURAL ROADMAP

HI-01 — Core nouns and gender
Continue concrete high-frequency masculine and feminine nouns. Gender is a
lexical property reinforced through adjectives and verb agreement.

HI-02 — Adjective agreement
Develop inflecting -ा adjectives across masculine/feminine singular/plural,
then invariant adjectives and common irregular lexical behavior.

HI-03 — Personal pronouns and social deixis
Establish मैं, हम, तू, तुम, आप, यह, वह, ये, वे in natural social contexts.
Develop proximal/distal demonstrative and third-person uses without forcing
an English he/she distinction into the pronouns themselves.

HI-04 — Present होना
Establish हूँ, हो, है, हैं in identification, description and location.
Reinforce respectful plural agreement with आप.

HI-05 — Habitual present
Develop -ता/-ती/-ते + होना across useful verbs, with enough person, number
and gender coverage to make the agreement system visible.

HI-06 — Negation and polar questions
Develop नहीं placement and clause-initial/interrogative क्या in ordinary
questions. Later distinguish interrogative क्या from lexical 'what' by
context rather than inventing separate Hindi forms.

HI-07 — Content questions
Develop कौन, क्या, कहाँ, कब, कैसे, क्यों, कितना and their oblique/agreement
forms where required.

HI-08 — Possession and availability
Develop के पास for alienable possession, genitive possession for relations
and inherent associations, and होना/मिलना constructions where Hindi
lexicalizes English have differently.

HI-09 — Direct objects and को
Contrast unmarked inanimate/nonspecific objects with animate, specific and
differentially marked objects using को. Avoid presenting object marking as
a rigid animacy rule.

HI-10 — Direct and oblique noun forms
Introduce common masculine -ा oblique changes and forms from other noun
classes through postpositional phrases. Plural oblique develops alongside
useful postpositions rather than as a table prerequisite.

HI-11 — Plural noun phrases
Develop productive masculine/feminine plural patterns, adjective agreement,
and direct/oblique plural contrasts.

POSTPOSITIONS AND NOUN-PHRASE STRUCTURE

HI-12 — में and पर
Location and basic spatial relations with में and पर, reusing known nouns
and introducing oblique forms naturally.

HI-13 — से
Develop source, instrument, comparison and agent-related uses in separate
clear contexts. Do not force them into one English gloss.

HI-14 — को
Expand recipients, goals, experiencers and object marking. Contrast functions
through whole clauses.

HI-15 — का/के/की
Develop the agreeing genitive postposition across possessor and possessed
noun phrases. Include pronoun-derived possessives मेरा, तुम्हारा, आपका,
उसका, हमारा and related forms.

HI-16 — other core postpositions
के लिए, के साथ, के बिना, के बाद, के पहले, तक, बारे में and other frequent
complex postpositions, introduced as useful phrases rather than an inventory.

HI-17 — Demonstratives and determiners
Develop यह/वह/ये/वे attributively and pronominally, साथ with quantifiers,
कुछ, कोई, हर, सब and related high-frequency determiners.

VERBAL SPINE

HI-18 — Imperatives
Develop imperative/request forms across तू, तुम and आप, including common
irregulars and politeness differences.

HI-19 — Progressive
Introduce रहा/रही/रहे + होना in present contexts, then combine with past
and future auxiliaries. Contrast ongoing progressive with habitual aspect.

HI-20 — Perfective morphology
Develop regular perfective forms and high-frequency irregular stems. Begin
with intransitive or otherwise transparent environments before making
ergative agreement the main novelty.

HI-21 — ने and ergative perfective clauses
Introduce ने with perfective transitive clauses and explicitly expose the
shift in agreement behavior. Use controlled noun/pronoun subjects and
objects before complex combinations.

HI-22 — Perfective object agreement
Develop agreement with unmarked objects and default masculine singular where
the relevant argument is postposition-marked. Teach through contrasts, not
a detached ergativity formula.

HI-23 — Imperfective past
Develop habitual past using था/थी/थे and distinguish repeated/background
events from perfective completed events.

HI-24 — Future
Develop synthetic future forms and gender/number agreement across common
verbs. Include होना and frequent irregularities early enough for ordinary
use.

HI-25 — Aspect/tense synthesis
Interleave habitual, progressive, perfective and future forms in contextual
prompts. English tense labels alone are not sufficient when Hindi aspect
requires a more specific choice.

HI-26 — Infinitive and oblique infinitive
Develop -ना as citation/nominal form and -ने in postpositional and modal
constructions such as के लिए, से पहले and देना/लगना environments as they
become useful.

MODALS, EXPERIENCERS, AND COMPLEX PREDICATES

HI-27 — चाहना and चाहिए
Distinguish wanting to act from wanting/needing an entity and the broader
modal uses of चाहिए. Develop experiencer marking where natural.

HI-28 — सकना
Ability with verb stem + सकना, including agreement and tense/aspect
combinations.

HI-29 — पड़ना and necessity
Develop पड़ना-based obligation and contrast it with चाहिए and other modal
frames.

HI-30 — लगना and experiencer predicates
Develop liking, seeming/feeling, beginning and other common लगना
constructions in separately controlled senses.

HI-31 — देना, लेना and light verbs
Introduce ordinary lexical uses first, then productive vector/light-verb
combinations such as कर देना and ले लेना where aspectual/pragmatic meaning
is clear.

HI-32 — करना compounds
Develop high-frequency noun/adjective + करना predicates as a major lexical
expansion mechanism, while treating each established collocation as real
Hindi vocabulary rather than assuming unrestricted productivity.

HI-33 — होना compounds and stative predicates
Develop common noun/adjective + होना expressions and contrasts with करना.

HI-34 — जाना as motion and vector
Establish ordinary जाना before developing its use in compound verbs and
passive constructions.

PRONOUNS, ARGUMENTS, AND CLAUSE STRUCTURE

HI-35 — Oblique pronouns
Develop मुझे/मुझको, तुम्हें/तुमको, उसे/उसको, हमें, उन्हें and postpositional
pronoun forms in natural recipient, experiencer and object contexts.

HI-36 — Reflexive अपना
Introduce अपना/अपनी/अपने as subject-oriented possession and contrast with
ordinary possessives in contexts where English is ambiguous.

HI-37 — Relative-correlative structure
Develop जो...वह, जहाँ...वहाँ, जब...तब, जैसा...वैसा and related structures
as a characteristic Hindi clause-organizing system.

HI-38 — कि complement clauses
Develop कि after speech, thought, knowledge and related predicates.

HI-39 — Coordination
और, लेकिन, या, भी, न...न and common coordinating/discourse structures.

HI-40 — कारण, purpose and result
क्योंकि, इसलिए, ताकि and infinitival purpose constructions in natural
multi-clause sentences.

HI-41 — Conditionals
अगर/यदि...तो and participial/conditional patterns from ordinary open
conditions toward counterfactual meanings.

HI-42 — Word order and information structure
Develop neutral SOV tendencies, scrambling, topicalization, focus particles
and postverbal material where natural. Avoid claiming that Hindi word order
is either rigid SOV or freely interchangeable.

HI-43 — Emphasis and particles
ही, भी, तो, तक, मात्र/सिर्फ and other focus/scalar particles through
contrasts whose English prompts make scope recoverable.

VOICE AND NONFINITE STRUCTURE

HI-44 — Passive
Develop जाना-based passive constructions, including tense/aspect agreement
and the pragmatic distribution of passive versus active Hindi.

HI-45 — Conjunctive participle
Develop -कर/-के for sequential and linked actions, including lexicalized
high-frequency combinations.

HI-46 — Relative participial/adjectival structures
Develop हुआ and other participial modifiers in natural noun phrases.

HI-47 — Causatives
Introduce productive causative morphology and common lexicalized causative
pairs with attention to argument marking.

HI-48 — Reduplication
Develop distributive, iterative, approximative and expressive reduplication
patterns characteristic of ordinary Hindi.

HI-49 — Comparison
Develop से comparative constructions, ज्यादा/कम, सबसे superlatives and
lexical comparison patterns.

HI-50 — Numbers, classifiers and measure expressions
Cardinals, ordinals, dates, age, prices, time and measure phrases. Hindi
number words require substantial lexical acquisition and should develop
longitudinally rather than being postponed until a single giant number unit.

LEXICON, REGISTER, AND LITERACY

HI-51 — Everyday lexical domains
Family, food, home, travel, work, education, health, shopping, technology,
weather, emotions and ordinary social life develop in parallel.

HI-52 — Verb argument structure and postposition choice
Revisit common verbs with their natural object marking, experiencers,
postpositions and complement structures.

HI-53 — Compound verbs and lexical aspect
Expand vector verbs देना, लेना, जाना, पड़ना, उठना, बैठना and others through
attested common combinations, emphasizing meaning and register rather than
mechanical compositional rules.

HI-54 — Idiom and phraseology
Common idioms, formulae, collocations, greetings, politeness routines and
conversational chunks.

HI-55 — Formal and Sanskrit-derived vocabulary
Develop educated written/public Hindi, tatsama vocabulary and formal
derivational patterns after the ordinary spoken backbone is strong.

HI-56 — Persian-Arabic-derived and shared Hindustani vocabulary
Develop common shared vocabulary and register contrasts without marking
ordinary Hindustani words as somehow non-Hindi.

HI-57 — English loans and code-mixing
Teach established loans, ordinary bilingual lexical practices and register
effects where relevant to contemporary educated usage.

HI-58 — Orthographic depth
Develop spelling contrasts, conjunct consonants, nukta letters, anusvara
versus chandrabindu conventions, punctuation and formal written norms
through actual vocabulary and text.

HI-59 — Hindi-Urdu continuum
Build comprehension of important lexical/register correspondences and,
if later desired, an Urdu-script literacy strand. Do not make Urdu script a
prerequisite for Hindi progress.

HI-60 — Regional and social variation
Introduce important colloquial, regional, generational and social variants
with explicit labels where exact matching requires them. Standard Hindi
remains the initial production backbone.

MATURE EDUCATED-ADULT DEVELOPMENT

HI-61 — Narrative and discourse
Longer event sequences, reference tracking, reported speech, viewpoint,
aspect choice and discourse connectors.

HI-62 — Institutions and public life
Government, law, education, health, economics, work, media and civic
vocabulary across neutral and formal registers.

HI-63 — Science, arts and abstract discourse
Broad educated vocabulary, argumentation, interpretation and technical-
general concepts.

HI-64 — Literature and cultural language
Common literary vocabulary, poetic/formal constructions and culturally
important expressions likely to be encountered by educated readers.

HI-65 — Semantic precision
Near-synonyms, register contrasts, polysemy, collocation and argument-
structure distinctions across high-frequency vocabulary.

HI-66 — Educated-adult long tail
Continue ordinary and educated vocabulary, phraseology, derivation,
register and discourse breadth without manufacturing obscure grammar merely
because the curriculum is mature.

CARD-SPECIFIC RULES

1. EnglishPrompt must be unique and identify one intended Target.
2. Use Devanagari Targets from the first card. InputHint is normally blank.
3. Lexical verbs normally use the -ना citation form.
4. Mark speaker gender in English prompts whenever first-person Hindi
   agreement would otherwise be ambiguous.
5. Mark addressee register (intimate/familiar/respectful) whenever तू/तुम/आप
   or associated agreement is not recoverable from English.
6. Mark subject gender where English he/she already supplies it; do not
   pretend वह itself encodes that distinction.
7. Avoid unnecessary एक for English indefinite articles.
8. Postpositions should be learned with the oblique forms they license.
9. Do not describe को as a simple accusative marker in learner-facing cards;
   expose its distribution through examples.
10. Ergative ने and perfective agreement require prompts whose event
    interpretation and arguments select a clear Target.
11. Hindi punctuation should be conventional and internally consistent.
    The seed uses danda (।) for declaratives and ? for questions.
12. Do not create transliteration cards merely to teach Devanagari.
13. Common spelling/pronunciation phenomena such as schwa deletion should
    not be represented by nonstandard spellings.
14. When colloquial and formal alternatives are both useful, give them
    separate disambiguated prompts rather than multiple exact-match Targets.

DEVELOPMENT POLICY

The first 100 cards are the empirical opening. Audit genuine linguistic or
prompt errors, but do not rebuild the seed merely to force every later unit
into serial order.

The next expansion should broaden plural/gender agreement and habitual
present while opening में/पर/से, direct versus oblique noun forms, को,
का/के/की possessives, pronoun obliques and progressive रहा. Past/perfective
material may begin once enough argument marking is visible; do not postpone
ने until every postposition or noun class has been exhausted.

The seed's चाहिए, चाहना and बच्चे को examples are intentional previews of
later systems and should be reinforced longitudinally.

AMENDMENT NOTE

Version 0.1 is the first Hindi syllabus. It was written after a 100-card
prototype. The prototype showed that the common Polyglot seed works well
for Hindi provided the language is allowed to assert gender agreement,
multiple second-person registers, head-final syntax, postpositional
possession/object marking and gender-sensitive habitual predicates
immediately.
