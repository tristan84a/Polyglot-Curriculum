PURPOSE
This is the canonical introduction map for Gujarati. The original 15 exploratory cards at IDs 5600000–5600014 are preserved unchanged; 85 additions at IDs 5600015–5600099 form a systematic 100-card seed. The introduction map develops grammar, script and vocabulary in a widening, prerequisite-aware spiral toward educated-adult breadth. Passing a unit opens a branch; it does not mean the branch has been exhausted.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND EXACT MATCH
Canonical Target uses contemporary standard Gujarati in Gujarati script, with full vowel signs, conjuncts and punctuation. InputHint is normally blank: the user types Gujarati directly. Preserve orthographic distinctions, including anusvara, virama and vowel length where conventional. Gujarati script differs from Devanagari; do not use Hindi spelling or transliteration as the answer. Regional and colloquial variants can be taught later with explicit labels. The original micro deck remains intact even where it previews structures or uses a less specific English prompt.

Gujarati is predominantly SOV and has grammatical gender (masculine, feminine, neuter), number, case/postpositional marking, verbal agreement, tense/aspect distinctions, and politeness-sensitive pronouns. Its morphology and syntax differ substantially from the Bengali and Hindi openings; do not transplant paradigms uncritically.

EARLY PRINCIPLES
1. Teach pronouns હું, તું, તમે, તે, અમે, આપણે and તેઓ in meaningful contexts. તમે is respectful singular or plural; English 'you' must specify the intended address where needed. Inclusive આપણે contrasts with exclusive અમે.
2. Teach present nominal identity with છું/છે/છો and negation with નથી; do not assume an invariant English-style 'is'.
3. Gujarati adjectives may inflect for gender/number: મોટું ઘર, મોટી ગાડી, નાનું પુસ્તક. Some adjectives such as લાલ are invariable. Establish the contrast through examples, not artificial exhaustive tables.
4. The original micro cards introduce possession with તેની પાસે ... છે; extend to મારી પાસે and negative absence with નથી. Distinguish possession from locative expressions.
5. Basic locatives use -માં and familiar location expressions: શાળામાં, ઘરે, અહીં, ત્યાં.
6. Introduce content questions શું, કોણ, ક્યાં and polar questions with શું; their placement is not simply English word order.
7. Teach SOV word order and initial present verb patterns with જોઉં છું, પીઉં છું, ખાઉં છું, પીવે છે and ખાઓ છો. Gujarati verbs require careful attention to person and gender in aspectual forms.
8. Ordinary present and negative constructions can depend on habitual participles and gender: હું દૂધ પીતો નથી is male-speaker wording. Explicitly specify speaker gender in prompts when it changes the answer.
9. Noun plurality and oblique forms, postpositions, and genitive નો/ની/નું agreement should be introduced progressively.
10. Later distinguish perfective transitive alignment and ergative -એ from present nominative patterns; do not project present agreement rules onto all tenses.
11. Keep past, future, habitual, progressive and perfective patterns separate initially, then combine them with time adverbs and familiar vocabulary.
12. Gujarati script, phonology and sandhi require recurring exposure alongside grammar, not an isolated spelling block.
13. Avoid long unproductive conjugation drills: establish a representative pattern, then expand with varied verbs, objects and natural clauses.
14. Vocabulary must expand alongside grammar through daily life, culture, technology, public institutions, science and abstract expression.

CARD GENERATION RULES
- CSV columns: ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech.
- Permanent Gujarati ID allocation: 5600000–5699999.
- Preserve original 15 cards and IDs; append new IDs sequentially.
- EnglishPrompt unique within Gujarati; one canonical Target per card.
- Use standard Gujarati script and normal punctuation; InputHint normally blank.
- Explicitly distinguish familiar/respectful address, inclusive/exclusive we, and speaker gender when relevant.
- Respect adjective gender/number agreement and Gujarati verb/person patterns.
- Introduce ergative perfective constructions as a separate later system.
- Keep sentences independently meaningful when shuffled and use new vocabulary throughout.
- Treat colloquial or regional alternatives as labelled variants, not silent replacements.

CURRENT FRONTIER
At 100 cards, the next frontier is GU-21: masculine/feminine/neuter adjective contrasts. Consolidate the first-person, third-person and polite present forms, then add more adjective agreement with a small set of known nouns. Continue with plural and oblique forms, genitive નો/ની/નું and dative/accusative -ને before expanding into past tense and ergative perfective patterns.
