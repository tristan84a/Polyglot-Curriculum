LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC AUTHORING PRINCIPLES
1. Mongolian is canonically subject-object-verb (SOV), with postpositions, suffixal case marking and no grammatical gender or articles. Third-person тэр is not inherently he rather than she; English he/she prompts may share the same Mongolian pronoun.
2. Personal pronouns include би, чи, та, тэр, бид and тэд. Чи is familiar singular; та is respectful singular and may also address multiple people. Prompt for respectful/plural address when the distinction matters.
3. Adjectives usually precede nouns and do not agree for person, number or gender. Bare nominal and adjectival predicates are common (Би оюутан. Байшин том.). Бол is an emphatic/topic-introducing particle, not a mandatory English-style copula.
4. Numerals precede nouns; do not mechanically pluralize nouns after numbers. Numerals may have attributive allomorphs (гурав → гурван, тав → таван).
5. Plural suffixes -ууд/-үүд, -нууд/-нүүд and -нар are lexically and contextually conditioned and often omitted when number is clear.
6. Possessive noun phrases often use genitive forms (миний, чиний, түүний); possession/existence can be expressed with dative possessor plus байна or байхгүй. Keep distinct the nominal negator биш and existential negative байхгүй.
7. Basic polar questions often use уу/үү or юу/юү depending on phonology and syntax, while content questions may include вэ/бэ. Avoid teaching one immutable question particle.
8. Case morphology is stem-sensitive and vowel-harmonic. Introduce real forms: accusative -ыг/-ийг/-г, genitive -ын/-ийн/-ны/-ний, dative-locative -д/-т and variants, ablative -аас/-ээс/-оос/-өөс, instrumental -аар/-ээр/-оор/-өөр, and comitative -тай/-тэй/-той. Do not force marked accusative on every indefinite object.
9. Citation verbs normally use -х (унших, идэх, явах). Productive habitual/general present often uses -даг/-дэг/-дог/-дөг; contemporaneous ongoing action uses converbs such as -ж/-ч plus байна. These are distinct aspectual choices, not mechanically interchangeable English presents.
10. The negative, past, future/prospective and evidential systems need distinct introductions; verbal negation may involve -гүй, үгүй, биш and auxiliary patterns. Do not treat English do-not as a single Mongolian suffix.
11. Spelling and sound are not identical: vowel length, harmony, reduced vowels, palatalization and consonant alternations should be reinforced by authentic material. Make the meaning and context sufficient for any exact-match translation.
12. Each EnglishPrompt must remain unique; every row must stand alone after shuffling. The single canonical Target is not a claim that alternative Mongolian expressions are invalid. Prefer natural English prompts over cryptic grammatical clues, and add qualifications only when they resolve consequential ambiguity.
13. Preserve existing card IDs and established micro prompts exactly; use monotone new IDs in the Mongolian allocation 12900000–12999999. CSV columns: ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech.
