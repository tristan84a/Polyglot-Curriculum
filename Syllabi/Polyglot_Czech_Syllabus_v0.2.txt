1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This syllabus defines the canonical curriculum sequence for Czech in Polyglot. It governs systematic development after the standardized 15-card micro opening.

Czech is taught as Czech rather than as a transformation of Polish, Russian, Ukrainian, Slovak or English. Related Slavic languages may provide useful comparative editorial models but Czech usage and Czech prerequisite structure determine this syllabus.

All Targets use authentic Czech orthography from first exposure. Czech diacritics are required where standard orthography requires them. InputHint is normally blank.

2. CURRICULUM DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Vocabulary, morphology and syntax develop together in a widening spiral. Establish useful lexical material before demanding large paradigms; introduce one major grammatical contrast at a time where practical; teach distinctions through controlled natural examples; develop vocabulary continuously; introduce productive rules before marginal exceptions; and teach aspect, case government, word order, clitic placement, phraseology, semantics and register longitudinally.

Write natural ordinary English prompts and natural Czech Targets. Apply established course defaults silently. Parenthetical qualifiers are sparse early scaffolding and should not merely reveal the grammatical construction. Exact matching identifies the canonical natural Target selected for a card rather than asserting that no alternative Czech expression exists.

Establish one representative productive pattern clearly; thereafter reduce paradigm density and favor selective contextual forms while vocabulary continues to expand. Subject pronouns should increasingly be omitted where Czech naturally omits them.

Avoid commas in card sentences whenever a natural alternative is available because the simple CSV workflow is comma-sensitive. Check the entire live deck plus each new batch for duplicate IDs and duplicate EnglishPrompt values.

3. WRITING SYSTEM AND INPUT

CS-01 — Czech orthography in ordinary vocabulary

Establish authentic Czech spelling through real vocabulary from the first card including á, č, ď, é, ě, í, ň, ó, ř, š, ť, ú, ů, ý and ž. Exact spelling is part of lexical knowledge. There is no alphabet or transliteration bootstrap and InputHint normally remains blank.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 2700000-2700014 are the standardized 15-card micro opening. They preview nominative agreement, accusative inanimate direct objects, mít possession, present verbal morphology and numeral-driven plural forms. Preserve them as footholds rather than treating the corresponding syllabus units as complete.

Cards 2700015 onward form the syllabus-governed seed and continuing canonical deck.

4. STAGE I — SMALL SEED AND MINIMAL SENTENCE FRAME

CS-02 — Core concrete nouns
High-frequency people, family, home, objects, food, animals, places, time, nature and body vocabulary. Prefer nouns reusable in later case and agreement examples.

CS-03 — Core adjectives and oppositions
High-value descriptive pairs, colours, size, age, quality, temperature, speed, ease and physical properties.

CS-04 — Core verbs and infinitives
High-frequency equivalents of be, have, do/make, go, come, see, know, want, need, like, eat, drink, read, write, work, live, speak, understand, give and take. This remains a continuing lexical strand rather than a list that must be exhausted before sentences.

CS-05 — Personal pronouns and present identity
Já, ty, on, ona, ono, my, vy, oni/ony; present forms of být; predicate nouns and adjectives; natural omission of recoverable subject pronouns.

CS-06 — Basic present-tense clauses
Establish one representative present pattern and several high-frequency verbs in useful clauses. After the first pattern becomes visible, decline paradigm density rather than drilling every new verb through every person.

CS-07 — Negation
Verbal ne-, negative forms of být and mít, simple negative responses and natural negative concord.

CS-08 — Questions and answers
Ano, ne; kdo, co, kde, kam, odkud, kdy, proč, jak, kolik; yes/no and information questions.

5. STAGE II — NOMINAL SYSTEM FOUNDATIONS

CS-09 — Gender and animacy
Masculine, feminine, neuter; masculine animate/inanimate distinctions; common ending cues and useful exceptions.

CS-10 — Nominative agreement
Adjectives, demonstratives, predicate adjectives and selected plural patterns.

CS-11 — Accusative: direct objects
Main singular and plural patterns; masculine animate versus inanimate; adjective and demonstrative agreement.

CS-12 — Locative: location and topic
V/ve, na, o with locative; noun/adjective forms and common stem alternations.

CS-13 — Genitive
Possession, origin, absence, quantity, do, z/ze, od, bez and common lexical government. Do not import genitive-under-negation rules mechanically from other Slavic languages.

CS-14 — Dative
Recipients, beneficiaries, experiencers, k/ke and common dative-governing verbs and predicates.

CS-15 — Instrumental
S/se accompaniment, means/instrument, selected complements and full agreement.

CS-16 — Vocative
Productive direct-address forms for names, kinship terms, social nouns and common irregular patterns.

CS-17 — Case synthesis I
Mix all seven cases in natural short sentences without announcing which case is required.

6. STAGE III — PRESENT VERBS, REFLEXIVES, MODALS

CS-18 — Present conjugation patterns
Productive present patterns, high-frequency irregulars, stem alternations, person/number endings and pronoun omission. Use selective contextual coverage after initial pattern establishment.

CS-19 — Reflexive se and si
Inherent reflexives, reflexive and reciprocal meanings, dative si and initial unstressed placement.

CS-20 — Modal verbs and infinitives
Moci, muset, chtít, smět and other common modal constructions with infinitives.

CS-21 — Imperative
Informal singular, plural/formal, irregular imperatives, negative commands and polite alternatives.

CS-22 — Possession and svůj
Possessive pronouns, mít, genitive possession where natural and especially svůj for subject-coreferential possession.

7. STAGE IV — PAST, FUTURE, ASPECT AND MOTION

CS-23 — Past tense foundations
L-participle, gender/number agreement, first/second-person auxiliaries, third-person past without overt auxiliary and negation.

CS-24 — Past auxiliaries and clitic behavior
Jsem, jsi, jsme, jste as unstressed elements; interaction with se/si and pronoun clitics; neutral second-position tendencies.

CS-25 — Imperfective and perfective aspect
Completed event versus process/repetition; common aspect pairs; prefixal/suffixal relations; aspect as lexical grammar rather than a tense.

CS-26 — Imperfective future
Budu + infinitive across useful persons, negation and questions.

CS-27 — Perfective future
Present-form morphology with future reference and contrast with imperfective analytic future.

CS-28 — Motion verb foundations
Jít/chodit, jet/jezdit and directional versus habitual/non-directional motion.

CS-29 — Prefixed motion verbs
High-frequency formations such as přijít, odejít, vejít, vyjít, přijet and odjet; direction, aspect and case government.

CS-30 — Aspect synthesis
Past/present/future contexts; process, repetition, result, completion, sequence and lexical exceptions.

8. STAGE V — PRONOUNS, CLITICS, NUMERALS

CS-31 — Personal pronouns across cases
Long and clitic forms, prepositional n- forms, emphasis, dative and accusative pronouns.

CS-32 — Clitic clusters
Se/si, short pronouns, past auxiliaries, later conditional forms and neutral ordering.

CS-33 — Demonstratives and indefinites
Ten/ta/to, tento, někdo/něco, nikdo/nic, nějaký/žádný and related adverbs; negative concord.

CS-34 — Interrogative and relative pronouns
Kdo, co, který, jaký and čí across cases and meanings.

CS-35 — Cardinal numbers
Common numbers, compound numerals, 2–4 versus 5+ noun government and quantities.

CS-36 — Numeral syntax
Case behavior, quantified noun phrases, predicate agreement, animate/person forms and approximate quantities.

CS-37 — Ordinals, dates, time, age and measurement
Ordinals, calendar dates, clock time, age, prices, dimensions, years, centuries, fractions and decimals.

9. STAGE VI — ADJECTIVES, ADVERBS, COMPARISON

CS-38 — Adjective declension consolidation
Hard and soft adjective paradigms and high-value irregular/possessive patterns across cases.

CS-39 — Comparison
Comparatives, superlatives, irregular common forms, než and adverb comparison.

CS-40 — Adverbs
Productive adjective-to-adverb relations, lexical adverbs, degree and sentence adverbs.

CS-41 — Predicate and lexical adjective patterns
Productive contemporary patterns only; avoid treating historically broader short-form systems as ordinary modern productive grammar.

10. STAGE VII — WORD ORDER AND INFORMATION STRUCTURE

CS-42 — Neutral Czech word order
Common SVO baseline, subject omission, topic-comment organization, old/new information and neutral object placement.

CS-43 — Second-position clitics
Clitic placement after the first syntactic constituent; auxiliary/reflexive/pronominal clusters; conjunction and clause effects.

CS-44 — Focus and marked order
Contrastive fronting, sentence-final focus, emphatic pronouns and discourse-driven alternatives.

CS-45 — Word-order synthesis
Natural sentences combining case, clitics, aspect, pronoun omission and information structure.

11. STAGE VIII — CONDITIONALS, PASSIVES, IMPERSONALS

CS-46 — Conditional bych
Bych, bys, by, bychom, byste, by with l-participles, agreement, clitic placement, politeness and hypotheticals.

CS-47 — Kdyby and counterfactual meanings
Unreal present/future, unreal past and condition/result ordering.

CS-48 — Participial passive
Být + passive participle, agreement, agents where natural and comparison with preferred active/reflexive alternatives.

CS-49 — Reflexive passive and impersonal se
Institutional/general statements, signs, instructions and contrast with true reflexive meanings.

CS-50 — Other impersonal constructions
Weather, experiencers, necessity, general human subjects and impersonal predicates.

12. STAGE IX — SUBORDINATION

CS-51 — Basic conjunctions
A, ale, nebo, protože, proto, takže, když, jestli; coordination and subordination.

CS-52 — Complement clauses
Že, jestli/zda; statements and questions after verbs of knowing, thinking, saying, hoping, seeing and wanting.

CS-53 — Relative clauses
Který across gender/number/case; kdo/co where appropriate; prepositional relatives.

CS-54 — Temporal clauses
Když, až, než, dokud, jakmile; simultaneity, sequence and aspect.

CS-55 — Purpose, cause, result and concession
Aby, protože, proto, takže, i když/přestože and common alternatives.

CS-56 — Indirect speech
Reported statements/questions and Czech deictic/reference patterns without importing English sequence-of-tense rules.

13. STAGE X — WORD FORMATION

CS-57 — Verbal prefixes
Productive meanings, aspectual effects, lexicalized meanings and limits of predictability.

CS-58 — Secondary imperfectives and suffixation
Productive imperfectivization, repeated/ongoing meanings and derivational families.

CS-59 — Noun derivation
Professions/people, action/result nouns, abstracts, productive diminutives and lexicalized formations.

CS-60 — Adjective derivation
Relational, origin/material, possessive, negative and evaluative formations.

CS-61 — Compounds and international vocabulary
Native formations, compounds, learned vocabulary and Czech adaptation of borrowings.

14. STAGE XI — GOVERNMENT, SEMANTICS, COLLOCATION

CS-62 — Verb government
Case/preposition requirements especially where Czech differs from English.

CS-63 — Adjective and noun government
Required cases/prepositions and common semantic contrasts.

CS-64 — Preposition networks
V/ve, na, do, z/ze, od, k/ke, s/se, u, o, po, pro, přes, před, za, mezi, nad and pod in spatial and abstract uses.

CS-65 — Polysemy and lexical boundaries
Separate Czech meanings that collapse into one English gloss through natural contextual prompts where possible. Use parenthetical disambiguation only when context and course defaults cannot identify the intended distinction economically.

CS-66 — Collocations
Verb+noun, adjective+noun, adverb+adjective, institutional and everyday fixed preferences.

15. STAGE XII — STANDARD, SPOKEN AND COMMON CZECH

CS-67 — Standard versus ordinary spoken Czech
Neutral spoken variants and high-frequency Common Czech forms with their standard equivalents and contexts.

CS-68 — Common Czech morphology
Widely encountered adjective endings, ý/ej correspondences, common leveling and frequent colloquial forms where genuinely widespread. These are sociolinguistic variants rather than errors and are not universal Czech.

CS-69 — Spoken syntax and particles
Discourse particles, fillers, softeners, emphasis, ellipsis and response formulas.

CS-70 — Formal and written Czech
Formal correspondence, public/institutional language, written tendencies, polite address and titles.

CS-71 — Register synthesis
Parallel meanings in neutral, colloquial/informal and formal contexts where Czech genuinely distinguishes them.

16. STAGE XIII — PHRASEOLOGY AND PRAGMATICS

CS-72 — Everyday formulas
Greetings, leave-taking, thanks, apologies, requests, invitations, congratulations, condolences, service and telephone encounters.

CS-73 — Politeness and social distance

CS-74 — Idioms and fixed expressions

CS-75 — Discourse markers

25. CROSS-CURRICULAR REQUIREMENTS

Vocabulary, case government, aspect, word order, clitic placement, register, phraseology, exact orthography and naturalness remain longitudinal requirements after their first dedicated units. Later cards should increasingly resemble unrestricted adult Czech while retaining sufficiently specific natural English prompts.

26. CARD AUTHORING NOTES

EnglishPrompt: Use ordinary natural English. Prefer context to metalinguistic labels when a distinction can be made naturally. Parenthetical disambiguation may be used sparingly when lexical sense, register or another distinction genuinely cannot be recovered from context or established defaults.

Target: Use one natural Czech string. Do not distort Czech to mirror English structure.

InputHint: Normally blank. Do not use it for pronunciation, ASCII spelling or stripped diacritics.

Variants: When forms differ meaningfully by register, region, construction or curricular objective, use separate cards with naturally distinguishing prompts where practical rather than multiple accepted Targets.

27. COMPLETION PHILOSOPHY

A unit is established when its productive core is sufficiently represented that later cards can rely on it as background knowledge. Completion does not mean every lexical item using that structure has appeared.

Early units require enough varied examples to establish a pattern beyond memorized phrases. Later synthesis and lexical expansion repeatedly reactivate earlier grammar. Once the syllabus has been substantially traversed, use authentic contemporary Czech writing and speech for backward validation. Authentic sources diagnose productive gaps; they do not automatically become flashcards.

28. CURRENT SEED ALIGNMENT

The expanded 100-card seed establishes CS-01 through CS-08 and opens CS-09 and CS-10: authentic orthography, core lexical strands, personal pronouns and být, basic present clauses, negation, questions, noun gender/animacy awareness and initial nominative agreement. The original micro card Vidím dům. previews accusative direct objects and Vidím čtyři auta. previews later numeral syntax; neither moves the systematic frontier.

The forward frontier after the 100-card seed is:

CS-11 — Accusative: direct objects
