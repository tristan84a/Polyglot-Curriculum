PURPOSE
Graduate the original 15-card micro deck (6100000–6100014) into a systematic 100-card seed (6100000–6100099). The initial cards are preserved verbatim. This introduction map guides eventual educated-adult breadth rather than prescribing sealed chapters: opened branches continue to recur as the curriculum grows.

REFERENCE VARIETY, REGISTER AND SCRIPT
Use contemporary, natural Cambodian Khmer in standard Khmer orthography, not a transliterated Target. The seed uses neutral common expressions and familiar spoken constructions; later introduce formal/written alternatives explicitly rather than mixing registers without notice. The script is an abugida with two consonant series affecting the realization of dependent vowels, subscript consonants, inherent vowels, silent letters and specialized diacritics. Spaces are not reliable English-style word separators; retain normal Khmer spacing and punctuation rather than adding artificial spaces between every word. InputHint remains blank until a consistent, justified keyboard hint system is adopted. Khmer is not a tonal language; do not transfer Thai or Vietnamese tonal pedagogy to Khmer.

OPENING PRINCIPLES
1. Preserve the 15 micro cards and their IDs. They preview SVO word order, non-copular adjective predicates, ជា nominal identity, មាន possession and គ្រឿង for vehicles.
2. Khmer is broadly analytic: verbs do not conjugate for person or number. Temporal interpretation may be contextual and aspect/time markers are not one-for-one English tense inflections.
3. Introduce subject–verb–object clauses and postnominal modifiers through small controlled examples. An English 'is' with a noun often requires ជា, while ordinary adjective predicates do not require a universal copula.
4. Distinguish ជា for classification, នៅ for location, and មាន for having/existence; do not equate all with 'to be.'
5. Introduce the common negative frame មិន…ទេ; nominal identity uses មិនមែនជា…ទេ. Other negatives and modal scope belong later.
6. Controlled polar questions use តើ…ទេ or relevant មែនទេ patterns, but everyday speech has other possible formats; teach variation later rather than imposing one obligatory question frame.
7. Personal reference is socially contextual. ខ្ញុំ, អ្នក and គាត់ are useful early canonical choices, not universal equivalents of English I/you/he/she. គាត់ need not encode gender. Introduce pronoun avoidance, titles, kinship terms and hierarchy in later units.
8. Numerals follow counted nouns in many basic phrases, with classifiers such as ក្បាល for animals/other lexical classes, គ្រឿង for vehicles and នាក់ for people. Classifiers are not obligatory in every context; expand exceptions and optionality gradually.
9. Teach content questions with អ្វី, នរណា and ឯណា, keeping natural Khmer word order and independently interpretable prompts.
10. Build vocabulary and grammar together. Initial verbs: ញ៉ាំ, ផឹក, អាន, ឃើញ, ទៅ, មក, និយាយ and យល់.
11. Exact-match English prompts specify social reference, plurality or meaning only when these distinctions materially affect Target. An exact Target is canonical for the card, not the only possible Khmer translation.
12. Scripts, common grammar, social register and broader lexical domains should be revisited via controlled contrast, authentic-text inspection and widening-spiral practice.

CURRENT FRONTIER

The first 100 cards establish simple nouns, descriptive predicates, ជា identity, មិន…ទេ and មិនមែនជា…ទេ negatives, តើ questions, មាន possession, នៅ location, and simple everyday SVO clauses. Recycle initial material in independent natural sentences.

CARD GENERATION RULES
- ID allocation: 6100000–6199999. Original micro IDs 6100000–6100014 are stable. Append subsequent new cards from 6100100.
- Canonical CSV header: ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech. UTF-8 with BOM when practical.
- All EnglishPrompt values within Khmer must be unique; no exact duplicate cards.
- Target: ordinary written Khmer script with correct Unicode and natural word spacing. Do not romanize the Target.
- InputHint blank until a coherent Khmer input approach is chosen.
- Preserve existing cards even if later development chooses a slightly different register or lexical variant; introduce distinct variants with carefully differentiated prompts.
- Prefer plausible, idiomatic Khmer and exact-match prompts that stand alone after shuffling. Never rely on a preceding card to interpret an example.
- New grammar stays active in later lessons; avoid long blocks of isolated vocabulary or duplicate English-to-Khmer mappings.
- Check classifier semantics, register, orthographic sequencing and script entry carefully in subsequent batches.
