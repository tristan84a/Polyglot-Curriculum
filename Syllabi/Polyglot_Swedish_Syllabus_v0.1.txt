PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary
Swedish in Polyglot. It was written after an initial 100-card seed. German
and Icelandic provide useful Germanic comparison points, but Swedish
structure, vocabulary, pronunciation and usage remain authoritative.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a transcript. Vocabulary and
previously introduced grammar continue to develop longitudinally.

REFERENCE VARIETY ORTHOGRAPHY AND PRONUNCIATION

The initial production baseline is contemporary neutral Standard Swedish
(rikssvenska) as used in Sweden. Targets use ordinary Swedish spelling from
the first card including å, ä and ö. InputHint is normally blank.

Finland Swedish is a legitimate standard variety and receives an explicit
later strand. Regional Swedish, colloquial variation and dialects are
introduced later where useful rather than mixed silently into early exact-
match Targets.

Swedish pronunciation cannot be recovered completely from spelling. Vowel
quantity, consonant length, the sj and tj sounds, retroflexion and word
accent deserve explicit longitudinal attention. Ordinary written Targets
do not receive artificial pronunciation notation.

COURSE-WIDE SWEDISH PRINCIPLES

1. Begin with a small reusable seed and move rapidly into productive
   sentences.
2. Nouns belong primarily to common gender (en) or neuter gender (ett).
   Gender is lexical and should be reinforced whenever nouns recur.
3. The ordinary definite article is normally suffixed to the noun. Teach
   forms such as huset, mannen and kvinnan from the opening.
4. Attributive definite noun phrases generally involve double definiteness:
   a preposed determiner plus definite adjective plus suffixed noun. Develop
   this after basic indefinite and simple definite noun phrases are clear.
5. Plural formation has several productive classes plus irregular forms.
   Teach representative patterns through useful nouns rather than pretending
   that plural is predictable from meaning.
6. Adjectives distinguish common-gender singular, neuter singular and plural/
   definite forms. Predicative agreement provides an early entry point.
7. Some high-frequency adjectives such as bra are invariant. Use them
   naturally without obscuring productive agreement patterns such as
   stor/stort/stora and liten/litet/små.
8. Personal pronouns distinguish subject and object forms. Modern gender-
   neutral hen belongs to contemporary Swedish and should be introduced after
   the core pronoun system rather than treated as exotic.
9. Finite verbs do not agree with person or number. Do not waste cards
   repeating identical verb forms merely to imitate languages with personal
   conjugation; vary subjects when the sentence teaches something else.
10. Swedish main declarative clauses are verb-second. Teach V2 as a clause
    principle rather than an exception to English-like SVO.
11. Yes/no questions normally place the finite verb before the subject.
12. Inte placement depends on clause type. Establish ordinary main-clause
    placement and later contrast subordinate-clause word order.
13. Infinitives commonly use att, while modal verbs and several other
    constructions take a bare infinitive. Teach the contrast through actual
    constructions.
14. Present tense has productive patterns and important irregular forms.
    Principal parts become useful lexical information as past and supine
    morphology develops.
15. Past tense includes weak and strong verb classes. Common irregular verbs
    enter when their frequency warrants them.
16. The perfect and pluperfect use ha plus the supine. Keep the supine
    distinct from participial adjective forms.
17. Swedish has a productive passive in -s as well as periphrastic passive
    constructions. Develop their functions and register separately.
18. Deponent -s verbs are lexical verbs rather than ordinary passives and
    require their own treatment.
19. Possession uses possessive determiners/pronouns with agreement patterns
    such as min/mitt/mina and the reflexive sin/sitt/sina. Reflexive
    possession depends on the subject antecedent and deserves controlled
    contrast.
20. Swedish has no productive noun case system comparable to German or
    Icelandic. The genitive -s is developed as a possessive construction
    without inventing a broader declension paradigm.
21. Prepositions are learned with their idiomatic spatial temporal and
    abstract uses. English prepositions do not map one-to-one.
22. Particle verbs are central to ordinary vocabulary. Treat particles as
    part of lexical constructions and develop stress and word-order behavior
    longitudinally.
23. Separable-looking particle constructions should not be mechanically
    equated with German separable-prefix verbs.
24. Adverb placement and sentence adverbs interact with V2 and subordinate
    clause order. The main/subordinate contrast is a structural spine.
25. Relative clauses use som and related constructions without requiring an
    English-style relative-pronoun case paradigm.
26. Comparison includes productive -are/-ast patterns and common irregulars.
27. Numerals, dates, clock time and measurement receive ordinary usage
    development including conventions that differ from English.
28. Formal/informal address in contemporary Swedish is not modelled on
    German du/Sie. Modern du is broadly ordinary; historical or special
    polite usage is treated in its proper register.
29. Spoken Swedish includes common reductions and forms whose relationship to
    standard spelling should be taught for comprehension without replacing
    standard written Targets.
30. De is the initial standard written target for third-person plural subject
    "they". Spoken dom and evolving written usage receive explicit later
    treatment rather than being silently mixed into exact-match cards.
31. Contemporary neutral Sweden Swedish precedes strongly regional,
    historical or literary material.
32. Finland Swedish is developed later as a standard-variety strand where
    pronunciation vocabulary or usage differs meaningfully.
33. Vocabulary grows just in time. Similarity to German Icelandic Danish or
    Norwegian may assist explanation and lexical selection but never
    overrides Swedish usage.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1-100

The seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- authentic Swedish orthography;
- suffixed definite nouns;
- common versus neuter indefinite articles;
- predicative adjective agreement;
- first attributive adjective agreement;
- personal subject pronouns;
- present vara in ordinary clauses;
- ha possession;
- present verbs without person/number agreement;
- svenska as a language name;
- inte negation;
- verb-first polar questions;
- vad, vem and var;
- direct objects without case inflection;
- first common irregular presents through se, äta, dricka and vilja;
- vilja + bare infinitive;
- basic place adverbs.

The opening deliberately gives several subjects to invariant present verb
forms so the absence of person agreement becomes visible, but future
generation should not fill redundant paradigms once that fact is
established.

CARD-SPECIFIC RULES

1. EnglishPrompt must be unique and select one intended Swedish Target.
2. Use ordinary Swedish orthography including å, ä and ö.
3. InputHint is normally blank.
4. Avoid commas in card fields where a natural alternative exists.
5. Treat en/ett gender as lexical information and reinforce it through noun
   phrases rather than adding English grammatical labels to every noun card.
6. Once lack of person/number verb agreement is established, do not create
   complete subject paradigms whose Swedish Targets differ only in pronoun.
7. Distinguish common and neuter adjective forms where the morphology changes.
8. Do not force inflected alternatives for invariant adjectives such as bra.
9. Use de as the initial standard written Target for "they". Label spoken dom
   or other usage explicitly when it is being tested.
10. Distinguish infinitive with att from bare infinitive by construction.
11. Particle-verb prompts must provide enough context to select the intended
    lexical meaning.
12. Word-order cards must stand independently and contain enough context to
    select natural V2 or subordinate-clause order.
13. Do not treat Swedish genitive -s as evidence for a German-style noun case
    paradigm.
14. Pronunciation cards may use an appropriate explanatory prompt or
    InputHint where useful, but ordinary lexical Targets retain standard
    spelling.
15. Finland Swedish, regional, historical or strongly colloquial forms must
    be labelled when exact matching would otherwise imply neutral Sweden
    Swedish.

DEVELOPMENT POLICY

The first 100 cards are the empirical opening. Because the deck is now being
used in the Polyglot app, their IDs are live and must remain stable. Correct
genuine errors in place rather than renumbering existing cards.

The next expansion should deepen en/ett and plural noun morphology, adjective
agreement, V2, negation and questions while widening present verbs and opening
object pronouns, possessives, infinitive constructions and first past forms.
Do not postpone useful past-time language until every noun plural class is
exhausted.

German and Icelandic remain useful structural comparisons. Swedish's reduced
nominal morphology, suffixed definiteness, double definiteness, invariant
finite verb agreement, V2, particle verbs and main/subordinate word-order
contrast remain authoritative.

AMENDMENT NOTE

Version 0.1 is the first Swedish syllabus. It was written after a 100-card
seed and adopts contemporary neutral Standard Swedish in Sweden as the
initial production baseline.
