LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC AUTHORING GUIDANCE
Use standard conjunctive isiZulu spelling: grammatical prefixes and verbal morphology are generally written together with their hosts. Standard spelling does not mark lexical tone. Keep c, q, x clicks distinct, alongside aspirated and voiced combinations, and respect ordinary Unicode punctuation. InputHint is normally blank.

Zulu belongs to the Nguni branch of Bantu languages. Nouns carry class prefixes, and agreement with subject markers, adjectives, possessives, relatives and object markers is pervasive. Do not assume a single noun-class prefix determines every concord, nor treat noun classes as grammatical gender in the European sense. Introduce high-frequency class 1/2 (um-/aba-), class 9/10 (in-/izin-), class 7/8 (isi-/izi-) and additional classes through useful noun pairs. Class 1a/2a human names, kinship and loanwords deserve special attention.

Noun description uses class-dependent forms; common adjective roots such as -khulu, -ncane, -hle and -sha are not free-standing interchangeable adjectives. Distinguish predicative from attributive construction and use the appropriate concord. Preserve the original exploratory micro forms even if more idiomatic contextual alternatives exist.

The verbal complex contains subject concord, tense/aspect/mood, optional object concord, stem, extensions and final vowel. The present long/conjoint distinction (including -ya-) is structurally important; do not teach -ya- as a universal present-tense marker. First teach independent complete-clause forms, then introduce the short/conjoint form in object and complement contexts. Third-person singular subject markers can coincide for he and she; the English source prompt must not presume a grammatical sex contrast. Negative verb morphology changes prefixes and often final vowels.

Dictionary infinitives usually begin uku-; a lexical item such as ukudla is both the verbal infinitive 'to eat' and a noun meaning 'food'. Unique EnglishPrompt values remain mandatory; allowing distinct prompts to share a target is fine. No English articles map directly to Zulu. Avoid unnatural artificial distinctions merely to force one-to-one English translation.

Where common natural variants exist, choose one defensible canonical answer and make the English prompt self-contained, adding sparse qualifiers only when necessary. Respect standard regional variety and contemporary usage. Do not pack several new agreement or verbal mechanisms into one otherwise opaque sentence. Numerals, demonstratives, possessives, negation and relatives must be introduced by class-sensitive examples rather than English templates.

MICRO TO SEED
6000000–6000014: immutable original exploratory opening. Contains noun-class and adjective agreement previews, possession, a verb with an object, and a numeral phrase. These previews do not imply mastery of the corresponding branches.
6000015–6000106: systematic expansion with people, objects, classes, identity predicates, adjective predicates and attributes, plurals, negative identity/description, questions, uku- verbs, independent pronouns, present subject markers, simple present clauses, negative verbs and a few direct objects. Later constructions such as locatives and object concords remain to be systematically taught.

Earlier noun-class agreement and present negation must remain in active reinforcement.
