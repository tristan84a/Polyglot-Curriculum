POLYGLOT RUSSIAN SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1  Status: Draft for curriculum review
Charter version: 0.3  Encoding: UTF-8

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern Russian in
Polyglot. It specifies content, prerequisite order, and card strategy, not
learner progress. Routine card generation changes the manifest and CSV, not
this syllabus. Vocabulary and lexical usage grow continuously. Unit sizes
follow completion standards rather than fixed card quotas.

TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED means first demonstrated; ESTABLISHED means sufficiently
represented in the canonical sequence for basic later reuse; REUSABLE
means available naturally in subsequent cards. These are curriculum
states, not measures of learner mastery. A LEXICAL ANCHOR makes a pattern
visible without limiting vocabulary. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED identifies
material reserved for later units. A COMPLETION STANDARD states what
must be demonstrated before a unit is established.

COURSE-WIDE RUSSIAN PRINCIPLES

1. Use ordinary contemporary Russian Cyrillic in every Target from the
   first card. Romanization may be optional InputHint, never a substitute
   Target. Preserve ё where it is part of the chosen spelling; do not
   silently accept its replacement by е under exact matching.
2. Begin with useful words and naturally complete utterances. Use
   conventional noun citation forms and natural phrase prompts; do not
   insert needless subject pronouns or copulas to mirror English.
3. Gender, animacy, stress-sensitive pronunciation, case and aspect are
   longitudinal systems, not single table-memorization events.
4. Present tense of быть has ordinarily no overt copula in simple
   identification. Its past and future forms receive explicit treatment.
5. Establish a function of each case in controlled contrast before
   widening its endings and governance; singular forms precede most
   plural paradigms. Exceptions arrive when their use warrants them.
6. Introduce imperfective verbs and the present early. Introduce
   perfective partners with concrete completed events, then revisit
   aspect throughout tense, negation, imperative and discourse.
7. Teach motion verbs as paired systems of directionality, means and
   aspect in stages; do not reduce идти/ходить to arbitrary synonyms.
8. Use a broadly neutral contemporary standard as the initial reference.
   Spoken, formal, regional and literary differences follow when useful.
9. A card's single Target must be natural for its specific meaning and
   register. Disambiguate English prompts where aspect, case, word order,
   politeness or a lexical choice permits several valid answers.
10. Grammar is taught chiefly through controlled examples. Increase
    lexical variety after the new pattern is visible; established words
    and structures remain available across later units.

STAGE 1 — CYRILLIC THROUGH USEFUL VOCABULARY

PREREQUISITES: None. OBJECTIVE: Begin ordinary Russian spelling and
high-value concrete nouns together. LEXICAL ANCHORS: дом, книга, стол,
вода, город, человек. CARD STRATEGY: Use natural citation forms with
specific English prompts. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: People, objects, food
and places. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Alphabet-name quizzes, romanized
Targets, full declension and rare orthographic patterns. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Enough varied Cyrillic vocabulary is established to support
short sentences. LEADS TO: RU-03–RU-10.

RU-02 — Core qualities and actions
PREREQUISITES: RU-01 begun. OBJECTIVE: Build useful semantic anchors
without forcing premature agreement or conjugation. LEXICAL ANCHORS:
хорошо, плохо, много, читать, знать, работать. CARD STRATEGY: Initially
use natural standalone adverbs and verb citation forms; revisit actions
in finite sentences after RU-08. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Concrete daily
activities and qualities. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full adjective
paradigm and aspect-pair assumption for every verb. COMPLETION STANDARD:
A varied base supports the first predicates without long runs of
abstract verbs. LEADS TO: RU-06, RU-08, RU-11.

RU-03 — Personal and demonstrative reference
PREREQUISITES: RU-01. OBJECTIVE: Introduce я, ты, он, она, оно, мы, вы,
они and это where naturally used. CARD STRATEGY: Distinguish polite or
plural вы with prompt context; avoid mechanically repeating pronouns in
later sentences. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Oblique forms, full demonstrative
agreement and pragmatic nuance of ты/вы. COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic
person, number, gender and reference can be recognized and produced in
short utterances. LEADS TO: RU-04, RU-08.

RU-04 — Present identification and zero copula
PREREQUISITES: RU-01, RU-03. OBJECTIVE: Establish natural noun and
adjective predicates without an overt present-tense “is.” CORE PATTERN:
Это дом. Она врач. Дом большой. CARD STRATEGY: Contrast identification,
profession and description; supply context where English “it” is vague.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Occupations and common descriptions.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Past/future был/будет, instrumental predicate
alternatives and exhaustive short adjectives. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Several persons and both noun/adjective predicates establish the absent
present copula without unnatural English calques. LEADS TO: RU-05–RU-07.

RU-05 — Noun gender and basic adjective agreement
PREREQUISITES: RU-01, RU-04. OBJECTIVE: Make masculine, feminine and
neuter visible through useful nouns and attributive adjectives. LEXICAL
ANCHORS: новый дом, новая книга, новое окно. CARD STRATEGY: Contrast
one adjective across several nouns, then vary adjectives and nouns;
keep predicative forms distinct from attributive forms. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Oblique cases, plural agreement, exceptional gender endings
and short-form adjective distribution. COMPLETION STANDARD: Productive
basic singular agreement is demonstrated across all three genders.
LEADS TO: RU-14, RU-30.

RU-06 — Basic negation and yes/no questions
PREREQUISITES: RU-04. OBJECTIVE: Establish не and natural question
intonation/punctuation in simple predicates. CARD STRATEGY: Contrast
positive/negative meanings, then declarative/question contexts with
controlled English prompts. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: нет + genitive,
negative pronouns, scope contrasts and double negation. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Negation and basic questions work across several natural
sentences. LEADS TO: RU-07, RU-12.

RU-07 — Existence and possession with есть / нет
PREREQUISITES: RU-03, RU-04, RU-06. OBJECTIVE: Introduce У меня есть
книга and У меня нет книги as constructions, with the required genitive
form treated initially in a small controlled set. CARD STRATEGY: Do not
replace natural Russian possession with a literal “I have” calque;
contrast having an item with description where есть may be absent.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full genitive paradigm and all possessive
alternations. COMPLETION STANDARD: Several personal possessors and
concrete objects establish affirmative and negative constructions as
whole patterns. LEADS TO: RU-20, RU-25.

RU-08 — Imperfective present: first conjugation
PREREQUISITES: RU-02, RU-03. OBJECTIVE: Establish finite present forms
through a small coherent class, e.g. читать, работать. CARD STRATEGY:
Contrast persons and numbers before adding other classes; use natural
subject omission when recoverable. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Perfective
“present,” irregular stems, past tense and imperative. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Person/number endings are productive across multiple verbs
and natural short sentences. LEADS TO: RU-09–RU-11.

RU-09 — Imperfective present: second conjugation and common irregulars
PREREQUISITES: RU-08. OBJECTIVE: Add a coherent second conjugation, then
high-frequency stem changes such as говорить, видеть, хотеть, мочь as
separate patterns. CARD STRATEGY: Complete a regular contrast first;
introduce irregular verbs for their real uses, not as one dense list.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Exhaustive exception catalog. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Major endings and selected indispensable irregular forms can
be used in varied contexts. LEADS TO: RU-10, RU-13.

RU-10 — Early sentence synthesis
PREREQUISITES: RU-01–RU-09. OBJECTIVE: Combine identity, description,
negation, possession and present actions with fresh useful vocabulary.
CARD STRATEGY: Vary person and information focus only where the English
prompt remains unambiguous; keep Russian word order natural. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Short sentences no longer require a frozen anchor vocabulary
and all established patterns remain available. LEADS TO: RU-11 onward.

STAGE 2 — SENTENCE TOOLS AND FIRST CASE FUNCTIONS

RU-11 — Question words: кто, что, где, когда, как, почему.
RU-12 — Negation with verbs; contrast не with нет in their basic uses.
RU-13 — Infinitive complements and early modal constructions: хотеть,
мочь, надо/нужно, with distinctions introduced separately.
RU-14 — Nominative plural and adjective agreement; productive endings
before stem and stress exceptions.
RU-15 — Accusative singular of inanimate direct objects; contrast the
unchanged masculine/neuter forms with regular feminine -у/-ю.
RU-16 — Accusative animate masculine singular; use visible nominative /
genitive contrast rather than an abstract animacy table.
RU-17 — Location with в/на + prepositional singular; distinguish uses
lexically instead of imposing one English spatial rule.
RU-18 — Direction with в/на + accusative; contrast location versus goal.
RU-19 — Basic place, time and manner adverbs, including здесь/там and
сейчас/потом, in natural sentence positions.
RU-20 — Genitive singular for absence, possession and simple quantities;
revisit у меня нет with a widening noun inventory.
RU-21 — Simple sentence and case synthesis.

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Introduce each new case function with a limited
set of nouns whose endings expose the pattern. Vary gender and stem
classes after the function is clear; separate lexical choice of в/на
from the case contrast. Do not require a case-name answer.
COMPLETION STANDARD: The indicated function is established across
multiple lexical representatives, genders and sentence contexts as
appropriate; RU-21 mixes them without relying on isolated forms.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full plural oblique paradigms, all preposition
governance and unrestricted free word order.

STAGE 3 — PAST, FUTURE AND FIRST ASPECT CONTRASTS

RU-22 — Imperfective past: gender/number agreement and past time.
RU-23 — Past of быть and natural past identification/location.
RU-24 — Imperfective compound future: буду + infinitive.
RU-25 — First transparent imperfective/perfective pairs for process
versus completed bounded event; distinguish past contexts before
expanding forms.
RU-26 — Perfective simple future; contrast its form and meaning with
imperfective present and compound future.
RU-27 — Aspect in questions, negation and expected result: first
controlled contrasts, avoiding one-to-one English tense glosses.
RU-28 — Common nontransparent aspect partners as lexical families.
RU-29 — Past/future/aspect synthesis with context-rich prompts.

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Use time and outcome context to elicit one natural
aspect. Never present a perfective form as ordinary present tense.
Reintroduce aspect later with new constructions and discourse.
COMPLETION STANDARD: The learner can see the form/meaning distinction
across several verb families and future and past contexts.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Exhaustive aspect rules and all prefix semantics.

STAGE 4 — CASE SYSTEM IN USE

RU-30 — Accusative plural and animacy across nouns and adjectives.
RU-31 — Genitive plural: productive patterns, then common irregular and
zero endings, anchored to quantity and absence.
RU-32 — Dative singular: recipients, age and common experiencer patterns.
RU-33 — Dative plural and adjective/pronoun agreement.
RU-34 — Instrumental singular: means, accompaniment with с, and selected
predicate uses as separately contrasted constructions.
RU-35 — Instrumental plural and agreement.
RU-36 — Prepositional plural; location and aboutness with о/об.
RU-37 — Genitive after numbers and quantity: 1, 2–4, 5+, with agreement
and exceptional numeral forms developed incrementally.
RU-38 — Core preposition governance: к, от, из, с, без, для, после.
RU-39 — Personal pronouns in oblique cases, including н- after relevant
prepositions and stress-bearing variants where significant.
RU-40 — Possessive and demonstrative pronouns through core cases;
мой/твой/наш/ваш, свой and этот/тот in separate contrasts.
RU-41 — Case synthesis in connected everyday sentences.

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Contrast semantic roles, prepositions and endings
in small families; mix cases only after each principal function is
established. Keep animacy visible in both singular and plural.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Productive forms and indispensable irregulars
support natural use of each case and agreement in varied contexts.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Rare inflection classes, historical variants and
idiomatic governance best introduced with later lexical items.

STAGE 5 — VERBAL STRUCTURE AND MOTION

RU-42 — Imperative: productive singular and plural/polite forms.
RU-43 — Aspect choice in requests, instructions, warnings and negative
imperatives; context must distinguish process from expected result.
RU-44 — Reflexive -ся/-сь: common lexical and productive meanings.
RU-45 — Indefinite-personal and impersonal constructions with common
verbs; avoid artificial subject insertion.
RU-46 — идти/ходить: one-way underway versus habitual or multidirectional
motion on foot, with actual context rather than “go” alone.
RU-47 — ехать/ездить: corresponding motion by transport.
RU-48 — Destination, source and route with motion and their case frames.
RU-49 — Arriving, leaving and going in/out: motion prefixes attached to
appropriate base verbs, with aspect partners developed explicitly.
RU-50 — Additional unprefixed motion pairs: бегать/бежать,
летать/лететь, плавать/плыть and others by frequency.
RU-51 — Carrying/leading motion: нести/носить, вести/водить,
везти/возить; distinguish what or whom moves.
RU-52 — Motion and aspect synthesis: habitual, underway, round trip,
completed departure and arrival.

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Introduce one contrast at a time in grounded
scenes. A perfective prefixed motion verb and its imperfective partner
must not be equated mechanically with directional/indeterminate pairs.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Major combinations of means, direction, frequency,
prefix and aspect are recoverable from context across varied destinations.

STAGE 6 — NOUN PHRASES, COMPARISON AND NUMBER

RU-53 — Full adjective declension across established cases.
RU-54 — Hard/soft adjective stems, spelling constraints and stress
patterns where relevant to written forms.
RU-55 — Short adjectives: common predicative uses and meaning contrasts.
RU-56 — Comparative forms and чем; synthetic and analytic strategies.
RU-57 — Superlatives and degree expressions.
RU-58 — Numerals in dates, time, prices, ages and measurements; numeral
case forms expanded in useful contexts.
RU-59 — Ordinals and agreement.
RU-60 — Indefinite, negative and universal pronouns; никого/ничего and
obligatory negative concord in relevant constructions.
RU-61 — Noun-phrase and quantification synthesis.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Agreement, comparison and quantities operate in
natural phrases across relevant genders, numbers and cases. Rare numeral
variants can remain for advanced lexical expansion.

STAGE 7 — COMPLEX SENTENCES AND INFORMATION STRUCTURE

RU-62 — Coordination: и, а, но, или and semantic contrasts.
RU-63 — Subordinate clauses with что, потому что, если and когда.
RU-64 — Relative который: antecedent agreement and clause-internal
case, developed one role at a time.
RU-65 — Other relatives and correlatives: кто, что, где, куда, откуда.
RU-66 — Complement clauses, reported statements and questions with ли.
RU-67 — Conditions, temporal relations and counterfactuals with бы.
RU-68 — Flexible word order: topic, focus, given/new information and
intonation; distinguish neutral ordering from marked alternatives.
RU-69 — Negation scope and contrastive focus.
RU-70 — Clause and information-structure synthesis.

CARD STRATEGY: English prompts must supply discourse context when word
order or focus matters; avoid claiming every permutation is equivalent.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Each clause type is demonstrated with varied
pronoun and case roles, and natural information packaging is visible.

STAGE 8 — EXPANDED ASPECT, VOICE AND VERBAL DERIVATION

RU-71 — Aspect in repeated events, duration and completed sequences.
RU-72 — Aspect under negation, modal verbs and infinitive complements.
RU-73 — Prefixes and shifts in lexical meaning; distinguish new lexical
verbs from straightforward aspect partners.
RU-74 — Secondary imperfectives and iterative/habitual derivatives.
RU-75 — Passive with -ся and passive participles in appropriate registers.
RU-76 — Active and passive participles: present/past and aspect
constraints, beginning with forms encountered in ordinary prose.
RU-77 — Short passive participles and result/state contrasts.
RU-78 — Verbal adverbs (деепричастия), subject control and register.
RU-79 — Verbal morphology synthesis in connected prose.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Productive patterns, common irregulars and their
syntactic restrictions are shown across different verb families; rarer
forms may be primarily recognition oriented.

STAGE 9 — WORD FORMATION, ORTHOGRAPHY AND PRONUNCIATION

RU-80 — Productive noun, adjective and verb derivation and families.
RU-81 — Diminutives, augmentatives and their social/pragmatic force.
RU-82 — Prefix meaning and idiomatic lexicalization across families.
RU-83 — Spelling rules after velars, hushers and ц; endings in context.
RU-84 — Ь/ъ, palatalization and consonant alternations in real words.
RU-85 — Stress movement, vowel reduction and pronunciation differences
that affect comprehension; use audio/reference support where needed.
RU-86 — Orthographic conventions: ё/е in authentic sources, capitals,
punctuation, direct speech and abbreviations.
RU-87 — Derivational and literacy synthesis.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Common productive families and orthographic
alternations are usable in natural words and sentences. Never replace a
conventional spelling with a transcription to simplify production.

STAGE 10 — PHRASEOLOGY, REGISTER AND NATURAL USAGE

RU-88 — High-frequency verbs across senses and case frames: быть,
делать, идти, брать, дать, стоять and others longitudinally.
RU-89 — Conventional verb-noun and adjective-noun collocations.
RU-90 — Spatial, temporal and abstract preposition choices.
RU-91 — Particles and stance: же, ведь, ли, бы, вот, ну and others in
context, with pragmatic constraints.
RU-92 — Politeness, address, ты/вы, patronymics and social distance.
RU-93 — Conversational ellipsis, responses and spoken discourse.
RU-94 — Neutral, informal and formal written choices; bureaucratic
style where common, without making it the default.
RU-95 — Idioms, proverbs and figurative constructions of ordinary reach.
RU-96 — Phraseology and register synthesis.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Systematic coverage of high-frequency constructions,
collocations, pragmatic patterns and common polysemy, beyond a few
illustrative idioms. These domains continue expanding in later stages.
CARD STRATEGY: Give a concrete context and register in prompts; separate
valid alternative constructions into distinct objectives.

RU-101 — Body and health. RU-102 — Work and occupations.
RU-103 — Education. RU-104 — Travel and transportation.
RU-105 — Buildings, towns and public places.
RU-106 — Nature, plants and animals. RU-107 — Weather and environment.
RU-108 — Tools, materials and physical objects.
RU-109 — Technology and communications.
RU-110 — Commerce, money and finance.
RU-111 — Government, law and public institutions.
RU-112 — Science and mathematics. RU-113 — Arts, literature and culture.
RU-114 — Religion and philosophy.
RU-115 — Emotion, personality and human behaviour.
RU-116 — Abstract concepts and relationships.

COMMON OBJECTIVE: Broaden educated-adult vocabulary while recycling

contrastive examples; space confusable items. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Useful breadth in each domain, with no fixed word count; all domains
remain expandable.

STAGE 12 — EDUCATED-ADULT LEXICON

RU-117 — Lower-frequency verbs and aspect families.
RU-118 — Lower-frequency adjectives and concrete nouns.
RU-119 — Abstract and academic vocabulary.
RU-120 — Professional and institutional language.
RU-121 — General scientific and technical vocabulary.
RU-122 — Political, legal and economic vocabulary.
RU-123 — Cultural and literary vocabulary.
RU-124 — Fine semantic distinctions, near-synonyms and argument frames.
RU-125 — Advanced derivation, abbreviations and word families.
RU-126 — Less-common idioms, collocations and figurative senses.

COMMON OBJECTIVE: Approach the breadth and precision of an educated
adult. CARD STRATEGY: Use mature grammar freely, revealing register,

EXCLUDED: Narrow specialist terminology without broad relevance.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Coverage remains extensible, with substantial
rather than merely representative phraseology.

STAGE 13 — MATURE LANGUAGE COMPETENCE / LONG TAIL

RU-127 — Common regional and national-standard variation.
RU-128 — Generational and contemporary spoken usage.
RU-129 — Formal and literary constructions still encountered today.
RU-130 — Historical or Soviet-era vocabulary encountered in modern texts.
RU-131 — Specialized vocabulary of broad cultural relevance.
RU-132 — Less frequent but reasonable educated-adult words.
RU-133 — Idiomatic and figurative long tail.
RU-134 — Continuing lexical and constructional expansion.

COMMON OBJECTIVE: Move toward the practical asymptote of modern Russian
competence, not exhaustion of all attested vocabulary. CARD STRATEGY:
Select by exposure, usefulness and register; continue developing senses,
constructions, collocations and idioms. COMPLETION STANDARD: No absolute
terminal word count; RU-134 remains expandable.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Completion establishes canonical coverage, not learner mastery.
2. Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards
   increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3. New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled lexical
   load. Vocabulary keeps growing through every stage.
4. Cyrillic and conventional orthography apply from the first Target;
   InputHint may provide practical reading/input guidance after answering.
5. The scheduler supplies extra repetition without changing canonical
   introduction order.
6. Existing valid Russian cards remain valid. Fill prerequisite gaps
   through future generation rather than renumbering existing card IDs.
7. Amendments may split, merge or reorder units without renumbering cards.
8. Parallel units in a stage may interleave once their prerequisites are
   met; unit numbers state the default canonical path.

AMENDMENT POLICY

Change this syllabus only when the canonical Russian curriculum changes.
Record affected units, pedagogical rationale, impact on future or existing
cards, new version and concise history. Routine card generation does not
amend it.
