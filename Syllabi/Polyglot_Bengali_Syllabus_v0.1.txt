PURPOSE
This is the canonical introduction map for Bengali. Preserve the original 15 exploratory cards (4000000–4000014) unchanged. The 85 additions (4000015–4000099) form a systematic 100-card seed. REFERENCE VARIETY, REGISTER AND EXACT MATCH
Use modern standard Bengali orthography in Bengali script with appropriate vowel signs, consonant conjuncts and punctuation. The original micro deck uses পানি and গাড়ি; these and the new seed's পানির/পানি-related vocabulary are compatible with everyday Bangladeshi usage. Bangladesh and West Bengal differ in lexical preferences, pronunciation and some register choices (for example পানি versus জল); do not silently replace existing answers or imply one regional choice is universal. Later introduce regional alternatives with explicit labels.

The seed uses broadly standard colloquial Bengali sentence patterns, not highly literary সাধু ভাষা. Bengali has socially meaningful pronoun and verb agreement: intimate তুই, familiar তুমি and respectful আপনি, plus ordinary সে and respectful তিনি. English 'you' and 'he/she' are often insufficiently specific for exact-match testing; label intended social register when it changes the Target. Bengali third-person pronouns do not grammatically encode English he/she gender. When a human predicate noun has a gendered lexical form such as ছাত্র versus ছাত্রী, make the English prompt's intended gender explicit.

SCRIPT AND INPUT
Canonical Target uses Bengali script with normal diacritics and conjuncts; InputHint is normally blank. Preserve exact Unicode spelling and conventional punctuation. Teach inherent vowels, dependent vowel signs, hasanta, conjuncts, aspiration, vowel quality and keyboard entry longitudinally using familiar words. Do not romanize required answers.

OPENING GRAMMAR PRINCIPLES
1. Nominal and adjectival predicates normally lack an overt present-tense 'be': সে একজন ডাক্তার; বাড়িটা বড়. Do not introduce আছে as a universal copula.
2. Distinguish nominal identity negation নই/নয় from ordinary verbal negation with না and absence with নেই.
3. Teach the pronoun/address hierarchy and verb endings together; respectful তিনি requires corresponding respectful verb agreement.
4. Bengali word order is commonly SOV, with postpositions and case suffixes rather than English-style prepositions.
5. Possession often uses genitive possessor plus আছে: আমার একটা বই আছে. Absence uses নেই.
6. Introduce counting expressions with -টা/-টি for objects and -জন for people, without treating them as identical to obligatory articles.
7. Bengali present habitual verb forms encode person and address level: দেখি, দেখো, দেখে; খাই, খাও, খায়. Introduce a controlled representative pattern, then reinforce through new verbs rather than filling every paradigm.
8. The seed uses খাই for both eating and drinking certain liquids, a normal Bengali pattern. More specific পান করা and other expressions are introduced when context requires.
9. Introduce locatives with -এ/-তে and demonstrative adverbs এখানে/ওখানে; broaden to motion and postpositions later.
10. Questions distinguish কি (polar) from কী (what), with normal Bengali question syntax. Teach the diacritic contrast as meaningful in exact-match input.
11. Bengali number and noun phrase systems involve definiteness, classifiers, plurality and animacy/humanity; avoid assuming English articles or plural endings map one-to-one.
12. Build past, future, progressive and perfect forms only after an intelligible present-tense and agreement foundation.
13. Standard colloquial Bengali differs from formal/literary registers; explicitly label marked variants and regional preferences.
14. Maintain lexical expansion alongside grammatical development through everyday, professional, cultural and abstract domains.

- Preserve original 15 cards and append subsequent IDs in order.
- EnglishPrompt must be unique within Bengali and specify a single intended canonical Target.
- Use standard Bengali script and Unicode spelling; InputHint normally blank.
- Distinguish intimate, familiar and respectful pronouns and verb forms when relevant.
- Distinguish কি from কী, and নেই from নই/নয় and verbal না.
- Do not insert a present-tense copula into ordinary nominal/adjective predicates.
- Use counting expressions and case suffixes naturally, not by mechanically translating English articles.
- Prefer short independent cards and a widening spiral of new vocabulary with reused grammar.
- Keep regional variants labelled and avoid silently replacing existing micro-deck choices.
