PURPOSE
This is the canonical introduction map for Cantonese. The original 15-card exploratory deck is preserved verbatim at IDs 3100000–3100014; the 85 additional cards at 3100015–3100099 form a 100-card systematic seed. Develop a widening, prerequisite-aware curriculum toward educated-adult breadth, not a closed beginner phrasebook. A unit marks the first controlled introduction of a branch; older branches continue to receive reinforcement.

WRITING SYSTEM, INPUT AND EXACT MATCH
Canonical Target uses Traditional Chinese characters in natural written colloquial Hong Kong Cantonese, including 佢, 唔, 冇, 喺, 嘅, 啲 and other Cantonese-specific characters. Do not silently replace these with Standard Written Chinese or Mandarin constructions. Jyutping with tone numbers (1–6, with conventional treatment of checked syllables) belongs in InputHint as a pronunciation/typing aid, not in Target. Retain exact Unicode characters, punctuation, and orthographic distinctions. The app expects one canonical answer; when English underspecifies gender, number, deixis, aspect, classifier, register or possession, phrase the prompt to indicate the intended distinction or choose an explicitly stated teaching default.

Hong Kong Cantonese is the unqualified reference. Later introduce alternative Hong Kong spellings, Macau/Guangzhou usage, colloquial variants, formal written Chinese and code-switching with explicit labels. Written Cantonese does not map one-to-one to Standard Chinese; teach spoken grammar and the written colloquial register together. A Jyutping hint is not a substitute for teaching characters, tones or input methods.

OPENING PRINCIPLES
1. Preserve all 15 original cards, including their previews of classifiers 間/架, numeral 四, adjective predication, 係, 有, 見到 and 嘅. Do not infer that an early preview completes the corresponding grammar unit.
2. Teach nouns and high-frequency adjectives, then personal pronouns 我/你/佢/我哋/佢哋. 佢 does not encode English he/she gender.
3. Use 係 for identity and classification, but not as a universal copula before adjective predicates. Teach 間屋好大 and related natural patterns; 好 may be a neutral degree element, not always emphatic 'very'.
4. Use 唔 for ordinary negation, 冇 for lack/nonexistence and past-event negation in appropriate constructions. Do not conflate them.
5. Introduce A-not-A questions such as 係唔係, 有冇 and 飲唔飲. Later contrast final-particle questions (嗎, 呀, 呢, 咩, 㗎) and their pragmatics; avoid presenting them as interchangeable.
6. Distinguish 係 identity, 喺 location, 有 possession/existence and 冇 absence.
7. Introduce measure words as a core requirement for numerals/demonstratives: 個 for general items/people, 本 for books, 隻 for animals, 架 for vehicles, 間 for houses/rooms, and later 杯/張/條/件/把. For two counted objects use 兩 rather than 二 in ordinary numeral-classifier phrases.
8. Cantonese verb forms do not conjugate for person or tense. Build subject–verb–object clauses with familiar nouns and verbs before introducing aspect particles.
9. Introduce 咩, 邊個, 邊度 and later 點解, 幾時, 點樣 and 幾多 in natural question positions; do not impose English wh-fronting.
10. Introduce common verbs 食, 飲, 見到, 鍾意, 想, 識 and 講, then broaden to 去, 嚟, 做, 睇, 聽, 買 and 畀.
11. Use natural Cantonese possession with 有/冇 and possessive pronouns; distinguish 嘅 modifiers and classifier-based possessives when context requires.
12. Introduce 嘅 for attributive modification and other functions without claiming it has a single English equivalent.
13. Progress into aspect through 咗 (perfective), 緊 (progressive), 過 (experiential) and sentence-final/other uses of 啦 and 喇. Aspect is not an English tense paradigm; distinguish completion, current activity, experience and state change.
14. Teach sentence-final particles as grammatical and pragmatic resources, not optional decoration. Contrast 呀, 啊, 呢, 啫, 喎, 㗎, 啦 and combinations in controlled contexts.
15. Expand register and everyday Hong Kong code-switching only with explicit context. Do not let Mandarin or formal written norms displace the spoken Cantonese reference.
16. Vocabulary should grow alongside grammar through home, family, education, food, travel, health, work, media, public institutions, technology and abstract concepts.

CARD GENERATION RULES
- Six columns: ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech.
- Cantonese ID allocation: 3100000–3199999.
- Preserve original 3100000–3100014 exactly; append new cards from 3100015.
- EnglishPrompt unique within Cantonese; keep IDs stable and CSV row order pedagogically coherent.
- Use Traditional characters for Target and Jyutping tone-number romanization in InputHint.
- Use colloquial Hong Kong Cantonese, not Mandarin-based Standard Written Chinese.
- Keep prompts standalone, with one intended canonical target; disambiguate gender, deixis, classifier, aspect and register when necessary.
- Do not mechanically introduce 係 before adjective predicates.
- Use classifiers and 兩 naturally; avoid English-like bare numeral-noun combinations.
- Do not create duplicate targets merely to fill paradigms.
- Introduce productive patterns with a few controlled contrasts, then reinforce in a widening spiral.
- Use ordinary Chinese punctuation consistently and avoid artificial word spaces.
- Treat Jyutping as a helpful input/pronunciation hint, not the answer.

CURRENT FRONTIER
