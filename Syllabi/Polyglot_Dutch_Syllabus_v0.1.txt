PURPOSE AND REFERENCE VARIETY

Canonical syllabus for contemporary Standard Dutch (Nederlands). Use broadly neutral Netherlands Standard Dutch for initial productive Targets, with Belgian Standard Dutch (including Flemish usage) and regional varieties introduced as legitimate later comparisons. Use ordinary modern Dutch spelling and punctuation. InputHint is normally blank.

COURSE-WIDE RULES

1. Follow the Card Generation Protocol v1.0 and Charter v0.8. Syllabus units are introduction points, not sealed chapters; reinforce earlier grammar with new vocabulary in a widening spiral.
2. Preserve one natural exact-match Target per unique EnglishPrompt. Add qualifiers only where needed to distinguish register, meaning or variant.
3. Establish de versus het as lexical noun gender, and the attributive adjective -e rule with the neuter indefinite singular exception. Predicative adjectives do not take -e.
4. Distinguish niet (clausal or constituent negation) from geen (negating indefinite nouns). Do not present either as a universal English 'not'.
5. Finite Dutch verbs agree with person and number, unlike Norwegian and Danish. Present tense also has stem spelling adjustments; establish meaningful contrasts before reducing paradigm density.
6. Main-clause V2, yes/no inversion, subordinate verb-final clauses and separable verb placement are separate core systems and must receive controlled examples.
7. Introduce strong and weak past forms, hebben/zijn perfect selection, and the placement of past participles and infinitives cumulatively.
8. Standard Netherlands Dutch is the production baseline, not a claim that Belgian Dutch or other accents are incorrect.
9. Broaden vocabulary aggressively in example phrases and sentences while maintaining recognizable grammatical patterns.
10. Audit all IDs, EnglishPrompt uniqueness, spelling, Target naturalness and UTF-8 CSV formatting before committing.

MICRO DECK AND SEED

The original 15 micro cards 3200000–3200014 are preserved. The 94 added cards 3200015–3200108 open pronouns, zijn, de/het, adjective -e, plurals, negation, questions, present verbs and initial V2, with useful new vocabulary. The next active frontier is NL-15 (infinitives and citation forms); micro cards preview later grammar without marking it established.

DUTCH-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

De/het and adjective endings: de groene auto, het groene huis, een groene auto, een groen huis. Predicates: de auto is groen, het huis is groen. Negation: ik ben geen arts versus de trein is niet nieuw. V2: vandaag werk ik thuis. Present verbs: ik werk, jij werkt, hij werkt, wij werken; in yes/no questions with jij, the -t is normally absent (werk jij?). Use examples before abstract rule memorization.

In later stages, contrast finite main-clause placement with the verb-final subordinate clause, e.g. ik weet dat hij morgen komt. Teach separable verbs through meaningful clauses rather than treating a prefix as a freely detachable preposition. Use Dutch orthographic conventions including apostrophe plurals such as auto's and open/closed syllable changes such as stad/steden and huis/huizen. Treat lexical expansion as concurrent with grammar from the outset.
