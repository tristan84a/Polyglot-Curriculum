PURPOSE
This syllabus graduates the original 15-card Malay micro deck to a systematic 100-card seed, then provides the canonical order for opening productive grammatical and lexical branches. Units mark introductions, not closed chapters; re-use earlier patterns throughout a widening spiral. The course is distinct from Indonesian even where grammar and vocabulary overlap.

REFERENCE AND AUTHORING CONVENTIONS
Use mainstream contemporary Malaysian Malay in standard Rumi orthography with natural ordinary Malaysian expressions. InputHint is blank. Prefer stable, reasonably neutral written/spoken Malay in early cards: saya and awak are early pronoun anchors, without implying that awak is a universal respectful form of address. Later broaden anda, kamu, engkau, encik, puan, cik, abang, kakak and name/title-based address with their actual social contexts. Avoid silently replacing Malaysian words with Indonesian standards (e.g., kereta versus Indonesian mobil, bandar versus kota). Malaysian spelling conventions are authoritative for this deck.

Malay is predominantly analytic: ordinary verbs do not conjugate for person or tense, adjectives do not agree in gender or number, and nouns lack English-style articles. Word order, definiteness, voice, affixation, aspect, classifiers, reduplication and pragmatic register remain substantial productive topics. Use idiomatic English prompts with one canonical natural Target; exact matching does not imply other translations are wrong. Avoid artificial English prompting. Card IDs remain in the reserved 13400000–13499999 block.

IMPORTANT EARLY DISTINCTIONS
1. Nominal identity and adjective predicates ordinarily have no English 'be': Saya guru; Rumah itu besar. Reserve ialah/adalah and merupakan for appropriate, often formal constructions, and distinguish ada for existence, possession and availability.
2. Attributive adjectives normally follow nouns: rumah besar, kereta merah. Demonstratives follow modified noun phrases (buku ini, rumah itu), but stand independently in Ini buku saya.
3. Bukan is the normal negator of identifying nouns; tidak negates verbs and adjectives. Belum means 'not yet'; jangan is prohibitive.
4. First- and second-person reference is socially sensitive. Dia is gender-neutral; kita includes the addressee whereas kami excludes the addressee.
5. Possession uses noun + possessor (buku saya), -nya, ada, milik and punya in different contexts. Do not equate all with an English possessive form.
6. Di marks location, ke direction and dari source. Di as an independent preposition is distinct in spelling and function from verbal di- passive.
7. Verbs such as makan, minum, pergi, tidur and datang may naturally occur as bare stems. Other common predicates use prefixes (membaca, menulis, bekerja); introduce them through sentences and later develop meN- and ber- morphophonology.
8. Tense usually comes from time expressions or markers such as sudah, sedang, akan, belum, masih and pernah, not person/tense inflection.
9. A counted or contextual noun need not be reduplicated. Later distinguish morphological reduplication from plurality, plus orang, ekor, buah, helai, batang and other numeral classifiers.
10. Establish polite requests, particles and contact-language variation in their actual contexts. Malaysian colloquial forms such as tak, nak, lah and kan are later explicit register branches, not invisible substitutes for standard forms.
11. Contrast Malaysian Malay with Indonesian deliberately at an advanced stage: words, spelling, affixes, idioms, honorifics and practical mutual intelligibility, rather than mechanically making paired translation lists.

LONGITUDINAL PRACTICE
Every unit opens material that recurs in later cards. Reuse zero-copula predication, classifier expressions, question word placement and bare/prefixed verbs while adding vocabulary. Over time, broaden the neutral standard with explicitly contextual Malaysian spoken forms and documentary registers. Authentic Malaysian writing and speech eventually become the primary source of missing productive capabilities.

NEXT FRONTIER
MY-19 — Location, direction and source: di, ke and dari. The seed already contains di and ke examples; next explicitly introduce dari ('from') with movement and origin and reinforce di-versus-ke. Then move into classifiers, numeral phrases, reduplication, possession and temporal marking. The first meN-/ber- verbs in the seed are previews, not full treatment of the later morphology branch.
