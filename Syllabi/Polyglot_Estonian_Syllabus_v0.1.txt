AUTHORING CONVENTIONS
Use standard written Estonian and normal diacritics (õ, ä, ö, ü), with InputHint blank. Contemporary everyday written Estonian is the default; colloquial reductions and dialect forms are separate labelled strands. No grammatical gender or articles. Ta/tema may mean he or she. Ordinary present tense does not distinguish an English progressive from a simple present by a mandatory auxiliary. Estonian generally uses nominative, genitive and partitive as its central grammatical cases; partitive is central to quantities, negation and object aspect. Do not equate Estonian object cases with an English definiteness rule. Numerals greater than one typically govern partitive singular nouns in basic number phrases (kaks raamatut). Nominative plural is commonly -d but stems alternate. Personal pronouns have full and short forms (mina/ma, sina/sa, tema/ta, meie/me, teie/te, nemad/nad); use natural short forms in ordinary sentences. Polite singular teie/te is a later strand; the seed's 'you (plural)' uses teie.
The verb dictionary form is the -ma infinitive (lugema, sööma); the -da infinitive is a separate, important construction. Finite present verbs agree by person/number; present negation uses ei plus a non-finite stem form (ei loe), not a conjugating Finnish-style negative verb. Polar questions commonly use kas. Existence/possession commonly uses an adessive possessor (mul on, tal on); negative possession uses the partitive where appropriate. Location and direction require three-way internal (in/into/out of) and external (on/onto/off) case families. Natural case endings may trigger gradation and stem changes; teach actual forms, not mechanically attached endings.
Keep EnglishPrompt unique and independently meaningful after shuffling. A single canonical Target does not deny grammatical alternatives. Preserve live IDs and use the reserved 5100000–5199999 range.
