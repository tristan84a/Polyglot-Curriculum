POLYGLOT KOREAN SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1 Status: Current Charter version: 0.7

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern Korean in
Polyglot. It specifies content, prerequisite order, and card strategy rather
than learner progress. Routine card generation changes the Manifest and CSV,
not this syllabus.

Vocabulary grows continuously. Grammar units identify structural branches
that should be opened in a prerequisite-aware order; they are not sealed
chapters that must be exhausted before later material can begin.

The initial reference variety is contemporary standard South Korean Korean.
Common colloquial, regional, generational, literary, historical, and North
Korean distinctions are introduced later when they materially improve modern
comprehension or educated-adult competence.

TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED — A form, word, construction, orthographic element, or distinction
has begun to be demonstrated.

ESTABLISHED — The canonical curriculum has demonstrated an item sufficiently
for its basic operation to be assumed in later cards. This describes
curriculum state rather than learner mastery.

REUSABLE — Established material may recur naturally when another element is
the principal novelty.

LEXICAL ANCHOR — A word selected to make a grammatical, semantic, or
orthographic pattern visible. Anchors do not limit additional vocabulary.

CANONICAL SEQUENCE — The static authored introduction order. Scheduling may
adapt to performance but does not change curriculum order.

COMPLETION STANDARD — The coverage required before a unit's principal
concept may be treated as established.

COURSE-WIDE KOREAN PRINCIPLES

1. Korean is written in ordinary contemporary Hangul from the first card.
   There is no romanization bootstrap and romanization is not an accepted
   Target.
2. InputHint is normally blank. Korean is entered directly with a Korean
   keyboard. External keyboard practice may support the learner without
   becoming permanent curriculum.
3. Lexical verbs and descriptive verbs normally use the dictionary form in
   -다. Finite grammar is taught through natural sentences.
4. The initial conversational baseline is polite 해요체. Dictionary forms
   remain lexical citation forms rather than conversational sentence endings.
5. Korean does not require overt subjects or objects when discourse makes
   them recoverable. Cards should not mechanically preserve English
   pronouns.
6. Do not teach 당신 as the ordinary neutral equivalent of English "you".
   Second-person reference is often omitted or expressed through names,
   titles, kinship terms, or context. 당신 receives explicit later treatment.
7. Likewise 그 and 그녀 are not treated as compulsory everyday equivalents
   of English "he" and "she". Natural omission and nominal reference take
   precedence.
8. Topic 은/는 and subject 이/가 are distinct systems. Neither is reduced to
   a one-word English translation.
9. Object 을/를 is taught together with ordinary omission and information
   structure rather than as a mechanically obligatory accusative marker.
10. Korean descriptive verbs behave predicatively and inflect. They are not
    forced into an English adjective-plus-copula model.
11. Speech level, honorific marking, address terms, and social relationship
    are central grammar. Politeness is developed longitudinally rather than
    added as decorative vocabulary.
12. Word order is broadly head-final and commonly SOV but information
    structure and omission permit substantial variation. Static Targets
    represent one clearly cued natural realization rather than every
    grammatical ordering.
13. Particles are learned through contrasts and functions in sentences.
14. Korean spacing and standard orthography are part of the Target.
15. Pronunciation rules such as liaison, nasal assimilation, tensification,
    palatalization, and 받침 neutralization are taught against standard
    spelling rather than replacing spelling with phonetic transcription.
16. Native Korean and Sino-Korean numeral systems receive separate
    development together with the counters and domains that license them.
17. Productive morphology is introduced through controlled examples and then
    reused in a widening spiral. Do not repeat complete paradigms once a
    pattern is established.
18. New vocabulary increasingly enters through established sentence frames
    when isolation has no special pedagogical value.
19. Common contractions and spoken forms are introduced explicitly after the
    underlying structures are intelligible.
20. Contemporary broadly neutral South Korean usage is the initial
    production reference. Regional and North Korean variation is later
    descriptive material rather than error.

OPENING PACING DIRECTIVE

The first 100 cards form a compact seed. They begin with a small lexical
base and authentic Hangul, then quickly establish 이에요/예요, topic and
subject marking, descriptive predicates, 해요체 verbal clauses, objects,
안 negation, 아니다, polar questions, high-value interrogatives, 에 and
에서, 있다/없다, demonstratives, time expressions, and first -고 싶어요
desire clauses.

The shared Polyglot house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad seed is used
where helpful, but Korean diverges immediately where its structure requires
it. In particular there is no early artificial he/she/you pronoun paradigm.

STAGE 1 — AUTHENTIC HANGUL AND FIRST PREDICATES

KO-01 — Core nouns and authentic Hangul

OBJECTIVE: Begin useful concrete vocabulary in normal Hangul. Initial anchors
include 집, 남자, 여자, 아이 and other immediately reusable nouns.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Romanization drills and detached consonant/vowel-name
quizzes.

KO-02 — Core descriptive verbs

OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-value descriptive verbs such as 크다, 작다, 좋다,
나쁘다 in dictionary form and begin natural polite predication.

KO-03 — Copula 이다 with 이에요/예요

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary polite nominal identification and the
consonant/vowel-conditioned alternation between 이에요 and 예요.

KO-04 — Topic 은/는

OBJECTIVE: Introduce topic marking through clear discourse-neutral examples.
Do not equate it mechanically with English subjecthood.

KO-05 — Subject 이/가

OBJECTIVE: Introduce subject/focus marking and contrast it gradually with
은/는 through existential, descriptive, and identificational contexts.

KO-06 — Personal reference and omission

OBJECTIVE: Introduce 저 and 우리 while establishing omission of recoverable
subjects as normal Korean. Delay a forced English-style pronoun inventory.

KO-07 — Basic 해요체 present clauses

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive polite-present formation with a small set of
high-value verbs. Include 하다 compounds early.

KO-08 — Object 을/를

OBJECTIVE: Establish marked direct objects in ordinary transitive clauses and
allow later controlled omission where natural.

KO-09 — Verbal negation with 안

OBJECTIVE: Establish pre-verbal 안 in ordinary colloquial negation and
contrast it later with -지 않다.

KO-10 — Copular negation 아니다

OBJECTIVE: Establish 아니다 with subject marking on the nominal complement in
natural polite sentences.

KO-11 — Polar questions in 해요체

OBJECTIVE: Show that ordinary polite yes/no questions can use the same
clause-final morphology with interrogative intonation and punctuation.

KO-12 — Core question words

OBJECTIVE: Establish 어디, 뭐/무엇, 누구, 언제, 왜, 어떻게 in natural
questions. Develop particle-marked forms such as 누구를 and 무엇을 as needed.

KO-13 — 에: destination and static location

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency destination and existence/location uses
of 에.

KO-14 — 에서: action location and source

OBJECTIVE: Contrast action location with static 에 and later develop source
meanings.

KO-15 — 있다 and 없다

OBJECTIVE: Establish existence and ordinary possession with 있다/없다 while
distinguishing these from the copula and from location predicates.

KO-16 — Demonstratives 이/그/저

OBJECTIVE: Develop 이/그/저 before nouns and 이것/그것/저것 as pronouns with
natural discourse-distance meanings.

KO-17 — Basic time expressions

OBJECTIVE: Introduce 오늘, 내일, 어제, 지금 and other time expressions in
ordinary clause positions.

KO-18 — Desire with -고 싶다

OBJECTIVE: Establish first-person and question uses of -고 싶어요 without
prematurely generalizing third-person desire.

STAGE 2 — CORE NOMINAL MORPHOLOGY AND EVERYDAY CLAUSES

KO-19 — Plural 들

OBJECTIVE: Introduce 들 where Korean naturally marks plurality and establish
that plural marking is often unnecessary when number is already clear.

KO-20 — Genitive 의 and noun-noun possession

OBJECTIVE: Establish 의 while also showing ordinary omission in common
possessive compounds and relationships.

KO-21 — Comitative 와/과 and 하고

OBJECTIVE: Introduce neutral written/spoken comitative and coordination
patterns one register distinction at a time.

KO-22 — Dative/recipient 에게 and 한테

OBJECTIVE: Establish recipients and interpersonal direction with appropriate
register distinctions.

KO-23 — Instrumental/directional (으)로

OBJECTIVE: Develop means, route, direction, role, and material meanings
progressively rather than as one English equivalent.

KO-24 — From/to ranges: 부터 and 까지

OBJECTIVE: Establish spatial and temporal endpoints.

KO-25 — Also/even with 도

OBJECTIVE: Introduce additive 도 and its interaction with omitted case
particles in natural clauses.

KO-26 — Only with 만

OBJECTIVE: Establish restrictive focus and contrast with 도.

KO-27 — Contrastive topic and information structure

OBJECTIVE: Deepen 은/는 beyond first introduction through contrast, generic
topics, discourse continuity, and switching.

KO-28 — Particle omission in colloquial Korean

OBJECTIVE: Show common natural omission only after the relevant particles
are understood. Avoid treating omission as unrestricted.

KO-29 — First nominal synthesis

COMPLETION STANDARD: Topic, subject, object, location, possession, plurality,
coordination, recipient, instrument/direction, and basic focus particles can
combine in short natural sentences.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Unit completion establishes canonical coverage rather than learner mastery.
2. Established grammar and vocabulary remain active in later cards.
3. New structures normally begin with controlled lexical load and then enter
   increasingly varied contexts.
4. New vocabulary continues throughout every grammatical stage.
5. Standalone lexical cards are used when isolation has pedagogical value.
   Otherwise a new word may be introduced as the one new element in a
   sentence whose surrounding structure is already established.
6. Natural omission, information structure, politeness, and register must not
   be sacrificed merely to mirror an English prompt.
7. Exact-match prompts should state politeness, social relationship, temporal
   context, or discourse contrast when those materially determine the Target.
8. The scheduler supplies repetition; the curriculum supplies varied
   productive examples rather than duplicate cards.
9. Future amendments may split, merge, reorder, or refine units without
   renumbering stable live card IDs.
10. Once the major syllabus branches have been traversed, authentic modern
    Korean should increasingly drive backward-validation gap discovery.

AMENDMENT POLICY

Change this syllabus when the canonical Korean curriculum changes. Routine
card generation and learner progress do not amend it.

AMENDMENT HISTORY

Version 0.1
- Establishes contemporary standard South Korean Korean as the initial
  production reference.
- Begins in authentic Hangul without romanization or a preliminary alphabet
  deck.
- Uses polite 해요체 as the initial conversational baseline while retaining
  dictionary forms for lexical verbs and descriptive verbs.
- Avoids teaching 당신, 그 and 그녀 as mechanical equivalents of English
  pronouns and treats omission and social reference as foundational.
- Develops 은/는, 이/가, 을/를, 에, 에서, 있다/없다, demonstratives, and
  first polite verbal clauses in the opening seed.
- Gives speech levels, honorifics, counters, numeral systems, irregular
  morphology, relative clauses, quotation, modality, discourse, pronunciation
  rules, spacing, and register dedicated longitudinal treatment.
- Preserves continuous vocabulary growth and an indefinitely expandable
  educated-adult long tail.
