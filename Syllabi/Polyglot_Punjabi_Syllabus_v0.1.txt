PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for Punjabi in
Polyglot. The deck uses contemporary Indian/Eastern Punjabi written in
Gurmukhi as its unmarked production baseline, while its mature scope also
includes Shahmukhi literacy and Pakistani/Western Punjabi as an active
longitudinal strand.

The syllabus was written after an initial 100-card Gurmukhi seed. Hindi is
a useful Indo-Aryan reference implementation, but Punjabi grammar,
vocabulary, phonology, scripts and usage remain authoritative.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a transcript. Vocabulary and
previously introduced grammar continue to develop longitudinally.

REFERENCE VARIETY SCRIPTS AND PRONUNCIATION

An unqualified English prompt asks for contemporary Standard Punjabi in the
Indian/Eastern baseline and in Gurmukhi. Gurmukhi is therefore the canonical
initial Target script.

Shahmukhi is introduced later as a second Punjabi literacy system. Explicit
prompts such as "house (Shahmukhi)" may test the same Punjabi lexical item
in Shahmukhi. The curriculum should not duplicate every semantic card merely
to produce a second script version.

The script distinction overlaps with regional and national standardization
but is not identical to it. Pakistani/Western Punjabi receives an active
later strand for vocabulary, grammar, register and usage that meaningfully
differs from the Indian/Eastern default. A prompt labelled "(Pakistani)"
normally implies Shahmukhi unless another script is explicitly requested.
A prompt labelled only "(Shahmukhi)" tests script rather than asserting a
different regional form.

Indian Punjabi material that differs from a broader or Pakistani form may be
labelled "(Indian)" where contrast makes the qualifier necessary. Identical
material need not be duplicated merely to attach regional labels.

Ordinary Targets use normal contemporary spelling. Transliteration is not an
alternative Target. Punjabi's tonal contrasts are structurally important,
including tones historically associated with voiced aspirates and h-related
developments. Pronunciation receives explicit longitudinal treatment without
inventing tone marks in ordinary Gurmukhi or Shahmukhi Targets.

COURSE-WIDE PUNJABI PRINCIPLES

1. Begin with a small reusable seed and move quickly into productive
   Gurmukhi sentences.
2. Preserve normal Gurmukhi spelling including vowel signs, addak, tippi,
   bindi and pairin letters where conventional.
3. Punjabi is broadly head-final. Postpositions, SOV tendencies and verb
   complexes should be learned as Punjabi structures rather than rearranged
   to resemble English.
4. Nouns have masculine and feminine grammatical gender. Agreement is
   central across adjectives, participles and verbal constructions.
5. Inflecting adjectives such as ਵੱਡਾ and ਚੰਗਾ expose gender and number
   contrasts early. Invariant adjectives should be learned as they occur.
6. Personal reference includes ਮੈਂ, ਤੂੰ, ਤੁਸੀਂ, ਇਹ, ਉਹ and ਅਸੀਂ. English
   he/she distinctions are not mechanically encoded by ਇਹ/ਉਹ; agreement and
   context often supply gender.
7. ਤੂੰ and ਤੁਸੀਂ encode important intimacy/respect/number distinctions.
   Prompts must identify the intended social/number value when exact matching
   requires it.
8. Present habitual clauses expose gender and number agreement through
   participial forms together with the copular/auxiliary system. Speaker or
   subject gender must be stated in English when it changes the Target.
9. Punjabi permits natural argument omission where discourse licenses it,
   but do not force systematic pro-drop merely because agreement may reveal
   person or gender.
10. Negation with ਨਹੀਂ is introduced in ordinary clauses and later developed
    for scope, imperative/prohibitive patterns and complex predicates.
11. Polar questions can use clause-initial ਕੀ in clear neutral examples.
    Intonation and other natural interrogative patterns are developed later.
12. Content interrogatives such as ਕੀ, ਕੌਣ, ਕਿੱਥੇ, ਕਦੋਂ, ਕਿਉਂ and ਕਿਵੇਂ
    develop through ordinary clauses rather than isolated terminology.
13. Punjabi does not have an English-style article system. Do not insert a
    numeral merely to translate a/an unless the numeral or indefiniteness is
    actually intended.
14. Case relations develop through direct/oblique noun forms plus
    postpositions. Teach meaningful constructions before exhaustive
    paradigms.
15. ਨੂੰ has recipient, goal, experiencer and differential-object functions.
    Develop these through controlled contrasts rather than treating it as a
    simple translation of English "to".
16. Possession is not a single lexical have verb. ਕੋਲ constructions,
    genitives and existential patterns should be taught according to meaning.
17. Genitive forms agree with the possessed noun in important constructions.
    Possessive pronouns and genitive postpositions therefore belong to the
    agreement spine.
18. The infinitive in -ਣਾ is the normal lexical citation form for verbs and
    also participates in nominal and modal constructions.
19. Habitual, progressive, perfective and future systems must be developed as
    Punjabi aspect/tense constructions rather than English tense labels.
20. Perfective transitive clauses interact with ergative marking and
    agreement. Develop ਨੇ and its consequences explicitly when the opening is
    ready for past/perfective structure.
21. Light verbs and compound predicates are central to ordinary Punjabi.
    Introduce lexical meanings first where useful, then productive
    combinations longitudinally.
22. Motion, aspect and vector/light-verb combinations should be learned
    through frequent constructions rather than abstract inventories.
23. Imperatives and requests vary with social relationship and politeness.
    Develop ਤੂੰ and ਤੁਸੀਂ forms in appropriate contexts.
24. Postpositions and spatial expressions should be learned with the forms
    they govern and their idiomatic meanings.
25. Word order is flexible enough for information structure but not arbitrary.
    Neutral order, focus, topicalization and scrambling develop from ordinary
    examples.
26. Punjabi has important phonological contrasts not transparently represented
    for an English speaker. Aspirated/unaspirated series, retroflexion, vowel
    length, gemination and tone deserve explicit attention.
27. Gurmukhi literacy includes the independent vowel letters, matras, nasal
    signs, addak and conjunct/subjoined conventions actually used in modern
    Punjabi. Do not turn the curriculum into an alphabet recital before
    useful language begins.
28. Shahmukhi literacy is taught after a useful Punjabi base exists. Begin
    with familiar words and systematic letter/position behavior so that the
    learner is principally acquiring a second script rather than new grammar.
29. Shahmukhi cards should teach Punjabi orthography rather than generic Urdu
    spelling assumptions. Shared Perso-Arabic literacy is useful but Punjabi
    usage remains authoritative.
30. Pakistani/Western Punjabi is an active production and comprehension
    strand. Label meaningful departures from the Indian/Eastern default and
    avoid duplicating identical material solely for regional symmetry.
31. The absence of a qualifier is informative: Indian/Eastern Punjabi +
    Gurmukhi. Qualifiers appear when they change the intended answer.
32. Contemporary broadly neutral Punjabi precedes strongly regional,
    historical or literary material.
33. Vocabulary grows just in time. Similarity to Hindi, Urdu or another
    Indo-Aryan language may help explanation but never overrides Punjabi
    usage.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1-100

The seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- Gurmukhi as the sole initial Target script;
- early masculine/feminine adjective agreement;
- ਬੱਚਾ/ਬੱਚੀ as a visible gender contrast;
- ਮੈਂ, ਤੂੰ, ਤੁਸੀਂ, ਉਹ, ਇਹ and ਅਸੀਂ;
- present copular forms in identity and location;
- habitual present agreement through ਬੋਲਣਾ and ਕੰਮ ਕਰਨਾ;
- ਨਹੀਂ negation and ਕੀ polar questions;
- ਕਿੱਥੇ, ਕੀ and ਕੌਣ;
- possession with ਕੋਲ;
- first direct-object contrasts with and without ਨੂੰ;
- an early visible oblique form in ਬੱਚੇ ਨੂੰ;
- ਚਾਹੀਦਾ and ਚਾਹੁਣਾ as distinct high-frequency ways of expressing wants;
- respectful ਤੁਸੀਂ and gender-sensitive English prompting.

The opening is deliberately entirely Gurmukhi. Shahmukhi and Pakistani
variation begin only after the learner has a useful Punjabi base.

CARD-SPECIFIC RULES

1. EnglishPrompt must be unique and select one intended Punjabi Target.
2. An unqualified prompt uses the deck default: Indian/Eastern Punjabi +
   Gurmukhi.
3. InputHint is normally blank during the Gurmukhi opening.
4. Avoid commas in card fields where a natural alternative exists.
5. Preserve Gurmukhi orthography exactly. Do not replace normal spelling with
   transliteration.
6. State speaker/subject gender in English whenever Punjabi agreement makes
   the answer otherwise ambiguous.
7. State intimacy/respect/number when ਤੂੰ versus ਤੁਸੀਂ or related agreement
   makes the answer otherwise ambiguous.
8. Do not force English he/she distinctions into ਇਹ/ਉਹ lexical cards.
9. Postposition-sensitive prompts must provide enough meaning to select one
   intended construction.
10. Do not turn grammatical labels into the main learning task. Morphological
    prompts may be used sparingly where they clarify an otherwise invisible
    contrast before sentence reuse.
11. Aspect prompts must provide enough context to select the intended Punjabi
    form.
12. Explicit prompts such as "house (Shahmukhi)" may test second-script
    literacy. Do not duplicate the whole semantic curriculum by script.
13. A "(Pakistani)" label normally implies Shahmukhi and Pakistani/Western
    standard usage unless another script is explicitly requested.
14. A "(Shahmukhi)" label by itself tests script and does not imply that the
    lexical or grammatical form differs regionally.
15. Do not create a second regional card merely because Indian and Pakistani
    Punjabi have an identical form. Add regional cards when they teach a
    meaningful lexical grammatical orthographic or register distinction.
16. Strongly regional historical or literary forms must be labelled when
    exact matching would otherwise imply they are the neutral deck default.
17. Ordinary Targets do not carry invented tone marks. Tone belongs in
    pronunciation-focused instruction while normal spelling is preserved.

DEVELOPMENT POLICY

The first 100 cards are the empirical opening. Because repository decks are
now being used in the Polyglot app, their IDs are live and must remain stable.
Correct genuine errors in place rather than renumbering existing cards.

The next expansion should deepen noun/plural and adjective agreement,
postpositions, oblique forms, possession, pronouns and habitual structure
while opening progressive and first perfective/past contrasts. ਨੇ and
ergative perfective clauses should enter once enough ordinary transitive
language exists to make their agreement consequences visible.

Shahmukhi should not be rushed into the opening. Introduce it after a useful
Gurmukhi Punjabi base so familiar Punjabi words can carry the new script.
Pakistani Punjabi can then provide continuing Shahmukhi practice while
teaching genuine regional-standard differences.

Hindi and Urdu are useful Indo-Aryan comparison points. Punjabi tone,
agreement, postpositions, aspect, light verbs, Gurmukhi/Shahmukhi literacy
and Indian/Pakistani variation remain authoritative.
