POLYGLOT ARABIC SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1
Status: Current
Governing Charter: Polyglot Curriculum Charter v0.8
Encoding: UTF-8

1. PURPOSE AND VARIETY POLICY

This syllabus defines the canonical path for Arabic in Polyglot.

Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) is the unmarked backbone. Unless a prompt is
explicitly labelled otherwise, an Arabic card expects MSA.

Arabic differs from most Polyglot languages in the scale and functional
importance of regional spoken varieties. The curriculum therefore does not
postpone regional Arabic to a small late-stage variation unit. Major spoken
varieties will enter after a useful common/MSA foundation and then develop
as substantial longitudinal strands alongside MSA. Levantine is expected
to be the first such strand; Egyptian, Gulf and other major varieties may
follow when their treatment can be defined responsibly.

Regional cards must be explicitly disambiguated in EnglishPrompt. MSA
cards need no [MSA] label. The exact regional inventory and the point at
which each strand opens remain curriculum decisions to be refined from
card-development evidence.

2. EMPIRICAL OPENING

Cards 700000–700049 form the approved 50-card seed prototype.

The seed demonstrates that Arabic can move directly from a small lexical
base into substantial structure without verbs. It establishes an empirical
opening around:

- ordinary unvocalized Arabic orthography;
- a small noun/adjective seed;
- present nominal/adjectival predication without an overt copula;
- grammatical gender and adjective agreement;
- definiteness with ال;
- the contrast between predicative and attributive adjectives;
- masculine/feminine demonstratives هذا and هذه;
- demonstrative noun phrases and demonstrative predication;
- initial personal pronouns and nominal predicates;
- a first polar question with هل.

This seed is a pacing model, not a requirement that every later Arabic
sequence reproduce its exact card order.

3. ORTHOGRAPHY AND INPUT

Use conventional contemporary Arabic orthography from the first card.

Ordinary MSA Targets are normally unvocalized, matching normal adult
writing. Do not require short-vowel diacritics merely to make beginner
pronunciation explicit. Diacritics remain legitimate Arabic orthography
and should be introduced where they are conventionally required, where
they disambiguate something pedagogically important, or where the
curriculum specifically teaches them.

InputHint is normally blank because Arabic is entered directly in Arabic
script. Do not use InputHint as a general transliteration or pronunciation
field.

Right-to-left display of neutral punctuation is an interface/rendering
concern and does not change the logical Unicode order stored in the CSV.

4. ROADMAP PRINCIPLE

This syllabus supplies a path forward rather than a turn-by-turn transcript.

Prerequisite-safe detours are permitted. A structure may appear somewhat
earlier than its numbered unit when the card remains natural and
comprehensible. Conversely, part of an earlier unit may continue toward
establishment through later double-duty cards.

Unit numbering indicates useful introduction order and dependencies. It
does not require exhaustive serial mastery.

5. EARLY STRUCTURAL DEVELOPMENT

AR-01 — Small lexical seed and authentic script

OBJECTIVE: Establish a compact reusable set of high-value nouns and
adjectives directly in Arabic script.

EMPIRICAL MODEL: بيت، رجل، امرأة، طفل، كبير، صغير، جيد، سيئ.

AR-02 — Present nominal and adjectival predication

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary present-tense equational/descriptive clauses
without importing an English-style present copula.

EXAMPLES: البيت كبير. الرجل كبير. المرأة صغيرة.

AR-03 — Gender and adjective agreement

OBJECTIVE: Make masculine/feminine agreement visible through controlled
contrasts, then reinforce it longitudinally.

CARD STRATEGY: Do not turn every new adjective into a full paradigm once
the contrast is established.

AR-04 — Definiteness and attributive adjectives

OBJECTIVE: Establish ال and the contrast between an indefinite/predicative
adjective and a definite attributive adjective.

CORE CONTRAST:
البيت كبير. — The house is big.
البيت الكبير — the big house

AR-05 — Demonstratives

OBJECTIVE: Establish هذا and هذه in independent and noun-phrase use,
including the relationship between demonstratives and definite nouns.

AR-06 — Personal pronouns and nominal predicates

OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-value independent pronouns and use them in
natural verbless present predicates.

CARD STRATEGY: Expand the pronoun inventory as genuine person/number/gender
contrasts become useful; do not retain pronouns merely to mirror English
once omission or other discourse patterns become relevant.

AR-07 — Polar questions

OBJECTIVE: Establish هل questions in already-known nominal structures,
then reuse question formation with later verbal material.

AR-08 — Question words

OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-frequency interrogatives through controlled
nominal contexts before and alongside verbal expansion.

AR-09 — Negation of nominal predicates

OBJECTIVE: Establish natural MSA negation of present nominal/adjectival
predicates, including the behavior of ليس and its agreement forms.

AR-10 — Number: dual and plural foundations

OBJECTIVE: Introduce singular/dual/plural distinctions early enough that
later agreement and verbal systems do not silently assume an English-like
two-way number system.

CARD STRATEGY: Separate productive sound patterns from broken plurals.
Learn important broken plurals lexically while progressively exposing
recurring patterns.

AR-11 — Possession and construct state (الإضافة)

OBJECTIVE: Establish basic noun–noun possession and the definiteness
behavior of the construct state.

AR-12 — Prepositions and pronominal attachment

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency prepositions first with nouns and then
with attached pronouns as the morphology becomes useful.

AR-13 — Existence and location

OBJECTIVE: Establish common MSA existential and locative constructions
without forcing them into English copular structure.

6. VERBAL FOUNDATION

Arabic has no infinitive that should simply inherit the role played by
French or German infinitive cards. Verbal vocabulary and morphology should
therefore be introduced according to Arabic conventions rather than by
translating the European card architecture.

AR-14 — Verb citation and lexical representation

OBJECTIVE: Establish a consistent Polyglot convention for introducing
Arabic verbs.

CARD STRATEGY: Before a large verbal batch is generated, test the practical
relationship among the conventional third-person masculine singular
perfect citation form, imperfect form, verbal noun where useful, and root.
Do not make bare roots into ordinary English-to-Arabic vocabulary answers
when the lexical item is a verb.

AR-15 — Perfect foundation

OBJECTIVE: Establish the perfect through a small number of high-frequency,
structurally useful verbs and genuine person/number/gender contrasts.

AR-16 — Imperfect foundation

OBJECTIVE: Establish the imperfect system and its agreement morphology,
using known verbs before multiplying lexical stems.

AR-17 — Subject expression and verbal agreement

OBJECTIVE: Develop natural MSA subject expression, including when an
independent pronoun is unnecessary, and introduce the important interaction
between word order and agreement progressively.

AR-18 — Direct objects

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary transitive clauses and object expression
with controlled vocabulary.

AR-19 — Verbal negation

OBJECTIVE: Introduce major negation patterns in prerequisite-aware stages
rather than treating English "not" as one Arabic lexical item.

AR-20 — Verbal questions

OBJECTIVE: Reuse هل and introduce relevant question-word structures in
verbal clauses.

AR-21 — Future reference

OBJECTIVE: Establish common future marking after the imperfect is secure.

AR-22 — Imperative and prohibition

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency command/prohibition morphology after
the relevant imperfect patterns are available.

7. ROOTS, PATTERNS, AND DERIVATION

AR-23 — Root awareness

OBJECTIVE: Make consonantal roots useful for recognizing lexical
relationships without treating a root as though it were itself the normal
word meaning.

AR-24 — Form I pattern development

OBJECTIVE: Develop common Form I stem/vowel behavior through actual lexical
families rather than abstract tables detached from words.

AR-25 — Derived verb forms

OBJECTIVE: Introduce Forms II–X and other productive derivational
relationships according to frequency, transparency and lexical usefulness.

CARD STRATEGY: A form number is metalinguistic organization, not normally
an EnglishPrompt. Teach actual verbs and contrasts.

AR-26 — Verbal nouns and participles

OBJECTIVE: Develop masdars and active/passive participles as lexical and
grammatical resources in the contexts where they are used.

AR-27 — Weak, hollow, defective and doubled verbs

OBJECTIVE: Introduce major non-strong verb classes progressively after the
regular system provides a useful reference point. High-frequency members
may appear earlier when ordinary language requires them.

8. NOMINAL AND ADJECTIVAL EXPANSION

AR-28 — Broader plural systems

OBJECTIVE: Expand sound masculine, sound feminine and high-frequency broken
plural patterns longitudinally.

AR-29 — Dual expansion

OBJECTIVE: Develop dual morphology and agreement across useful nominal and
adjectival contexts.

AR-30 — Pronoun suffixes

OBJECTIVE: Establish attached possessive and object pronouns in their
natural environments.

AR-31 — Relative clauses

OBJECTIVE: Establish الذي/التي and the broader relative system with
attention to definiteness and resumptive/reference patterns where needed.

AR-32 — Comparison

OBJECTIVE: Establish comparative/superlative morphology and constructions
without assuming European adjective behavior.

AR-33 — Numbers and counted nouns

OBJECTIVE: Develop cardinal numbers, gender interactions, counted-noun
forms and agreement in manageable stages.

CARD STRATEGY: Arabic numeral grammar warrants systematic treatment because
its cells teach genuine contrasts; do not compress it into a few lexical
number cards.

AR-34 — Quantification

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency quantity expressions and their nominal
syntax.

9. CASE, MOOD, AND FORMAL MSA

Because ordinary unvocalized writing often leaves inflectional endings
invisible, case and mood must not be mistaken for nonexistent grammar.
They should be taught when the learner has enough structure for the
distinctions to be meaningful.

AR-35 — Case system foundation

OBJECTIVE: Establish nominative, accusative and genitive functions and the
contexts that license them.

AR-36 — Case endings and nunation

OBJECTIVE: Make written/spoken formal inflection intelligible without
requiring routine fully vocalized Targets.

AR-37 — Imperfect mood

OBJECTIVE: Establish indicative, subjunctive and jussive contrasts through
the particles and constructions that trigger them.

AR-38 — Formal agreement and word order

OBJECTIVE: Develop the interaction of VSO/SVO order, subject definiteness,
number and verbal agreement beyond beginner simplifications.

AR-39 — Passive

OBJECTIVE: Establish morphological passive patterns and their ordinary
formal uses.

AR-40 — Complex nominal syntax

OBJECTIVE: Expand construct chains, apposition, adjective stacking and
other mature noun-phrase structures.

11. REGIONAL SPOKEN ARABIC

Regional Arabic is a core longitudinal component of the Arabic curriculum,
not a token late appendix.

AR-51 — MSA-to-spoken transition

OBJECTIVE: Establish that ordinary spoken varieties are systematic
language varieties with substantial phonological, lexical, morphological
and syntactic differences from MSA.

CARD STRATEGY: Contrast only where useful. Do not imply that a regional
form is merely "incorrect MSA."

AR-52 — Levantine Arabic foundation

OBJECTIVE: Open the first substantial regional strand with ordinary
high-frequency spoken structures and vocabulary.

PROMPT POLICY: Regional prompts are explicitly labelled, for example
"I want to go home. [Levantine]". Equivalent unlabelled prompts remain
MSA by default.

AR-53 — Levantine longitudinal development

OBJECTIVE: Develop Levantine grammar, vocabulary, idiom and everyday
conversation deeply enough to represent a coherent spoken system rather
than a list of regionalisms.

AR-54 — Egyptian Arabic foundation
AR-55 — Egyptian longitudinal development
AR-56 — Gulf Arabic foundation
AR-57 — Gulf longitudinal development
AR-58 — Iraqi and other major eastern varieties
AR-59 — Maghrebi varieties
AR-60 — Cross-regional comprehension and register choice

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE: The exact subdivision, sequencing and depth of
regional strands should be revisited before each is opened. The labels
above are a roadmap, not a claim that every named regional category is
internally uniform or deserves identical treatment.

Regional material may begin before all advanced MSA units are complete
once the common foundation makes the contrasts intelligible. Thereafter
MSA and opened regional strands should grow in parallel.

16. GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. MSA is assumed unless a card explicitly identifies a regional variety.
2. The 50-card seed is the empirical model for the opening, not an exact
   future-generation script.
3. Introduction does not imply establishment. Reinforce structures
   longitudinally through double-duty cards.
4. Authentic Arabic orthography is used from first exposure.
5. Unvocalized ordinary writing is the default; diacritics are introduced
   for genuine orthographic, grammatical or disambiguating purposes.
6. Vocabulary grows just in time alongside structure.
7. Paradigms receive systematic coverage when their cells teach genuine
   contrasts; otherwise sample and reuse.
8. Arabic verbal vocabulary must follow an Arabic-appropriate citation
   strategy rather than an imported infinitive model.
9. Regional varieties are substantial curriculum strands and may open
   earlier than regional variation in most Polyglot languages.
10. Mature MSA and opened regional strands develop longitudinally in
    parallel.
11. The scheduler supplies repetition; the curriculum supplies varied
    examples. Exact duplicate cards do not belong in the CSV.
12. Local prerequisite-safe detours do not require syllabus amendment.

17. AMENDMENT POLICY

Amend this syllabus when the preferred Arabic curriculum path changes,
not merely when card generation advances or takes a local detour.

Particularly likely early review points are:

- the practical Polyglot citation convention for Arabic verbs;
- how much explicit case/mood vocalization is useful;
- the first point at which Levantine should enter;
- regional prompt labelling and subdivision;
- whether later card experience reveals a better ordering among the early
  nominal, verbal, number and possession systems.
