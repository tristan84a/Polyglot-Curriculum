PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern Ukrainian in Polyglot. It preserves the earlier Ukrainian-specific architecture: authentic Cyrillic from the beginning, productive vocative relatively early, longitudinal aspect, both analytic and synthetic imperfective futures, Ukrainian euphony and orthography, and later treatment of Russian-Ukrainian contact without making Russian the organizing principle of the course.

The syllabus is a sequence of introductions rather than a textbook chapter order. Vocabulary grows continuously. A construction may be previewed before its numbered unit and reinforced much later. Once a productive pattern is established, later cards should increasingly combine it with new vocabulary and other known structures rather than exhaust paradigms in isolation.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND WRITING

Contemporary broadly neutral standard Ukrainian is the unmarked reference variety. Use ordinary Ukrainian Cyrillic in every Target. There is no transliteration or alphabet-name bootstrap. InputHint is normally blank.

Ukrainian structure and usage are authoritative. Similarity to Russian may aid comparison but must not produce Russian calques, Russian spelling, or assumptions that cognates share meaning, government, aspect, stress or register.

ґ and г remain distinct according to conventional Ukrainian spelling. Apostrophe use, soft sign, йотовані letters and euphonic alternations such as у/в, і/й and з/із/зі are learned first through real language and later systematized.

COURSE-WIDE CARD PRINCIPLES

1. Write natural ordinary English prompts and natural Ukrainian Targets. Do not make either language imitate the other for flashcard convenience.
2. Apply established course defaults silently. Add a qualifier only when the intended Target deliberately departs from a default and the distinction cannot reasonably be inferred from natural English or context.
3. Parenthetical qualifiers are sparse early scaffolding rather than permanent translation language. Prefer natural contextual cues.
4. Exact matching identifies the canonical natural Target selected for a card; it does not claim that no other Ukrainian sentence could express the proposition.
5. Noun gender, animacy, case, adjective agreement, verbal aspect, stress and stem alternations are longitudinal systems rather than one-time paradigm lessons.
6. Ukrainian has seven cases. Case functions are established through controlled useful examples before paradigms broaden. The vocative is ordinary productive grammar rather than a decorative late exception.
7. Present nominal identification normally permits a zero copula. Teach є according to its actual existential, possessive, emphatic and contextual uses rather than as a mandatory equivalent of English is.
8. Imperfective and perfective aspect develop longitudinally. Aspect partners are lexical and morphological relationships rather than mechanically interchangeable translations.
9. Ukrainian imperfective future includes both analytic forms with бути and the synthetic future. Both receive explicit treatment.
10. Word order is flexible but not arbitrary. Neutral ordering, information structure, focus and rhythm develop progressively.
11. Establish a representative productive paradigm clearly when useful; thereafter reduce paradigm density and favor contextualized selective forms while vocabulary keeps expanding.
12. Avoid commas in card sentences whenever a natural alternative is available because the simple CSV workflow is comma-sensitive.
13. Check the entire live deck plus every new batch for duplicate IDs and duplicate EnglishPrompt values before committing.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 2300000-2300014 are the standardized 15-card micro opening. They deliberately preview accusative direct objects, possession with у + genitive and numeral-driven noun morphology before those systems are formally established. Preserve these cards as footholds rather than treating their presence as proof that the corresponding syllabus units are complete.

Cards 2300015 onward form the syllabus-governed seed and continuing canonical deck.

STAGE 1 — AUTHENTIC CYRILLIC AND CORE CLAUSES

UK-01 — Core concrete nouns and authentic Ukrainian Cyrillic
OBJECTIVE: Build a varied useful lexical base in ordinary Ukrainian spelling. Include people, objects, food, animals and places. Do not construct an alphabet inventory.

UK-02 — Core qualities and actions
OBJECTIVE: Add common adjectives, adverbs and verb infinitives without forcing premature paradigms. Vocabulary should remain semantically varied rather than clustering many easily confused abstract words.

UK-03 — Personal and demonstrative reference
OBJECTIVE: Establish я, ти, він, вона, воно, ми, ви, вони and це in natural use. Informal singular ти is the early default for ordinary second-person prompts; plural or polite ви should be established by natural context when possible.

UK-04 — Present identification and zero copula
OBJECTIVE: Establish natural nominal and adjectival predicates such as Це будинок, Вона лікарка and Будинок великий without inserting mandatory є.

UK-05 — Noun gender and basic adjective agreement
OBJECTIVE: Establish masculine, feminine and neuter singular nominative agreement through useful nouns and adjectives before widening to plural and oblique cases.

UK-06 — Basic negation and yes/no questions
OBJECTIVE: Establish не and ordinary polar questions across familiar predicates. Ukrainian does not require an English-style auxiliary for questions.

UK-07 — Existence and possession with є / немає
OBJECTIVE: Establish patterns such as У мене є книга and У мене немає книги while also showing contexts in which є is naturally absent. Treat these as Ukrainian constructions rather than literal have equivalents.

UK-08 — Present tense: first productive verb patterns
OBJECTIVE: Establish person/number endings through a small coherent set of imperfective verbs. One early pattern may receive relatively dense coverage; subsequent verbs use declining paradigm density.

UK-09 — Present tense: additional patterns and common irregulars
OBJECTIVE: Broaden present morphology to additional productive patterns and indispensable stem-changing or irregular verbs without creating an undifferentiated conjugation block.

UK-10 — Early sentence synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine identity, description, negation, possession, questions and present actions with continually expanding vocabulary and natural word order.

STAGE 2 — SENTENCE TOOLS AND FIRST CASE FUNCTIONS

UK-11 — Question words
OBJECTIVE: Establish хто, що, де, куди, звідки, коли, як, чому and other high-value interrogatives progressively in natural questions.

UK-12 — Negation with verbs
OBJECTIVE: Extend не across verbal predicates and distinguish ordinary verbal negation from немає and later negative-pronoun systems.

UK-13 — Infinitive complements and early modal constructions
OBJECTIVE: Establish common patterns with хотіти, могти, треба, потрібно, можна and related meanings one distinction at a time.

UK-14 — Nominative plural and adjective agreement
OBJECTIVE: Establish productive plural noun and adjective patterns before less common stem and stress exceptions.

UK-15 — Accusative singular: inanimate direct objects
OBJECTIVE: Establish prototypical direct objects beginning with forms that make the nominative/accusative relationship transparent. Earlier micro-deck examples are previews rather than completion of this unit.

UK-16 — Accusative singular: animacy and visible contrasts
OBJECTIVE: Establish animate masculine patterns and the role of animacy through concrete lexical contrasts.

UK-17 — Locative singular with в/у and на
OBJECTIVE: Establish common location expressions and the locative case. Teach lexical choice of в/у versus на separately from case endings.

UK-18 — Direction with в/у and на + accusative
OBJECTIVE: Contrast location with destination using familiar places and motion vocabulary.

UK-19 — Instrumental accompaniment with з
OBJECTIVE: Introduce a high-value instrumental function through transparent with relationships before broadening instrumental morphology.

UK-20 — Vocative: direct address
OBJECTIVE: Establish productive modern vocative forms with names, kinship terms and common forms of address. Use genuine address contexts rather than detached seventh-case tables.

UK-21 — Genitive singular
OBJECTIVE: Broaden the genitive from possession/немає into absence, source, quantity and other high-value functions.

UK-22 — Dative singular
OBJECTIVE: Establish recipients and common experiencer constructions including age and selected impersonal meanings where appropriate.

UK-23 — Basic place, time and manner adverbs
OBJECTIVE: Expand sentence tools such as тут, там, зараз, потім, сьогодні and завтра in natural positions.

UK-24 — First case and sentence synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine the principal early case functions across several lexical representatives. Do not ask for case names as answers.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Unit completion establishes canonical coverage rather than learner mastery.
2. Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3. New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled lexical load while vocabulary continues growing.
4. Existing valid Ukrainian cards remain valid when later reconciled with this syllabus. Fill prerequisite gaps by convergence rather than restarting or renumbering sound cards.
5. Stable live card IDs are not renumbered merely because syllabus organization changes.
6. Parallel units within a stage may interleave once prerequisites are satisfied; unit numbers state the default canonical path.
7. Previewing a later construction does not move the Manifest frontier.

CURRENT SEED ALIGNMENT

The 15-card micro deck previews several later systems but does not establish them. The expanded 100-card seed establishes UK-01 through UK-14 in a widening spiral: core vocabulary, personal reference, zero-copula predicates, gender/agreement, negation/questions, existence/possession, productive present morphology, sentence synthesis, interrogatives, verbal negation, early infinitive/modal constructions and nominative plural agreement.

The forward frontier after the 100-card seed is:

UK-15 — Accusative singular: inanimate direct objects
