PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern Polish
in Polyglot. It specifies content, prerequisite order, and card
strategy, not learner progress. Routine card generation changes the
manifest and CSV, not this syllabus.

Vocabulary and lexical usage grow continuously. Grammar units define
structural progression but do not freeze vocabulary. Unit sizes follow
completion standards rather than fixed card quotas.

The established Polyglot syllabi provide structural and editorial
models, but Polish is organized according to its own grammar, usage,
orthography, and prerequisite relationships. Similarity to Russian,
Ukrainian, or other Slavic languages may inform comparison but does not
make another language the structural authority for Polish.

TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED — A form, word, construction, orthographic element, or
distinction has begun to be demonstrated.

ESTABLISHED — The canonical curriculum has demonstrated the item
sufficiently for its basic operation to be assumed later. This describes
curriculum state, not learner mastery.

REUSABLE — An established item or structure may appear naturally in
later cards without being the principal teaching objective.

LEXICAL ANCHOR — A word deliberately selected to make a grammatical,
semantic, morphological, or orthographic pattern visible. Anchors do not
limit additional vocabulary.

CANONICAL SEQUENCE — The static pedagogical ordering of cards and
concepts. Scheduling frequency may adapt to performance; curriculum
order does not.

COMPLETION STANDARD — The coverage required before a unit’s principal
concept may be treated as established.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED — Material postponed because it would obscure the
current pattern, depends on later knowledge, or deserves separate
treatment.

COURSE-WIDE POLISH PRINCIPLES

1.  Use ordinary contemporary Polish orthography in every Target from
    the first card. Preserve all required diacritics: ą, ć, ę, ł, ń, ó,
    ś, ź, ż. Exact matching treats these as meaningful spelling
    distinctions.
2.  InputHint is normally blank. Polish is entered directly with an
    appropriate Latin-script keyboard; unfamiliar diacritics or
    pronunciation do not by themselves justify an InputHint.
3.  Begin with useful words and naturally complete utterances. Use
    conventional citation forms and natural Polish syntax rather than
    mirroring English mechanically.
4.  Every Target should be natural for its intended meaning and register
    and worth retaining permanently. Beginner difficulty is controlled
    by selection and sequencing, not beginnerized Polish.
5.  Noun gender, animacy/personhood, case, adjective agreement, verbal
    aspect, stem alternations, and stress-sensitive morphology are
    longitudinal systems rather than one-time paradigm lessons.
6.  Polish has seven cases. Case functions are established through
    controlled examples before paradigms are broadened. The vocative is
    treated as productive modern grammar whose distribution depends on
    lexical item, register, and address context.
7.  The gender system must not be reduced to masculine/feminine/neuter.
    Early singular gender provides a foundation, but masculine animate,
    masculine personal, and plural masculine-personal versus
    non-masculine-personal distinctions receive explicit development.
8.  Present-tense nominal identification uses być naturally; Polish is
    not taught through a Russian-style zero-copula model.
9.  Imperfective and perfective aspect develop longitudinally. Aspect
    partners are treated as lexical and morphological relationships, not
    as mechanically interchangeable translations.
10. Polish non-past perfective forms normally have future reference. The
    imperfective future with forms of być and an infinitive or past-like
    form is developed separately and naturally.
11. Word order is flexible but not arbitrary. Neutral ordering,
    information structure, focus, clitic placement, rhythm, and
    discourse context are developed progressively rather than treating
    all permutations as equivalent.
12. Pronunciation and spelling relationships such as ó/u, rz/ż, ch/h,
    nasal vowels, palatalization, and consonant alternations are learned
    through real vocabulary before later systematization. Conventional
    spelling is never replaced by phonetic transcription.
13. Productive patterns precede exceptions where possible.
    High-frequency irregular forms may appear early when ordinary
    language requires them.
14. Grammar is normally learned through controlled examples rather than
    terminology questions.
15. A card’s single Target must be natural and sufficiently determined
    by its English prompt. Prompts should disambiguate aspect, case,
    lexical choice, register, motion, gender/personhood, or information
    structure where necessary.
16. High-frequency words develop longitudinally across senses,
    constructions, case frames, collocations, idioms, derivational
    families, and register.
17. Contemporary broadly neutral standard Polish is the initial
    reference variety. Common colloquial, regional, historical,
    literary, and generational forms are introduced later and described
    without displacing the standard.
18. Similarity to other Slavic languages may assist explanation and
    lexical selection, but Polish usage, government, aspect, spelling,
    stress, personhood distinctions, and conventional phraseology remain
    authoritative.

OPENING PACING DIRECTIVE

Polish begins with a compact reusable seed, not a preliminary miniature
dictionary. The preferred opening anchors are dom, mężczyzna, kobieta,
dziecko and duży, mały, dobry, zły. Authentic Polish spelling applies
immediately.

After these anchors, move promptly into reference, być, and simple
identification/description. Additional nouns, adjectives, and verbs are
introduced just in time for structures that can use them. PL-02 must not
be implemented as a long run of standalone infinitives. Action vocabulary
may begin with one or two useful verbs when finite present-tense work is
near enough to reuse them.

The early French deck is an approved pacing/interleaving model wherever
Polish permits analogous cards. Polish copula usage, gender, case,
agreement, subject expression, and other Polish structures override
literal copying.

STAGE 1 — AUTHENTIC ORTHOGRAPHY AND FIRST STRUCTURES

PL-01 — Core concrete nouns and authentic Polish spelling

PREREQUISITES: None. OBJECTIVE: Begin ordinary Polish spelling and
high-value concrete nouns together. LEXICAL ANCHORS: dom, książka, stół,
woda, miasto, człowiek. CARD STRATEGY: English meaning → natural Polish
citation form. Select useful vocabulary that naturally exposes ordinary
letters and diacritics rather than constructing an alphabet inventory.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: People, objects, food, animals, and places.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Alphabet-name quizzes, ASCII substitutes for
Polish letters, full declension, and detached spelling drills.
COMPLETION STANDARD: A small reusable noun seed is sufficient to begin
PL-03/PL-04; broader noun vocabulary continues just in time through later
units. LEADS TO: PL-03–PL-10.

PL-02 — Core qualities and actions

PREREQUISITES: PL-01 begun. OBJECTIVE: Build useful descriptive and
action vocabulary without forcing premature agreement or conjugation.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: dobrze, źle, dużo, czytać, wiedzieć, pracować. CARD
STRATEGY: Use natural standalone adverbs and verb infinitives where
appropriate; adjective citation forms may appear lexically before their
agreement system is taught. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Daily activities,
common qualities, and concrete actions. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full
adjective paradigms, full conjugation, and an aspect-pair requirement
for every new verb. COMPLETION STANDARD: A small adjective seed supports
immediate description. Standalone action vocabulary remains minimal until
it can be reused in finite clauses; no broad infinitive inventory is
required. LEADS TO: PL-05, PL-08, PL-11.

PL-03 — Personal and demonstrative reference

PREREQUISITES: PL-01. OBJECTIVE: Introduce ja, ty, on, ona, ono, my, wy,
oni, one and to where naturally used. CARD STRATEGY: Make oni versus one
visible as a genuine Polish plural distinction but postpone the full
masculine-personal system. Do not mechanically retain subject pronouns
once verb morphology makes them unnecessary. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED:
Oblique pronoun forms, honorific Pan/Pani systems, and full
demonstrative agreement. COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic person, number,
singular gender, and plural reference operate in short utterances. LEADS
TO: PL-04, PL-08, PL-15.

PL-04 — Present identification with być

PREREQUISITES: PL-01, PL-03. OBJECTIVE: Establish jestem, jesteś, jest,
jesteśmy, jesteście, są in natural identification, classification,
occupation, and description. CORE PATTERNS: To jest dom. Ona jest
lekarką. Dom jest duży. CARD STRATEGY: Use natural Polish predicate
structures rather than mapping every English “be” sentence to one fixed
construction. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Occupations, identities, and
common descriptions. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Past/future być, advanced
instrumental predicates, and colloquial copular ellipsis. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Common present-tense uses of być are established across
persons and natural predicate types. LEADS TO: PL-05–PL-07.

PL-05 — Noun gender and basic adjective agreement

PREREQUISITES: PL-01, PL-04. OBJECTIVE: Make masculine, feminine, and
neuter singular gender visible through useful nouns and attributive
adjectives. LEXICAL ANCHORS: nowy dom, nowa książka, nowe miasto. CARD
STRATEGY: Contrast one adjective across genders before widening the
lexicon. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Masculine subgenders, oblique cases,
plural agreement, and less productive ending classes. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Basic singular nominative agreement is productive across all
three genders. LEADS TO: PL-14, PL-16, PL-57.

PL-06 — Basic negation and yes/no questions

PREREQUISITES: PL-04. OBJECTIVE: Establish nie and natural yes/no
question formation, including czy where appropriate. CARD STRATEGY:
Contrast positive and negative predicates, then declarative and question
contexts. Do not imply that every spoken yes/no question requires czy.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Negative pronouns, genitive under negation,
scope-sensitive negation, and emphatic constructions. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Negation and basic questions work across several natural
utterance types. LEADS TO: PL-07, PL-12.

PL-07 — Existence and possession with być / mieć

PREREQUISITES: PL-03, PL-04, PL-06. OBJECTIVE: Establish common
existential and possession patterns with jest/są, nie ma, and mieć while
distinguishing their ordinary functions. CARD STRATEGY: Avoid forcing
one English “have/there is” pattern onto all Polish contexts.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full genitive paradigms, possessive pronouns, and
advanced existential word order. COMPLETION STANDARD: Several possessors
and concrete referents establish affirmative and negative
existence/possession naturally. LEADS TO: PL-21, PL-26.

PL-08 — Present tense: first productive verb patterns

PREREQUISITES: PL-02, PL-03. OBJECTIVE: Establish finite present forms
through a small coherent set of imperfective verbs. CARD STRATEGY:
Contrast persons and numbers systematically before adding more stem
alternations; allow natural subject omission. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED:
Perfective future interpretation, past tense, imperative, and dense
irregularity. COMPLETION STANDARD: Person/number marking is productive
across several verbs and natural short sentences. LEADS TO: PL-09–PL-11.

PL-09 — Present tense: additional patterns and common irregulars

PREREQUISITES: PL-08. OBJECTIVE: Broaden present-tense conjugation to
additional productive patterns and indispensable high-frequency
irregular or stem-changing verbs. CARD STRATEGY: Establish coherent
contrasts before exceptions; do not present a single undifferentiated
conjugation table. COMPLETION STANDARD: Major present endings and
selected high-frequency irregularities operate across varied contexts.
LEADS TO: PL-10, PL-13.

PL-10 — Early sentence synthesis

PREREQUISITES: PL-01–PL-09. OBJECTIVE: Combine identity, description,
negation, existence, possession, questions, and present actions with
fresh useful vocabulary. CARD STRATEGY: Keep Polish word order natural
and vary information focus only where the prompt remains exact-match
compatible. COMPLETION STANDARD: Short sentences use a growing lexicon
and all established patterns without dependence on a tiny anchor set.
LEADS TO: PL-11 onward.

STAGE 2 — SENTENCE TOOLS AND FIRST CASE FUNCTIONS

PL-11 — Question words

OBJECTIVE: Establish kto, co, gdzie, dokąd, skąd, kiedy, jak, dlaczego,
który and other high-value interrogatives progressively in natural
questions.

PL-12 — Negation with verbs

OBJECTIVE: Extend nie across verbal predicates and distinguish ordinary
verbal negation from nie ma and later negative-pronoun systems.

PL-13 — Infinitive complements and early modal constructions

OBJECTIVE: Establish common patterns with chcieć, móc, musieć, trzeba,
można and related meanings one distinction at a time.

PL-14 — Nominative plural: first noun and adjective patterns

OBJECTIVE: Establish common plural forms and adjective agreement while
keeping masculine-personal morphology initially controlled.

PL-15 — Masculine-personal versus non-masculine-personal plural: first
contrast

PREREQUISITES: PL-03, PL-05, PL-14. OBJECTIVE: Establish oni versus one
and the first visible agreement consequences through concrete
human/non-human groups. CARD STRATEGY: Begin with transparent
noun/adjective/pronoun contrasts; develop the system longitudinally
rather than as one exhaustive table. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full virile
plural declension and numeral consequences. COMPLETION STANDARD: The
learner can recognize that Polish plural agreement depends on
masculine-personal status, not simply plural number. LEADS TO: PL-34,
PL-57, PL-63.

PL-16 — Accusative singular: inanimate direct objects

OBJECTIVE: Establish prototypical direct objects, beginning with forms
that make nominative/accusative relationships transparent.

PL-17 — Accusative singular: masculine animacy

OBJECTIVE: Establish the genitive-like accusative of masculine animate
nouns through concrete contrasts. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full
masculine-personal plural and fine variation in animate/inanimate
lexical classification.

PL-18 — Locative singular with w and na

OBJECTIVE: Establish common location expressions and the locative case
in controlled spatial contexts. CARD STRATEGY: Teach lexical choice of w
versus na separately from the case ending itself.

PL-19 — Direction with do + genitive and na + accusative

OBJECTIVE: Establish common destination patterns and contrast them with
location using familiar places and motion vocabulary.

PL-20 — Instrumental accompaniment with z

OBJECTIVE: Introduce a high-value instrumental function through
transparent “with” relationships before broadening instrumental
morphology.

PL-21 — Genitive singular: absence, possession, source, and quantity

OBJECTIVE: Broaden the genitive from nie ma into several high-value
functions using controlled noun classes.

PL-22 — Dative singular: recipients and experiencers

OBJECTIVE: Establish recipients and common experiencer constructions
through high-frequency verbs and expressions.

PL-23 — Vocative: direct address

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive common vocative forms with names,
kinship terms, titles, and ordinary forms of address. CARD STRATEGY: Use
actual address contexts and contrast nominative where modern usage
permits or prefers it. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Rare, archaic, and
unstable variants. COMPLETION STANDARD: Common masculine and feminine
address patterns are established without implying that vocative and
nominative are freely interchangeable in every context. LEADS TO: PL-44,
PL-100.

PL-24 — Basic place, time, frequency, and manner adverbs

OBJECTIVE: Expand useful sentence tools such as tutaj, tam, teraz,
potem, dzisiaj, jutro, często, zawsze, dobrze, szybko in natural
positions.

PL-25 — First case and sentence synthesis

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Introduce each new case function with a limited
set of nouns whose forms expose the pattern. Widen gender and stem
classes after the function is visible. Do not ask for case names as
answers.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Principal early case functions are established
across multiple lexical representatives and can combine in short natural
sentences.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full plural oblique paradigms, exhaustive
prepositional government, rare declensions, and unrestricted word-order
variation.

STAGE 3 — PAST, FUTURE, AND FIRST ASPECT CONTRASTS

PL-26 — Imperfective past: gender and number

OBJECTIVE: Establish past forms with familiar imperfective verbs,
including gender distinctions in singular and the first plural agreement
contrasts.

PL-27 — Past of być and natural past predicates

OBJECTIVE: Establish byłem/byłam, byłeś/byłaś, był/była/było, byli/były
and related forms in natural identification, location, and description.

PL-28 — Imperfective future with być + infinitive

OBJECTIVE: Establish będę + infinitive across persons with familiar
imperfective verbs.

PL-29 — Imperfective future with być + past-like form

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary alternatives such as będę czytał/czytała,
including gender agreement, without claiming that all analytic variants
are pragmatically identical in every context.

PL-30 — First imperfective/perfective contrasts

OBJECTIVE: Introduce transparent aspectual pairs in contexts contrasting
ongoing/repeated/process meanings with completed bounded events.

PL-31 — Perfective future

OBJECTIVE: Establish that perfective non-past forms ordinarily refer to
future completed events and contrast them with imperfective present and
imperfective future.

PL-32 — Aspect in questions, negation, and expected result

OBJECTIVE: Begin context-sensitive aspect selection without reducing the
system to English tense labels.

PL-33 — Common nontransparent aspect partners

OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-frequency aspectual relationships that must be
learned substantially as lexical families.

PL-34 — Past-tense masculine-personal plural

PREREQUISITES: PL-15, PL-26. OBJECTIVE: Establish byli, robili, czytali
and corresponding non-masculine-personal forms such as były, robiły,
czytały in meaningful human and non-human group contrasts. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Plural past agreement reflects the core masculine-personal
distinction across several verb families.

PL-35 — Past/future/aspect synthesis

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: English prompts should provide enough temporal,
iterative, process, result, and participant-gender context to elicit one
intended aspect and form.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Aspect is visible across several verb families, and
past, imperfective future, and perfective future are structurally
established.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Exhaustive prefix semantics, fine discourse-level
aspect choice, and low-frequency aspectual derivation.

STAGE 4 — CASE SYSTEM IN USE

PL-36 — Accusative plural and masculine-personal distinctions

OBJECTIVE: Extend direct-object marking to plural noun phrases,
including masculine-personal forms and agreement.

PL-37 — Genitive plural

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive genitive plural patterns, then common
zero endings and irregularities in quantity, absence, negation, and
established prepositional functions.

PL-38 — Dative plural and agreement

OBJECTIVE: Extend recipient and experiencer patterns to plural noun
phrases.

PL-39 — Instrumental singular beyond accompaniment

OBJECTIVE: Establish means/instrument, predicate nominals after być
where required, and other high-value instrumental uses as distinct
constructions.

PL-40 — Instrumental plural and agreement

OBJECTIVE: Broaden instrumental morphology to plural noun phrases.

PL-41 — Locative plural

OBJECTIVE: Extend established location/aboutness functions to plural
forms and relevant prepositions.

PL-42 — Genitive under negation

OBJECTIVE: Establish the productive relationship between negated
transitive predicates and genitive objects while distinguishing contexts
and variation that do not fit a simplistic automatic conversion rule.

PL-43 — Core preposition government

OBJECTIVE: Establish frequent prepositions and their case frames in
semantic families rather than as an arbitrary list.

PL-44 — Personal pronouns in oblique cases

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency pronoun forms through already known
case functions, including stressed/full and clitic-like variants where
ordinary usage requires them.

PL-45 — Possessive and demonstrative pronouns through cases

OBJECTIVE: Develop mój, twój, nasz, wasz, swój, ten, tamten and related
forms through established case functions.

PL-46 — Vocative expansion and address patterns

OBJECTIVE: Broaden vocative morphology, adjective+noun address, names,
titles, kinship terms, and socially natural address.

PL-47 — Case synthesis in connected everyday sentences

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Contrast semantic roles, prepositions, endings,
animacy, and personhood in small families. Mix cases only after each
principal function is visible.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Productive singular and plural case forms plus
indispensable irregulars support natural use of all seven cases in
common functions.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Rare historical endings, marginal variants, and
idiomatic government better learned with later lexical items.

STAGE 5 — VERBAL STRUCTURE, IMPERATIVE, REFLEXIVES, AND MOTION

PL-48 — Imperative

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive imperative forms with familiar verbs
before widening irregularity and periphrastic commands.

PL-49 — Aspect choice in requests and commands

OBJECTIVE: Develop aspect in instructions, requests, warnings, and
negative imperatives through context rather than one English gloss.

PL-50 — Reflexive się: lexical and productive uses

OBJECTIVE: Establish common reflexive, reciprocal, middle-like,
anticausative, and impersonal uses while keeping lexicalized verbs
distinct from purely compositional reflexives.

PL-51 — Placement of się and unstressed pronouns

OBJECTIVE: Develop ordinary placement tendencies and restrictions
without turning flexible Polish word order into arbitrary movement.

PL-52 — Impersonal and indefinite-personal constructions

OBJECTIVE: Establish common clauses with -no/-to, się, third-person
forms, and other strategies that do not require an English-style
explicit subject.

PL-53 — iść / chodzić: movement on foot

OBJECTIVE: Establish current one-directional motion versus habitual,
repeated, or multidirectional motion through actual contexts.

PL-54 — jechać / jeździć: movement by transport

OBJECTIVE: Establish the corresponding transport contrast and its
lexical frames.

PL-55 — Destination, source, route, and motion case frames

OBJECTIVE: Integrate motion with established cases and prepositions,
including do, z/ze, od, przez, w, na and po as appropriate.

PL-56 — Prefixed motion verbs

OBJECTIVE: Develop common meanings such as arrival, departure, entry,
exit, approach, passage, and return with appropriate aspectual
relationships.

PL-57 — Additional motion families

OBJECTIVE: Expand to high-frequency movement verbs by usefulness,
keeping directionality, repetition, and lexical meaning distinct.

PL-58 — Carrying, leading, and transporting

OBJECTIVE: Establish common verbs such as nieść/nosić, prowadzić,
wieźć/wozić and their aspectual and argument-structure relationships.

PL-59 — Motion and aspect synthesis

COMPLETION STANDARD: Major combinations of means, direction, frequency,
prefix, case frame, and aspect can be recovered from context across
varied destinations and situations.

STAGE 6 — ADJECTIVES, PRONOUNS, COMPARISON, AND NUMBER

PL-60 — Full adjective declension

OBJECTIVE: Extend adjective agreement systematically across established
cases, genders, numbers, animacy, and masculine-personal distinctions.

PL-61 — Possessive, demonstrative, interrogative, and relative agreement

OBJECTIVE: Consolidate adjective-like pronoun paradigms through
meaningful noun phrases rather than isolated tables.

PL-62 — Comparative adjectives and adverbs

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive synthetic and analytic comparison,
suppletive common forms, and natural comparative constructions.

PL-63 — Superlatives

OBJECTIVE: Establish naj- and ordinary superlative/degree strategies
with appropriate syntax.

PL-64 — Cardinal numerals: one, two, three, four

OBJECTIVE: Establish the distinctive agreement and case behaviour of low
cardinals in high-value contexts.

PL-65 — Cardinal numerals: five and above

OBJECTIVE: Establish genitive-governing patterns and their interaction
with verb agreement progressively.

PL-66 — Masculine-personal numerals and plural personhood

OBJECTIVE: Develop forms and constructions such as dwaj/dwóch,
trzej/trzech and their syntactic consequences without presenting all
variants at once.

PL-67 — Collective numerals

OBJECTIVE: Introduce dwoje, troje and other common collective numerals
with the noun classes and meanings that license them.

PL-68 — Numerals in dates, time, prices, ages, and measurements

OBJECTIVE: Develop numbers in high-value real contexts while expanding
case behaviour progressively.

PL-69 — Ordinals and agreement

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinal morphology and agreement through dates,
sequence, floors, rankings, and other ordinary contexts.

PL-70 — Indefinite, negative, and universal pronouns

OBJECTIVE: Establish forms with ktoś/coś, nikt/nic, każdy, wszyscy and
related systems together with Polish negative concord.

PL-71 — Noun-phrase and quantification synthesis

COMPLETION STANDARD: Agreement, comparison, reference, personhood, and
quantity operate naturally across established case functions. Rare
numeral variants remain available for later lexical expansion.

STAGE 7 — COMPLEX SENTENCES AND INFORMATION STRUCTURE

PL-72 — Coordination

OBJECTIVE: Establish i, a, ale, albo, lub, czy and their principal
semantic and stylistic contrasts.

PL-73 — Core subordinate clauses

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency complement, causal, conditional, and
temporal links such as że, bo, ponieważ, jeśli/jeżeli, kiedy, gdy.

PL-74 — Relative który

OBJECTIVE: Establish antecedent agreement and clause-internal case one
role at a time.

PL-75 — Other relatives and correlatives

OBJECTIVE: Expand kto, co, gdzie, dokąd, skąd, kiedy, jak and related
relative/correlative structures.

PL-76 — Reported statements and indirect questions

OBJECTIVE: Establish common complement patterns and indirect-question
constructions, including czy and interrogative words.

PL-77 — Conditional mood with by

OBJECTIVE: Establish conditional morphology and meanings through clear
present/future hypothetical and counterfactual contexts.

PL-78 — Mobile conditional particles and person marking

OBJECTIVE: Develop forms such as zrobiłbym, gdybym, żebyś and the
placement and attachment behaviour of by-person endings in natural
constructions.

PL-79 — żeby and purpose/complement clauses

OBJECTIVE: Establish common purpose, desired-action, command-reporting,
and complement patterns while distinguishing them from że clauses.

PL-80 — Flexible word order and information structure

OBJECTIVE: Distinguish neutral orders from marked topic/focus
arrangements and show how context affects constituent placement.

PL-81 — Clitic ordering and prosodic placement

OBJECTIVE: Develop ordinary positioning of unstressed pronouns, się,
conditional particles, and related weak elements in natural sentences.

PL-82 — Negation scope and contrastive focus

OBJECTIVE: Develop meanings that cannot be captured by treating nie as a
simple word-level switch.

PL-83 — Clause and information-structure synthesis

CARD STRATEGY: English prompts must provide discourse context when
focus, word order, clitic placement, or conjunction choice affects the
intended Target.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Major clause types operate across varied pronoun
and case roles, with natural Polish information packaging visible.

STAGE 8 — EXPANDED ASPECT, VOICE, AND VERBAL DERIVATION

PL-84 — Aspect in repeated events, duration, and completed sequences

PL-85 — Aspect under negation, modal constructions, and infinitive
complements

PL-86 — Prefixes and lexical meaning

OBJECTIVE: Distinguish productive aspectual relationships from prefixes
that create substantially new lexical verbs.

PL-87 — Secondary imperfectives and derived aspect families

OBJECTIVE: Develop productive and high-frequency derived imperfectives
without implying that every aspect pair is mechanically predictable.

PL-88 — Habitual and iterative derivation

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive or common iterative/habitual
relationships where modern Polish maintains meaningful contrasts.

PL-89 — Passive with być / zostać + passive participle

OBJECTIVE: Establish state/result and eventive passive strategies
through natural contrasts.

PL-90 — Passive and impersonal się

OBJECTIVE: Compare passive-like, middle, impersonal, and active
alternatives according to natural Polish usage rather than English voice
preferences.

PL-91 — Passive participles and result/state distinctions

OBJECTIVE: Develop productive passive participial morphology and its
adjectival or resultative uses.

PL-92 — Active adjectival participles

OBJECTIVE: Establish forms in -ący as productive modifiers, including
agreement and their relationship to finite relative clauses.

PL-93 — Contemporary adverbial participles

OBJECTIVE: Establish present forms in -ąc and anterior forms in
-wszy/-łszy, their aspect restrictions, subject control, punctuation,
and register.

PL-94 — Verbal morphology synthesis in connected prose

COMPLETION STANDARD: Productive aspectual, voice, participial, and
derivational systems are represented across several verb families, while
lexicalized or stylistically restricted forms are distinguished from
ordinary productive usage.

STAGE 9 — WORD FORMATION, ORTHOGRAPHY, AND PRONUNCIATION

PL-95 — Productive noun, adjective, and verb derivation

OBJECTIVE: Develop useful word families and recurring affixes without
assuming semantic transparency.

PL-96 — Diminutives, augmentatives, and expressive derivation

OBJECTIVE: Establish common productive forms together with pragmatic,
emotional, and register effects.

PL-97 — Prefix meaning and lexicalization

OBJECTIVE: Revisit prefixes across lexical families, separating
productive meaning tendencies from conventional lexical meanings.

PL-98 — Palatalization and consonant alternations in morphology

OBJECTIVE: Systematize alternations already encountered in declension,
conjugation, derivation, and comparison.

PL-99 — ó / u and rz / ż

OBJECTIVE: Consolidate major spelling contrasts and morphologically
useful alternations through real word families without pretending that
all spellings are synchronically predictable.

PL-100 — ch / h and other orthographic contrasts

OBJECTIVE: Develop conventional spelling, productive alternations where
present, and lexical learning where spelling is not predictable.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1.  Unit completion establishes canonical coverage, not learner mastery.
2.  Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards
    increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3.  New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled
    lexical load. Vocabulary continues growing through every stage.
4.  Conventional contemporary Polish orthography, including required
    diacritics, applies from the first Target. InputHint is normally
    blank because Polish is typed directly in its own script.
5.  Pronunciation, keyboard familiarity, alphabet reference, and
    spelling explanation may be supported externally without replacing
    authentic Targets.
6.  Later cards progressively develop common words across senses,
    constructions, aspect families, case frames, collocations, idioms,
    derivational families, and register.
7.  The scheduler supplies extra repetition without changing canonical
    introduction order.
8.  Existing valid Polish cards remain valid if later reconciled with
    this syllabus. Fill meaningful prerequisite gaps by convergence
    rather than restarting or renumbering sound cards.
9.  Future amendments may split, merge, reorder, or refine units without
    renumbering existing stable card IDs.
10. Parallel units within a stage may interleave once their
    prerequisites are satisfied; unit numbers state the default
    canonical path.
11. Similarity to Russian, Ukrainian, Czech, Slovak, or another Slavic
    language may assist explanation and lexical selection, but Polish
    usage and structure remain authoritative.

AMENDMENT POLICY

Change this syllabus only when the canonical Polish curriculum changes.
Routine card generation and learner progress do not amend it.

An amendment should identify affected units, state the pedagogical
change, say whether it affects future cards only or requires
existing-card correction, increment the version, and preserve a concise
amendment record.
