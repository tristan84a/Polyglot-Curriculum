POLYGLOT ICELANDIC SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1
Status: Current
Charter version: 0.8
Encoding: UTF-8

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary
Icelandic in Polyglot. It was written after an initial 100-card seed and
uses that seed together with the German curriculum and other successful
Polyglot decks as design evidence.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a transcript. Icelandic morphology is
rich enough that many systems must develop longitudinally. A case form or
verb pattern may appear naturally before its formal unit when the card is
comprehensible.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND ORTHOGRAPHY

The production baseline is contemporary Standard Icelandic as used in
Iceland. Targets use ordinary Icelandic spelling from the first card
including á, ð, é, í, ó, ú, ý, þ, æ and ö. InputHint is normally blank.

Modern Icelandic is the target. Older Icelandic and Old Norse are historically
and structurally relevant and may later form a reading and historical strand
but should not displace contemporary usage.

COURSE-WIDE ICELANDIC PRINCIPLES

1. Use authentic Icelandic orthography immediately. Do not transliterate þ
   or ð or strip diacritics for beginner convenience.
2. Nouns have masculine, feminine or neuter gender. Gender is a persistent
   lexical property and should become visible through articles, pronouns,
   adjective agreement and inflection.
3. Icelandic has four cases: nominative, accusative, dative and genitive.
   Develop them through grammatical functions and government rather than
   requiring a complete declension table before useful sentences.
4. The ordinary definite article is usually suffixed to the noun. Teach
   forms such as húsið, maðurinn and konan from the opening rather than
   importing a German-style free article system.
5. The free definite article belongs to particular noun-phrase structures
   and should be introduced where natural rather than as the default
   translation of English "the."
6. There is no ordinary indefinite article. English a/an normally requires
   no Icelandic determiner.
7. Adjectives agree in gender, number and case and have strong and weak
   declensions. Predicative agreement provides an early entry point before
   full attributive declension.
8. Noun declension contains productive families and important irregular
   forms. Teach useful patterns but retain lexical information where forms
   are not safely predictable.
9. Personal pronouns distinguish three genders in the third-person plural:
   þeir, þær and þau. Mixed-gender reference normally uses neuter plural þau.
10. Finite verbs mark person and number. Present paradigms deserve systematic
    treatment where their cells expose genuine contrasts.
11. Strong and weak verbs are central lexical/morphological classes.
    Productive weak patterns should become clear without postponing common
    strong and irregular verbs.
12. Icelandic is fundamentally verb-second in main declarative clauses.
    Teach the finite verb as the second constituent rather than treating
    subject-first SVO as the underlying rule.
13. Yes/no questions commonly place the finite verb before the subject.
14. Negation with ekki interacts with clause architecture. Begin with clear
    main-clause placement and develop scope and subordinate placement later.
15. Subjects are not normally omitted merely because verb morphology makes
    person recoverable. Do not import pro-drop behavior from unrelated
    languages.
16. Verbs and prepositions govern cases. Case selection belongs with the
    lexical/constructional pattern and should be reinforced longitudinally.
17. Direct objects are frequently accusative but Icelandic also has
    lexically governed dative and genitive objects. Do not teach accusative
    as a universal object case.
18. Dative subjects and other non-nominative subject constructions are
    ordinary Icelandic grammar and deserve explicit development after the
    basic case system is intelligible.
19. Possession uses genitive noun phrases and possessive pronouns with
    important word-order and agreement behavior.
20. Reflexive sig and possessive sinn require antecedent-sensitive treatment
    and should not be collapsed into ordinary third-person pronouns.
21. The middle voice -st is productive and lexicalized in important ways.
    It is not merely a mechanical passive suffix.
22. Modern spoken and written Icelandic are close but register, pronunciation
    and usage still vary. Mature stages should develop natural colloquial
    language alongside formal literacy.
23. Vocabulary grows just in time. Rich morphology is not a reason to freeze
    the lexicon while paradigms are completed.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1–100

The seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- authentic Icelandic orthography;
- suffixed definite articles across three genders;
- predicative adjective gender agreement;
- indefinite noun phrases without an article;
- the personal-pronoun system including þeir/þær/þau;
- present vera through ordinary clauses;
- additional nouns and adjectives;
- present verbal morphology through vinna and tala;
- ekki negation;
- verb-first polar questions;
- hvað, hver and hvar;
- possession with eiga;
- direct objects through sjá;
- first visible nominative/accusative contrasts including
  maður → mann and maðurinn → manninn;
- vilja + infinitive.

The opening deliberately allows case to become visible before a complete
declension system is taught. This is the intended Polyglot model: case is
encountered through useful language and then systematized.

INITIAL STRUCTURAL ROADMAP

IS-01 — Core nouns and grammatical gender
Continue useful nouns across masculine, feminine and neuter classes. Treat
gender as lexical knowledge and begin exposing common declension families.

IS-02 — Suffixed definite article
Develop singular and plural definite forms across genders and cases. Relate
article endings to noun declension through examples rather than one giant
table.

IS-03 — Core adjectives and predicative agreement
Develop masculine, feminine and neuter predicative forms and plural
agreement using common adjectives.

IS-04 — Personal pronouns
Establish singular/plural subject pronouns including þeir/þær/þau and later
their oblique forms.

IS-05 — Vera
Develop present vera across person and number in identity, description and
location.

IS-06 — Present weak verbs
Establish productive weak present patterns with several useful verbs and
systematic person/number contrasts.

IS-07 — Common strong and irregular presents
Introduce high-frequency strong/irregular verbs early enough for ordinary
language to remain natural.

IS-08 — Main-clause V2
Generalize finite-verb second position beyond subject-first sentences by
fronting time, place and other constituents.

IS-09 — Ekki negation
Develop ordinary main-clause placement and later contrast subordinate-clause
placement and scope.

IS-10 — Polar and content questions
Establish verb-first yes/no questions and interrogatives including hvað,
hver, hvar, hvert, hvaðan, hvenær, hvernig and af hverju.

CASE AND NOUN-PHRASE SPINE

IS-11 — Accusative direct objects
Broaden accusative singular/plural forms and article/adjective agreement
through ordinary transitive clauses.

IS-12 — Dative foundation
Introduce recipients, instrumental/relational uses and high-frequency
dative-governing prepositions and verbs.

IS-13 — Genitive foundation
Develop possession and common genitive-governing constructions before
expanding to less frequent formal uses.

IS-14 — Case paradigms by noun class
Systematize representative strong and weak masculine, feminine and neuter
declensions after meaningful case functions are already familiar.

IS-15 — Definite article across cases
Develop suffixed article morphology together with the noun forms it attaches
to.

IS-16 — Indefinite and definite adjective declension
Introduce strong versus weak attributive adjective forms through natural
noun phrases and determiners.

IS-17 — Demonstratives
Develop þessi, sá and related gender/number/case paradigms through useful
reference.

IS-18 — Possessive pronouns
Develop minn, þinn, hans, hennar, okkar, ykkar and þeirra with natural
agreement and word order.

IS-19 — Reflexive possessive sinn
Contrast sinn with ordinary third-person possessives according to subject
reference.

IS-20 — Quantifiers and case
Develop allur, enginn, einhver, margur/margir, mikill and other useful
quantifiers with their agreement and case behavior.

VERBAL SPINE

IS-21 — Infinitives
Develop lexical infinitives and að + infinitive where required. Distinguish
bare infinitives after modal-like verbs from að-infinitives.

IS-22 — Weak past
Introduce major weak past formations through high-frequency verbs and
person/number forms.

IS-23 — Strong past
Develop ablaut patterns through useful strong verbs. Treat principal parts
as lexical information where necessary.

IS-24 — Perfect
Develop hafa + past participle for completed/relevant past situations and
contrast with simple past usage.

IS-25 — Pluperfect
Develop hafði + participle once perfect and past hafa are established.

IS-26 — Future reference
Teach ordinary present-tense future reference and munu + infinitive in its
actual contemporary functions rather than assuming English will maps
one-to-one.

IS-27 — Modal verbs
Develop vilja, geta, mega, þurfa, eiga and skulu in their principal modal
uses and syntactic patterns.

IS-28 — Imperative
Develop singular/plural commands and common irregular forms.

IS-29 — Present participle
Introduce -andi forms in their genuine adjectival/adverbial and lexical
uses.

IS-30 — Past participles and agreement
Develop participial morphology beyond the invariant perfect contexts where
agreement becomes relevant.

IS-31 — Passive
Introduce vera + participle and verða + participle constructions with
appropriate aspect/meaning contrasts.

IS-32 — Middle voice -st
Develop reciprocal, reflexive, passive-like and lexical -st verbs through
actual high-frequency items. Avoid assigning one mechanical meaning to -st.

IS-33 — Reflexive sig
Develop sig and its case forms with antecedent-sensitive examples.

IS-34 — Verb government
Build lexical families of accusative-, dative- and genitive-governing verbs.
The governing case is part of knowing the construction.

PREPOSITIONS AND SPACE

IS-35 — Accusative-governing prepositions
Introduce common prepositions in meanings that govern accusative.

IS-36 — Dative-governing prepositions
Develop high-frequency dative government through concrete spatial and
relational examples.

IS-37 — Alternating prepositions
Contrast motion/direction and location or other semantic distinctions where
the same preposition selects accusative versus dative.

IS-38 — Genitive-governing prepositions
Develop common genitive prepositions and fixed constructions.

IS-39 — Location and direction
Systematize hvar, hvert and hvaðan together with adverbs and prepositional
phrases for location, destination and source.

IS-40 — Place-name case and preposition patterns
Develop í/á and case selection with countries, towns, regions, institutions
and common locations as lexicalized usage requires.

NUMBER AND AGREEMENT

IS-41 — Nominative plurals
Develop common plural noun classes and adjective agreement.

IS-42 — Oblique plurals
Expand accusative, dative and genitive plural morphology longitudinally.

IS-43 — Cardinal numbers one to four
Develop gender/case inflection of einn, tveir, þrír and fjórir because these
forms encode genuine grammatical contrasts.

IS-44 — Higher cardinal numbers
Develop five and above together with counted noun behavior and ordinary
number expressions.

IS-45 — Ordinals
Develop ordinal agreement and uses in dates, rankings and sequences.

IS-46 — Dates and clock time
Build Icelandic conventions for dates, time, weekdays and schedules.

IS-47 — Age
Teach ordinary age expressions and relevant case/copular structures.

IS-48 — Comparison
Develop comparative and superlative adjective/adverb forms including common
irregulars.

CLAUSE ARCHITECTURE

IS-49 — Fronting within V2
Time, place, objects and other constituents in first position with the
finite verb retained second.

IS-50 — Adverb placement
Develop ekki and other sentence adverbs relative to finite/nonfinite verbs
and clause type.

IS-51 — Coordination
Develop og, en, eða, heldur and common coordination patterns.

IS-52 — Að complement clauses
Introduce finite complement clauses after verbs of saying, thinking,
knowing and perception.

IS-53 — Relative clauses
Develop sem relatives and the role of resumptive/prepositional structures
where natural.

IS-54 — Causal and purpose clauses
Develop af því að, vegna þess að, til að and related structures.

IS-55 — Temporal clauses
Develop þegar, áður en, eftir að, meðan and related sequencing.

IS-56 — Conditionals
Develop ef clauses from ordinary real conditions toward hypothetical and
counterfactual systems.

IS-57 — Subjunctive foundation
Introduce present and past subjunctive morphology through environments that
actually license or favor it rather than as an abstract parallel paradigm.

IS-58 — Reported speech and sequence
Develop tense/mood choices in reported thought and speech.

IS-59 — Non-nominative subjects
Develop dative and accusative subject constructions with high-frequency
predicates and preserve their syntactic subject behavior.

IS-60 — Impersonal constructions
Weather, necessity, evaluation and other impersonal patterns.

WORD FORMATION AND LEXICON

IS-61 — Compound nouns
Develop productive Icelandic compounding and linking behavior through common
lexical families.

IS-62 — Derivational suffixes
Develop productive noun/adjective/verb derivation through actual vocabulary.

IS-63 — Prefixes and particles
Develop productive and lexicalized prefixed forms without assuming complete
semantic predictability.

IS-64 — Strong-verb families
Use recurring ablaut relationships to support lexical organization after
enough verbs make the patterns useful.

IS-65 — Irregular noun families
Consolidate high-frequency nouns whose stems or endings require lexical
attention.

IS-66 — Adjective families
Develop irregular comparison, stem alternations and lexicalized adjective
constructions.

IS-67 — Verb-particle and idiomatic constructions
Broaden ordinary verb combinations and case-governed idioms.

REGISTER AND VARIATION

IS-68 — Everyday spoken Icelandic
Develop contractions, discourse patterns and ordinary conversational
choices while retaining standard orthographic Targets.

IS-69 — Formal written Icelandic
Develop denser noun phrases, formal connectives and structures common in
educated prose.

IS-70 — Pronunciation and spelling relationships
Address major phonological processes that are not transparent from spelling
where they materially aid comprehension. Orthography remains canonical.

IS-71 — Contemporary lexical innovation
Develop productive native coinages, borrowings and actual modern usage
without treating prescriptive preference as a complete description of the
language.

IS-72 — Regional and social variation
Introduce high-value contemporary variation when useful for comprehension.

IS-73 — Older Icelandic and Old Norse continuity
Build recognition of historically important forms and vocabulary only after
the modern system is secure. Historical comparison supports modern literacy
but is not the production baseline.

CARD-SPECIFIC RULES

1. EnglishPrompt must be unique and select one intended Icelandic Target.
2. Use ordinary Icelandic spelling including þ, ð and diacritics.
3. InputHint is normally blank.
4. Avoid commas in card fields where a natural prompt or Target can be
   written without them. CSV quoting should not be required for ordinary
   card generation.
5. English prompts should identify grammatical gender, case, number or
   discourse context when needed to select one exact Target.
6. Do not supply an Icelandic indefinite article where Icelandic naturally
   has none.
7. Definite noun cards should use the ordinary suffixed article unless the
   intended construction specifically requires a free article.
8. Case-sensitive prompts must distinguish forms when English alone would
   otherwise leave the intended Icelandic form ambiguous.
9. Do not assume every direct object is accusative; verb government remains
   authoritative.
10. Adjective cards should expose agreement economically. Fill a paradigm
    when each cell teaches a genuine contrast; otherwise reinforce known
    morphology through varied sentences.
11. Strong-verb principal parts may need lexical support but should not
    become detached terminology exercises.
12. Modern Icelandic is unmarked. Historical or explicitly regional material
    must be labelled when exact matching requires it.

DEVELOPMENT POLICY

The first 100 cards are the empirical opening. Correct genuine errors but do
not rebuild the seed merely to make it mirror later unit numbering.

The next expansion should broaden nominative plural and adjective agreement
while developing accusative objects, dative location/government, genitive
possession, additional present verbs, V2 fronting and question words.
Useful past forms may begin before the noun declension system is exhaustive.

German is a useful comparison for pacing, V2, strong/weak verbs, case and
Germanic vocabulary. Icelandic remains authoritative wherever its suffixed
article, four-case morphology, adjective agreement, pronouns, verb system
or syntax differs.

AMENDMENT NOTE

Version 0.1 is the first Icelandic syllabus. It was written after a 100-card
seed. The prototype showed that the shared Polyglot lexical seed works well
if Icelandic is allowed to expose its suffixed definite article, three-way
gender agreement, full third-person plural gender distinction, finite verb
morphology, verb-first questions and first accusative object forms almost
immediately.
