PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary Romanian in Polyglot. It governs systematic development after the standardized 15-card micro opening. Vocabulary grows continuously while grammar develops in a widening spiral rather than in sealed textbook blocks.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND WRITING

Contemporary standard Romanian as used in Romania is the unmarked reference variety. Targets use current Romanian orthography with diacritics including ă, â, î, ș and ț. InputHint is normally blank.

Moldovan regional usage and the Romanian of the Republic of Moldova belong to later comparison and variation work. They do not require a separate initial curriculum.

COURSE-WIDE CARD PRINCIPLES

1. Write natural ordinary English prompts and natural Romanian Targets. Do not make either language imitate the other for flashcard convenience.
2. Apply established course defaults silently. Add a qualifier only when the intended Target deliberately departs from a default and the distinction cannot reasonably be inferred from ordinary English or context.
3. Parenthetical qualifiers are sparse early scaffolding. Prefer natural contextual cues for number, gender, politeness and register.
4. Exact matching identifies the canonical natural Target selected for a card; it does not claim that no other Romanian wording could express the proposition.
5. Romanian permits subject pronoun omission. Establish pronouns explicitly early but increasingly prefer natural pro-drop when person is clear from the verb.
6. The postposed definite article is a central longitudinal system. Teach it through useful noun phrases rather than as a detached ending inventory.
7. Noun gender and number interact with article and adjective agreement. Romanian's traditional neuter class behaves broadly as masculine in the singular and feminine in the plural; establish this through lexical examples before abstract synthesis.
8. Direct-object marking with pe and clitic doubling develops progressively. Do not map pe mechanically onto English animacy or definiteness.
9. Dative and genitive forms overlap substantially but their functions should be introduced separately before synthesis.
10. The subjunctive with să is central to ordinary Romanian and should appear earlier than a Romance-language template based on infinitival complements might suggest.
11. Establish representative productive verb patterns clearly; thereafter reduce paradigm density and favor contextualized selective forms while vocabulary keeps expanding.
12. Avoid commas in card sentences whenever a natural alternative is available because the simple CSV workflow is comma-sensitive.
13. Check the entire live deck plus every new batch for duplicate IDs and duplicate EnglishPrompt values before committing.

MICRO DECK AND SEED TRANSITION

Cards 2500000-2500014 are the standardized 15-card micro opening. They preview the postposed definite article, adjective agreement, pro-drop, present verbal morphology, indefinite articles and numeral-driven plural forms. Preserve them as footholds rather than treating those syllabus units as complete.

Cards 2500015 onward form the syllabus-governed seed and continuing canonical deck.

STAGE 1 — CORE NOMINAL AND CLAUSAL SYSTEM

RO-01 — Core concrete vocabulary
OBJECTIVE: Build a varied useful lexical base with noun gender learned as part of lexical knowledge rather than through artificial gender labels in English prompts.

RO-02 — Personal pronouns and pro-drop
OBJECTIVE: Establish eu, tu, el, ea, noi, voi, ei and ele while showing that subject pronouns are normally omitted when person is clear and no contrast is intended.

RO-03 — Present of a fi
OBJECTIVE: Establish sunt, e/este, suntem and sunteți in natural identification and description. Introduce both neutral full and common short third-person forms appropriately.

RO-04 — Indefinite articles un and o
OBJECTIVE: Establish common singular indefinite noun phrases with masculine/neuter un and feminine o.

RO-05 — Noun gender and adjective agreement
OBJECTIVE: Establish masculine and feminine singular agreement plus representative neuter nouns through natural descriptions.

RO-06 — Postposed definite article: singular
OBJECTIVE: Establish common singular definite forms such as casa and bărbatul through familiar vocabulary. The micro deck already previews casa and mașina.

RO-07 — Basic negation
OBJECTIVE: Establish nu with copular and familiar verbal predicates.

RO-08 — Yes/no questions
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary Romanian polar questions without importing English auxiliary structure.

RO-09 — Plural nouns
OBJECTIVE: Establish representative productive plural patterns through several lexical classes rather than a single artificial rule.

RO-10 — Plural adjective agreement
OBJECTIVE: Combine plural nouns and adjectives while beginning the characteristic singular/plural behavior of neuter nouns.

RO-11 — Definite article: plural
OBJECTIVE: Establish common plural definite forms in natural noun phrases and sentences.

RO-12 — Demonstratives and early noun-phrase synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Introduce acesta/aceasta and common natural demonstrative patterns while recycling article, number and agreement.

STAGE 2 — FIRST VERBAL SYSTEM

RO-13 — Infinitive citation forms
OBJECTIVE: Establish short infinitive lexical forms such as a vedea, a lucra, a citi and a locui as dictionary anchors.

RO-14 — Present tense: first productive pattern
OBJECTIVE: Establish one representative regular present pattern with enough person/number coverage to make the system visible.

RO-15 — Additional present conjugation patterns
OBJECTIVE: Broaden present morphology across -a, -ea, -e and -i/-î verb classes with declining paradigm density.

RO-16 — High-frequency irregular present verbs
OBJECTIVE: Establish indispensable verbs such as a avea, a vrea, a putea and a merge through natural sentences.

RO-17 — Verbal negation and questions
OBJECTIVE: Extend nu and natural question formation across familiar present-tense predicates.

RO-18 — Question words
OBJECTIVE: Establish cine, ce, unde, când, cum, de ce and cât progressively in natural questions.

RO-19 — Present-tense synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine pro-drop, common present verbs, negation, questions and expanding vocabulary.

STAGE 3 — OBJECTS, PREPOSITIONS AND SĂ COMPLEMENTS

RO-20 — Unmarked direct objects
OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary inanimate and nonspecific direct objects before marked personal objects.

RO-21 — pe with personal direct objects
OBJECTIVE: Introduce pe through clear human and personal-reference contexts without reducing the system to a simple animate-object rule.

RO-22 — Accusative clitic pronouns
OBJECTIVE: Establish mă, te, îl, o, ne, vă, îi and le progressively through useful clauses.

RO-23 — Clitic doubling
OBJECTIVE: Introduce common doubling patterns once both pe-marking and clitic objects have independent footholds.

RO-24 — Core prepositions
OBJECTIVE: Establish în, la, din, de, cu, pentru, fără and other high-value prepositions through concrete relations.

RO-25 — Location, destination and source
OBJECTIVE: Build common place expressions with familiar motion and location verbs.

RO-26 — să + present subjunctive
OBJECTIVE: Establish the central Romanian complement pattern after wanting, ability/possibility, necessity and related predicates.

RO-27 — a vrea să
OBJECTIVE: Develop ordinary want constructions through multiple persons and lexical verbs.

RO-28 — trebuie să and modal expressions
OBJECTIVE: Establish necessity and other high-frequency impersonal/modal constructions.

RO-29 — First object/complement synthesis
OBJECTIVE: Combine objects, pe, clitics, prepositions and să complements in controlled natural sentences.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Unit completion establishes canonical coverage rather than learner mastery.
2. Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3. New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled lexical load while vocabulary continues growing.
4. Stable live card IDs are not renumbered merely because syllabus organization changes.
5. Previewing a later construction does not move the Manifest frontier.
6. Parallel units may interleave once prerequisites are satisfied.
