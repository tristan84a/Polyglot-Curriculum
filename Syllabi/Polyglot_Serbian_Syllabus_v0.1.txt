PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary
Standard Serbian in Polyglot. It was written after an initial 100-card
Cyrillic seed. Russian and Polish are useful Slavic reference
implementations but Serbian structure and usage remain authoritative.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a transcript. Vocabulary and
previously introduced grammar continue to develop longitudinally.

REFERENCE VARIETY SCRIPT AND PRONUNCIATION

The initial production baseline is contemporary Standard Serbian as used in
Serbia with Ekavian forms as the unmarked early target. Standard Ijekavian
Serbian is a legitimate standard variant and receives an explicit later
strand rather than being treated as an error.

Cyrillic is the canonical script for the initial deck and syllabus. Serbian
Latin script is introduced later as parallel literacy because both scripts
are in ordinary contemporary use. Targets should not mix scripts
accidentally.

Ordinary Serbian spelling does not mark lexical pitch accent. Canonical
Targets therefore use normal unaccented orthography. Pronunciation and the
four-accent system may be developed separately without adding accent marks
to ordinary Target forms.

COURSE-WIDE SERBIAN PRINCIPLES

1. Begin with a small reusable seed and move quickly into productive
   sentences in authentic Cyrillic.
2. Serbian has masculine feminine and neuter gender. Adjectives and other
   agreeing words develop across gender number and case.
3. Case is longitudinal. Establish useful functions in controlled examples
   before widening paradigms.
4. Animacy matters especially in masculine accusative forms and should
   emerge through visible contrasts.
5. Serbian normally permits subject omission. Pronouns are taught explicitly
   but neutral finite clauses should omit them when person is recoverable.
6. Present forms of бити are overt clitics in ordinary copular clauses.
   Their placement belongs to the broader clitic system.
7. Enclitics are structurally central. Auxiliary forms pronoun clitics and
   related elements require progressive work on second-position clustering
   and natural ordering.
8. Negation with ordinary verbs uses не. The negative present of бити uses
   fused forms such as нисам and није.
9. Да ли is a productive neutral question strategy. Other question patterns
   and clitic-sensitive alternatives can be added after the opening.
10. Present-tense conjugation should be learned through productive classes
    plus high-frequency irregular verbs rather than one giant table.
11. Aspect is lexical and grammatical. Imperfective/perfective partners
    develop longitudinally across past future infinitival and imperative
    contexts.
12. Serbian has seven cases in the standard descriptive system:
    nominative genitive dative accusative vocative instrumental and
    locative. Case names organize the syllabus but cards should primarily
    teach meaningful forms and constructions.
13. Prepositions and case government are learned together through concrete
    contrasts such as у + locative for location and у + accusative for
    destination.
14. The vocative remains productive in direct address and deserves ordinary
    usage examples rather than treatment as a historical curiosity.
15. Possessives agree adjectivally with the possessed noun. Genitive
    possession and dative possessive constructions are developed where
    natural.
16. Serbian distinguishes short and long adjective forms in parts of the
    paradigm and usage. Introduce the distinction through actual contexts
    rather than forcing an English article analogy.
17. Past tense is built with the l-participle and present clitic forms of
    бити. Gender and number agreement are therefore integral to early past
    usage.
18. Future I uses forms of хтети with an infinitive and has important
    orthographic/clitic behavior. Teach natural written forms and sentence
    structure.
19. Future II and the conditional are later finite systems with their own
    subordinate and modal functions.
20. The infinitive and да + present overlap in important domains. Contemporary
    Serbian usage should be taught directly; do not impose Croatian or
    Bulgarian preferences onto Serbian.
21. Motion verbs and prefixes develop in stages with aspect direction and
    lexical meaning.
22. Reflexive се is both a clitic and part of numerous lexical grammatical
    constructions. Reflexive reciprocal impersonal and passive-like uses
    should be distinguished by examples.
23. Word order is flexible but not arbitrary. Information structure clitic
    placement focus rhythm and discourse all constrain natural ordering.
24. Comparative and superlative morphology should include productive forms
    and frequent irregulars.
25. Numbers interact with noun form and case in ways that require dedicated
    development rather than English-style plural substitution.
26. Serbian derivation and verbal prefixes are major sources of vocabulary
    growth and aspectual contrast.
27. Ekavian and Ijekavian differences should be labelled when exact matching
    would otherwise allow more than one standard answer.
28. Bosnian Croatian and Montenegrin are extremely close reference varieties
    but are not silently substituted for Serbian targets. Shared material
    may be reused where genuinely identical.
29. Contemporary broadly neutral Serbian precedes literary historical and
    strongly regional material.
30. Vocabulary grows just in time. Similarity to Russian Polish or other
    Slavic languages is useful evidence but never overrides Serbian usage.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1-100

The seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- Serbian Cyrillic from the first card;
- demonstrative identification with ово је;
- masculine feminine and neuter adjective agreement;
- personal pronouns followed by natural subject omission;
- present clitic forms of бити in location and description;
- negative бити through није;
- да ли polar questions;
- present-tense читати and радити;
- где шта and ко;
- ordinary verbal negation;
- first direct-object accusatives;
- имати for possession;
- хтети with да + present;
- у + locative for location versus у + accusative for destination;
- да and не as yes/no responses.

The opening deliberately uses Ekavian forms and Cyrillic only.

CARD-SPECIFIC RULES

1. EnglishPrompt must be unique and select one intended Serbian Target.
2. Use Serbian Cyrillic for the canonical early Target.
3. InputHint is normally blank.
4. Avoid commas in card fields where a natural alternative exists.
5. Use Ekavian as the unmarked early production target. Label Ijekavian when
   it is specifically being tested.
6. Do not add Latin-script alternatives to Cyrillic Targets. Latin literacy
   receives its own later cards.
7. Use explicit subject pronouns when teaching pronouns or when discourse
   motivates them. Otherwise model natural subject omission.
8. Mark second-person number/register in English prompts when required.
9. Case-sensitive prompts must provide enough meaning to select one form.
10. Do not turn case names into the main learning task. Morphological prompts
    such as “house (accusative)” may be used sparingly when they clarify an
    otherwise invisible contrast before sentence reuse.
11. Aspect prompts must provide enough temporal/result context to select one
    intended verb.
12. Clitic cards must stand independently and not rely on adjacency.
13. Do not treat да + present as a mechanical replacement for every
    infinitive or vice versa.
14. Ordinary Targets do not carry pitch-accent marks.
15. Preserve Serbian letters exactly including ј љ њ ћ ђ џ.
16. Regional related-language or historical forms must be labelled when
    exact matching would otherwise imply they are the neutral Serbian target.

DEVELOPMENT POLICY

The first 100 cards are the empirical opening. Correct genuine errors but do
not rebuild it merely to match later unit numbering.

The next expansion should broaden plural and accusative morphology while
developing present conjugation across additional verb classes; genitive
dative instrumental and locative functions; possession and existence;
pronoun clitics; and the first aspect pairs. Past-time language may begin
once the l-participle and бити clitics are sufficiently clear. It need not
wait for exhaustive case paradigms.

Russian and Polish are useful Slavic comparison points. Serbian clitic
placement aspect case usage Ekavian/Ijekavian variation and infinitive versus
да + present remain authoritative.

AMENDMENT NOTE

Version 0.1 is the first Serbian syllabus. It was written after a 100-card
Cyrillic seed. The prototype confirms that the shared Polyglot opening
transfers well while immediately exposing specifically Serbian features:
overt present copular clitics; pro-drop; three-gender agreement; да ли
questions; accusative marking; and the location/destination contrast with
у + locative versus у + accusative.
