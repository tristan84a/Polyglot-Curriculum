PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for French in
Polyglot. It specifies what is taught, its dependency order, and how
major concepts should be represented in cards. It does not record
progress. Routine card generation therefore does not amend this
document.

Vocabulary acquisition is continuous. Grammar units define structural
progression but do not freeze vocabulary. Units have no fixed card
quota; a unit is complete when its canonical sequence satisfies its
completion standard.

TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED — A form, word, construction, or distinction has begun to be
demonstrated.

ESTABLISHED — The canonical curriculum has demonstrated the item
sufficiently for its basic operation to be assumed later. This describes
the curriculum, not learner performance.

REUSABLE — An established item or structure may appear naturally in
later cards without being the principal teaching objective.

LEXICAL ANCHOR — A word deliberately selected to make a grammatical or
semantic pattern visible. Anchors do not limit additional vocabulary.

CANONICAL SEQUENCE — The static pedagogical ordering of cards and
concepts. Scheduling frequency may adapt to performance; curriculum
order does not.

COMPLETION STANDARD — The coverage required before a unit’s principal
concept may be treated as established.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED — Material postponed because it would obscure the
current pattern, depends on later knowledge, or deserves separate
treatment.

COURSE-WIDE PRINCIPLES

1.  Vocabulary grows throughout all stages; grammar is not demonstrated
    indefinitely with a tiny closed vocabulary.
2.  When grammar is new, lexical novelty is initially restrained enough
    that the pattern remains visible. Lexical freedom increases as the
    pattern becomes established.
3.  Productive rules precede exceptions and competing paradigms.
4.  Grammar is normally learned through controlled examples rather than
    terminology questions.
5.  A lexical item may appear alone before becoming reusable in
    sentences.
6.  Established grammar may be combined freely in later units.
7.  French orthography, accents, agreement, punctuation and
    capitalization are part of the target.
8.  Common contemporary French precedes literary, archaic and unusually
    formal structures.
9.  Neutral, informal and formal usage are eventually distinguished
    explicitly.
10. Metropolitan French provides the initial grammatical reference
    point; important Canadian and other major francophone usage is later
    taught explicitly.

OPENING PACING DIRECTIVE

The historical early French card sequence is an approved Polyglot pacing
model. Begin with the compact seed maison, homme, femme, enfant, grand,
petit, bon, mauvais, then move almost immediately into article-bearing
phrases, adjective agreement, être, and short sentences. Additional nouns,
adjectives, pronouns, and verbs should arrive in small reusable increments.

FR-01 and FR-02 therefore do not require large standalone inventories
before FR-03 onward begins. Their completion standards are satisfied
initially by a small productive seed; lexical breadth continues
longitudinally. The historical first roughly 50–60 cards may be reused
closely where still consistent with the current syllabus. Later historical
choices such as very early negation or inversion questions are not made
canonical merely by this pacing endorsement.

STAGE 1 — BOOTSTRAP

FR-01 — Core concrete nouns

PREREQUISITES: None. OBJECTIVE: Establish a useful stock of highly
concrete nouns while making gender and article-bearing noun phrases
visible. LEXICAL ANCHORS: maison, homme, femme, enfant, livre, voiture,
chat, chien. CARD STRATEGY: Begin with high-frequency imageable nouns;
reuse them quickly in descriptive and verbal sentences. VOCABULARY
DEVELOPMENT: Add concrete everyday nouns that support many later
constructions. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Large thematic lists, rare
synonyms and specialist vocabulary. COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough varied
nouns exist to support early sentences without constant lexical
repetition. LEADS TO: FR-03, FR-04, FR-06 and later sentence
construction.

FR-02 — Core adjectives

PREREQUISITES: FR-01 beginning noun stock. OBJECTIVE: Introduce common
descriptions and prepare adjective agreement. CORE PATTERN: Adjectives
may vary for gender and number. LEXICAL ANCHORS: grand, petit, bon,
mauvais, nouveau, vieux. CARD STRATEGY: Establish useful meanings before
systematic inflection; reuse adjectives across nouns. VOCABULARY
DEVELOPMENT: Add high-frequency physical and evaluative adjectives.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Fine placement distinctions and irregular
comparison. COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough adjective variety exists to
demonstrate agreement without relying on one or two words. LEADS TO:
FR-04, FR-06, comparison and advanced adjective placement.

FR-03 — Gender and definite/indefinite articles

PREREQUISITES: FR-01. OBJECTIVE: Establish le/la/l’/les and un/une/des
and make gender a persistent lexical property. CORE PATTERN: le, la, l’,
les; un, une, des. CARD STRATEGY: Contrast masculine/feminine and
singular/plural in ordinary phrases and sentences; introduce elision
naturally. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: New nouns continue reinforcing
article/gender patterns. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Partitives, à/de
contractions, advanced omission. COMPLETION STANDARD: All basic definite
and indefinite forms appear across gender and number. LEADS TO: FR-04,
FR-18, FR-21.

FR-04 — Basic adjective agreement and placement

PREREQUISITES: FR-01–03. OBJECTIVE: Establish basic gender/number
agreement and normal placement. CORE PATTERN: Regular -e feminine and -s
plural first. CARD STRATEGY: Establish the regular pattern before
irregularities; contrast the same adjective across noun genders/numbers.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Continue noun/adjective growth. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Rare irregular forms and subtle position-dependent meaning.
COMPLETION STANDARD: All four basic agreement combinations occur with
multiple nouns/adjectives. LEADS TO: FR-06, FR-72, FR-124.

FR-05 — Subject pronouns

PREREQUISITES: Minimal vocabulary. OBJECTIVE: Establish je, tu, il,
elle, on, nous, vous, ils, elles. CARD STRATEGY: Teach meaning and
social/number distinctions, especially tu/vous and practical on; reuse
immediately with verbs. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Stressed pronouns and
advanced discourse uses of on. COMPLETION STANDARD: All subject pronouns
can support later controlled verb examples. LEADS TO: FR-06 onward.

FR-06 — Être: identity and description

PREREQUISITES: FR-02–05. OBJECTIVE: Establish present être through
identity, classification and description. CORE PATTERN: suis, es, est,
sommes, êtes, sont. CARD STRATEGY: Build the paradigm through meaningful
sentences; mix persons after systematic introduction. VOCABULARY
DEVELOPMENT: Occupations, identities, nationalities and descriptions may
grow cautiously. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Systematic c’est/il est
contrast, compound tenses and passive. COMPLETION STANDARD: All present
forms occur in ordinary sentences and later mixed contexts. LEADS TO:
FR-10, FR-27 and later constructions.

FR-07 — Avoir: possession and basic expressions

PREREQUISITES: FR-05 and basic noun phrases. OBJECTIVE: Establish
present avoir in possession and common expressions. CORE PATTERN: ai,
as, a, avons, avez, ont. CARD STRATEGY: Begin with possession, then a
limited set of common avoir expressions. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT:
Possessions, family and basic physical-state vocabulary. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Passé composé auxiliary use until FR-46. COMPLETION STANDARD:
All present forms are demonstrated and reused. LEADS TO: FR-25, FR-46.

FR-08 — Basic negation with ne…pas

PREREQUISITES: FR-06–07 and usable verbs. OBJECTIVE: Establish standard
basic negation and finite-verb placement. CORE PATTERN: ne/n’ + finite
verb + pas. CARD STRATEGY: Pair affirmative/negative meanings across
subjects and verbs; retain standard written ne. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED:
jamais, plus, rien, personne and informal ne-dropping. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Negation occurs across multiple subjects/verbs including
elision. LEADS TO: FR-33 and later tense-specific negation.

STAGE 2 — PRESENT-TENSE FOUNDATION

FR-09 — Regular -er verbs

PREREQUISITES: FR-05 and basic sentence structure. OBJECTIVE: Establish
the principal productive present -er paradigm. CORE PATTERN: -e, -es,
-e, -ons, -ez, -ent. LEXICAL ANCHORS: parler plus at least one other
useful regular -er verb. CARD STRATEGY: Introduce infinitives, singular
then plural forms; use additional verbs to demonstrate productivity;
later mix persons. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Add useful verbs and
compatible nouns/adverbs without obscuring morphology. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Spelling-change subclasses until the regular paradigm is
secure. COMPLETION STANDARD: All person/number forms occur with multiple
verbs and in mixed sentences. LEADS TO: FR-10 and all later finite-verb
work.

FR-10 — Present-tense subject–verb agreement

PREREQUISITES: FR-05–09. OBJECTIVE: Generalize the relationship between
grammatical subject and finite verb. CARD STRATEGY: Vary pronoun and
lexical-noun subjects; mix être, avoir and regular verbs. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Agreement is demonstrated with pronouns and noun subjects
across number. LEADS TO: All later verb paradigms.

FR-11 — High-frequency irregular verbs I: aller, faire

PREREQUISITES: FR-09–10. OBJECTIVE: Introduce indispensable irregular
paradigms without weakening the regular model. LEXICAL ANCHORS: aller,
faire. CARD STRATEGY: Establish each separately before mixing; build
useful motion/activity expressions. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Places and
everyday activities. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Futur proche as a systematic
tense until FR-62. COMPLETION STANDARD: All common present forms of both
verbs are demonstrated and reused. LEADS TO: FR-62 and present
synthesis.

FR-12 — Regular -ir verbs: finir-type

PREREQUISITES: FR-05, FR-09–10. OBJECTIVE: Establish the productive
present-tense finir-type -ir paradigm. CORE PATTERN: finis, finis,
finit, finissons, finissez, finissent. LEXICAL ANCHORS: finir, choisir.
CARD STRATEGY: Introduce one infinitive before finite forms; establish
singular then plural forms; use a second verb to prove productivity; mix
only after systematic presentation. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Continue
high-frequency vocabulary; keep earliest examples transparent, then
increase lexical novelty. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: partir, sortir, dormir,
servir, venir and other non-finir-type -ir verbs. COMPLETION STANDARD:
All six forms appear; at least two verbs demonstrate productivity; -iss-
plural is clear; forms are reused outside paradigm order. LEADS TO:
FR-15, FR-16 and later tenses.

FR-13 — Regular -re verbs

PREREQUISITES: FR-09–12. OBJECTIVE: Establish a principal productive -re
present pattern. CORE PATTERN: A transparent model such as vendre.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: vendre plus a compatible reinforcing verb. CARD
STRATEGY: Build the paradigm systematically and demonstrate transfer.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Highly irregular -re verbs such as prendre.
COMPLETION STANDARD: The basic pattern appears with more than one
compatible verb. LEADS TO: FR-14–16.

FR-14 — High-frequency irregular verbs II: venir, prendre, voir

PREREQUISITES: Established present agreement and regular paradigms.
OBJECTIVE: Add indispensable irregular verbs while preserving distinct
paradigms. CARD STRATEGY: Introduce one paradigm at a time; mix only
after each is visible. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Motion, handling and
perception vocabulary. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: An undifferentiated
“irregular verb list.” COMPLETION STANDARD: Each paradigm has practical
person/number coverage in natural sentences. LEADS TO: FR-15–16.

FR-15 — Other common irregular present-tense verbs

PREREQUISITES: FR-14. OBJECTIVE: Expand the usable present system with
high-frequency irregular/stem-changing verbs. CARD STRATEGY: Organize
genuine families where possible; otherwise establish high-value verbs
individually. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Continuous. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED:
Low-frequency literary/specialist irregularities. COMPLETION STANDARD: A
practical core supports unrestricted basic present sentences. LEADS TO:
FR-16.

FR-16 — Present-tense synthesis

PREREQUISITES: FR-09–15. OBJECTIVE: Integrate regular and irregular
present forms without signaling conjugation class. CARD STRATEGY: Mix
persons, lexical subjects, negation and increasingly varied vocabulary.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Increase breadth relative to earliest morphology
units. COMPLETION STANDARD: Present tense functions as reusable
background grammar. LEADS TO: All subsequent stages.

STAGE 3 — BUILDING THE SIMPLE SENTENCE

FR-17 — Direct objects

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary transitive sentences and subject/object
distinction through use. CARD STRATEGY: Use clear verb/object
combinations and vary both arguments. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Strong
opportunity for concrete nouns and transitive verbs. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Object pronouns until FR-37. COMPLETION STANDARD:
Direct-object noun phrases are routine in affirmative/negative
sentences.

FR-18 — Partitive articles

OBJECTIVE: Establish du, de la, de l’, des for unspecified quantities.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: Common foods, drinks and materials. CARD STRATEGY:
Contrast countable and mass-like contexts naturally. VOCABULARY
DEVELOPMENT: Food, drink, materials and consumption. DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDED: Advanced article omission. COMPLETION STANDARD: All forms
occur in natural contexts.

FR-19 — Quantity and expressions with de

OBJECTIVE: Establish beaucoup de, peu de, assez de, trop de and
quantity + de. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Numbers, containers, measures and
consumption. COMPLETION STANDARD: Several common quantity constructions
work across noun types.

FR-20 — Prepositions of place

OBJECTIVE: Build common spatial relations: dans, sur, sous, devant,
derrière, près de, loin de, etc. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Places, rooms,
furniture and geography. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Abstract idiomatic
prepositional uses. COMPLETION STANDARD: A practical spatial system
supports ordinary location sentences.

FR-21 — À and de with articles

OBJECTIVE: Establish au, aux, du, des alongside à la/de la/à l’/de l’.
CARD STRATEGY: Use destinations, locations, origins and associations
naturally. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Every idiomatic verb-preposition
pairing. COMPLETION STANDARD: Contracted/uncontracted forms are
routinely selected.

FR-22 — Basic adverbs

OBJECTIVE: Add common manner, frequency, time and degree adverbs.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: bien, mal, souvent, toujours, parfois, vite, lentement,
déjà. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Fine compound-tense placement. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Several major semantic classes are reusable.

FR-23 — Possessive determiners

OBJECTIVE: Establish mon/ma/mes through leur/leurs and the distinction
between possessor and possessed-noun agreement. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT:
Family, body and possessions. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Possessive pronouns
until FR-78. COMPLETION STANDARD: All possessor persons and major noun
combinations appear.

FR-24 — Demonstrative determiners

OBJECTIVE: Establish ce, cet, cette, ces. DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED:
Demonstrative pronouns until FR-77. COMPLETION STANDARD: All basic forms
are established.

FR-25 — Numbers, age, dates and time

OBJECTIVE: Provide functional numbers and calendar/time language,
including avoir + age. CARD STRATEGY: Build number formation
systematically in practical contexts. VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Days,
months, dates and time periods. COMPLETION STANDARD: Everyday numerical,
age, date and time expressions are usable.

FR-26 — Il y a and existence/location

OBJECTIVE: Establish il y a as the ordinary existential construction.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Past/future forms until those tense systems
exist. COMPLETION STANDARD: Existence statements are reusable without
treating il as referential.

FR-27 — C’est / ce sont versus il/elle est

OBJECTIVE: Establish the major practical distinction between
identification/classification and personal-subject description. CARD
STRATEGY: Controlled contrasts rather than rules alone. VOCABULARY
DEVELOPMENT: Professions, identities and descriptions. COMPLETION
STANDARD: Canonical contexts are repeatedly contrasted.

FR-28 — Simple sentence synthesis

OBJECTIVE: Integrate expanded simple sentences before questions,
commands and pronoun substitution. CARD STRATEGY: Mix articles, objects,
adverbs, prepositions, possession, demonstration, numbers and existence.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Accelerate everyday vocabulary while grammar is
familiar. COMPLETION STANDARD: Simple declaratives support substantial
lexical variety.

STAGE 4 — QUESTIONS, COMMANDS AND NEGATION

FR-29 Yes/no questions: intonation Objective: ordinary yes/no questions
using declarative order and interrogative intonation/punctuation.

FR-30 Est-ce que questions Objective: establish est-ce que as a
productive neutral question frame.

FR-31 Basic question words Objective: establish qui, que/qu’est-ce que,
où, quand, pourquoi, comment, combien and other high-value
interrogatives one function at a time.

FR-32 Inversion Objective: establish standard inversion and its register
relative to earlier question strategies.

FR-33 Expanded negation Objective: add jamais, plus, rien, personne and
other high-frequency negative constructions, individually before mixing.

FR-34 Affirmative imperative Objective: establish practical tu, nous and
vous commands.

FR-35 Negative imperative Objective: combine imperative structure with
established negation.

FR-36 Question and command synthesis Objective: mix question strategies,
question words, negation and commands in realistic exchanges; expand
conversational vocabulary.

STAGE 5 — OBJECT PRONOUNS AND PRONOMINAL VERBS

FR-37 Direct-object pronouns Objective: establish me, te, le, la, nous,
vous, les before the finite verb; begin by replacing familiar noun
objects.

FR-38 Indirect-object pronouns Objective: establish common indirect
personal objects, especially lui and leur, in contrast with direct
objects.

FR-39 Y Objective: establish y first for concrete place reference, then
appropriate à-complements.

FR-40 En Objective: establish en for de-complements, quantities and
partitive/indefinite reference.

FR-41 Multiple object pronouns Objective: establish common pronoun
ordering incrementally rather than as one ordering chart.

FR-42 Reflexive/pronominal verbs Objective: establish reflexive pronouns
and common pronominal verbs; distinguish genuinely reflexive meanings
from lexicalized pronominal verbs. Vocabulary: daily routine and
personal care.

FR-43 Pronouns with commands Objective: establish object-pronoun
placement/forms in affirmative and negative imperatives.

FR-44 Object-pronoun synthesis Objective: integrate direct, indirect, y,
en, reflexive and common multiple-pronoun structures until reusable.

STAGE 6 — PAST TIME I: PASSÉ COMPOSÉ

FR-45 Regular past participles Objective: establish -é, -i and -u
patterns using known verbs.

FR-46 Passé composé with avoir Objective: establish auxiliary avoir +
past participle as the principal compound-past pattern.

FR-47 Common irregular past participles Objective: add high-frequency
irregular participles, prioritizing already known verbs.

FR-48 Passé composé with être Objective: establish common être-auxiliary
verbs as a practical lexical set.

FR-49 Agreement with être Objective: systematically contrast participle
gender/number agreement.

FR-50 Passé composé of pronominal verbs Objective: combine established
pronominal verbs with compound-past formation.

FR-51 Negation and questions in the passé composé Objective: apply
established question/negation systems to compound verbs.

FR-52 Past-participle agreement with preceding direct objects Objective:
establish the principal avoir-agreement rule using transparent
pronoun/object contrasts.

FR-53 Passé composé synthesis Objective: make completed past events
reusable background grammar while expanding event vocabulary.

STAGE 7 — PAST TIME II: IMPARFAIT AND NARRATION

FR-54 Formation of the imparfait — productive nous-stem formation,
including être. FR-55 Past states and continuing situations — associate
imparfait with states/background circumstances. FR-56 Habitual/repeated
past actions — develop habitual past with suitable time expressions.
FR-57 Background description — use imparfait for setting, weather,
appearance and circumstance. FR-58 Passé composé versus imparfait —
build the aspectual contrast through paired contexts, not English
substitution rules. FR-59 Interrupted and simultaneous actions — extend
the aspectual contrast to interacting events. FR-60 Plus-que-parfait —
establish anteriority to a past reference point. FR-61 Past-time
narrative synthesis — integrate all three systems in connected
narratives.

STAGE 19 — MATURE LANGUAGE COMPETENCE / LONG TAIL

FR-175 Regional variation commonly encountered by educated speakers.
FR-176 Metropolitan, Canadian and other major francophone usage. FR-177
Generational and contemporary usage. FR-178 Formal and literary
vocabulary. FR-179 Historical vocabulary still encountered in modern
texts. FR-180 Specialized vocabulary of broad cultural relevance. FR-181
Rare but reasonably educated-adult vocabulary. FR-182 Idiomatic and

COMMON OBJECTIVE Approach the practical asymptote of the Polyglot
definition of a complete modern language curriculum: vocabulary,
constructions, idioms, registers and cultural-linguistic distinctions a

CARD STRATEGY Selection becomes increasingly frequency-, usefulness-,
register- and exposure-sensitive. Mature grammar is assumed.

COMPLETION STANDARD There is intentionally no absolute terminal word
count. FR-183 is expandable. “Complete” describes direction and scope,
not exhaustion of every possible French word.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1.  Unit completion means its canonical sequence adequately demonstrates
    its objective; it does not claim learner mastery.
2.  Once established, grammar becomes reusable unless temporary
    simplification is pedagogically useful.
3.  Vocabulary introduced at any point remains reusable.
4.  Later cards increasingly combine multiple established structures
    naturally.
5.  New grammar should normally be the principal structural novelty in
    its earliest cards.
6.  The scheduler, not curriculum order, supplies extra repetition for
    difficult material.
7.  Future amendments may split, merge, reorder or refine units without
    renumbering existing card IDs.

AMENDMENT POLICY

This syllabus changes only when the canonical French curriculum changes.
Generating cards or advancing through a unit does not amend it.

An amendment should identify affected units, state the pedagogical
change, say whether it affects future cards only or requires
existing-card correction, increment the version, and preserve a concise
amendment record.
