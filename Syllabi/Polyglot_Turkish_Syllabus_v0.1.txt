PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern standard
Turkish in Polyglot. It follows the Polyglot Curriculum Charter v0.7:
a small reusable lexical seed moves immediately into productive
structure; vocabulary expands just in time alongside grammar; first
introduction is distinct from establishment; and previously introduced
structures should be reinforced longitudinally through later double-duty
cards.

Turkish structure is authoritative. Existing Polyglot sequences may be
reused where they fit naturally, but Turkish is not to be distorted to
resemble French, German, Polish, Hungarian, or another language.

The syllabus is designed toward ordinary educated-adult competence
rather than a finite beginner endpoint. Vocabulary, agglutinative
morphology, syntax, evidential and temporal distinctions, phraseology,
register, orthography, and natural usage continue into an expandable
long tail.

OPENING PROTOTYPE AND PACING DIRECTIVE

The published 50-card Turkish prototype was designed before this full
syllabus as a deliberate stress test. It is now the empirical opening to
be reconciled against the syllabus rather than discarded merely because
the specification was written later.

Preferred initial lexical seed:

ev — house
erkek — man
kadın — woman
çocuk — child
büyük — big
küçük — small
iyi — good
kötü — bad

The prototype then moves promptly into ordinary Turkish predication,
bir and bu, personal predicate endings, natural subject omission, değil
negation, polar questions, and the first noun plurals.

The prototype establishes these principles early:

1. Third-person present nominal and adjectival predicates normally have
   no overt copular word: Ev büyük. Doktor.

2. First- and second-person present nominal/adjectival predicates expose
   personal suffixes directly: İyiyim. İyisin. Doktorum. Doktorsun.

3. Turkish is ordinarily pro-drop when person is recoverable from the
   predicate. Personal pronouns are taught as lexical forms but should
   not be mechanically retained in neutral sentences.

4. o is gender-neutral and may correspond to English he, she, or it.

5. bir must develop as both numeral and indefinite determiner without
   being treated as a mechanical equivalent of English a/an.

6. değil supplies nominal/adjectival negation and itself carries personal
   marking where required: İyi değilim.

7. Polar questions use the separate question clitic/particle
   mı/mi/mu/mü, with vowel harmony and person marking developing through
   real examples.

8. Noun plural -lar/-ler makes vowel harmony visible early. Its
   introduction does not make plurality or vowel harmony complete.

9. Turkish morphology should be learned as productive suffix
   architecture through accumulated words and sentences, not as a
   preliminary table of all possible suffixes.

10. Negation, questions, plurality, vowel harmony, and personal marking
    are longitudinal systems. The opening gives each enough exposure to
    become intelligible, then later cards should reinforce them while
    introducing new material.

The opening prototype is a pacing model, not a permanent 50-card quota.

STAGE 1 — SMALL SEED AND PRESENT NOMINAL/ADJECTIVAL PREDICATION

TR-01 — Core concrete nouns and authentic Turkish orthography

OBJECTIVE: Establish a very small reusable noun seed while exposing
ordinary Turkish spelling, including ı, İ, ö, ü, ç, ğ, and ş as they
naturally arise.

LEXICAL ANCHORS: ev, erkek, kadın, çocuk.

CARD STRATEGY: Isolated lexical cards are appropriate for the seed; move
into productive sentences immediately.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough nouns exist to support early predication and
determiner use. Broader noun vocabulary develops continuously.

TR-02 — Core descriptive adjectives

PREREQUISITES: TR-01 begun.

OBJECTIVE: Establish a small useful adjective seed.

LEXICAL ANCHORS: büyük, küçük, iyi, kötü.

CARD STRATEGY: Teach citation forms and immediately reuse them as
predicates.

COMPLETION STANDARD: The seed supports productive description.

TR-03 — Third-person present nominal and adjectival predication

PREREQUISITES: TR-01–TR-02.

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary present third-person predicates without an
independent copular word.

CORE PATTERNS: Ev büyük. Kadın iyi. Doktor.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: var/yok existence, olmak as a lexical verb, past
copular morphology, and formal or emphatic copular forms.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic third-person nominal and adjectival
predication is established as ordinary Turkish structure.

TR-04 — bir and bu

OBJECTIVE: Establish bir first as one/indefinite and bu as proximal
demonstrative, using natural nominal predicates.

CORE PATTERNS: Bu bir ev. Bu bir çocuk. Bu bir doktor.

CARD STRATEGY: Do not imply that Turkish requires bir wherever English
uses a/an. Its numeral, indefinite, and later discourse-sensitive uses
develop longitudinally.

TR-05 — Personal pronouns and gender-neutral o

OBJECTIVE: Establish ben, sen, o, biz, siz, onlar as reference forms and
make explicit that o does not encode grammatical gender.

CARD STRATEGY: Pronouns may be learned as lexical forms. Do not preserve
them in ordinary sentences merely because the English prompt has an
explicit subject.

COMPLETION STANDARD: The six basic pronouns are available as lexical
reference forms and ordinary subject omission is anticipated.

TR-06 — Present personal predicate suffixes

PREREQUISITES: TR-02–TR-05.

OBJECTIVE: Establish personal marking on ordinary nominal/adjectival
predicates while preserving zero third-person singular.

CORE PATTERNS: İyiyim. İyisin. İyi. İyiyiz. İyisiniz. Doktorum.
Doktorsun. Doktoruz.

CARD STRATEGY: Prefer omitted pronouns where natural. Introduce suffix
allomorphy through real predicates rather than a detached paradigm.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Core singular and plural personal predication is
intelligible and ready for longitudinal reinforcement.

TR-07 — Nominal/adjectival negation with değil

OBJECTIVE: Establish değil after nominal/adjectival predicates and its
personal marking.

CORE PATTERNS: Ev büyük değil. İyi değilim. İyi değilsin.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic predicative negation has enough controlled
exposure to recur in later material. It remains a longitudinal strand.

TR-08 — Polar questions with mı/mi/mu/mü

OBJECTIVE: Establish the separate polar-question element, its vowel
harmony, and initial interaction with personal marking.

CORE PATTERNS: Ev büyük mü? Kadın iyi mi? İyi miyim? İyi misin?

CARD STRATEGY: Keep question morphology visible initially, then reuse
questions throughout later units rather than treating this as a sealed
question block.

COMPLETION STANDARD: The basic polar-question pattern is intelligible
across representative predicate shapes and can be reinforced later.

TR-09 — Noun plural -lar/-ler

OBJECTIVE: Introduce noun plurality and the first highly transparent
two-way vowel-harmonic suffix family.

CORE PATTERNS: evler, çocuklar; Evler büyük. Çocuklar küçük.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full vowel-harmony theory, plural possessives,
case-marked plurals, and every interaction with agreement.

COMPLETION STANDARD: -lar/-ler is introduced productively and available
for reuse; later cards continue varying plurality.

STAGE 2 — BASIC VERBAL CLAUSES AND CORE NOMINAL MORPHOLOGY

TR-10 — Basic present/progressive verbal clauses

OBJECTIVE: Introduce a small set of high-value verbs through natural
finite clauses, beginning with the ordinary -iyor progressive/present
system where context supports it.

CARD STRATEGY: Add verbs just in time. Do not create a long preliminary
infinitive list. Omit subject pronouns where person marking makes them
unnecessary.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic intransitive and transitive verbal clauses
exist sufficiently to support object marking and verbal negation.

TR-11 — Accusative -(y)ı/-(y)i/-(y)u/-(y)ü and object definiteness

PREREQUISITES: TR-10.

OBJECTIVE: Establish marked specific/definite direct objects and contrast
them with ordinary unmarked nonspecific objects.

CARD STRATEGY: Use matched natural contrasts where possible. Avoid
reducing Turkish accusative use to English definiteness alone.

COMPLETION STANDARD: The central marked-versus-unmarked object contrast
is inferable and reusable.

TR-12 — Verbal negation -ma/-me

PREREQUISITES: TR-10.

OBJECTIVE: Distinguish verbal negation from nominal/adjectival değil and
establish its position inside the verb complex.

CARD STRATEGY: Recycle previously introduced persons, questions, nouns,
and adjectives where natural.

TR-13 — Verbal polar questions

PREREQUISITES: TR-08, TR-10.

OBJECTIVE: Extend mı/mi/mu/mü into finite verbal clauses and consolidate
its placement relative to tense/aspect/person morphology.

TR-14 — Demonstratives and basic noun phrases

OBJECTIVE: Expand bu to şu and o, distinguish demonstrative determiner
and pronoun uses, and establish ordinary noun-phrase order.

TR-15 — Cardinal numbers and singular nouns after numerals

OBJECTIVE: Establish basic numbers and the Turkish pattern in which nouns
normally remain singular after numerals.

CORE CONTRAST: iki ev, not a mechanically English-like plural noun.

TR-16 — Basic vowel harmony as a productive system

OBJECTIVE: Make explicit through accumulated plural, question, accusative,
and verbal examples that suffix vowels are selected systematically.

CARD STRATEGY: Continue teaching harmony through meaningful morphology;
do not replace usage with abstract vowel-class quizzes.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Common two-way and four-way suffix choices are
predictable for ordinary stems, with exceptions reserved for later
treatment.

TR-17 — Buffer consonants and basic consonant alternations

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency morphophonology such as y-buffering
and common p/ç/t/k alternations where suffixation naturally exposes them.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Exhaustive phonological exception lists.

TR-18 — Agglutinative suffix order

OBJECTIVE: Make Turkish word structure increasingly transparent as an
ordered sequence of stem plus derivational/inflectional morphology.

CARD STRATEGY: Build only from already understood suffixes. Do not create
artificially long forms merely to display agglutination.

STAGE 3 — CASE, LOCATION, EXISTENCE, AND POSSESSION

TR-19 — Dative -(y)a/-(y)e

OBJECTIVE: Establish destination, recipient, and other high-frequency
dative functions through natural clauses.

TR-20 — Locative -da/-de/-ta/-te

OBJECTIVE: Establish static location and expose consonant-conditioned
allomorphy alongside vowel harmony.

TR-21 — Ablative -dan/-den/-tan/-ten

OBJECTIVE: Establish source/from relations and reinforce the same
morphophonological architecture.

TR-22 — Genitive -(n)ın/-(n)in/-(n)un/-(n)ün

OBJECTIVE: Introduce possessor marking as a prerequisite to the full
possessive construction.

TR-23 — Possessive suffixes

OBJECTIVE: Establish core possessed-noun morphology across persons and
representative stem types.

CARD STRATEGY: Prefer natural possessed forms and phrases over English
possessive-pronoun substitution.

TR-24 — Genitive-possessive constructions

PREREQUISITES: TR-22–TR-23.

OBJECTIVE: Establish Turkish possessor + possessed agreement and contrast
it with simple English 's/of mappings.

TR-25 — Compound nouns and indefinite noun compounds

OBJECTIVE: Establish common noun-noun compounds with the appropriate
compound marker on the head noun and distinguish them from definite
genitive-possessive constructions.

TR-26 — var and yok

OBJECTIVE: Establish existence/nonexistence and ordinary possession
constructions.

CORE PATTERNS: Evde çocuk var. Evde çocuk yok. Kitabım var.

CARD STRATEGY: Contrast with zero-copula predicates and with değil so
that distinct Turkish systems remain visible.

TR-27 — Case, possession, and existence synthesis

OBJECTIVE: Integrate plural, case, possessive morphology, var/yok,
questions, and negation through natural double-duty cards.

STAGE 4 — TENSE, ASPECT, AND MOOD

TR-28 — -iyor progressive/present consolidation

OBJECTIVE: Broaden the initially introduced -iyor system across persons,
negation, questions, vowel alternations, and common lexical classes.

TR-29 — Aorist/geniş zaman

OBJECTIVE: Establish habitual, generic, dispositional, and other core
uses of the aorist with representative affirmative morphology.

CARD STRATEGY: Treat the aorist as a Turkish tense/aspect category, not
as a one-to-one English simple-present ending.

TR-30 — Aorist negation and person interaction

OBJECTIVE: Establish the distinctive negative aorist pattern and its
person marking without burying it among unrelated exceptions.

TR-31 — Direct/simple past -di

OBJECTIVE: Establish the witnessed/direct past family across persons,
negation, and questions, while leaving discourse-level evidential
contrasts for explicit later comparison.

TR-32 — Inferential/evidential past -miş

OBJECTIVE: Establish core inferential, reportative, and resultative
functions of -miş and its ordinary pragmatic range.

TR-33 — -di versus -miş

OBJECTIVE: Contrast direct/simple and inferential/reportative past
meanings through controlled contexts rather than translating both merely
as English past.

TR-34 — Future -(y)acak/-(y)ecek

OBJECTIVE: Establish future morphology and its person/negation/question
interactions.

TR-35 — Necessitative -malı/-meli

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinary necessity/obligation morphology and
distinguish it from later lexical/modal alternatives.

TR-36 — Ability/possibility -(y)abil/-(y)ebil

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive ability/possibility morphology,
including negation and tense combinations progressively.

TR-37 — Conditional -sa/-se

OBJECTIVE: Establish core conditional morphology and ordinary
hypothetical uses.

TR-38 — Imperative and optative/request forms

OBJECTIVE: Establish common command, request, suggestion, and wish forms
in a frequency- and register-aware sequence rather than as one giant
paradigm.

TR-39 — Copular past, inferential, and conditional on nominal predicates

OBJECTIVE: Extend nominal/adjectival predication beyond the present using
forms historically associated with imek and their modern suffixal or
separate realizations.

TR-40 — Tense/aspect/mood synthesis

OBJECTIVE: Mix established verbal and nominal systems across person,
polarity, question force, and discourse context while broadening
vocabulary.

STAGE 5 — NON-FINITE VERBAL SYSTEM AND COMPLEX NOMINALIZATION

TR-41 — Infinitives -mak/-mek

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive infinitival use beyond lexical citation

TR-42 — Verbal nouns in -ma/-me

LATE CURRICULUM — PARALLEL DEVELOPMENT

From approximately this point onward, unit numbering records editorial
introduction order rather than a requirement that one mature domain be
exhausted before another begins. Once prerequisites are available,
register, phraseology, broad lexical domains, educated-adult vocabulary,
regional usage, and long-tail material should develop in parallel and
reinforce one another.

Later units should be introduced in roughly the numbered order,
relatively quickly, and then revisited longitudinally. The learner need
not master one lexical domain before another begins, nor exhaust
phraseology before advanced vocabulary or variation develops. Numbering
remains useful for first introduction and controlled-document
organization; it is not a mandate for serial mastery.

GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1. Unit completion means its canonical sequence adequately demonstrates
   its objective; it does not claim learner mastery.

2. Introduction is not establishment. A structure may receive enough
   controlled exposure to become intelligible and then continue toward
   establishment through later double-duty cards.

3. Once established, grammar becomes reusable unless temporary
   simplification is pedagogically useful.

4. Vocabulary introduced at any point remains reusable.

5. Later cards should increasingly combine multiple previously
   introduced structures naturally. A new case card may also reinforce
   plurality, negation, a question, or an earlier tense where this does
   not obscure the principal novelty.

6. The scheduler, not curriculum order, supplies extra repetition for
   difficult material.

7. Subject pronouns should not be inserted into neutral Turkish
   sentences merely because the English prompt contains an explicit
   subject.

8. Vowel harmony and consonant alternation should be learned through
   productive morphology and accumulated examples rather than detached
   preliminary memorization.

9. Agglutinative morphology should develop as ordered, meaningful suffix
   architecture. Do not use spectacularly long words merely to advertise
   agglutination.

10. Nominal/adjectival predication, verbal tense/aspect, evidentiality,
    case, possession, voice, non-finite structures, and information
    structure should develop longitudinally rather than receive one
    isolated exposure.

11. Negation with değil and verbal -ma/-me must remain distinct systems.

12. Polar-question mı/mi/mu/mü should recur across predicate types,
    tenses, persons, and later structures.

13. Turkish direct-object marking must be taught according to Turkish
    specificity/definiteness and discourse structure, not by mechanically
    copying English article use.

14. bir should not be inserted merely because English has an indefinite
    article.

15. Future amendments may split, merge, reorder, or refine units without
    renumbering existing stable card IDs.

AMENDMENT POLICY

This syllabus changes only when the canonical Turkish curriculum changes.
Generating cards or advancing through a unit does not amend it.

An amendment should identify affected units, state the pedagogical
change, say whether it affects future cards only or requires
existing-card correction, increment the version, and preserve a concise
amendment record.

AMENDMENT HISTORY

- Uses the published 50-card Turkish prototype as an empirical stress

- Distinguishes first introduction from establishment and directs future

- Uses prerequisite-aware early staging and parallel mature-language

- Ends in an expandable educated-adult long tail rather than a fixed card
