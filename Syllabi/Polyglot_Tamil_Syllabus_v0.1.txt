PURPOSE
This is the canonical introduction map for the Tamil curriculum. Preserve the original exploratory micro deck, IDs 3500000–3500014, unchanged. The additional 85 cards, IDs 3500015–3500099, create a systematic 100-card seed. Units open prerequisite-aware grammar and lexical branches; they are not closed chapters. Maintain a widening spiral of controlled examples, reusable vocabulary and progressively richer structures.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND DIGLOSSIA
The seed uses standard modern written Tamil in Tamil script. Tamil has substantial differences between formal written and everyday spoken varieties. Do not silently mix literary/written verb endings with colloquial reductions or present a Chennai spoken form as universally standard. Teach common spoken Tamil deliberately later, with context and regional labels (including Sri Lankan Tamil). The original 15 cards are preserved even where their phrasing previews structures not yet systematically taught.

Tamil is predominantly SOV, with agglutinative nominal case marking and rich verbal agreement. Unlike Vietnamese or Cantonese, Tamil requires careful development of person, number, gender/rationality, honorific distinctions, tense and verb stem classes. Introduce each new contrast in small controlled sets, then reduce paradigm density as the patterns become familiar.

SCRIPT, INPUT AND EXACT MATCH
Canonical Target uses Tamil script and standard orthography; InputHint is normally blank. Preserve Unicode spelling, vowel signs, pulli and relevant morphophonological distinctions. The user types the Tamil answer directly. EnglishPrompt must be unique within Tamil and should specify the gender, formality, inclusive/exclusive we or speaker sex when those affect the intended answer. Do not use an unqualified English 'he' for an honorific அவர் if a distinct ordinary form அவன் is intended.

EARLY GRAMMAR PRINCIPLES
1. Nominal identity in the present often has no overt equivalent of English 'is': இது ஒரு புத்தகம்; அவன் ஒரு மாணவன். Do not insert இருக்கிறது as a universal copula.
2. Adjectival predicates may use neuter forms: வீடு பெரியது; கார் சிறியது. Attributive adjectives such as பெரிய, சிறிய, புதிய precede nouns.
3. Tamil distinguishes inclusive நாம் from exclusive நாங்கள்; label this contrast explicitly in English prompts.
4. Tamil personal reference involves ordinary masculine அவன், feminine அவள், honorific அவர், neuter அது, and distinct plural and polite forms. The original micro card uses honorific அவர் for 'He'; preserve it as a preview and make later contrasts explicit.
5. The seed's possessive constructions என்னிடம் ... இருக்கிறது and ... இல்லை are locative-possession patterns, not a transitive 'have' conjugation.
6. Basic locative -இல் appears in வீட்டில் and பள்ளியில்; more case and postpositional systems are introduced later.
7. Accusative -ஐ appears in பூனையை, நாயைப், புத்தகத்தைப், காரைப் and plural objects. Sandhi and optional object marking need longitudinal treatment.
8. Questions use -ஆ in polar interrogatives and independent question words such as என்ன, யார், எங்கே.
9. Present verbs such as பார்க்கிறேன், குடிக்கிறேன் and சாப்பிடுகிறேன் show first-person endings; contrasts with குடிக்கிறான், குடிக்கிறாள் and குடிக்கிறாயா open person/gender/number agreement.
10. Introduce a representative tense/person paradigm, then broaden verb classes, past and future systems without mechanically filling every paradigm for every new verb.
11. Rational/human versus non-rational/neuter distinctions affect agreement and pronouns; introduce through meaningful noun and sentence contrasts.
12. Honorific and polite language is not a mere optional suffix. Develop appropriate pronoun–verb agreement and social contexts.
13. Tamil's spoken/written differences must be visible to the learner; later colloquial cards should explicitly identify register rather than appearing as contradictory canonical translations.
14. Continue expanding nouns, verbs and adjectives alongside grammatical structure, eventually covering educated-adult technical, civic, literary and abstract vocabulary.

SEED AUDIT

Seed introduces core people, animals, household nouns, adjectives, numerals, personal pronouns, inclusive/exclusive first-person plural, nominal identity, negation, neuter adjective predicates, possession with -இடம், absence with இல்லை, location, questions, accusative objects, and first present-tense agreement contrasts. Some seed examples anticipate later case, sandhi and verb-class units; preview is not mastery.

CARD GENERATION RULES
- Six columns: ID,Language,EnglishPrompt,Target,InputHint,PartOfSpeech.
- Permanent Tamil allocation: 3500000–3599999.
- Keep the original 15 cards and IDs unchanged. Append cards sequentially.
- Use Tamil script with normal punctuation and accurate combining characters.
- EnglishPrompt unique within Tamil; each card has one intended canonical Target.
- Specify gender, politeness, register, and inclusive/exclusive we where the English is ambiguous.
- Do not mechanically add a present-tense copula to nominal or adjectival predicates.
- Use noun case suffixes and verbal agreement naturally, with controlled first introductions.
- Keep examples short and independently meaningful after shuffling.
- Prefer a widening spiral of vocabulary and grammar over repetitive verb tables.
- Label colloquial versus standard written Tamil explicitly in later development.

CURRENT FRONTIER
At 100 cards the next forward frontier is TA-21: present neuter and plural/honorific agreement. Consolidate first-person and ordinary masculine/feminine present forms with one familiar verb, then introduce neuter and plural/honorific forms. Broaden dative/genitive and instrumental cases in controlled examples, and only then develop past and future stem classes. Keep script practice and vocabulary growth active.
