POLYGLOT HEBREW SYLLABUS

Version: 0.1
Status: Current
Charter version: 0.8
Encoding: UTF-8

PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for contemporary
Israeli Hebrew in Polyglot. It was written after an initial 100-card seed
and uses that seed, together with successful Polyglot decks, as design
evidence.

The syllabus is a roadmap rather than a transcript. A useful structure may
appear before its formal unit when the card remains comprehensible, and
introduced systems may continue toward establishment longitudinally.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND ORTHOGRAPHY

The initial production target is ordinary contemporary Israeli Hebrew.
Targets use normal unpointed Hebrew spelling. Niqqud is not a preliminary
script layer and is not normally supplied merely to make pronunciation
explicit. It may enter later where conventional, pedagogically useful, or
needed to discuss a genuine ambiguity.

Biblical, Mishnaic and earlier Hebrew are historically central but are not
the unmarked production target. They enter later as literary, religious,
historical and register-sensitive comprehension strands.

COURSE-WIDE HEBREW PRINCIPLES

1. Use ordinary Hebrew script from the first card. Transliteration is not
   an alternative Target and InputHint is normally blank.
2. Modern unpointed spelling is the normal adult orthography. Do not create
   a niqqud-dependent beginner dialect of the written language.
3. Hebrew has masculine/feminine grammatical gender and extensive
   gender/number agreement. English prompts must identify speaker,
   addressee or referent gender when needed for exact matching.
4. Present-tense verb forms behave morphologically like participles and
   distinguish gender and number but not person. Pronouns therefore remain
   important in many present clauses.
5. Present nominal/adjectival predicates normally have no overt copula.
   Do not insert a Hebrew equivalent merely because English uses am/is/are.
6. Definite ה־ interacts with attributive adjective definiteness. Contrast
   הבית גדול with הבית הגדול early and reinforce the distinction.
7. Hebrew has no ordinary indefinite article. Do not mechanically translate
   English a/an with אחד.
8. את marks definite direct objects in the central modern system. It is not
   an ordinary translation of English 'the' or an all-purpose accusative
   marker; teach it through contrasts such as רואה בית / רואה את הבית.
9. יש and אין are foundational existential constructions and participate
   in possession with ל־ clitics: יש לי, אין לי, etc.
10. Prepositions frequently combine with pronominal suffixes. Treat common
    inflected prepositions as central morphology, not peripheral idiom.
11. Lexical verbs are introduced with the infinitive where a natural modern
    infinitive is available, while verbal morphology is organized around
    roots and binyanim only as those concepts become useful.
12. Roots are lexical relationships, not standalone words. Do not make bare
    consonantal roots ordinary English-to-Hebrew answers.
13. Binyan patterns are important organizing knowledge but actual verbs,
    meanings and inflected forms remain the curriculum. Do not imply that
    a root+binyan combination has a fully predictable lexical meaning.
14. Hebrew tense/aspect morphology is not built by adding an English-style
    auxiliary to an invariant verb. Past and future person/gender/number
    paradigms deserve systematic coverage when their cells teach genuine
    contrasts.
15. Future morphology also underlies important modal, imperative and
    prohibitive constructions; develop these relationships longitudinally.
16. Possessive suffixes and של constructions both belong to modern Hebrew.
    Their distribution and register should be learned from natural phrases,
    not treated as mechanically interchangeable.
17. Construct state (סמיכות) is a productive noun-phrase system and should
    be introduced through frequent lexical and compositional examples.
18. Vocabulary grows just in time. Do not complete root tables, binyan
    inventories or noun-pattern catalogues before useful language proceeds.
19. Spoken-standard forms and formal written norms may differ. Begin with
    broadly neutral contemporary usage and label significant colloquial or
    formal alternatives when exact matching requires it.
20. Hebrew's historical layers strongly affect modern vocabulary and
    phraseology. Historical explanation may clarify a modern pattern but
    should not displace contemporary usage as the production target.

EMPIRICAL OPENING: CARDS 1–100

The seed establishes:
- the shared house/man/woman/child and big/small/good/bad anchors;
- normal unpointed Hebrew script;
- masculine/feminine adjective agreement;
- present nominal/adjectival predication without a copula;
- definite ה־ and predicative versus attributive adjective definiteness;
- זה/זאת and demonstrative noun phrases;
- gendered singular and plural personal pronouns;
- gender/number-sensitive present forms through לדבר and לעבוד;
- לא negation and intonation/punctuation polar questions;
- איפה, מה and מי;
- existential יש/אין;
- possession through יש + ל־;
- definite-object את contrasted with an unmarked indefinite object;
- infinitives and רוצה + infinitive.

The seed deliberately includes the metalinguistically glossed את card only
after several concrete object examples. Future development should make the
construction ordinary through usage rather than multiplying terminology
cards.

INITIAL STRUCTURAL ROADMAP

HE-01 — Core nouns and gender
Continue high-frequency masculine and feminine nouns, including useful
pairs and nouns whose ending does not transparently predict gender.

HE-02 — Adjective agreement
Develop masculine/feminine singular/plural adjective agreement through
noun phrases and predicates. Productive endings precede less predictable
lexical patterns where practical.

HE-03 — Definiteness and adjective position
Reinforce ה־ and the agreement in definiteness required by attributive
adjectives. Contrast predicative and attributive structures longitudinally.

HE-04 — Demonstratives
Develop זה, זאת/זו, אלה and demonstrative noun phrases, including ordinary
agreement and definiteness behavior.

HE-05 — Personal pronouns
Establish the full useful pronoun inventory and its gender/number contrasts.
Treat masculine plural as the ordinary mixed-gender form where contemporary
usage calls for it while later addressing inclusive-language innovations
and variation.

HE-06 — Present nominal clauses
Identity, classification, description and location without an overt present
copula. Introduce הוא/היא/הם as copular-like pronouns only where natural
modern syntax actually uses them.

HE-07 — Present verbal predicates
Develop masculine/feminine singular/plural present forms across a small
number of useful verbs before multiplying lexical stems.

HE-08 — Negation and polar questions
Establish לא with verbal and nominal/adjectival predicates and ordinary
yes/no question formation. Introduce האם later as a more formal/written
question marker rather than requiring it in ordinary conversation.

HE-09 — Content questions
Develop מי, מה, איפה/היכן, מתי, למה/מדוע, איך/כיצד, כמה and relevant
register contrasts.

HE-10 — Plural nouns
Develop common masculine -ים and feminine -ות patterns while learning
frequent irregular and gender-crossing plurals lexically.

EXISTENCE, POSSESSION, OBJECTS, AND PREPOSITIONS

HE-11 — יש and אין
Develop existence and absence across locations, quantities and noun types.

HE-12 — Possession with יש/אין + ל־
Expand יש לי, יש לך, יש לו/לה, יש לנו, יש לכם/לכן, יש להם/להן and negative
אין forms. Treat the inflected preposition as a genuine paradigm because
the cells encode useful person/gender/number contrasts.

HE-13 — Definite direct objects and את
Establish את with definite noun phrases, names and pronouns as appropriate,
contrasted with indefinite objects that lack את.

HE-14 — את + pronominal suffixes
Develop אותי, אותך, אותו, אותה, אותנו, אתכם/אתכן, אותם/אותן through natural
direct-object clauses.

HE-15 — Core prepositions
Introduce ב־, ל־, מ־, על, עם, אצל, לפני, אחרי, בשביל and other frequent
prepositions through concrete spatial, directional and relational meanings.

HE-16 — Inflected prepositions
Develop high-frequency pronominal paradigms such as לי, בי, ממני, עליי,
איתי and אצלִי families according to actual frequency. Do not assume every
preposition inflects by one identical template.

HE-17 — של possession
Develop של + noun/pronoun possession, including שלי, שלך, שלו, שלה and
plural forms, before and alongside construct-state possession.

HE-18 — Construct state (סמיכות)
Introduce high-frequency construct pairs and productive noun+noun
relationships. Develop definiteness and plural construct forms through
actual phrases.

VERBAL SPINE

HE-19 — Infinitives and lexical verbs
Continue high-frequency infinitives as practical lexical anchors. Relate
them to present forms without pretending the infinitive transparently
generates every paradigm.

HE-20 — Root awareness
Introduce roots as recurring consonantal lexical relationships. Use real
families; do not make root extraction an end in itself.

HE-21 — Pa'al/Qal foundation
Develop common regular and high-frequency Pa'al verbs across present, past,
future and infinitive relationships.

HE-22 — Past tense
Introduce suffixal past morphology with verbs whose forms expose person,
number and gender contrasts clearly. Cover enough of a paradigm to reveal
the system, then reuse it longitudinally.

HE-23 — Future tense
Develop prefixal future morphology and its person/gender/number contrasts,
including high-frequency irregular forms.

HE-24 — Imperatives and requests
Introduce ordinary commands and requests, including the relationship
between formal morphological imperatives, future-based colloquial commands,
נא and politeness strategies.

HE-25 — Prohibition
Develop אל + relevant future/jussive-like forms and contrast with לא
negation.

HE-26 — Pi'el
Introduce frequent Pi'el verbs and their characteristic present/past/future/
infinitive relationships through useful lexical families.

HE-27 — Hif'il
Develop frequent causative and lexical Hif'il verbs without claiming every
meaning is compositionally causative.

HE-28 — Hitpa'el
Develop common reflexive/reciprocal and lexical Hitpa'el verbs.

HE-29 — Nif'al
Develop common passive, middle and lexical Nif'al verbs.

HE-30 — Pu'al and Huf'al
Introduce these primarily passive binyanim according to actual frequency,
after their active counterparts provide useful anchors.

HE-31 — Weak and irregular roots
Develop hollow, final-he, initial-yod/nun, guttural and other morphophonemic
classes longitudinally. High-frequency irregular verbs may precede the
formal unit when needed.

HE-32 — Tense and binyan synthesis
Interleave tense, person, gender, number and binyan across natural contexts
rather than maintaining isolated paradigm blocks.

NOUNS, NUMBERS, AND MODIFIERS

HE-33 — Gender beyond transparent endings
Develop common nouns whose grammatical gender must simply be learned,
including important exceptional plural behavior.

HE-34 — Number and agreement
Reinforce plural noun/adjective/pronoun agreement and introduce dual forms
where they remain productive or lexically important.

HE-35 — Cardinal numbers
Develop the characteristically Hebrew gender behavior of numbers and counted
nouns in manageable stages. High-frequency numbers should appear early;
the full system develops longitudinally.

HE-36 — Ordinals, dates, time and age
Use real date/time/age expressions and the gender forms they require.

HE-37 — Comparison
Develop יותר, פחות, הכי and מן/מ־ comparative structures, including formal
and colloquial distribution.

HE-38 — Quantifiers and indefinites
כל, הרבה, מעט, כמה, משהו, מישהו, אף אחד and related polarity/quantity
expressions.

HE-39 — Possessive suffixes
Develop common suffixed possession where productive/current, while
distinguishing formal/literary preference from ordinary של constructions.

CLAUSE STRUCTURE

HE-40 — Coordination
ו־, אבל, או, גם, אלא and common discourse coordinators.

HE-41 — כי and complement clauses
Develop content/reason clauses with כי and other complementizers according
to meaning and register.

HE-42 — ש־ clauses
Develop relative and complement uses of ש־, including preposition+pronoun
strategies where English uses a relative pronoun object.

HE-43 — אם and conditionals
Develop אם from open conditions through unreal/counterfactual structures
with the tense/modal combinations actually used in modern Hebrew.

HE-44 — Purpose and result
כדי, כדי ש־, בשביל, לכן and related purpose/result constructions.

HE-45 — Temporal clauses
כש־, כאשר, לפני ש־, אחרי ש־, בזמן ש־ and related sequencing structures.

HE-46 — Object and argument structure
Revisit verbs with את, prepositional complements and bare objects so lexical
government becomes part of vocabulary knowledge.

HE-47 — Word order and information structure
Develop neutral SVO tendencies alongside topicalization, subject omission,
postverbal subjects and marked orders in contexts that make their discourse
function meaningful.

HE-48 — Existential and impersonal extensions
Develop צריך, אפשר, אסור, כדאי and other common modal/impersonal predicates,
including gender/person behavior and infinitival complements.

DERIVATION, LEXICON, AND REGISTER

HE-49 — Noun and adjective patterns
Develop common mishkal patterns as aids to lexical recognition and
derivation without pretending pattern meaning is mechanically predictable.

HE-50 — Root families and semantic networks
Revisit productive roots across verbs, nouns and adjectives, distinguishing
transparent families from historical or semantically divergent relatives.

HE-51 — Prefixes, suffixes and compounds
Develop productive modern derivation, borrowed morphology and compounds
through actual vocabulary.

HE-52 — Everyday lexical domains
Family, food, home, transport, work, school, health, shopping, technology,
weather, emotions and ordinary social life develop in parallel.

HE-53 — Idiom and collocation
Common fixed expressions, light-verb-like constructions, greetings,
politeness routines and lexical collocations.

HE-54 — Spoken Israeli Hebrew
Develop common reductions, discourse particles, slang and colloquial syntax
after a broadly neutral backbone exists. Label variants when exact matching
requires it.

HE-55 — Formal written Hebrew
Develop formal vocabulary, syntactic preferences, passive constructions,
nominalizations and institutional prose used in educated writing.

HE-56 — Historical layers in modern vocabulary
Show how Biblical, Mishnaic, Medieval and revived/modern formations feed
contemporary Hebrew where that knowledge improves present-day comprehension.
Historical provenance is explanatory, not a separate production target.

HE-57 — Biblical Hebrew comprehension
After modern Hebrew is mature enough, introduce high-value differences in
vocabulary, morphology, syntax and orthography needed for culturally
important Biblical texts. Keep these cards explicitly distinguishable from
ordinary modern production when forms conflict.

HE-58 — Rabbinic and liturgical language
Develop important prayers, formulae, Mishnaic/Rabbinic expressions and
traditional vocabulary according to cultural frequency and learner value,
with register/source labels where necessary.

HE-59 — Contemporary variation
Develop gender-inclusive innovations, regional/social variation, loanwords
and competing pronunciations/spellings where they materially affect modern
comprehension or production.

MATURE EDUCATED-ADULT DEVELOPMENT

HE-60 — Narrative and discourse
Longer event sequences, reported speech, viewpoint, tense choice, reference
tracking and discourse cohesion.

HE-61 — Institutions and public life
Government, law, education, health, economics, military/security vocabulary,
media and civic language, handled as ordinary lexical domains rather than
ideological content.

HE-62 — Science, technology and abstract discourse
Broad educated vocabulary, technical-general terms, explanation,
argumentation and interpretation.

HE-63 — Arts, literature and cultural discourse
Modern literary vocabulary, criticism, music, film, visual arts and
culturally important references.

HE-64 — Semantic precision and register
Near-synonyms, polysemy, collocation, root-family false friends, formal/
colloquial contrasts and pragmatic nuance.

HE-65 — Educated-adult long tail
Continue vocabulary, phraseology, derivation, register and discourse breadth
without manufacturing obscure grammar merely because the deck is mature.

CARD-SPECIFIC RULES

1. EnglishPrompt must be unique and select one intended Hebrew Target.
2. Use normal unpointed Hebrew Targets; InputHint is normally blank.
3. Mark speaker gender in first-person present prompts whenever Hebrew
   agreement would otherwise be underdetermined.
4. Mark addressee gender/number when second-person forms differ.
5. Mark third-person gender/number where English does not already determine
   the intended form.
6. Do not add אחד merely to translate English a/an.
7. Do not insert an overt present copula into ordinary nominal/adjectival
   clauses.
8. Definite attributive adjectives normally repeat definiteness with ה־.
9. Use את only where the Hebrew object construction licenses/requires it;
   do not infer it from English transitivity alone.
10. Infinitive lexical cards should use ordinary contemporary forms.
11. Root and binyan terminology is generator-facing organization unless a
    terminology card has independent learner value.
12. When unpointed spelling creates a genuine exact-match ambiguity, resolve
    it in the EnglishPrompt/context rather than defaulting the entire deck
    to niqqud.
13. Colloquial/formal/historical alternatives receive separate,
    disambiguated prompts when both deserve active production.
14. Punctuation follows normal contemporary Hebrew usage; right-to-left
    rendering quirks are interface issues, not reasons to alter logical text.

DEVELOPMENT POLICY

The first 100 cards are the empirical opening. Correct genuine errors, but
do not rebuild the seed merely to make later syllabus numbering perfectly
serial.

The next expansion should broaden plural agreement and present verbs while
developing יש/אין possession paradigms, את objects, core prepositions,
של possession, construct-state examples and a first controlled past-tense
system. Root/binyan awareness can begin as soon as repeated lexical families
make it useful; it should not delay ordinary sentences.

Past and future need not wait until every noun, plural or preposition has
been exhausted. Hebrew morphology should become an interleaved background
system in the same way case/aspect systems do in other mature Polyglot
decks.

AMENDMENT NOTE

Version 0.1 is the first Hebrew syllabus. It was written after a 100-card
prototype. The prototype showed that the common Polyglot seed works well
for Hebrew when the language is allowed to reveal present zero-copula
predication, adjective definiteness, gendered present participles,
existential possession and definite-object את immediately.
