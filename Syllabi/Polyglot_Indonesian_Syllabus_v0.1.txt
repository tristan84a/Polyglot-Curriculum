PURPOSE

This syllabus establishes the canonical introduction route for Indonesian, retaining the 15-card standardized micro opening and building productive ordinary language with a small reusable seed. Numbered units open branches rather than seal chapters. Previously introduced vocabulary, grammar and discourse patterns continue to recur in the widening spiral.

REFERENCE VARIETY AND CARD CONVENTIONS

Use contemporary standard Indonesian in ordinary modern writing and broadly natural speech. The early register uses saya and Anda as stable learner anchors, while recognizing that Anda can sound formal or impersonal in many actual conversations. Broaden aku, kamu, kalian, Bapak, Ibu and context-sensitive address later, and do not silently blend colloquial Jakartan forms with the standard in early Targets.

Write natural idiomatic English prompts and one natural Indonesian Target for exact-match practice. Other translations may be valid; the Target is a canonical instructional choice, not an assertion of uniqueness. Keep standard spelling and punctuation. Indonesian uses Latin script; InputHint remains blank. Use the six-column curriculum schema. ID block: 4800000–4899999.

Indonesian is predominantly analytic, without grammatical gender, articles like English a/the, agreement of adjectives with noun gender/number, or person/tense inflection on ordinary verbs. Avoid teaching nonexistent conjugation paradigms. Word order, aspect markers, voice, affixation, reduplication and pragmatic context require sustained attention.

EARLY LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC DECISIONS

1. Ordinary nominal identity and adjective predicates usually need no English-style 'be': Dia dokter; Rumah itu besar. Later introduce adalah/merupakan in the contexts where they are natural and ada/berada for existence/location.
2. Basic noun modifiers normally follow the noun: mobil merah, rumah besar. ini and itu follow the noun in definite demonstrative noun phrases; contrast the independent pronouns Ini/Itu.
3. Establish tidak for verbal/adjectival negation versus bukan for nominal identification and classification. Expand to belum and jangan when ready.
4. Indonesian pronouns encode an important inclusive/exclusive contrast: kita includes the addressee, kami excludes the addressee. Dia is not gender-marked.
5. Possession and attribution can be expressed by juxtaposition (buku saya), the suffix -nya, punya and milik; develop their use through natural contexts rather than a single English 'of' pattern.
6. Establish locative di, directional ke and source dari as separate prepositions. Distinguish these free forms from affixal di- in passive constructions.
7. Use natural spoken and written interrogative options progressively: apakah, intonation, apa, siapa, di mana, ke mana, kapan, bagaimana, mengapa/kenapa.
8. Introduce meN- verb formations only as needed alongside common verbs; do not imply all lexical verbs require meN-. Explain assimilation and nasal allomorphy systematically once examples make it meaningful.
9. 'Plural' need not be explicitly marked where a numeral, quantifier or context suffices. Noun reduplication and classifier-like orang/ekor/buah are useful later branches, not obligatory word-for-word English plural markers.
10. Tense is normally inferred from context or signalled by time expressions and aspect/modality markers such as sudah, sedang, akan, belum, masih, pernah. Do not build present/past/future conjugation tables.
11. Voice and valency are central to mature Indonesian: meN- active, di- passive, bare passive constructions with agents, ber-, ter-, -kan, -i, peN-, per- and nominalizing -an/ke-...-an each deserve controlled treatment.
12. Register is a core longitudinal strand: colloquial nggak/gak, enggak, udah, mau, aku/kamu, speech particles and Jakarta-influenced usage should later be identified explicitly without treating them as universal replacements for the standard.

MICRO DECK AND SYSTEMATIC SEED

Cards 4800000–4800014 are the existing standardized micro opening; preserve all fifteen cards and their IDs. They preview demonstratives, invariant verbal predicates, meN- (melihat), punya possession, number phrases and noun modification. A preview does not automatically move the forward syllabus frontier.

Cards 4800015–4800099 extend this to a 100-card systematic seed. They develop familiar pronouns, identity and adjectival predication, nominal and verbal negation, polar and content questions, locations, possession and several high-frequency verbs. The aim is a readable productive opening, not exhaustive command of any branch.

LONGITUDINAL DEVELOPMENT

The numbered units mark the introduction frontier; they are not mastery gates. The first 100 cards deliberately preview several later units. Extend vocabulary while cycling established predication, question forms and common verbs. Broaden colloquial and formal usage with explicit control over register. After the main branches have been meaningfully introduced, use authentic contemporary Indonesian to identify remaining productive gaps indefinitely.

NEXT FRONTIER

ID-18 — Location versus direction: di, ke and dari. The seed already introduces di and ke, so next establish dari with clear location/source contrasts, and broaden ordinary motion sentences. Then develop demonstrative noun phrases, counting, noun reduplication and early temporal expression while reusing previous structures. The meN- pattern is previewed in melihat, membaca and menulis; systematic explanation belongs to ID-56.
