POLYGLOT CURRICULUM MANIFEST

Status: Current
Encoding: UTF-8

1. PURPOSE

This manifest is the compact progress and allocation record for the
Polyglot curriculum repository.

It does not define pedagogy. The Polyglot Curriculum Charter governs
system-wide curriculum design, and each language syllabus governs that
language's canonical sequence. The language CSV is authoritative for
cards that actually exist and for their row order.

The manifest records stable language ID allocations, current card-file
extent, syllabus version, and a concise curriculum-frontier checkpoint.
Routine card generation may update this manifest without amending the
Charter or a language syllabus.

2. ID ALLOCATION

Polyglot currently uses integer language blocks of 100,000 IDs.

Language    Reserved ID range    Current used IDs     Current cards
French      100000-199999        100000-100599        600
German      200000-299999        200000-200139        140
Japanese    300000-399999        300000-300119        120
Polish      400000-499999        400000-400050        51
Hungarian   500000-599999        500000-500035        36
Turkish     600000-699999        600000-600049        50
Arabic      700000-799999        700000-700049        50
Ojibwe      800000-899999        800000-800099        100

These ranges are reservations, not curriculum-size targets. Unused IDs
within a language's block remain reserved for that language. Stable IDs
must not be reused or renumbered merely because curriculum units are
later split, merged, reordered, or reclassified.

Before assigning an ID block to a new language, consult this manifest
and reserve a new non-overlapping range deliberately.

ARABIC
Syllabus: Syllabi/Polyglot_Arabic_Syllabus_v0.1.txt
Syllabus version: 0.1
Card file: Cards/Arabic.csv
Cards: 50
Used IDs: 700000-700049
Checkpoint: Arabic has begun with a 50-card Modern Standard Arabic seed
prototype under the Charter v0.8 prototype-first workflow. MSA is the
unmarked backbone of the Arabic curriculum; substantial spoken regional
content is intended to begin earlier and develop more extensively than
regional variation in most other Polyglot languages, with regional cards

Future extension should continue from the current CSV, test the Arabic-
appropriate verb citation strategy before a large verbal batch, and use
the syllabus as a path rather than a sealed unit sequence.


OJIBWE
Syllabus: Syllabi/Polyglot_Ojibwe_Syllabus_v0.1.txt
Syllabus version: 0.1
Card file: Cards/Ojibwe.csv
Cards: 100
Used IDs: 800000-800099
Checkpoint: Ojibwe has advanced to a deliberately exploratory 100-card
prototype under the Charter v0.8 prototype-first workflow. Double Vowel
orthography is canonical, with the University of Minnesota Ojibwe
People's Dictionary serving as the principal initial lexical/reference
anchor but not the exclusive source. The opening establishes noun
animacy through plural and demonstrative contrasts, paired
inanimate/animate predicate verbs, and the animate/inanimate
demonstrative system. The second fifty begins interrogatives and personal
pronouns, noun-derived verbs of being, first-person VAI morphology,
mino-ayaa and a polar question, further animate/inanimate lexical
evidence, and a tightly controlled introduction to dependent nouns and
possession through =niijaanis- and =iinizis-. The English demonstrative
prompts mark animacy only; English this/these and that/those already mark
number. Ojibwe remains the first explicit test of the Charter's
documentation-limited-language policy: proceed as deeply as reliable
evidence permits and do not manufacture unsupported forms or usage.
Review and audit this 100-card prototype against Ojibwe syllabus v0.1
before generating a substantially larger batch.

4. MAINTENANCE RULES

Update this manifest when:
- a new language receives an ID allocation;
- a language card CSV is created;
- routine card generation materially advances a curriculum frontier;
- a syllabus version changes in a way relevant to the checkpoint;
- an ID allocation convention changes.

Do not use this manifest to replace the language syllabus or CSV.
For routine extension, the relevant syllabus and current language CSV
remain the primary sources. The manifest is a checkpoint and
cross-language allocation record.

Card counts are descriptive only. They are not completion quotas and do
not determine when a syllabus unit is established.
