POLYGLOT CURRICULUM MILESTONE EXAMPLES

Version: 0.1
Status: Placeholder / For Later Review
Encoding: UTF-8

1. PURPOSE

This document is an illustrative companion to the Polyglot Curriculum
Charter and Card Generation Protocol.

It is not a syllabus, proficiency scale, CEFR mapping, card-count target,
or generation rule. Its purpose is to give future curriculum authors and
generators concrete examples of what Polyglot curricula may look like at
different approximate depths.

Milestone examples are descriptive targets, not sequencing requirements.
IDs indicate approximate curriculum depth. They do not reserve those IDs,
impose proficiency boundaries, require particular structures to appear by
those points, or imply that difficulty must increase monotonically.

Different languages may reach comparable curriculum maturity through very
different structures. Examples are anchors, not templates.

This v0.1 file is deliberately rudimentary. It exists as a controlled
placeholder to be reviewed and expanded as more canonical decks reach
useful milestones.

2. APPROXIMATE MILESTONES

~50 CARDS — SEED / PROTOTYPE

Typical purpose:
- establish a small reusable lexical base;
- enter productive target-language structure quickly;
- expose important language-specific properties;
- reveal design questions that should shape the syllabus.

Current empirical examples include French, German, Japanese and Arabic.
Their openings are intentionally not parallel.

French quickly develops articles, copular clauses and gender/agreement.

German develops articles, copular clauses and early case-sensitive noun
phrase behavior.

Japanese introduces useful verbs early so that particles and productive
verbal clauses can emerge almost immediately.

Arabic demonstrates that a substantial opening can develop without verbs:
present nominal predication, gender/agreement, definiteness, attributive
versus predicative adjectives, demonstratives, pronouns and a first polar
question already provide meaningful structure.

These differences are evidence that a common Polyglot philosophy does not
require a common card template.

~600 CARDS — ROBUST BEGINNER CURRICULUM

Illustrative maturity:
- core sentence architecture is productive;
- common questions and negation are established or well underway;
- ordinary high-frequency vocabulary has grown substantially;
- major early inflectional/agreement systems are reusable;
- at least some time reference beyond the simplest present/nonpast system
  is normally opening where relevant;
- cards increasingly combine previously established structures.

French currently provides the principal empirical example at this depth.
Other languages should be added here when their canonical decks genuinely
reach comparable maturity.

~800 CARDS — EARLY INTERMEDIATE EXPANSION

Illustrative maturity:
- multiple grammatical systems interact in ordinary sentences;
- reference, comparison, modality, reflexive/pronominal systems or their
  language-specific analogues are developing;
- past/nonpast or other major temporal/aspectual contrasts are increasingly
  reusable where relevant;
- vocabulary expansion is less tightly constrained by one new structure at
  a time.

The exact systems represented here will differ sharply by language.

~2,500 CARDS — MATURE INTERMEDIATE CURRICULUM

Illustrative maturity:
- most high-value grammatical architecture is established or actively
  interacting;
- complex clauses and discourse relationships are ordinary curriculum
  material;
- tense, aspect, mood, case, voice, particles, agreement, honorifics or
  other language-specific systems combine rather than appearing as isolated
  lessons;
- vocabulary, collocation and idiom increasingly share the organizing role
  previously dominated by grammar.

~10,000 CARDS — BROAD MATURE CURRICULUM

Illustrative maturity:
- grammar has largely ceased to be the main source of curriculum expansion;
- common and educated vocabulary breadth dominates;
- collocation, phraseology, register, semantic precision and institutional
  language are major strands;
- mature grammar is reused freely;
- later cards need not be structurally harder than earlier cards.

~20,000–25,000+ CARDS — EDUCATED-ADULT LONG TAIL

Illustrative maturity:
- broad educated-adult lexical coverage;
- lower-frequency but reasonably encountered vocabulary;
- fine semantic distinctions and near-synonyms;
- mature idiom, collocation and figurative language;
- formal, institutional, academic and culturally important registers;
- regional, historical or literary material where relevant to modern
  educated competence;
- continuing breadth without deliberate obscurity.

There is no requirement that a language reach a particular card count, and
there is no absolute terminal milestone. The educated-adult complete set is
a direction and practical asymptote, not an exhaustive dictionary.

3. SAMPLE-CARD POLICY

Future versions should add small groups of representative real cards at
these milestones.

A useful default is approximately 5–8 cards per language per milestone,
selected to show the character of the curriculum rather than to prove that
a checklist has been completed.

Prefer actual canonical cards over hypothetical examples. Where a
hypothetical example is genuinely useful, label it explicitly as
illustrative and do not allow it to become an accidental requirement.

At the time of v0.1:
- several languages provide genuine seed-level evidence;
- French provides the strongest genuine evidence at higher current depths;
- German, Japanese, Arabic and other languages should populate later
  milestones as their real decks grow.

4. INTERPRETATION RULES

1. Similar card counts do not imply parallel grammar.
2. A milestone is evidence of approximate curriculum maturity, not learner
   mastery.
3. A language may encounter an advanced-looking structure early because it
   is basic to that language.
4. A language may postpone a structure prominent in another language
   because it is irrelevant, absent, or depends on different prerequisites.
5. Orthographic complexity may affect early pacing without changing the
   small-seed/productive-structure principle.
6. Regional or register variation may enter at radically different depths
   in different languages. Arabic is an important example.
7. Difficulty need not rise with card ID. Mature curricula often expand
   sideways into breadth and precision.
8. The current canonical CSV and syllabus remain authoritative for each
   language. This document never overrides them.

5. FUTURE REVIEW

When enough empirical material exists, review whether the milestone
descriptions above remain useful and whether the approximate depths should
be changed, merged or supplemented.

Likely future work:
- add representative real French cards at ~600, ~800 and later depths;
- add German and Japanese examples as those decks mature;
- add Arabic examples showing both MSA development and, eventually,
  labelled regional strands;
- compare structurally related languages to show useful reuse without
  mechanical copying;
- decide whether the seed/prototype milestone belongs permanently in this
  document or is sufficiently covered by the Protocol.
