PURPOSE

This syllabus defines the canonical pedagogical path for modern Ukrainian
in Polyglot. It specifies content, prerequisite order, and card strategy,
not learner progress. Routine card generation changes the manifest and CSV,
not this syllabus.

Vocabulary and lexical usage grow continuously. Grammar units define
structural progression but do not freeze vocabulary. Unit sizes follow
completion standards rather than fixed card quotas.

The established Polyglot syllabi provide structural and editorial models,
but Ukrainian is organized according to its own grammar, usage, orthography,
and prerequisite relationships. Similarity to Russian may inform comparison
but does not make Russian the structural authority for Ukrainian.


TERMINOLOGY

INTRODUCED — A form, word, construction, orthographic element, or
distinction has begun to be demonstrated.

ESTABLISHED — The canonical curriculum has demonstrated the item
sufficiently for its basic operation to be assumed later. This describes
curriculum state, not learner mastery.

REUSABLE — An established item or structure may appear naturally in later
cards without being the principal teaching objective.

LEXICAL ANCHOR — A word deliberately selected to make a grammatical,
semantic, morphological, or orthographic pattern visible. Anchors do not
limit additional vocabulary.

CANONICAL SEQUENCE — The static pedagogical ordering of cards and concepts.
Scheduling frequency may adapt to performance; curriculum order does not.

COMPLETION STANDARD — The coverage required before a unit's principal
concept may be treated as established.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED — Material postponed because it would obscure the
current pattern, depends on later knowledge, or deserves separate treatment.


COURSE-WIDE UKRAINIAN PRINCIPLES

1.  Use ordinary contemporary Ukrainian Cyrillic in every Target from the
    first card. There is no transliteration or alphabet-name bootstrap.
2.  InputHint is normally blank. Ukrainian is entered directly with a
    Ukrainian keyboard; unfamiliar script, pronunciation, or keyboard layout
    does not by itself justify an InputHint.
3.  Begin with useful words and naturally complete utterances. Use
    conventional citation forms and natural Ukrainian syntax rather than
    mirroring English mechanically.
4.  Every Target should be natural for its intended meaning and register
    and worth retaining permanently. Beginner difficulty is controlled by
    selection and sequencing, not beginnerized Ukrainian.
5.  Noun gender, animacy, case, adjective agreement, verbal aspect, stress,
    and stem alternations are longitudinal systems rather than one-time
    paradigm lessons.
6.  Ukrainian has seven cases. Case functions are established through
    controlled examples before paradigms are broadened; the vocative is
    treated as an ordinary productive part of modern Ukrainian, not an
    ornamental late exception.
7.  Present-tense nominal identification normally permits a zero copula.
    є is taught according to its actual existential, possessive, emphatic,
    and contextual uses rather than as a mandatory equivalent of English
    "is."
8.  Imperfective and perfective aspect develop longitudinally. Aspect
    partners are treated as lexical and morphological relationships, not as
    mechanically interchangeable translations.
9.  Ukrainian imperfective future includes both analytic forms with бути
    and the synthetic future. Both belong to ordinary modern competence and
    receive explicit treatment.
10. Word order is flexible but not arbitrary. Neutral ordering, information
    structure, focus, rhythm, and discourse context are developed
    progressively rather than treating all permutations as equivalent.
11. Ukrainian-specific phonological and orthographic alternations, including
    у/в, і/й, з/із/зі, apostrophe use, soft sign, and relevant consonant or
    vowel alternations, are learned through real language and later
    systematized explicitly.
12. ґ and г are distinct letters and sounds in standard Ukrainian and are
    preserved according to conventional spelling.
13. Productive patterns precede exceptions where possible. High-frequency
    irregular forms may appear early when ordinary language requires them.
14. Grammar is normally learned through controlled examples rather than
    terminology questions.
15. A card's single Target must be natural and sufficiently determined by
    its English prompt. Prompts should disambiguate aspect, case, lexical
    choice, register, motion, or information structure where necessary.
16. High-frequency words develop longitudinally across senses,
    constructions, case frames, collocations, idioms, and register.
17. Contemporary broadly neutral standard Ukrainian is the initial
    reference variety. Common colloquial, regional, historical, literary,
    and contact-influenced forms are introduced later and described without
    displacing the standard.
18. Russian similarity must not encourage Russian calques, Russian
    orthography, or assumptions that cognate words have identical meaning,
    government, aspect, stress, or register.


STAGE 1 — AUTHENTIC CYRILLIC AND CORE LEXICON

UK-01 — Core concrete nouns and authentic Ukrainian Cyrillic

PREREQUISITES: None.
OBJECTIVE: Begin ordinary Ukrainian spelling and high-value concrete nouns
together.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: дім, книга, стіл, вода, місто, людина.
CARD STRATEGY: English meaning → natural Ukrainian citation form. Select
useful vocabulary rather than constructing an alphabet inventory.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: People, objects, food, animals, and places.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Alphabet-name quizzes, transliterated Targets, full
declension, and detached spelling drills.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Enough varied useful Cyrillic vocabulary is established
to support the first short utterances.
LEADS TO: UK-03–UK-10.

UK-02 — Core qualities and actions

PREREQUISITES: UK-01 begun.
OBJECTIVE: Build useful descriptive and action vocabulary without forcing
premature agreement or conjugation.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: добре, погано, багато, читати, знати, працювати.
CARD STRATEGY: Use natural standalone adverbs and verb infinitives where
appropriate; adjective citation forms may appear lexically before their
agreement system is taught.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Daily activities, common qualities, and concrete
actions.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full adjective paradigms, full conjugation, and an
aspect-pair requirement for every new verb.
COMPLETION STANDARD: A varied lexical base supports the first predicates
without a dense run of abstract vocabulary.
LEADS TO: UK-05, UK-08, UK-11.

UK-03 — Personal and demonstrative reference

PREREQUISITES: UK-01.
OBJECTIVE: Introduce я, ти, він, вона, воно, ми, ви, вони and це where
naturally used.
CARD STRATEGY: Distinguish singular informal ти from plural or polite ви
through prompt context; do not mechanically retain pronouns in later
sentences when Ukrainian naturally omits or deemphasizes them.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Oblique pronoun forms, full demonstrative agreement,
and fine pragmatic distinctions.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic person, number, gender, and reference operate in
short utterances.
LEADS TO: UK-04, UK-08.

UK-04 — Present identification and zero copula

PREREQUISITES: UK-01, UK-03.
OBJECTIVE: Establish natural nominal and adjectival predicates without
inserting a mandatory present-tense copula.
CORE PATTERNS: Це дім. Вона лікарка. Дім великий.
CARD STRATEGY: Contrast identification, occupation, and description.
Introduce є only where Ukrainian naturally calls for it.
VOCABULARY DEVELOPMENT: Occupations, identities, and common descriptions.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Past/future forms of бути and the wider discourse
distribution of є.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Several natural predicate types establish the ordinary
zero-copula pattern without English calques.
LEADS TO: UK-05–UK-07.

UK-05 — Noun gender and basic adjective agreement

PREREQUISITES: UK-01, UK-04.
OBJECTIVE: Make masculine, feminine, and neuter visible through useful nouns
and attributive adjectives.
LEXICAL ANCHORS: новий дім, нова книга, нове місто.
CARD STRATEGY: Contrast one adjective across genders before widening the
lexicon.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Oblique cases, plural agreement, possessive
paradigms, and less productive ending classes.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Basic singular nominative agreement is productive
across all three genders.
LEADS TO: UK-14, UK-31.

UK-06 — Basic negation and yes/no questions

PREREQUISITES: UK-04.
OBJECTIVE: Establish не and natural yes/no question formation.
CARD STRATEGY: Contrast positive and negative predicates, then declarative
and question contexts with unambiguous English prompts.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Negative pronouns, genitive under negation,
scope-sensitive negation, and emphatic constructions.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Negation and basic questions work across several
natural utterance types.
LEADS TO: UK-07, UK-12.

UK-07 — Existence and possession with є / немає

PREREQUISITES: UK-03, UK-04, UK-06.
OBJECTIVE: Establish common existence and possession patterns such as
У мене є книга and У мене немає книги, while distinguishing contexts in
which є is naturally absent.
CARD STRATEGY: Treat the constructions as Ukrainian patterns rather than
literal equivalents of English "have."
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full genitive paradigms and the complete range of
existential word order.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Several possessors and concrete referents establish
affirmative and negative possession/existence naturally.
LEADS TO: UK-21, UK-26.

UK-08 — Present tense: first productive verb patterns

PREREQUISITES: UK-02, UK-03.
OBJECTIVE: Establish finite present forms through a small coherent set of
imperfective verbs.
CARD STRATEGY: Contrast persons and numbers systematically before adding
more stem alternations; permit natural subject omission.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Perfective future interpretation, past tense,
imperative, and dense irregularity.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Person/number marking is productive across several
verbs and natural short sentences.
LEADS TO: UK-09–UK-11.

UK-09 — Present tense: additional patterns and common irregulars

PREREQUISITES: UK-08.
OBJECTIVE: Broaden present-tense conjugation to additional productive
patterns and indispensable high-frequency irregular or stem-changing verbs.
CARD STRATEGY: Establish coherent regular contrasts before exceptions; do
not present a single undifferentiated conjugation table.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Major present endings and selected high-frequency
irregularities operate across varied contexts.
LEADS TO: UK-10, UK-13.

UK-10 — Early sentence synthesis

PREREQUISITES: UK-01–UK-09.
OBJECTIVE: Combine identity, description, negation, possession, questions,
and present actions with fresh useful vocabulary.
CARD STRATEGY: Keep Ukrainian word order natural and vary information focus
only where the prompt remains exact-match compatible.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Short sentences use a growing lexicon and all
established patterns without dependence on a tiny anchor set.
LEADS TO: UK-11 onward.


STAGE 2 — SENTENCE TOOLS AND FIRST CASE FUNCTIONS

UK-11 — Question words

OBJECTIVE: Establish хто, що, де, куди, звідки, коли, як, чому and other
high-value interrogatives progressively in natural questions.

UK-12 — Negation with verbs

OBJECTIVE: Extend не across verbal predicates and distinguish ordinary
verbal negation from немає and later negative-pronoun systems.

UK-13 — Infinitive complements and early modal constructions

OBJECTIVE: Establish common patterns with хотіти, могти, треба, потрібно,
можна and related meanings one distinction at a time.

UK-14 — Nominative plural and adjective agreement

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive plural noun and adjective patterns before
less common stem and stress exceptions.

UK-15 — Accusative singular: inanimate direct objects

OBJECTIVE: Establish prototypical direct objects, beginning with forms that
make the nominative/accusative relationship transparent.

UK-16 — Accusative singular: animacy and visible contrasts

OBJECTIVE: Establish animate masculine patterns and the role of animacy
through concrete lexical contrasts rather than an abstract table.

UK-17 — Locative singular with в/у and на

OBJECTIVE: Establish common location expressions and the locative case in
controlled spatial contexts.
CARD STRATEGY: Teach lexical choice of в/у versus на separately from the
case ending itself.

UK-18 — Direction with в/у and на + accusative

OBJECTIVE: Contrast location with destination using familiar places and
motion vocabulary.

UK-19 — Instrumental accompaniment with з

OBJECTIVE: Introduce a high-value instrumental function through transparent
"with" relationships before broadening instrumental morphology.

UK-20 — Vocative: direct address

OBJECTIVE: Establish the productive modern vocative with names, kinship
terms, common forms of address, and suitable nouns.
CARD STRATEGY: Use actual address contexts so the form has a communicative
function rather than appearing as a detached seventh-case table.
DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Rare, stylistically marked, and unstable variants.
COMPLETION STANDARD: Common masculine and feminine vocative patterns are
established sufficiently for ordinary direct address.
LEADS TO: UK-43, UK-91.

UK-21 — Genitive singular: absence, possession, source, and quantity

OBJECTIVE: Broaden the genitive from немає into several high-value
functions using controlled noun classes.

UK-22 — Dative singular: recipients and experiencers

OBJECTIVE: Establish recipients and common experiencer constructions,
including age and selected impersonal meanings where appropriate.

UK-23 — Basic place, time, and manner adverbs

OBJECTIVE: Expand useful sentence tools such as тут/тут, там, зараз, потім,
сьогодні, завтра and common manner expressions in natural positions.

UK-24 — First case and sentence synthesis

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Introduce each new case function with a limited set of
nouns whose forms expose the pattern. Widen gender and stem classes after
the function is visible. Do not ask for case names as answers.

COMPLETION STANDARD: The principal early functions are established across
multiple lexical representatives and can combine in short natural
sentences.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Full plural oblique paradigms, exhaustive
prepositional government, rare declensions, and unrestricted word-order
variation.


STAGE 3 — PAST, FUTURE, AND FIRST ASPECT CONTRASTS

UK-25 — Imperfective past

OBJECTIVE: Establish past forms and gender/number marking with familiar
imperfective verbs.

UK-26 — Past and future of бути

OBJECTIVE: Establish був/була/було/були and future forms in natural
identification, location, existence, and other contexts.

UK-27 — Analytic imperfective future

OBJECTIVE: Establish буду + infinitive across persons with familiar
imperfective verbs.

UK-28 — Synthetic imperfective future

OBJECTIVE: Establish forms such as читатиму as an ordinary Ukrainian future
strategy and relate them to the analytic future without claiming complete
pragmatic identity in every context.
CARD STRATEGY: Begin with transparent familiar verbs and vary person
systematically.

UK-29 — First imperfective/perfective contrasts

OBJECTIVE: Introduce transparent aspectual pairs in contexts contrasting
ongoing/repeated/process meanings with completed bounded events.

UK-30 — Perfective future

OBJECTIVE: Establish that perfective non-past forms ordinarily refer to
future completed events and contrast them with imperfective present and
both imperfective future strategies.

UK-31 — Aspect in questions, negation, and expected result

OBJECTIVE: Begin context-sensitive aspect selection without reducing the
system to English tense labels.

UK-32 — Common nontransparent aspect partners

OBJECTIVE: Introduce high-frequency aspectual relationships that must be
learned substantially as lexical families.

UK-33 — Past/future/aspect synthesis

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: English prompts should provide enough temporal,
iterative, process, or result context to elicit one intended aspect and
future construction.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Aspect is visible across several verb families, and
past plus both imperfective future strategies and perfective future are
structurally established.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Exhaustive prefix semantics, fine discourse-level
aspect choice, and low-frequency aspectual derivation.


STAGE 4 — CASE SYSTEM IN USE

UK-34 — Accusative plural and animacy

OBJECTIVE: Extend direct-object marking and animacy across plural nouns,
pronouns, and established agreement patterns.

UK-35 — Genitive plural

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive genitive plural patterns, then common
zero endings and irregularities in quantity, absence, and other established
functions.

UK-36 — Dative plural and agreement

OBJECTIVE: Extend recipient and experiencer patterns to plural noun phrases.

UK-37 — Instrumental singular beyond accompaniment

OBJECTIVE: Establish means/instrument, selected predicate complements, and
other high-value instrumental uses as distinct constructions.

UK-38 — Instrumental plural and agreement

OBJECTIVE: Broaden instrumental morphology to plural noun phrases.

UK-39 — Locative plural

OBJECTIVE: Extend established location/aboutness functions to plural forms
and relevant prepositions.

UK-40 — Genitive after numbers and quantity

OBJECTIVE: Establish Ukrainian numeral-governed noun patterns progressively,
including distinctions after один, два/три/чотири, higher numerals, and
quantifiers.

UK-41 — Core preposition government

OBJECTIVE: Establish frequent prepositions and their case frames in semantic
families rather than as an arbitrary list.

UK-42 — Personal pronouns in oblique cases

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency pronoun forms through already known case
functions, including prepositional variants where required.

UK-43 — Possessive and demonstrative pronouns through cases

OBJECTIVE: Develop мій, твій, наш, ваш, свій, цей, той and related forms
through established case functions.

UK-44 — Vocative expansion and address patterns

OBJECTIVE: Broaden vocative morphology, adjective+noun address, names,
patronymics where appropriate, titles, and socially natural address.

UK-45 — Case synthesis in connected everyday sentences

COMMON CARD STRATEGY: Contrast semantic roles, prepositions, and endings in
small families. Mix cases only after each principal function is visible.
Maintain animacy and agreement contrasts.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Productive singular and plural case forms plus
indispensable irregulars support natural use of all seven cases in common
functions.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Rare historical endings, marginal variants, and
idiomatic government better learned with later lexical items.


STAGE 5 — VERBAL STRUCTURE, IMPERATIVE, REFLEXIVES, AND MOTION

UK-46 — Imperative

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive singular and plural/polite imperative forms
with familiar verbs before widening irregularity.

UK-47 — Aspect choice in requests and commands

OBJECTIVE: Develop aspect in instructions, requests, warnings, and negative
imperatives through context rather than one English gloss.

UK-48 — Reflexive -ся / -сь

OBJECTIVE: Establish common lexical, reciprocal, middle-like, and productive
reflexive uses while respecting ordinary placement and variation.

UK-49 — Impersonal and indefinite-personal constructions

OBJECTIVE: Establish common Ukrainian clauses that do not require an
English-style explicit subject.

UK-50 — іти / ходити and movement on foot

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency directional and habitual/repeated motion
contrasts through actual contexts.

UK-51 — їхати / їздити and movement by transport

OBJECTIVE: Establish the corresponding transport contrast and its lexical
frames.

UK-52 — Destination, source, route, and motion case frames

OBJECTIVE: Integrate motion with established cases and prepositions.

UK-53 — Prefixed motion verbs

OBJECTIVE: Develop common meanings such as arrival, departure, entry, exit,
approach, and passage with appropriate aspectual relationships.

UK-54 — Additional motion families

OBJECTIVE: Expand to high-frequency movement verbs by usefulness, keeping
directionality, repetition, and lexical meaning distinct.

UK-55 — Carrying and leading motion

OBJECTIVE: Establish common verbs for carrying, transporting, and leading,
with their argument structures and aspectual families.

UK-56 — Motion and aspect synthesis

COMPLETION STANDARD: Major combinations of means, direction, frequency,
prefix, case frame, and aspect can be recovered from context across varied
destinations and situations.


STAGE 6 — ADJECTIVES, PRONOUNS, COMPARISON, AND NUMBER

UK-57 — Full adjective declension

OBJECTIVE: Extend adjective agreement systematically across established
cases, genders, and numbers.

UK-58 — Possessive, demonstrative, interrogative, and relative agreement

OBJECTIVE: Consolidate adjective-like pronoun paradigms through meaningful
noun phrases rather than isolated tables.

UK-59 — Comparative forms

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive synthetic and analytic comparison and
natural comparative constructions.

UK-60 — Superlatives

OBJECTIVE: Establish най- and other ordinary superlative/degree strategies
with appropriate syntax.

UK-61 — Numerals in dates, time, prices, ages, and measurements

OBJECTIVE: Develop cardinal numerals in high-value real contexts while
expanding their case behaviour progressively.

UK-62 — Ordinals and agreement

OBJECTIVE: Establish ordinal morphology and agreement through dates,
sequence, floors, rankings, and other ordinary contexts.

UK-63 — Collective and other Ukrainian numeral patterns

OBJECTIVE: Introduce common collective numerals and other language-specific
quantity patterns where ordinary usage warrants them.

UK-64 — Indefinite, negative, and universal pronouns

OBJECTIVE: Establish productive forms and Ukrainian negative-concord
patterns through controlled contrasts.

UK-65 — Noun-phrase and quantification synthesis

COMPLETION STANDARD: Agreement, comparison, reference, and quantity operate
naturally across established case functions. Rare numeral variants remain
available for later lexical expansion.


STAGE 7 — COMPLEX SENTENCES AND INFORMATION STRUCTURE

UK-66 — Coordination

OBJECTIVE: Establish і, й, та, а, але, або, чи and their principal semantic
contrasts, including ordinary euphonic and stylistic distribution.

UK-67 — Core subordinate clauses

OBJECTIVE: Establish high-frequency complement, causal, conditional, and
temporal links such as що, тому що, якщо, коли.

UK-68 — Relative який

OBJECTIVE: Establish antecedent agreement and clause-internal case one role
at a time.

UK-69 — Other relatives and correlatives

OBJECTIVE: Expand хто, що, де, куди, звідки and related relative/correlative
structures.

UK-70 — Reported statements and indirect questions

OBJECTIVE: Establish common complement patterns and indirect-question
constructions, including чи where appropriate.

UK-71 — Conditions and би / б

OBJECTIVE: Establish real and counterfactual conditional patterns, then
develop the clitic variants according to ordinary phonological context.

UK-72 — Flexible word order and information structure

OBJECTIVE: Distinguish neutral orders from marked topic/focus arrangements
and show how context affects constituent placement.

UK-73 — Negation scope and contrastive focus

OBJECTIVE: Develop meanings that cannot be captured by treating не as a
simple word-level switch.

UK-74 — Clause and information-structure synthesis

CARD STRATEGY: English prompts must provide discourse context when focus,
word order, or conjunction choice affects the intended Target.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Major clause types operate across varied pronoun and
case roles, with natural Ukrainian information packaging visible.


STAGE 8 — EXPANDED ASPECT, VOICE, AND VERBAL DERIVATION

UK-75 — Aspect in repeated events, duration, and completed sequences

UK-76 — Aspect under negation, modal constructions, and infinitive
complements

UK-77 — Prefixes and lexical meaning

OBJECTIVE: Distinguish productive aspectual relationships from prefixes that
create substantially new lexical verbs.

UK-78 — Secondary imperfectives and derived aspect families

OBJECTIVE: Develop productive and high-frequency derived imperfectives
without implying that every aspect pair is mechanically predictable.

UK-79 — Passive constructions with -ся and passive participles

OBJECTIVE: Establish natural Ukrainian passive strategies with attention to
register and competition with active or impersonal alternatives.

UK-80 — Passive participles and result/state distinctions

OBJECTIVE: Develop productive passive participial morphology and its
adjectival or resultative uses.

UK-81 — Active participial meanings and preferred modern alternatives

OBJECTIVE: Teach forms actually encountered while making visible that some
active-participial patterns are limited, stylistically marked, or often
replaced by relative clauses/adjectival constructions in contemporary
standard Ukrainian.
CARD STRATEGY: Do not import Russian participial productivity into
Ukrainian.

UK-82 — Verbal adverbs (дієприслівники)

OBJECTIVE: Establish common adverbial-participle forms, aspect relations,
subject control, punctuation, and register.

UK-83 — Verbal morphology synthesis in connected prose

COMPLETION STANDARD: Productive aspectual, voice, participial, and
derivational systems are represented across several verb families, while
recognition-oriented or stylistically restricted forms are clearly
distinguished from ordinary productive usage.


STAGE 9 — WORD FORMATION, EUPHONY, ORTHOGRAPHY, AND PRONUNCIATION

UK-84 — Productive noun, adjective, and verb derivation

OBJECTIVE: Develop useful word families and recurring affixes without
assuming semantic transparency.

UK-85 — Diminutives, augmentatives, and expressive derivation

OBJECTIVE: Establish common productive forms together with their pragmatic
and emotional force.

UK-86 — Prefix meaning and lexicalization

OBJECTIVE: Revisit prefixes across lexical families, separating productive
meaning tendencies from conventional lexical meanings.

UK-87 — у / в alternation

OBJECTIVE: Establish the major euphonic and contextual factors governing
у/в while recognizing lexicalized forms and stylistic variation.

UK-88 — і / й / та and related euphonic alternation

OBJECTIVE: Develop ordinary conjunction and glide alternations as features
of fluent contemporary Ukrainian rather than arbitrary spelling swaps.

UK-89 — з / із / зі and phonological ease

OBJECTIVE: Establish common prepositional variants and their distribution
through real phrases.

UK-90 — Apostrophe, soft sign, and йотовані letters

OBJECTIVE: Systematize spelling and pronunciation patterns already
encountered in vocabulary, including distinctions that affect morphology
or word recognition.

UK-91 — г / ґ and other Ukrainian sound-letter distinctions

OBJECTIVE: Consolidate language-specific orthographic contrasts in real
lexical items without transliteration cards.

UK-92 — Consonant and vowel alternations in morphology

OBJECTIVE: Develop productive alternations appearing in declension,
conjugation, derivation, and comparison.

UK-93 — Stress and pronunciation

OBJECTIVE: Develop stress movement, vowel/consonant realization, and other
pronunciation features that materially affect comprehension or production;
use external audio/reference support where useful.

UK-94 — Orthographic conventions and punctuation

OBJECTIVE: Establish capitalization, punctuation, quotation/direct-speech
conventions, abbreviations, and other ordinary written norms.

UK-95 — Derivational and literacy synthesis

COMPLETION STANDARD: Common productive word families, euphonic alternations,
orthographic rules, and pronunciation-sensitive patterns operate in natural
words and sentences. Conventional spelling is never replaced with
transcription to simplify production.


STAGE 10 — PHRASEOLOGY, REGISTER, AND NATURAL USAGE

UK-96 — High-frequency verbs across senses and case frames

OBJECTIVE: Develop common verbs longitudinally across their major senses,
aspect families, complements, constructions, and collocations.

UK-97 — Conventional verb-noun and adjective-noun collocations

UK-98 — Spatial, temporal, and abstract preposition choices

OBJECTIVE: Expand beyond introductory case rules into conventional lexical
government and semantic contrasts.

UK-99 — Particles and stance

OBJECTIVE: Develop high-frequency particles and discourse words in context,
including pragmatic force that lacks simple English equivalents.

UK-100 — Politeness, address, ти / ви, names, and patronymics

OBJECTIVE: Integrate pronoun choice, vocative forms, names, patronymics,
titles, and social distance in contemporary contexts.

UK-101 — Conversational ellipsis, responses, and spoken discourse

UK-102 — Neutral, informal, and formal written choices

OBJECTIVE: Develop register-sensitive vocabulary and constructions without
making bureaucratic or literary language the default.

UK-103 — Russian-Ukrainian contact and interference

OBJECTIVE: Establish important distinctions where widespread contact makes
Russian-like forms, calques, cognates, stress patterns, government, or
semantic assumptions a realistic source of confusion.
CARD STRATEGY: Teach correct Ukrainian through positive contextual contrasts;
do not turn the curriculum into a catalogue of errors or treat ordinary
variation as contamination.

UK-104 — Idioms, proverbs, and figurative constructions of ordinary reach

UK-105 — Phraseology and register synthesis

COMPLETION STANDARD: High-frequency constructions, collocations, pragmatic
patterns, common polysemy, and socially relevant register distinctions have
systematic breadth rather than token representation. These domains continue
expanding in all later stages.

CARD STRATEGY: Give concrete context and register in prompts. Separate valid
alternative constructions into distinct objectives when exact matching
requires it.

STAGE 12 — EDUCATED-ADULT LEXICON

COMMON OBJECTIVE: Approach the breadth, precision, phraseology, and
orthographic competence expected of a typical educated adult.

CARD STRATEGY: Use mature grammar freely. Continue developing aspect,
government, preferred combinations, register, polysemy, and semantic
boundaries rather than reducing later vocabulary to translation pairs.

DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: Narrow specialist terminology without broad
educated-adult relevance.

COMPLETION STANDARD: Coverage remains extensible, with substantial rather
than merely representative phraseology and lexical usage.

COMMON OBJECTIVE: Move toward the practical asymptote of modern Ukrainian
competence: vocabulary, grammar, morphology, constructions, orthography,
phraseology, registers, variation, and cultural-linguistic distinctions a
typical educated adult might reasonably know and encounter.

CARD STRATEGY: Select by exposure, usefulness, semantic range, and register.
Continue developing senses, constructions, collocations, idioms, case
frames, aspectual relationships, and orthographic knowledge.

COMPLETION STANDARD: There is intentionally no absolute terminal card or
word count. UK-145 remains expandable. "Complete" describes direction and
scope, not exhaustion of every attested Ukrainian word, dialect form, or
specialist expression.


GLOBAL COMPLETION AND REUSE RULES

1.  Unit completion establishes canonical coverage, not learner mastery.
2.  Established vocabulary and grammar become reusable; later cards
    increasingly combine them in natural sentences.
3.  New structural concepts normally appear first with controlled lexical
    load. Vocabulary continues growing through every stage.
4.  Ukrainian Cyrillic and conventional contemporary orthography apply from
    the first Target. InputHint is normally blank because Ukrainian is typed
    directly in its own script.
5.  Pronunciation, stress, keyboard familiarity, and alphabet reference may
    be supported externally without becoming transliteration cards.
6.  Later cards progressively develop common words across senses,
    constructions, aspect families, case frames, collocations, idioms, and
    register.
7.  The scheduler supplies extra repetition without changing canonical
    introduction order.
8.  Existing valid Ukrainian cards remain valid if later reconciled with
    this syllabus. Fill meaningful prerequisite gaps by convergence rather
    than restarting or renumbering sound cards.
9.  Future amendments may split, merge, reorder, or refine units without
    renumbering existing stable card IDs.
10. Parallel units within a stage may interleave once their prerequisites
    are satisfied; unit numbers state the default canonical path.
11. Similarity to Russian or another Slavic language may assist explanation
    and lexical selection, but Ukrainian usage and structure remain
    authoritative.


AMENDMENT POLICY

Change this syllabus only when the canonical Ukrainian curriculum changes.
Routine card generation and learner progress do not amend it.

An amendment should identify affected units, state the pedagogical change,
say whether it affects future cards only or requires existing-card
correction, increment the version, and preserve a concise amendment record.
